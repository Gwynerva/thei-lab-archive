## The walk

Out at nine with Dad, up onto the West Cliff, past the whalebones, then over the bridge, up the 199 steps and along the cliff top past the abbey to Saltwick Bay. Low tide, cold, the sea flat as a plate. Dad doesn't really do talking on a walk, he does pointing.

He pointed at a fence post, alone on the grass, with a big bite of nothing between it and the edge. When he was my age, he said, that fence had a whole field in front of it. He pointed at the path, which has been moved inland twice since I was little, and the old path just stops in the air. He pointed at the caravans, which are a row shorter than they were.

## The question

Then he stood at the top of the bay with his hands in his pockets and said:

> You're the one with the maps. You tell me how fast it's going.

And that was it. That's the dissertation. How fast is Whitby falling into the sea? Old maps, old aerial photos, maybe a drone if the university will lend me one, and the cliffs at Saltwick as the test. I could feel my head start to fizz on the walk back. Dad looked very pleased with himself and pretended not to be.
