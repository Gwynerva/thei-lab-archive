## The shop

Christmas Eve morning in the chandlery with Dad, as usual. The bell over the door, the paraffin stove, the smell of tarred rope and WD-40 and Dad's tea. Ropes, shackles, a fisherman who wanted a new pair of gloves for his wife ("she'll know they're from the chandlery, but she likes that"), and a man who wanted a single brass screw and talked to Dad about it for half an hour. Dad found him the screw. Dad always finds the screw.

::media[The rowing boats below the quay, outside the shop]{file="whitby-harbour-2"}

Dad closed at one, swept the floor, and gave me a coil of old cord he'd been saving "for your crafts". I have no crafts. He seems to think I will.

## Under the stairs

Then, looking for wrapping paper under the stairs at home, I found the cello. My school cello, in its case, where I left it at sixteen. I opened it. The strings are slack, the bow hair has gone a bit grey and the scratch on the front still looks like Norway. I played an open C and it sounded like a cow in a cave. Closed it again quickly. But I opened it. Mum saw me from the kitchen door and didn't say a thing, which for Mum is a speech.

Clare and Dev arrived at six with a car full of presents and a very large cheese.
