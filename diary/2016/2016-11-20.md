## At Nana's

Sunday lunch with Nana June at the Bay. Up the steep lane with Dad carrying the pudding, the rowan in her garden bare now but for a few red berries the birds haven't found. Inside it was exactly the same as always: the clock that runs four minutes fast on purpose, a quilt half-done on the frame by the window, and Grandad on the mantelpiece in his cap on the deck of a ship, squinting at whoever took the photo.

Roast chicken, then her curd tart, which nobody else in the family can make, though Mum has tried for thirty years. She wanted to hear all about the flat and Callum's cooking, and then all about the cliffs. When I told her the question, she nodded as if she'd been waiting for it. She said that when she was a girl there was a lane at the bottom of the village that went down to more houses, and now there's just the sea wall and the slipway. "The sea takes its time," she said, "and then it doesn't."

## Going

At the door, when we were going, she held my hand in both of hers and said:

:::quote[Nana June, on the doorstep]{align="center"}
Never trust a calm sea, pet. It's only thinking.
:::

::media[The Bay at dusk as we drove away]{file="robin-hoods-bay-6"}

On the train home I wrote it on the first page of a new section in this notebook, in capitals: HOW FAST IS WHITBY FALLING INTO THE SEA? Then I fell asleep against the window till Berwick.
