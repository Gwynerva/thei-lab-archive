The night of three pieces of coursework.

## The kitchen table

I set up in the kitchen because it's the warmest room and closest to the kettle. Laptop, the big screen borrowed from Priya's room, every lecture handout in piles on the floor in a ring around my chair, like a little stone circle of panic. A sign on the door: WORKING. FEED ME BUT DO NOT SPEAK.

Midnight, everything else cleared off the table, and a kettle that never got cold. The remote sensing report was done by then, almost respectably:

1. Remote sensing. Done at 23.52. Proud.

Then the long bit:

2. The Python script, which worked at one, stopped working at quarter to two for no reason, and started again at half two when I turned the laptop off and on again, which I'm not putting in the write-up.
3. The poster. Three in the morning, and I suddenly understood what colours the river should be. Did it in an hour and a half. It's the best thing I've made on this course.

## Fuel

Callum came in off his shift at one and made toasties without being asked, and then made more at four. Priya went to bed at eleven and left a note on the kettle: YOU ARE BRILLIANT. Total for the night: seven teas, two coffees, a family bag of crisps, four toasties, and a satsuma I found in my coat.

Sunrise at about half eight, grey, over the roofs. I watched it from the kitchen window, and the Forth was the colour of a pencil line.
