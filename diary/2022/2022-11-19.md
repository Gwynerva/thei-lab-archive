## The autumn concert

Seven o'clock in a church hall in Sandviken, two hundred chairs and every one of them taken, coats steaming on the backs, the smell of wet wool and coffee from the kitchen at the back.

We played the Elgar first. My first concert since Leith, my first in Norway, and for the first eight bars my bow shook so much it bounced on the string — and then the cellos came in under the violins in the second phrase, all seven of us, and I forgot to be frightened, because there was the sound, warm and enormous, and partly coming out of me.

::audio[The Serenade, the way it sounded in my head tonight]{file="music-strings-1" title="Elgar, Serenade for Strings"}

## In the audience

Jonas in the second row with a programme rolled into a tube, holding it like a telescope. Beside him, in a tweed jacket and a tie I've never seen, Sverre — who'd told me he doesn't go out in the evenings any more, and came, and clapped with his hands up by his face like a man at a football match. Anna and two people from work at the back.

## The Holberg

Then the Holberg Suite, Grieg's, in Grieg's own city, which made the old men in the front row sit up and hum along. In the Rigaudon we went fast and nobody fell off. The applause went on long enough that the conductor made us stand up twice.

::media[The cello at home afterwards, bow resting, done]{file="cello-1"}

Afterwards Sverre shook my hand and said in English, "Very good. Your grandfather would have liked it." He never met my grandfather. I didn't correct him. I think he meant someone's grandfather; everybody's.
