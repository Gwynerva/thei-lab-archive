## The shortest day

The sun rose at quarter past nine and set at three, and that was the day. At half past three, in the blue dark, we gathered at the white house: Jonas, Anna, two people from the workshop, my desk partner from the orchestra, and the girl from the flat below Sverre's, who is nine and had heard there would be candles. Twelve lanterns, a tealight in each, hung from poles and carried up the lane behind the house and on up the path towards Sandvikspilen: a slow line of coloured light going up the hill, like a very small procession, which it was.

Sverre watched from his window and raised his coffee cup.

## At the top

At the viewpoint we hung them along the railing in order, January to December, and stood with the city below us — every street lit, the harbour black, the ferries going out to the islands like lanterns themselves. January blew out. We lit it again. Someone poured *gløgg* from a thermos into paper cups. The girl from downstairs said October was the best one, the rain one, and I said that's the one I made with the most feeling.

## Turning

From tomorrow the days get longer: a minute, then two, then ten, then everything. I said so, out loud, to everyone, and they cheered, though they're Norwegian and have heard it every year of their lives.

We carried the lanterns down still lit. Twelve months of this year, in paper, in a line, coming down a hill in the dark.
