## Rain on the roof

Rain: day 6. I've wanted to do this since my first week in the flat: record the rain on the roof. The flat is at the top of the white house, under the slope of the roof, and when it rains properly it's like living inside a drum — every drop on the slates above the bedroom, the gutters running, the downpipe gargling by the window.

So last week I bought a small handheld recorder, a pair of headphones that close over the ears, and a fluffy windshield that Biscuit thinks is a rival. Tonight at eleven, with the lights off and the rain at its heaviest, I put the recorder on the sill under the roof window, pressed the red button, and sat absolutely still on the bed for twenty minutes.

## What I heard

Played back in the headphones it's different from being inside it: closer, bigger, more detailed. You can hear separate drops on the glass, the steady hiss behind them on the slates, a gust that throws a handful at the window all at once, the gutter overflowing on the left. At about minute fourteen there's a thump I think is Biscuit jumping off the wardrobe. I'm leaving it in.

## The kit

- the recorder, small enough for a coat pocket
- the windshield, which is essential, because the wind here is personal
- the headphones
- a notebook for the time, the place, the weather, and whatever else could be heard

I'll keep [a list of the kit](ref:page:recording-kit) as it grows. I'm calling the whole thing Sounds of Bergen. It starts with rain, because what else could it start with.
