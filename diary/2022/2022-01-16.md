Out on the fjord in the morning from a little beach north of the city, all eight of us zipped into drysuits like orange seals. Flat calm, two degrees, the water so clear I could see stones three metres down and so cold that when I put a hand in it hurt in the bones. Nobody fell in, which Jonas said was a first for a January group and "almost disappointing".

We paddled along the shore to a rock with a seal on it, who looked at us the way Biscuit looks at the hoover, and back.

::media[The fjord from the beach at ten in the morning, the light only just arriving]{file="byfjorden-1"}

Afterwards, packing up, I asked him — casually, I hope — whether people ever build their own. He said he's helped build eleven at the workshop: plywood ones, stitched and glued, five metres long; that it takes a winter and a summer and some of the next winter; and that there's space on the bench from February if I'm serious. I said I'd think about it. I have thought about nothing else since four o'clock.

Nana would say *never trust a calm sea*. Nana would also have been first into the boat.
