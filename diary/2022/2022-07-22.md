Took the cello to Leif, a luthier with a workshop up a lane near the old town: three rooms, instruments hanging from the ceiling like hams. He looked at the seam, ran his thumbnail along it, and said in broad Bergen dialect that it was "nothing, a good sign". The seams are glued weaker than the wood on purpose, he says, so that when the wood moves, the seam lets go before the top cracks. "The cello is protecting itself." Hide glue, clamps, a week.

::media[Leif's bench, bows waiting, his knives in rows on the wall]{file="cello-bow-2"}

He asked about the scratch shaped like Norway. I asked him please not to touch it. He said he wouldn't dream of it.
