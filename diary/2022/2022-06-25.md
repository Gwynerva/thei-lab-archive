A letter today, forwarded from Leith by Marta with a sticky note on the front — *thought you'd want this one by post, not by photo* — in Fergus's square handwriting, on the bindery's own paper.

He's closing Lowe Bindery. More than thirty years in the same shop in Stockbridge, and the lease is up, and his hands are "not what they were, though they were never what they ought to have been". The presses go to a college. The type goes to a man in Fife. The shop will be a coffee place, he hears, "which Stockbridge needs like a hole in the head".

:::quote[Fergus, in his letter]{align="center"}
A book is a promise that somebody will open it again. Keep making promises, Mira.
:::

In the envelope, wrapped in a piece of marbled paper: his bone folder, the one he used every Tuesday when he taught us, worn smooth and yellow and shaped to his hand. He says I'm to use it, not put it on a shelf.

::media[Fergus's bone folder, worn to the shape of his hand]{file="bookbinding-tools-1"}

I learned [the coptic stitch](ref:project:coptic-notebooks "Tuesday evenings in 2017, at his bench") from him on Tuesday evenings in that shop, with the rain on the window and the radiator banging. I'm going to write back on paper, by hand, with the old address on the envelope one last time:\
Fergus Lowe\
Lowe Bindery, for a few weeks more\
Stockbridge, Edinburgh
