Waffles.

He met me at the bottom of the funicular at eleven and we walked up instead, which was his idea and a good one, because walking next to someone is easier than sitting opposite them. Up through the wet woods on the zigzag road, snow at the top, the city laid out underneath in grey and white with the light coming and going on the water.

At the café at the top there were waffles: heart-shaped, five hearts to a round, with *brunost* sliced thin on top so that it melts, or with sour cream and strawberry jam. He had both. I had both. We talked for three hours: boats, Whitby, Hardanger where he grew up, his mother's apple trees, my dad's rope, Nana, the cello I don't play enough, whether a cat can be taught to like water (no).

::media[Heart-shaped, as they all are here, which felt like a lot for a first date]{file="waffles-1"}

We walked down in the dark with head torches. He walked me to the gate in Sandviken and said *takk for i dag* — thanks for today — and I said it back, and then neither of us left for a while.

I met him four weeks ago, upside down in a swimming pool, at [a kayak course](ref:event:kayak-course#:~:text=Jonas). I'm not going to write any more than this. I'm going to sit here with the cat and smile at the wall.
