## Christmas Eve, Norwegian style

In Norway the 24th is the day — the dinner, the presents, the candles, all of it on Christmas Eve — and Jonas, politely but firmly, let it be known that he could wait until the 25th for everything except one present each. So after tea we had the Norwegian bit: one present each, by the fire.

## The book

I gave Mum the book.

Dark green cloth over boards, her mother's colour; the title in small gold letters on the spine, *June and Arthur, 1952–1959*; inside, forty-one letters, Nana's and Grandad's, typed out as they wrote them and sewn by hand, with a photograph of Nana at twenty-three on the quay at Whitby, the year he went to sea, at the front.

She opened it and turned the pages for a long time without saying anything. Then she found the letter from March 1953, the one where Nana tells Grandad she's learned to make rowan jelly and he'd "better come home and eat it", and she read it out to all of us. Halfway through her voice went, and Dad took the book and read the rest, which I have never heard him do — read anything out loud, ever.

Then Mum said, "She'd have hated the fuss," and held the book against her chest until bedtime.

## The rest

Jonas gave Dad a hand-forged marlinspike from a smith in Hardanger. Dad gave Jonas a coil of tarred marline, which in our family is a declaration of something. Jonas gave me a small brass compass, old, in a wooden box, that he'd found in Bergen and cleaned. Mum gave Jonas a jar of rowan jelly. Everybody, it turned out, had given everybody something about the sea.
