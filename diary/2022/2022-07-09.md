Lyderhorn, the fifth of seven: the one out on the western edge of the city, standing over the islands with its feet in the fjord. Up from Loddefjord on a steep path through birch and heather, the last bit on bare rock. From the top, the whole outer coast — Askøy, Sotra, the channels and the skerries, the open sea a line of silver beyond them, the ferries going back and forth like shuttles on a loom.

Jonas says Lyderhorn is where Bergen's witches used to meet, in the old stories, to brew up storms for the ships. Today there were only us, two ravens, and a man with a thermos who'd been up every Saturday for thirty years and told us so.
