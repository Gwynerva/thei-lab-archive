## An audition, at thirty

Ten in the morning, a school hall in Sandviken, two people at a desk: the conductor, small, with reading glasses on a chain, and the principal cellist (retired from the city orchestra, white hair, a red jumper).

I was more nervous than for the B1. My hands were wet. I tuned for so long that the conductor asked kindly whether I'd like a glass of water, and I said yes, and then I couldn't drink it because I was holding the cello.

## The prelude

Then I played the Bach prelude, the G major, the one I learned in Leith the winter before lockdown and recorded the summer after. It went — mostly. The opening was shaky and the middle was good, and in the long climb towards the end I lost my place for about four bars and then found it again, and the last chord rang round the hall in a way that made me sit very still afterwards.

::audio[The prelude as it should sound; mine sounded about seventy per cent like this]{file="music-bach-cello-1" title="Prelude, Suite No. 1"}

## The Holberg

Then the excerpt from the Holberg, sight-reading the bowings, and some scales, and a conversation in Norwegian about why I stopped playing at sixteen and started again at twenty-five. I said because I'd missed it. The principal cellist said "*Godt svar*" — good answer.

## The result

They rang at six. I'm in. The back desk of the cellos, Tuesday nights, a concert in November. I walked home with the cello on my back and the whole of Sandviken smelling of cut grass and the sea.
