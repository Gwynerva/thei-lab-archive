We drove east along the E10 all day — tunnels, bridges, beaches of white sand under snow, which shouldn't be possible — and stopped for the night at a guesthouse above Uttakleiv, because the forecast said the sky would clear. It did.

## Half past nine

Priya saw it first, from the guesthouse window: a pale stripe over the mountains across the bay, like a cloud lit from inside. We put on everything we owned and walked down to the beach. By ten the stripe was green, and by quarter past it was moving — folding, rippling, pouring down the sky in curtains, faster than I thought light could move, with a pink hem along the bottom when it was strongest. It made no sound. It felt as if it should.

::media[The rocks at Uttakleiv at dusk, before the sky started]{file="uttakleiv-2"}

I lay on my back in the snow on the beach and Priya lay next to me and we said nothing for forty minutes except, now and then, "Look" — which was pointless, because there was nowhere else to look.

## Mira

Nana named me after a star that comes and goes. It isn't up in March — the Whale it lives in sets with the sun this time of year — but I looked for it anyway, out of habit, and said goodnight to it, wherever it was.

> The wonderful one. It comes and goes, but it always comes back.

That's what Grandad said about the star, Nana said, and she said it about me when I left for Edinburgh. I said it to the sky tonight. The sky was busy.
