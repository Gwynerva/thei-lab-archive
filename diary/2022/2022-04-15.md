## On the flat

*Langfredag*, Good Friday — the long Friday — and it was long.

Cross-country skis are two long thin planks that want to go in different directions, one each. Jonas showed me the movement — kick, glide, kick, glide — then glided away to show me what it looks like done properly, and I kicked and fell over. I fell eleven times before lunch. I counted. Once going uphill, which I'm told is unusual; once standing still, which I'm told is very unusual.

## Lunch on a snowdrift

At lunchtime everybody on the plateau stops, sits down in the snow out of the wind, facing the sun, and eats an orange and a particular chocolate bar, which every Norwegian has eaten on skis since they were three. Whole families of them in a row on the snowdrifts, like seals on a rock. We did too. It was the best orange I have ever eaten.

## The afternoon

After lunch something clicked — or rather glided. Ten metres without falling. Then twenty. Then a long gentle slope down to a frozen lake, which I went down sideways, shrieking, and stayed upright all the way to the bottom. Jonas cheered from the top as if I'd won something.

Bruises: several. Sunburn: nose. Pride: unreasonable.
