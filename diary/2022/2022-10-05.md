## The joke

At lunch — Rain: day 3 — someone in the office said it had rained every day since the summer, someone else said it hadn't, and they argued about it for twenty minutes using the evidence of their own coats. I said I could settle it with data. That was a mistake, because they said, "Go on, then."

## The app

So tonight I wrote a small app that counts rain days. It takes the daily rainfall for Bergen from [the Meteorological Institute](https://www.met.no "the Norwegian Meteorological Institute, which publishes its observations for anyone to use") and the readings from my own station on the balcony, decides whether each day was wet or dry, and shows one big number: how many days in a row it has rained. Today it says 3. Underneath is a strip of the last year, a square for each day, blue for wet and white for dry. It is mostly blue.

The rules, which were argued over at length and are now final:

:::list{counter="lower-roman"}
1. A day runs from midnight to midnight, Bergen time.
2. A day is dry if less than 0.2 mm falls, and wet if more than 0.5 mm does. Drizzle counts. Mist doesn't.
3. If my balcony gauge and the official station disagree, the balcony gauge wins, because it's mine.
4. If anyone disputes the count, Sverre's barometer is consulted, and its decision is final.
:::

I've put it on a page at work. Eleven people have looked at it already.
