## The stent

Into the heart unit at eight with Mum and Clare. Dad in a bed by the window, in a gown with boats on it — he noticed; he pointed them out; "wrong sort of boat" — looking smaller than he has ever looked and complaining about the tea.

They went in through his wrist with a thin wire, all the way up to his heart, and found the narrowing on a screen: one of the arteries on the front of the heart, most of the way closed. They put in a stent — a little tube of metal mesh, folded up on a balloon and opened in place to hold the artery open — while he was awake and watching it on the monitor. He said afterwards it looked like "a chart of the Esk at low water". The cardiologist laughed. Dad didn't; he meant it.

## Afterwards

Two hours later he was sitting up eating toast, pink in the face for the first time since I arrived, telling the man in the next bed about cable-laid rope. Mum held his hand and said nothing for a long time. Clare went out into the corridor, rang Dev and cried, and came back and said it was hay fever.

:::private
At four in the morning, before any of this, I sat in the corridor and was more frightened than I've been since Nana. I couldn't think of anything but the bench halfway up the 199 steps. If he'd been on the steps. If he'd been out in a boat. I said things in my head to nobody that I'm not going to write down, even here.
:::

He's to stay two more nights. I'm sleeping in my old room under the shelf of swimming medals, and I can hear the sea.
