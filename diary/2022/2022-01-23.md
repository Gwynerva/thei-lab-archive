## Snow

Twenty centimetres overnight, which in Bergen is news; it usually falls as rain, or melts by lunch. The whole of Sandviken went quiet and white. No buses on the hill, children on tea trays in the lane, and a man in a suit skiing to work along the pavement with his briefcase in a rucksack, which nobody but me seemed to find remarkable.

## Biscuit

Biscuit had seen snow before — a sprinkling on the balcony in Leith, which he ignored. This was the real thing. I opened the door to the steps and he walked straight out into it, sank to his chest, and stopped. Then he lifted each paw in turn and shook it, very slowly, like a man stepping out of a puddle in good shoes. Then he sat down in it. Then he looked back at me as if I had arranged this.

Then something clicked, and he ran in circles round the garden making noises I've never heard from him, ate some, refused to come in for twenty minutes, and at last came in to sit on the radiator and steam.

## The snowman

I built a very small snowman on the balcony rail, with peppercorns for eyes and a sprig of rosemary for a nose. Biscuit watched from behind the glass with an expression I can only call pity. By three o'clock it had slid off the rail into the garden, where Sverre found it and stood it up again on his own windowsill. It's still there tonight, looking out at the street, a little smaller.
