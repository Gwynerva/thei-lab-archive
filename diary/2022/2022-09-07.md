Opened the shop for Dad this morning, because he made me promise to from his hospital bed, as if the town might run out of shackles. Nine till four behind the counter at Testwell's Chandlery: sold twenty metres of three-strand, a boathook, a tin of varnish and a great many explanations. Everyone who came in asked after him. A fisherman I've known since I was six brought him a lobster, alive, in a bucket. It's in the sink. I don't know what Dad will do with a lobster.

::media[The boats at the quay below the shop, the morning I opened up]{file="whitby-harbour-2"}

The shop smells exactly as it did when I was eight — tar and hemp and paraffin — and between customers I sat on the stool behind the counter and felt about eight.
