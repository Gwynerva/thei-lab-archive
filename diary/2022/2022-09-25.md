## Glassing the hull

The day. We started at eight with the workshop heated to twenty and the doors shut, both of us in respirators and white overalls, like people in a film about a disease.

The cloth went on dry first, as we'd rehearsed: rolled out down the length of the upturned hull and smoothed with our hands from the middle outwards until it lay flat and quiet on every curve. Then the epoxy, mixed in small batches, poured on and spread with a plastic squeegee, pulled slowly across so it soaks in.

And this is the bit nobody tells you. As the resin wets the cloth, the cloth disappears. It goes from white to clear, patch by patch, and the plywood comes up through it — the grain, the pinkish brown, the pencil marks I made in March — like a photograph coming up in a tray. By eleven the whole hull was glossy and transparent, and I could see every seam I'd filleted, under glass, forever.

## Then

Then the waiting. You can't touch it, you can't let dust near it, you can't open the doors. We sat on upturned buckets and ate sandwiches with the respirators hanging round our necks and watched it cure, which is like watching paint dry, except that it's your boat, so it's more like watching a sunrise.

A second coat this week, then the fill coats, then the inside. The workshop smells of resin, and I suspect so do I.
