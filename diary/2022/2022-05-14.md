## The road

Out of Bergen at seven, east along the fjords to Kvanndal, and the little ferry across to Utne: twenty minutes on the open deck, the Hardangerfjord wide and green-blue and dead calm, the snow on Folgefonna across the water lit up like a lamp.

## The blossom

Then the orchards. I knew about them and I wasn't ready. Every slope above the fjord is planted with fruit trees in rows, and every tree was out at once — apple, pear, plum, cherry — white and pale pink, with the fjord below and the snow above, mile after mile. The air smelled of honey. The bees were so loud you could hear them from the road.

## Sigrid

Jonas's mother, :hint[Sigrid]{note="Sigrid Brekke: apples, cider, and an opinion on everything"}, is small and brown and quick, and was up a ladder when we arrived. She came down, shook my hand hard, looked at me for a long moment and said in English, "So you are the one building a boat with my son." Then she handed me a basket and took me round all hundred and twenty trees, naming every variety and what was wrong with it: Gravenstein, Aroma, Summerred, and an old one with no name that her grandfather planted.

> Apples are like people. The ones that grow on a hard slope taste of something.

Lunch on the steps: bread, *brunost*, last year's apple juice. She asked about Dad's shop and Mum's garden and Nana, and whether I can make jam (yes) and drive a tractor (no; "you'll learn").

On the way back to the ferry Jonas pointed up the hillside at an old white house standing on its own above the orchards, with more windows than seemed reasonable. "Ragnhild's," he said. I counted them from the ferry. I got to fourteen before a headland took the house away.

:::private
At the gate, while Jonas was loading the van, Sigrid said quietly, "*Han er glad i deg*" — he's fond of you — "look after it." I didn't know what to say, so I said *ja*.
:::
