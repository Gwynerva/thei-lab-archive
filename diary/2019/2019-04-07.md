Day trip to Nara on a slow train. About a thousand deer live in and around the park there, wild and sacred and entirely aware of both.

## The bow

You buy a stack of round crackers from an old woman at a stall, and the deer know before you've paid. The trick, which nobody told me and everyone knew, is that if you bow to a deer it bows back, a quick dip of the head, and then it would like its cracker. I bowed to a young stag. He bowed. I bowed again; he bowed again, slightly irritably, as if I was being slow. It was the most courteous negotiation of my life.

::media[A doe and her fawn on the path, waiting for someone to bow]{file="nara-deer-2"}

Then a doe ate the corner of my map out of my back pocket. Between Biscuit and the deer of Nara, no map of mine is safe.

## The hall

Tōdai-ji: the great wooden hall with the great bronze Buddha in it, so big you can't take it in at once and have to look at it in parts, like a mountain. The hand. The face. The curls. There's a pillar with a hole in its foot the size of the Buddha's nostril, and children crawl through it for luck. I watched for a while and decided I'm a grown woman with a rucksack, not built for nostrils.

::media[The Great Buddha Hall, and the size of the sky over it]{file="todai-ji-1"}

At the far edge of the park, among bare trees by a stream, one old deer on its own, not interested in crackers or in me. I filmed it for a minute because it was the quietest thing I saw all day.
