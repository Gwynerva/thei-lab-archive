## A day in the county archive

The early train down the main line, which stops at Northallerton as if by accident, and a walk through a market town to a building that looks like a dentist's and holds four hundred years of the county's paper.

Rules: bag in a locker, pencil only, no water, no coats. Foam wedges to rest the books on, and long snakes of lead weights in cloth to hold the pages down, which I want for home. The archivist, a quiet woman in a cardigan, brought my documents on a trolley one at a time, as if she were introducing me to relatives.

## The tithe map

The tithe map of the township that takes in Saltwick, drawn in the 1840s to work out who owed what to the church: every field numbered, every field named. *Cliff Close. Far Nab Field. Long Sea Pasture.* And there, at the top of Saltwick, a field called **Sea End** that isn't there any more: on today's map it's sea. The cliff edge on the tithe map runs about sixty metres out from where it runs now, if I've lined it up right, which over a hundred and seventy years is a little faster than the dissertation's average. This coast has never been average.

## The rest

The estate plan was beautiful and useless: a gentleman's park drawn in watercolour with the sea a pale wash at the edge, not measured, only admired. The surveyor's notebooks were one man's pencilled angles and distances from a summer in 1852, with sketches of the cliffs in the margins, just like mine. I sat with them for an hour. I didn't want to give them back.
