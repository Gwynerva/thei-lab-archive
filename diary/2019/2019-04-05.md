## Five in the morning

Alarm at 4.45. First train south to Inari, in a carriage with two schoolboys asleep on each other and a man in a suit reading a paperback. The shrine at the bottom was lit and empty, and a cleaner was sweeping the gravel in long, patient strokes.

## The gates

The path up the mountain runs through thousands of torii, orange gates set so close together that they make a tunnel: you walk inside an orange corridor with the light coming through the gaps in stripes. Each gate was given by someone, a family or a business, and their names and the date are painted down the back in black, so going up you see only orange, and coming down you read a whole city's list of hopes.

The foxes are the messengers of the shrine, stone ones everywhere, some with a key in their mouths and some with a jewel, almost all in red bibs. Nana will want to know who knits the bibs. I'll tell her it's a mystery.

## The view

About halfway up there's a crossing of paths with a bench and the whole of Kyoto below, and that's where I watched the light come: the sun rose behind the mountain, behind me, and the city in front lit up from the far hills inwards, roof by roof. I'd climbed for an hour and a half and seen perhaps ten people. Going down at eight, I met the city coming up, a solid river of it, everyone smiling, everyone saying excuse me. I said *sumimasen* about two hundred times.
