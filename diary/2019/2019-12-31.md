## Hogmanay

Priya arrived at three off the Plymouth train with a bottle of something from Devon and her hair cut short, and the flat filled up with orchestra people and Forthline people and Ines with a cake. Biscuit retreated to the top of the wardrobe and received visitors there, one at a time, like a pope.

At half eleven we all walked up Calton Hill in the cold with bottles in our pockets: thousands of people on the top, the castle lit up across the valley, the street party a roar from Princes Street below. At midnight the fireworks went up off the castle rock and the whole city cheered, and Priya and Marta and I held on to each other on the wet grass and sang the song nobody knows the words of after the first verse.

## The last page

Back at the flat at two, people asleep on every surface, I found I'd come to the last page of this notebook. It seems right. It's the fourth since I started in October 2016, and they're all on the shelf, fat with tickets and photos and glue.

This year, a line each:

1. A map online, and a cat home.
2. Japan, alone, and not lonely.
3. The coast with Dad, every step of it.
4. Nana at ninety, under the rowan.
5. Nine more Munros, and a half marathon in under two hours.
6. A lopsided mug.

Next year I think I'll keep this on the computer instead, typed, so it can't run out of pages. Then one day I'll type these four in too, and the whole thing can sit on a shelf of its own somewhere, [all in order](/diary/). Happy new year, notebook. Thank you for holding all of it.
