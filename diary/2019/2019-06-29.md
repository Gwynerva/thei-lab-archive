## The summer concert

A full church: every seat, and people standing at the back by the tea urn. Priya in the front row with a bag of sweets she wasn't supposed to open, Ines on the end with her phone out and, astonishingly, Callum, up from Glasgow for the night, waving a programme like a flag.

The Dvořák went better than it has ever gone. The opening, the cello tune I've been frightened of since January: we played it together, eleven of us as one, and didn't rush. The last movement nearly ran away with us and Agnes reined it in with one look. In the Elgar, in the slow movement, the church went so quiet I could hear the lights hum.

::audio[The Elgar's first movement, recorded from the back by Ines]{file="music-strings-1" title="Elgar, Serenade for Strings"}

Afterwards, at the pub on the Shore, Callum said, "I didn't know you could do that." Neither did I. Twelve years without playing, then a year and a half of scales in a cold bedroom, and tonight I was one of the four. Possibly five.
