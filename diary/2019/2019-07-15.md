Robin Hood's Bay to Cloughton. Nana waved us off from her door at eight in her dressing gown, and we went down the lane, along the beach at low tide to Boggle Hole (Dad's card: *two hours either side*), and up the cliffs to Ravenscar.

Ravenscar is the town that never was. The Victorians planned a whole seaside resort on the top of the cliff: roads laid out, drains dug, plots sold, a station built. Then nobody came, because the beach is two hundred metres below and you can't get down to it. The roads are still there in the grass, going nowhere. I loved it; it's a map of something that never happened. Dad thought it a shocking waste of drains.

::media[The old station at Ravenscar, waiting for a train since 1965]{file="ravenscar-1"}

Seals hauled out on the rocks below, a dozen of them, grey and silver like wet luggage. Then Hayburn Wyke, where a little waterfall drops straight onto the beach, and a pub in Cloughton with a room above it. Sixteen kilometres. Dad asleep by nine again. I'm beginning to think it's me.
