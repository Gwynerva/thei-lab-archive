## First pottery class

Tuesday evenings, ten weeks, in a studio in an old stable behind a street in Stockbridge. Eight of us, and a teacher with clay to the elbows who said, before we'd taken our coats off, "You'll make terrible pots for weeks. That's the course."

She was right. The wheel is the most humbling machine I have ever met. The clay has to go dead still in the middle of the wheel before you can do anything with it, centred, and it doesn't want to be centred; it wants to wobble off and hit you, and it did, twice. I got it centred once in two hours, for about four seconds, and felt it go quiet under my hands, like a horse deciding to trust you. Then I opened it up too fast and it collapsed into a sort of wet ashtray.

::media[The teacher's pot on the wheel, to show us how it's meant to look]{file="video-pottery-1"}

Everything I own is covered in clay, including, somehow, the inside of my ear. I came home and Biscuit sniffed my hands and sneezed. I'm completely in love with it.
