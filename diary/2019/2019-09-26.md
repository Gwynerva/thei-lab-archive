## The talk

Two in the afternoon, a hall of a hundred and twenty, and a clicker that worked one time in two. I was the third speaker after lunch, which everyone knows is the graveyard slot. My hands shook for the first two slides and then stopped, the way they do in the first bars of a concert, when the thing you've practised takes over from the thing you're frightened of.

They laughed at the joke. Only the one, but they laughed: the slide of a street in Fife with the model's blue stain on it, and beside it a photo of the same street after the real storm, with a man in a dressing gown standing in exactly the blue. I said, "We aim for accuracy, not dressing gowns." It got a laugh in four languages.

## The questions

Six of them. A woman from Rotterdam asked how we deal with drains we've never seen, and I said badly, honestly, and then explained how; she nodded and wrote it down, and that nod is the best thing that's happened to me this year after Biscuit. A man from a ministry asked about the cost. A student asked whether the code was open, and I had to say not yet, and felt it.

## After

In the evening I walked to the city hall, where there's a thing I'd read about: a bronze bolt in a pillar, the zero that every height in the country is measured from, and beside it glass columns of water showing the sea level at the coast right now and, high above your head, the height the water reached in the flood of 1953. I stood in front of it for a long time and thought of Dad, and of the firm in Hull that rounds.
