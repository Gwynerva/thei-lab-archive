## Number twenty

The Lawers range from the old car park above Loch Tay: up the long shoulder of Beinn Ghlas first, a Munro in its own right and number nineteen, then along the ridge and up the last steep cone of [Ben Lawers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Lawers "the highest hill in the southern Highlands") itself, number twenty, 1,214 metres, the tenth-highest mountain in Britain. Alone, in clear air, the first properly clear day on a hill all year.

At the top there's a trig point and the tumbled stones of a huge cairn some Victorians built in 1878 to push the summit over four thousand feet, which it didn't, and which has since fallen down. I liked them for trying.

## From the top

Everything. Loch Tay below, long and silver, like something spilled. Schiehallion to the north, my number ten, its cone as sharp as a child's drawing. Ben Nevis far off in the north-west, grey and huge and not yet mine. Glencoe. The Cairngorms. A sea of cloud filling the glens to the north, with the tops standing out of it like islands. I sat by the trig point for an hour and ate everything in my bag.

## The lochan

I came down the other way, off the north side, steep and rough, to the lochan that lies in the corrie under the summit: Lochan nan Cat, the little loch of the cats, which pleased me for obvious reasons. The water was so clear and still that I could see the bottom a long way out, and on the bottom, in the shallows, tufts of stiff dark-green leaves like little bunches of quills standing up out of the silt. A man with a hand lens and a notebook, sitting on a rock, told me what it was: quillwort, a relative of the clubmosses, of all things, that lives its whole life underwater in cold clean lochans like this one and has been doing so since about the time of the dinosaurs. It grows slowly and wants nothing but to be left alone. I sat with my boots off and my feet in the water, which was very cold, and looked at it for a long time.

Twenty. Two hundred and sixty-two to go. At this year's rate I'll be sixty; at last year's, seventy-one. Progress.
