## Arriving

Two days in and I've stopped walking into people. I came in on Tuesday night through Osaka, slept twelve hours in a room in Kyoto the size of my bed at home, and woke up to a city that was pink at the edges. On the front of every newspaper, two characters in black brush: [Reiwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reiwa "the name of the new era, from May"), the name of the new era, announced on Monday. A shop near my room has it written on a sheet of paper in the window, huge and still wet-looking, and people stop to photograph it.

## Kiyomizu-dera, in the morning

Up the hill with what felt like the whole of Kyoto, to the temple on its wooden stage over the trees. Cherry everywhere below it, in clouds; a red pagoda standing out of the clouds like a lighthouse out of fog. I didn't mind the crowds. Everyone was looking at the same thing and nobody was in a hurry.

## Maruyama Park, at night

The reason for coming in April at all. In the middle of the park stands one old weeping cherry, propped on crutches, lit from below at night, and it's the most beautiful tree I have ever seen. Under it and all around, people sit on blue plastic sheets with picnics and beer: office workers still in their ties, grandmothers, students, one family with a tiny dog in a pushchair. Stalls along the paths sell grilled squid on sticks, chicken, sweet potato, little balls of batter with octopus inside. I sat on a step by myself and ate a stick of chicken and watched the petals come down through the light, and wasn't lonely at all, which I'd been afraid of.
