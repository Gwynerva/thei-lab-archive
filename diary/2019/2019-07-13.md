Staithes to Whitby, the second half of the first stage, and the bit of coast I grew up looking at from the other end.

Runswick Bay first, its cottages tumbled down a green hill as if someone had dropped a box of them; then the long pull over to Kettleness, where there used to be a village until one winter night in 1829 the cliff it stood on slid into the sea. Everyone got out. Dad told me this the way he tells you about a boat that sank at its moorings: not unkindly, but as if it should have known better. Then the old railway line round to Sandsend, through the cuttings, and along the sands at low tide, Dad checking his watch against his card and saying "we've an hour" in a voice that meant forty minutes.

The abbey came up on the skyline at Sandsend and stayed there, getting bigger, for the last five kilometres. Up the West Cliff past the whalebones, down into town, over the bridge, and home. Mum had a pie in the oven and the bath run. We'd walked from Saltburn to our own front door. Dad said it was "not bad", which from him is a medal.
