Last day. Two sessions in the morning on storm surges, and one on maps for people who can't read maps, which was the best talk of the conference and given by a woman who drew everything by hand. Then I skipped the closing remarks, which I'm told is what everyone does.

In the afternoon, an old covered passage full of second-hand bookstalls, where I found a book with marbled endpapers, a fine feathery pattern in tan and red and blue, and the bookseller told me the paper was made here in Amsterdam, in 1823. I couldn't afford the book. He let me photograph the endpaper, which is what I wanted anyway.

::media[Marbled in Amsterdam, 1823, inside the cover of a book I couldn't afford]{file="marbled-paper-4"}

Flew home at six. Edinburgh in the dark, raining, of course. Marta had left a note on the door: *How was it? Biscuit says nothing.*
