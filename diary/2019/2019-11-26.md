## A night without power

I came home from pottery at half nine to a street with no lights. Not just ours: the whole of this end of Leith Walk dark, the shop fronts dark, the traffic lights dark, and the buses feeling their way through the junction like big lit fish. The stair was black. I went up the four flights with my phone torch and my clay-covered bag, counting the steps (seventy-two, as ever; I counted anyway).

Marta was already in, sitting on the kitchen floor with every candle we own, which is two, both of them birthday candles. The fridge was silent. The flat was silent. You don't know how much a house hums until it stops: the fridge, the router, the server in the cupboard, the boiler, the clock on the cooker, all gone, and underneath, a silence like snow.

Biscuit's eyes in the torchlight: two green coins on top of the wardrobe.

## In the dark

We couldn't read; there weren't enough candles. We couldn't watch anything. So we played. Marta got her violin and I got the cello, by feel, and we played everything we both knew by heart, which turned out to be four carols, the opening of the Dvořák, and *Happy Birthday*. Then she stopped and I went on alone with the prelude: the first fourteen bars, all I've got by heart so far, in the dark, slowly, as Mrs Ogilvie says. I couldn't see my left hand. I didn't need to. It sounded like water running somewhere in another room.

The lights came back at twenty past one, after we'd blown out the candles and gone to bed: all at once, the fridge shuddering into life, the server fan spinning up, and we both said *oh* through the wall as if someone had walked in on us. I lay awake a long time listening to the house humming again, and missed the quiet.
