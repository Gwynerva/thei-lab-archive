First day of the course. Twelve of us in a scout hut that smelled of damp canvas and orange squash: two hill runners, a mountain-bike guide, a man who leads walking holidays for the over-sixties, three students from a climbing club, and the rest of us, who just walk a lot and have begun to wonder what we'd do if.

The morning was the primary survey, again and again, until it came out of my mouth without me: danger, response, airway, breathing, circulation. The afternoon was outside on the hill above the hut in drizzle, finding casualties who'd been briefed to be difficult. Mine was a student lying in the heather with a pretend broken ankle who kept asking for his mum. I splinted it with a rolled-up sit mat and two triangular bandages and was disproportionately proud.

Home, bath, bed. Biscuit sniffed my trousers for a long time, the way he does when I've been somewhere interesting.
