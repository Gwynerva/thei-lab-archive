It's online. The [Munro map](ref:project:munro-map "coloured as I climb them") lives at a proper address now, and anyone in the world who wants to know which :hint[Munros]{note="Scottish mountains over 3,000 feet; there are 282 of them"} a woman in Leith has climbed can find out. Thirteen red dots and two hundred and sixty-nine grey ones. It looks like a measles chart of the Highlands.

::link[The Munro map, live]{url="https://example.org/munros"}

## How it works

Very simply. One page, one file of hills, a background map so faded you could miss it. Click a dot and you get its name and height and, if it's red, the day I climbed it and a line about the day. When I climb another, I change one word in the file from *false* to a date, and the dot turns red. That's the whole machine.

## Who looked

Priya, from her bed, at 9.14, with a row of little mountains. Mum, who said it was lovely and asked how to make it bigger. Dad, by text, half an hour later:

> Looks good. Where's Ben Nevis.

Ben Nevis is grey, Dad, because I haven't climbed it. I'm saving it. He knows this. He asked so I'd have to say it again.

Two hundred and sixty-nine to go. At my present rate, I'll finish when I'm sixty-five.
