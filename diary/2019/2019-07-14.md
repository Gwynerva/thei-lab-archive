## Saltwick, again

Whitby to Robin Hood's Bay today, the short stage, eleven kilometres, and the one I know best: up the 199 steps, past the abbey, out along the cliff by the caravans to Saltwick Bay, which is where [the dissertation](ref:project:whitby-erosion) happened.

I went looking for the fence post. In April 2017 I used one particular post at the top of the cliff as a control point for the drone survey: painted orange, numbered, measured to the centimetre. It's gone. The fence has been moved back twice since, the farmer told us, and the old line of it is somewhere out in the air above the beach. Dad stood with me at the edge, looked down at the slumped clay and the rocks, and said, "Your nine metres, was it?" It was. He remembered.

## The foghorn and the gulls

On past the lighthouse and the old foghorn station, which used to sound in fog loud enough, Dad says, to hear in the shop with the door shut. Gulls all the way along, the cliffs full of them, a racket like a school playground. I recorded a minute of it for the flat in Leith, where the gulls are smaller and ruder.

## Nana's

Into the Bay by the top road and down the steep lane to Nana's for tea. She'd been told we were walking and had been watching the path from her window since two. Scones, jam, and the rowan tree in full leaf. She made Dad take his boots off on the step and he did, without a word, the way he did when he was courting Mum, she says. We're sleeping here tonight, me on the sofa under one of her quilts and Dad in the little back room, all three of us in bed by half nine.
