## The lopsided mug

It survived. It came out of the kiln this evening still warm, and it is the most beautiful lopsided mug in Scotland. The glaze went dark blue-green as promised, darker where it pooled at the bottom inside, paler where it ran thin over the rim, and on the side where the wall leans out it broke into a lighter streak, like a wave going over a rock. It leans about five degrees to the left if you hold the handle in your right hand. It holds one cup of tea and a bit. The handle fits three fingers and was pulled, the teacher says, *with conviction*.

Everybody else's mugs came out straight and dull. I'm not saying that out of pride. Well, a bit.

::media[The teacher's shelf of old mugs, for us to learn from; mine is not among them, and is better]{file="mug-2"}

I carried it home on the bus wrapped in a scarf on my knee, the way I brought Biscuit home in February, and for the same reason. Tea in it at eleven at night. It dribbles slightly if you drink from the low side, so you learn to drink from the high side. Nana would say it has character. Dad would say it isn't level. Both true.
