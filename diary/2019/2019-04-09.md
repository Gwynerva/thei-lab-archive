## Takijiri

Train down the coast to Tanabe yesterday; the big bag sent ahead to tonight's inn this morning; then the bus inland up the river to Takijiri, where the old road leaves the valley. There's a small shrine at the start, the first of the :hint[ōji]{note="the small wayside shrines along the Kumano Kodo, a hundred or so of them"}, and I stood in front of it not knowing what to do, and did what the woman in front of me did: bowed twice, clapped twice, bowed once.

The [Kumano Kodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumano_Kod%C5%8D "the old pilgrim roads of the Kii peninsula") is a thousand years old, more or less. Emperors walked it, which surprised me; I'd assumed emperors were carried. It starts by going straight up.

## Up

And I mean straight. Stone steps and roots, through cedar and cypress so tall it's dim at midday. Twenty minutes up there's a rock with a crack in it that you're supposed to crawl through, to be born again. I crawled through. Came out the same, only muddier.

## Takahara

Only four kilometres today, but up all of it, and at the end a village strung along a ridge with the whole range of mountains in front of it, layer after layer going blue, like paper cut-outs held up to a lamp. They call it the village in the mist, and in the evening the mist came up out of the valleys right on time. The inn gave me a room facing it. I sat at the window with tea until it was dark and drew the day in the margin of this page: the river, the steps, the rock, the ridge.
