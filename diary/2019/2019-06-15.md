## Ninety

Nana June is ninety today. We had her party in the garden of the cottage at Robin Hood's Bay, under the rowan tree, because she wouldn't hear of a hall: *I'm not having people I don't know eat sandwiches at me*. So we were twenty-two in a garden meant for six, with neighbours leaning over the wall, a trestle table borrowed from the chapel, and the sea at the bottom of the lane glittering as if it had been booked.

Mum's cake had ninety candles on it, almost, until Dad pointed out the fire risk and Mum took twenty off. Clare and Dev came from Leeds with a hamper. Nana sat in her good chair with a blanket over her knees, in June, and received everyone like a small queen.

## The map

I gave her the map: the Bay in the middle, the cottage, the tree, and lines going out across the sea to the ports where Grandad Arthur sailed, each ending in a tiny ship. She looked at it for a long time with her glasses on and then with them off. Then she put her finger on Valparaíso and said, "He brought me a shawl from there. I've still got it," and made me go and fetch it. It was in the bottom drawer, wrapped in tissue, the colour of the inside of a shell.

## What she said

Somebody asked her, near the end, what the secret was. She said there wasn't one, and then, after a while:

:::quote[Nana June, at ninety]{align="center"}
Never trust a calm sea, and never leave a hem hanging. The rest you can work out.
:::

:::private
She's very small now. When I hugged her I could feel every bone in her back, like a bird's. She got tired after an hour and Mum took her in to lie down, and she came back out at five and pretended she hadn't. Her hands shake when she holds a cup. She knew everyone, though, all day; she called me *pet* and Clare *love*, the right way round. I'm writing it down because I want to remember that she knew everyone.
:::

Three good things: the shawl; the neighbours over the wall singing; Nana's finger on Valparaíso.
