## Scarborough to Filey Brigg

The last day, fourteen kilometres. Out of Scarborough along the South Bay in the morning, past people walking dogs and a man raking the sand, round Cayton Bay and up onto the long flat clifftop fields where the path finally runs out of coast to follow. And then there it was: Filey Brigg, a long low arm of rock running out into the sea, the end of the Cleveland Way. There's a stone at the end of the path that says so. Dad touched it, like a swimmer touching the wall. I didn't say anything. Neither did he. Then we went out onto the Brigg itself, on the flat ledges, as far as the tide would let us, and stood there with the North Sea on three sides.

## What we talked about

I worried at the start that we wouldn't know what to say to each other for six days. We didn't, mostly, and it turned out not to matter. We talked about tides, and boats, and the shop, and once, on the top at Boulby, about Grandad Arthur, whom Dad liked a great deal and whom I don't remember. He told me that the first time he met Mum's parents, Arthur took him down to the harbour and asked him to tie a bowline, and he did, and Arthur said, "You'll do." That's the longest story Dad has ever told me.

## Added up

- Six days, about eighty kilometres.
- Saltburn to Filey, every step of it.
- Three ice creams each, one of them at nine in the morning.
- Seals: twelve. Fulmars: countless. Arguments: none.
- One tide table, proved right by another.
