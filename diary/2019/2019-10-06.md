## 1\:58\:41

Glasgow, the half marathon. Callum's sofa last night, pasta at the café, an alarm at six, and a bus into town full of people in shorts in October looking grimly at the sky.

The start in George Square was a sea of thousands in bin bags, shivering and bouncing. Ines found me in the crowd, kissed me on the cheek and vanished towards the front, where the fast people go. Then a hooter, and nothing happened for four minutes, and then we were shuffling, and then we were running.

## The middle

Over the river and out through the south side; a long drag up through a park full of autumn and people holding out jelly babies on paper plates; back along the Clyde, wide and grey and slow. At ten miles everything started to hurt in a general way, like weather. At eleven a woman with a sign that said *YOU'RE NOT EVEN TIRED* made me laugh so hard I nearly stopped. At twelve I thought about Nana, of all people, and *never leave a hem hanging*, and kept going.

## The end

The finish on Glasgow Green, and the big clock over it. I'd hoped, secretly, not in the spreadsheet, for under two hours. The clock said two hours and three minutes as I came under it, but the clock starts with the hooter, and I started four minutes later, and my own watch, when I could focus on it, said **1\:58\:41**. Under two hours by a minute and nineteen seconds. I sat down on the grass and cried, which apparently everyone does, so nobody minds.

Ines had finished twenty minutes earlier and had already been for coffee. Then Callum's café: a bacon roll, a slice of carrot cake on the house, and Callum telling everyone who came in that I'd just run a half marathon, which they all pretended to find impressive. ~~Never again.~~ We'll see.
