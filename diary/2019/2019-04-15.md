I'd saved this. A ramen shop under the railway arches by the station: eight stools at a counter, and a ticket machine at the door with forty buttons, none of them in English. A man in a suit behind me in the queue saw me standing in front of it like a cow at a gate, pressed two buttons for me without a word, bowed, and went back to his place. I still don't know exactly what I ate. It came with an egg, a slice of pork and a sheet of seaweed stuck in the side like a sail, and the broth was pale and thick, and it was the best thing I have ever eaten, standing up or sitting down.

::media[The bowl, and some things on the side I didn't order but ate]{file="ramen-bowl"}

Everyone at the counter ate in total silence, very fast, slurping, and when I slurped too the cook looked at me and nodded once.

Then I walked until midnight. Across a bridge with trains going over and under at once, along a canal, through a park where a man was practising the saxophone alone under a tree. The tower, red and white, lit orange at the end of a street. I wasn't looking for anything. I bought a postcard of a cat on a wall in Yanaka that I'll never send, a pair of chopsticks for Marta and a little folded paper crow for Nana.

Two weeks ago I was frightened of the ticket machine, in every sense. Tonight a stranger pressed the buttons for me. Both were fine.
