## A stranger fixed my bug

A pull request on tideline this morning, from someone I've never heard of, with a profile picture of a sailing dinghy and an address somewhere in Tasmania. The title was polite:

> Fix the sign of the nodal correction for K1. Predictions at Hobart were out by a few centimetres at the diurnal peaks; with this they match the published tables.

I read it four times. K1 is one of the constituents that matter where the tide comes once a day rather than twice, and I'd copied a formula from an old manual and got one sign the wrong way round. In Whitby, where the tide is a tidy twice-a-day sort of tide, you'd never notice. In Hobart, where the once-a-day part matters more, it shows. Nobody in Leith would ever have found it. Someone at the other end of the world did, and fixed it, and wrote a test, and said thank you for the library.

::card[How the constituents add up, and the one I had backwards]{to="ref:section:tideline/harmonics#h:Constituents"}

I merged it at lunchtime with my hands shaking slightly, which is ridiculous. It's a sign. A minus that should have been a plus. But somebody I'll never meet read my code carefully enough to care, and made it better, for nothing. I don't think I understood open source until today.
