Short day: Cloughton to Scarborough, nine kilometres, done by lunch. So in the afternoon we sat on the harbour wall with fish and chips, and I got the laptop out of the bottom of my bag (Dad: "you brought that") and ran tideline for Scarborough for the week, and we compared it with the printed card from the shop, high water by high water.

It agreed to within four minutes and five centimetres everywhere, except one evening high water on Thursday, where it was seven minutes out. Dad looked at the two columns for a long time. Then he said, "Yours is right. The card's from a firm in Hull, they always round." I've never been so pleased by anything in my life, the promotion included.

::media[The harbour from the castle, the tide on its way in, as predicted]{file="scarborough-2"}

Up to the castle in the evening; the whole of South Bay in the low sun, the town packed into it. Dad bought us each an ice cream and we ate them on a bench with the wind blowing the tops off.
