## Christmas Day

Mum fetched Nana from the Bay in the morning, in the car, wrapped in a tartan rug and her good coat and the shawl from Valparaíso, and she sat in the big chair by the fire all day like the middle of a map, with everyone arranged around her.

Presents. Dad unrolled the long map of the Cleveland Way on the living room floor, all the way from the window to the door, got down on his knees and followed it with his finger from Saltburn to Filey without a word, and then said, "You've got Boggle Hole in the wrong place." I haven't. He knows I haven't. Mum cried at the cat card, which is what she does. Nana opened her little map of the Bay, looked at it for a long time, put it in her handbag and snapped the clasp shut, which is the highest honour in her gift.

## Dinner

Fourteen pounds of turkey for six people. Crackers, hats, the jokes read out by Dev in a different accent each time. Nana in a paper crown, eating a little of everything and telling Dev, who has heard it before, about the parrot that swore in Portuguese. Clare asleep on the sofa by four, after a night shift on the twenty-third, her feet on Dev.

::media[The fire in the evening, everybody half asleep]{file="video-fire-1"}

:::private
Nana dozed off at the table between the pudding and the cheese, her chin on her chest, and nobody said anything; we just talked more quietly. When she woke she didn't know for a moment where she was, and looked at Mum as if trying to place her, and then it passed and she asked for the cheese. Mum kept a hand on her arm the whole time after that. I saw Dad see it.
:::

In the evening Clare and I washed up together like when we were children, her washing and me drying, the radio on, and talked about nothing, and it was the best part of the day.

Three good things: Nana's handbag snapping shut; Dad on his knees on the map; Clare's hands in the sink.
