Takahara to Chikatsuyu, ten kilometres through the forest, and I met four people all day: two Australians, a Japanese couple in matching hats, and in the afternoon an old man coming the other way with a small saw, who stopped and told me something long and kind in Japanese and pointed at the sky. It rained twenty minutes later. I think that's what he said.

The road goes from shrine to shrine. Some are a stone and a sign; one had a little statue of an emperor sitting on a cow and a horse at the same time, which is something I'd like explained. The forest is the main event, though: cedars as straight as masts, moss on everything, the light green and wet. For a long stretch the path ran beside a stream, and I sat on a stone and recorded it, because I knew I'd want it in Leith in November.

Tonight a :hint[minshuku]{note="a family-run guesthouse; you eat what they cook, with everyone else"} in Chikatsuyu, in a valley with a river. Dinner was eleven small dishes, of which I could name four. A hot bath with the window open onto the river and the frogs. My legs are fine. My shoulders are not.
