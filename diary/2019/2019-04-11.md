The long day: eighteen kilometres, Chikatsuyu to the edge of Hongū's sacred ground, up and down over three passes.

In the morning, at Tsugizakura, a row of enormous cedars hundreds of years old whose branches all grow towards the south, towards the waterfall at Nachi, people say, though a forester would say towards the light. A little later the spring at Nonaka: clear water in a stone basin under a bamboo spout, where travellers have been drinking for centuries. I drank. It tasted of cold stone. I filled my bottle with it as if it were a souvenir.

::media[The spring at Nonaka, and the steps down to it]{file="kumano-kodo-4"}

After lunch the path went quiet and long, and I went quiet with it. You stop talking to yourself after a while, even in your head. Somewhere in the afternoon I realised I hadn't thought about the Perth flood model since Tuesday.

The last pass came down in the dusk to a farmhouse inn below Hosshinmon-ōji, which means something like the gate of awakening. The inn's grandmother met me on the road with an umbrella I didn't need and wouldn't let me carry my own bag the last fifty metres. Tomorrow: the gate, and Hongū.
