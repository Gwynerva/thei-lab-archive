## The regatta

Whitby Regatta: three days of the town going mad on the water and on the quays. Flags strung across the harbour from every mast, a fair on the West Side with rides that scream, crowds on the bridge, and the rowing races, which are the point, and which Dad takes as seriously as other men take football.

The races are between the town's crews in heavy fixed-seat boats, from the harbour mouth up past the bridge, and people bet on them in pints. Dad stood on the end of the pier with his arms folded and told me which crew was going to win and why (stroke rate, mostly, and "that lad at three's not pulling his weight"), and he was right twice out of three.

::media[A tall ship come in for the weekend, the abbey on top]{file="whitby-harbour-1"}

In the afternoon we minded the shop together. He sold rope, shackles and three pairs of wellies; I sold a map of the harbour I drew when I was fourteen, which has hung on the wall by the till all these years and which a man from Leeds wanted so much that Dad, to my horror, let him have it. "You can draw another," he said. Then in the evening, from the end of the pier: a :hint[coble]{note="the flat-bottomed fishing boat of this coast, high at the bow, built to land on beaches"} with lights strung up its mast going out past the lighthouse, fireworks over the abbey, and Dad saying *oh, very nice* at every single one.
