## On

At ten past nine this morning the weather station went online. The first reading from the balcony, outside, real: 13.2 °C, 78 per cent humidity, 1012 hectopascals and falling, wind from the west at nine kilometres an hour, no rain. Ten minutes later, another one. Then another. It doesn't sound like much. I sat and watched the numbers arrive all morning, like someone watching a kettle that, for once, was boiling.

## What it's for

Nothing, really. Edinburgh has proper weather stations, run by people who know what they're doing. But this one is ours: the weather of one balcony on the top floor of one stair in Leith, written down every ten minutes. It's the walk again, the same small place looked at closely enough that it starts to tell you things.

## What it told me on the first day

- the balcony gets the sun from half past eleven until five, and is three degrees warmer than the street then
- the wind up here is gustier than the forecast thinks
- Biscuit sits on the bracket under the rain gauge at night: the anemometer twitches every time he jumps down

Marta says it's the first of my projects she can check from bed. She has already used it to decide against a jacket, and was wrong, because the balcony is three degrees warmer than everywhere else. That's on the list now too.
