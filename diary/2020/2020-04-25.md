Day 34.

## One sheet

The Dunn family tree, on one big sheet of paper, at last. Six months of evenings since November: old census pages and parish registers on the laptop, Nana's phone calls, Mum's shoebox of photographs with the names on the back in three different hands. Five generations, more names than I expected, the women's maiden names in brackets, and the sea in it everywhere: fishermen, net-menders, a lot of people who lived within a mile of the water and never left it, and Arthur, who went everywhere.

I drew it like a map, because that's what I know how to draw: the generations as contour lines, the oldest at the top like high ground, and everybody running down from there to Clare and me at the bottom, like water finding its way to the sea.

## The tricky bits

- a great-great-grandmother who is forty in one census and forty-two in the next, ten years later
- two brothers, both called John, a year apart, because the first one died
- Arthur's ships, which Nana remembers by the colour of the funnel

The book that helped most with the old Whitby end of it wasn't a register at all but this, which I read on the laptop at two in the morning:

## For Nana

I photographed the sheet on the floor, with Biscuit kept firmly out of shot, and sent it to Mum to print and take round. Nana rang at teatime. She'd found her own name, and her mother's, and said nothing for a long while. Then: "You've put me near the top."

"That's where you are, Nana."

She laughed, and said that made her sound like a mountain.
