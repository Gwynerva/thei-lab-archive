## The fast way down

Ran up Arthur's Seat from the Dunsapie side in twenty minutes, very pleased with myself, and came down the steep path towards the Crags far too fast. On a loose bit of scree my left foot stopped and the rest of me didn't. Something in my left knee went *pop*, not loud, but I heard it from the inside, and then I was sitting on the path looking at the sky.

## Home

A couple with a dog stopped. The man gave me his arm down to the road and called a taxi, and I said thank you about forty times. Two metres went out of the window and I didn't care.

By teatime the knee was the size of a grapefruit. Clare on the phone, in her A&E voice: rest, ice, compression, elevation, and don't be a hero. Dev on video, poking his own knee to show me where: sounds like the meniscus, maybe more. Marta brought frozen peas and the good cushion.

::media[Salisbury Crags and the path down, in better times]{file="arthurs-seat-3"}

The worst of it isn't the pain, which comes and goes. It's the noise I heard inside my own leg, which I keep hearing again every time I shut my eyes.

Walk 173 was the taxi to the stair door and the stair. Does that count? Tomorrow I'll decide.
