Rocks for beginners: done. The certificate came in at four, "with merit", which I'll take.

## Four months of rocks

I signed up in April because lockdown made everyone sign up for something, and because I've spent half my life standing on the Yorkshire coast without knowing what it's made of. Sixteen weeks, two evenings a week, a lecturer in a cardigan with a cat that walked across the camera at least once an hour, and a forum full of retired engineers arguing about granite.

What stays with me is mostly about the coast I grew up on:

:::list{counter="lower-roman"}
1. Whitby's cliffs are Jurassic mudstone and sandstone, laid down in a shallow warm sea when this bit of the world sat much nearer the equator.
2. Jet is a fossil tree, something like a monkey puzzle, squashed for 180 million years. Nana's brooch is a tree.
3. There are dinosaur footprints at Saltwick, in the same rocks I spent a year measuring fall into the sea, and I never once looked down for them.
:::

## What I'll do with it

Next time I'm home I'm going down onto the scar at Saltwick at low tide with Dad and the course notes, to look for the footprints properly. And I'm going to ask Nana to tip out her bucket of ammonites on the kitchen table so I can tell her how old they are. She'll say "older than me, then", and be right.
