## The office

St Patrick's Day, and the last day in the office for nobody said how long. Twelve people packing quietly. Dougie went round with a roll of bin bags and a roll of tape and stuck a label on everything that was leaving: *Mira, monitor*, *Ines, plant*, *Dougie, kettle*. Then he took the kettle label off again, because who'd keep the office going. We argued about the kettle for ten minutes. It was the best ten minutes of the day.

## The bus

Carried the second monitor home on the bus, which was nearly empty, the monitor on the seat beside me like a passenger. The driver said, "Working from home?" and I said yes, and he said, "Lucky," and I've been thinking about it all evening.

:::private
Dougie took me aside by the window before I left. The councils will keep paying, he thinks, but two contracts are already "paused", and if this goes on past the summer there'll be no rises this year, maybe a cut. He said it the way Dad says things he's worried about: quietly, to the window.
:::

## The desk

The box room is now the office: the monitor on the old sewing table, the laptop on two dictionaries, Biscuit on the chair until further notice. Marta has the kitchen table. We have agreed on lunch at one o'clock, together, like a canteen of two.
