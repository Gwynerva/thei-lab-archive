Walk 145: Ulva.

## Getting there

Up at four, the car-club car on the empty road to Oban, and onto the first ferry to Mull with a car deck full of campervans and everybody on the top deck pretending not to be thrilled. Then across Mull on single-track roads with passing places, and sheep that don't believe in them, to the jetty at Ulva Ferry.

## The ferry

To call the little ferry across to Ulva you slide a board on the jetty so the red side shows, and wait, and a man comes over in a boat. That's the whole system, and it works, and I'd like everything in my life to work like that.

## Cragaig

Walked the track out west along the south shore, past the old townships, low walls in the bracken where whole families lived before they were cleared off the island. It's very quiet there. Not empty-quiet: full of something. Seals on the rocks off the shore, a buzzard, and a sea eagle (I think: a huge one, a door with wings) going over towards Gometra. At the end there's Cragaig, a house by the water with nobody in it.

::media[Cragaig on Ulva, a red roof and its reflection, and no one at all]{file="bothy-1"}

I sat on the shingle for half an hour and drew the island in the back of the map, the track as a dotted line, the house as a red square, a tiny eagle. Camped at the head of Loch na Keal with the tent door open to the water and an oystercatcher complaining about me until dark.
