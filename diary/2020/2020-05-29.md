No number today. I've stopped counting the days; I'll keep counting the walks.

## Out

Marta and I left at seven with sandwiches made with Hamish-bread and walked out through Canonmills and Morningside to Swanston. It is around five miles, if "around" is generous. On the farm road at Swanston the hills came up in front of us all at once, green and brown and enormous after two months of tenements, and I had to sit down on the verge.

## Up

Caerketton, then Allermuir, slowly. Skylarks going up out of the heather like sparks. From the top: the whole city, the Forth, Fife, the Bass Rock a white crumb in the haze, and somewhere down there among the roofs our street, the tangle of walks, the whole small world of the last nine weeks. It looked tiny. It looked like a map of somewhere I used to live.

## Down

Walked home the same way, very slowly, feet like loaves. Walk 67: twenty-nine kilometres, the longest of the lot by a very long way. Marta fell asleep on the sofa with her boots on, and Biscuit lay down on her as if she were a new kind of rug.
