## In

Portobello at nine, the tide coming in, the sea the colour of a teaspoon. There were maybe thirty people already in the water along the beach, in everything from wetsuits to what looked like pyjamas. Fourteen degrees, said a man with a thermometer on a string, who swims every day of the year and was wearing a bobble hat in the water.

I went in up to my knees and stood there making noises. Then my waist (worse). Then the man said "shoulders, and breathe out", and I did, and for about five seconds my whole body shouted at me, and then it stopped shouting and just hummed.

## Out

Eight minutes. Marta timed it and held the towel and laughed exactly as promised. My hands went red, then white, then red again, and I couldn't do the buttons on my coat, so Marta did them up for me like I was five.

::media[The old groyne at Portobello in the morning light, and the bit of sea I was in]{file="portobello-beach-1"}

## After

The after is the part nobody told me about. Two hours of feeling as if I'd been rinsed clean. Tea from the flask tasted like the best tea of my life. I'm going back.

Three good things:

1. The man with the thermometer.
2. Marta and the buttons.
3. The hum.
