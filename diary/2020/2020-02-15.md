## The shop

Woke in my old room at Mum and Dad's to the window shaking in its frame. By nine Dad and I were down at the chandlery with the sandbags he keeps in the back for exactly this, and the flood boards for the door, which are older than me and fit like they know it. The Esk was already high and brown at the bridge, the tide pushing up against all that rain coming down off the moors, the two of them meeting under the swing bridge and arguing.

Dad said nothing all morning but "mind your fingers" and "that one's for the drain". At twelve he put the kettle on in the back and we drank tea standing up among the ropes, listening to the wind try the door.

## The piers

Both piers closed, of course, and half of Whitby standing at the end of them anyway, behind the barriers, being told off by a man in a yellow coat. The sea came over the West Pier in great grey slabs and broke white all along the foot of the cliff. I've seen big seas here all my life and I still made a noise like a child.

## Nana

Rang Nana in the Bay at lunchtime. She'd put a rolled towel along the bottom of the back door and was doing the crossword. "It's only weather," she said. She's lived through ninety winters on that coast and I believe her about most things, but not that one.

[Storm Dennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_Dennis "a week after Ciara, as if one wasn't enough") is on every channel tonight, mostly for the floods further south. Mum made a birthday cake for herself, five days early, because "somebody has to", and we ate it with the radio on.
