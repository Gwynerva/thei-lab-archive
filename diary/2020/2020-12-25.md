## Morning

Walk 277 at eight, before anyone else was out: Leith Walk empty from top to bottom, frost on the cars, a gull on every lamp post. Then stockings. Marta made one for Biscuit with a catnip mouse in it, and he's still not over it.

## The call

Whitby at eleven, on the laptop: Mum and Dad in paper hats at their kitchen table, Clare and Dev from Leeds in a square of their own (Clare worked last night and was asleep with her eyes open). Mum held up my loaf card on her fridge, and next to it the cat-on-a-map card I sent Nana last year, which came back to her in a box of things from the cottage.

We opened presents on camera, which is a strange kind of theatre. All but one.

:::spoiler
Mum's present is a little book I bound from Nana's recipe cards, written out again in my best hand: rowan jelly, parkin, curd tart, the lot. It's in a box under her tree, and she's promised not to open it until she's with somebody she can cry with. If you're reading this, Mum: not yet.
:::

## Dinner

Marta and me: a chicken (a turkey for two would have been showing off), roast potatoes, sprouts, the leftover pierogi fried in butter, and a Christmas pudding Mum posted in November. Crackers from the corner shop, with the worst jokes yet. Biscuit had the chicken skin and a quiet afternoon.

::media[Biscuit on his present, asleep before the pudding]{file="orange-cat-2"}

## Not Whitby

It's the first Christmas I've ever spent away from Whitby, and the first without Nana anywhere, and those two are so tangled together I can't tell which one I'm missing when. But it was a good day. That's allowed.
