Two hours on the phone with Nana, the family tree on my knee. Her father mended nets; her mother took in sewing and taught Nana to hem before she could read. Names I'd never heard: a great-aunt who went to Canada and wrote twice and then never again, a little brother who died at four of something that would be nothing now.

Nana told it all very plainly, like a list of tides, and then said she'd better go, the kettle. I sat on the floor for a long time afterwards with the phone in my lap.
