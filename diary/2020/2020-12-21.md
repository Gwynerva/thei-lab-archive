## The shortest day

Sunrise at 8.42, sunset at 15.40: just under seven hours of daylight, and most of it the colour of dishwater. I worked through the middle of it with the lamp on and the station logging 4 degrees and a light drizzle that never quite committed. From tomorrow the days get longer. I've never been so glad of a bit of astronomy.

## Two planets

Walk 273 at dusk, up Calton Hill with the sweater on under my coat, to see the two planets: Jupiter and Saturn so close tonight they looked like one fat star low in the south-west over the castle. Through Dad's old binoculars, propped on the railing, they came apart into two, and I could just see Saturn was the wrong shape, the way it's supposed to be. [The great conjunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_conjunction "the closest since 1623, and the closest anyone could actually see since 1226"), the radio calls it. There were maybe twenty of us up there, spread out along the wall, all looking the same way.

## The robin

On the way up, a robin was singing in the dark by the steps, the way they do under street lamps. I stood still and recorded it, and a crow had its say too.
