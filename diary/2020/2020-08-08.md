## Arrochar

Walk 138 was Beinn Ime, the highest of the Arrochar Alps and the one nobody photographs, because it stands next to the Cobbler.

Up from Succoth at the head of Loch Long, the zigzags through the forest first, then the long glen under the Cobbler itself, whose three rocky tops look like a sculpture somebody gave up on halfway. I'd forgotten how busy the Cobbler gets: a queue at the foot of its path, all at two metres, all in shorts. Everyone turned off towards it. I kept going, over the bealach and up the big grassy back of Beinn Ime, and had the top to myself.

## The top

Cloud came and went. When it went, the hills round Loch Lomond were laid out to the east with Ben Lomond's cone standing up out of them, and to the west the sea lochs went out towards the islands like fingers.

## Down

Came down the way I went up, my knees complaining on the steep bit through the forest (noted, ignored). Sat in the car at the bottom eating oatcakes, sunburnt on one side only, and drove home with the windows down and Loch Lomond flashing between the trees. Fourteen kilometres, a thousand metres up and the same down, and every one of them earned.
