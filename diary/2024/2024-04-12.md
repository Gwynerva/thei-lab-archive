## The letter

I gave it to Anna at nine, in the small meeting room, the same one as in January. Four lines, signed, in an envelope, though she'd only have to scan it. Here they are, because I want to remember I could be that brief:

> I'm resigning from my position as cartographer, with my last day on 31 May. Thank you for two good years and one hard one, and for the way you handled the hard one. I'm going to make maps on my own. I'd like us to stay friends.

## Anna

She read it twice. Then she laughed, not unkindly, and said she'd had a bet with herself that it would come before Easter. She said she was sad and not surprised, and that the second was a compliment. She said I'd be good at it, the maps of my own, and that I should invoice her first.

We went out for lunch, a proper one, at the café by the fish market, and didn't talk about charts once. We talked about her family and about Isla and about the kayak, and she asked if I'd take her out in it in the summer.

## After

Walked home in a hailstorm that turned to sun halfway up the hill. Jonas was in the workshop corner; he looked up, saw my face and switched off the saw. I told him and he lifted me off the floor, which he has never done before and which I did not enjoy, and laughed about anyway.

Seven weeks left. Then nothing, and then everything.
