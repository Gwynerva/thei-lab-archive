## The morning

Up at six, out at half seven, the bus along the harbour in the dark like a hundred mornings before. My pass still worked. That was the first surprise: I'd half expected the world to have cancelled it.

At the office, a pot of tulips on my desk, a card signed by the whole team, and my old mug, washed. Somebody had taken the release countdown off the big screen. I noticed it straight away, the blank where it used to be.

## What Anna said

Anna took me into the small meeting room with two coffees and shut the door. She'd planned it all: mornings only, eight till half twelve, no release work, no evenings, the little harbours of the south coast to tidy at my own speed. Then she said the thing I've since written inside the cover of my notebook:

> Half is a number, not a verdict.

I didn't cry, which I'd been sure I would. We talked about harbours for twenty minutes and it was nearly normal.

## After

Left at twelve thirty-one. Walked home the long way, along Bryggen and up through the lanes, the light low and gold on the wet cobbles, and by Sandviken I was so tired I lay down on the sofa in my coat.

But I went. Biscuit came and sat on my chest and looked at me as if I'd been somewhere far away, which I suppose I had.
