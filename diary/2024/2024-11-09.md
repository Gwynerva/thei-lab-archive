## The road there

Out of Bergen at seven, the long road through Norheimsund in the dark, then the ferry from Kvanndal with the fjord flat as pewter and the light only just coming. From the ferry you can see Utne — the hotel, the church, the boathouses — and above it, if you know where to look, a white house in the trees. Jonas knew where to look.

Sigrid met us off the ferry with a thermos and the face of a woman who has already decided, and got into the passenger seat because she said she'd know the turning. She didn't. Jonas had the key in his pocket, in Ragnhild's envelope, where it has been since August.

## The house

An 1891 farmhouse on a shelf of land above the fjord, timber, painted white sometime before I was born. The :hint[tun]{note="the farmyard: the open ground the house, the barn and the outbuildings stand around"} is grass now, with the barn on one side and an old storehouse on the other. Behind it, the orchard: forty trees, Sigrid says, though I counted thirty-eight and she said two were "resting".

Inside: cold, clean, smelling of woodsmoke and apples. A kitchen nobody has changed since 1962. A sitting room with Ragnhild's chair still by the window. Steep stairs. Upstairs, three small rooms with sloping ceilings. And windows everywhere. We counted them, all fourteen, each a slightly different size, each with its own piece of the water.

From the front step you look across the water to the mountains, and on a clear day to the white of [Folgefonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folgefonna "the ice cap, there all year round") above them.

## What Nana would say

She'd have walked through every room touching the doorframes, said "make do and mend", and then asked what the damp was like. The damp is in the north room. The roof needs someone who knows roofs.
