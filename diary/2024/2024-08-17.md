## The test

Ninety minutes on a thirty-foot boat with an examiner, a second candidate who was seventeen and fearless, and a wind that couldn't make up its mind. We left the pontoon, hoisted, tacked up the sound, gybed back, picked up a mooring buoy, and did the man-overboard drill with a real lifebuoy instead of Gunnar the fender.

I was steady. I didn't know I would be. In November I couldn't sit through a phone call.

## The bit I got wrong

Coming back alongside I came in too fast, and too fast is the only thing that matters alongside. I saw it, said "going round again" out loud, and went round again. The examiner wrote something down. Afterwards he told me that saying it out loud was the thing he'd been waiting for. Nana would have liked him.

> Never trust a calm sea. And never trust a quay you're coming at too fast.

She only ever said the first half. I've added the second.

## Afterwards

A pass, and a paper with a stamp on it. Jonas was waiting on the quay with two ice creams, as if I were nine, and we ate them sitting on a bollard. Two summers of lessons, and I can take a boat out and bring it back without anyone shouting.

Three good things:

1. "Going round again", said out loud.
2. The seventeen-year-old passed too, and hugged me.
3. Ice cream on a bollard.
