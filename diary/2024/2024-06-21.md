## The last morning

Up at five in the albergue at Teo and out at half past in the dark with a head torch, because everyone said to arrive early, before the square fills. Twelve kilometres. Eucalyptus, then suburbs, then a long climb into the old town on granite worn smooth by a thousand years of people doing exactly this.

I heard the bagpipes before I saw anything — a piper in the arch under the bishop's palace, playing for coins, the sound filling the tunnel — and then I came out of the arch into the square, and there it was.

## The square

The cathedral, enormous, the two towers going up and up, the stone gold in the morning. People lying on their backs on the paving with their packs for pillows, looking at it. People crying. People taking photographs of their boots. I did all three.

## The certificate

At the pilgrims' office they asked where I'd started and why. I said Porto, and *for my health*, which is a box on the form, and true. The Compostela is in Latin and has my name on it as *Miram*, which made me laugh in the queue. The last stamp in my credencial says 21.06.2024, which is how I write dates now, the Norwegian way, with dots, and I hadn't noticed I'd changed until I saw it there.

## Tonight

Rang Jonas from the square. Rang Mum, who cried. Sent Ines a photo of the stamp. Sat on the steps until it got dark at ten, thinking about the autumn, and the rock on Løvstakken I couldn't get up from, and every kilometre since, and feeling, for the first time in a long time, that I'd arrived somewhere on purpose.
