## Getting there

Bergen to Amsterdam, Amsterdam to Leeds Bradford, the bus into town in the rain, which felt like home twice over. Dev met me at the station with the car seat already in the back, empty. He says he likes looking at it.

## Isla

She was asleep when I got there, in a basket on the living room floor under a blanket Mum knitted, one hand up by her ear. Clare in a dressing gown, grey with tiredness and glowing with it. She didn't say hello. She said, "Wash your hands and sit down," and put Isla in my arms.

She weighs nothing. She weighs more than anything I've ever held. She opened her eyes — dark blue, Dad's, everyone says — and looked at a point just past my left ear for a long time, as if there were something interesting there, and went back to sleep.

I didn't move for an hour and twenty minutes. My arm went dead. Clare took a photo, then took the baby, then made me a cup of tea, which she says is the order of things now.

## Clare

Late, with Isla fed and down and Dev asleep sitting up on the sofa, Clare and I sat in the kitchen and talked the way we haven't since we were teenagers. About the birth, which was long. About my autumn, which I told her all of. She said, *you know you're allowed to just stop*. I said I know. I'm learning it.
