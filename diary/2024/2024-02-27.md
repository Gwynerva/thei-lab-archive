## Week four

[Lettering for maps](ref:project:lettering "Tuesdays at two, above the framer's") is eight of us round a big table: two architects, a retired surveyor, a tattoo artist, a girl who wants to paint names on boats, and me. The teacher lettered maps by hand for the national survey before the computers took the job off her, and she hasn't a scrap of bitterness about it.

## What we did

- Roman capitals with a broad nib, five times each, slowly
- spacing, which is the hard part: the letters aren't spaced evenly, the *space between them* is
- a label along a curve, a river's name following its river

## What I learned

Mine are too tight. Seven years of fitting names into small harbours has taught me to cram. She took my sheet and drew a little bracket of air between each pair of letters and said *let them breathe*. Which is also what everybody has been telling me since October.

The surveyor, who is seventy-something and letters like an engraver, leaned over and said that on the old maps the names were always the last thing on, and the first thing anyone looked at. I'd never thought of it like that. The whole chart is for the name.
