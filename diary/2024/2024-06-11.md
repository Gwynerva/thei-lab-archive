## Rain

It rained from Esposende to Viana do Castelo, twenty-five kilometres of it, steady and warm, the kind that comes in sideways off the sea and finds its way in at the collar however you zip your jacket. Ines, who had promised me Portuguese sun, apologised every hour as if it were her fault.

I didn't mind. I'm from Bergen now. We walked through pine woods with the rain hissing in the needles, and crossed little rivers on stone bridges, and every café we came to we went into, and every café gave us a coffee and a stamp. I have four stamps for today, all of them running at the edges.

## Viana

We came over the long iron bridge into Viana at four, the river wide and brown below, and the rain stopped as if someone had turned off a tap. The town was washed clean, white houses and granite, and the cathedral door was open.

::media[The cathedral door at Viana, pink, still wet]{file="viana-do-castelo-1"}

We sat inside for a while, dripping on the floor. I'm not religious. I sat there anyway and thought about Nana, who would have hated the rain and loved the door.

## Feet

The left heel is a blister now, officially. Ines lanced it on the albergue bunk with my needle and her lighter and a great deal of commentary.
