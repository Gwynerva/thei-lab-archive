## Evening one

Twelve faces in twelve small windows at seven o'clock, and me in the thirteenth with my hair still wet from the rain. I had written down my first sentence so I wouldn't freeze, and then didn't need it.

What we did:

1. Opened QGIS, and I explained why the screen looks like the cockpit of an aeroplane
2. Loaded a coastline, a river and a set of points — the sheep man's sheep, as it happens
3. Changed one colour, then another, until the map looked as if somebody had chosen it
4. Saved it, closed it and opened it again, so they'd believe it was still there

::media[Somebody's first map, colours chosen, roads and rivers in their places]{file="qgis-screenshot-3"}

## What I noticed

The ones who said they were hopeless with computers were fine. The ones who said they'd "played with it a bit" had questions about things I'd never seen. The archaeologists know more about coordinate systems than I do, and kindly didn't say so.

Teaching is like the Spoke Room was: you stand beside someone while they mend their own bike. You don't take the spanner.

## After

Ninety minutes, and I was shaking afterwards like after a concert. Jonas had made soup. I ate two bowls and slept like a stone.
