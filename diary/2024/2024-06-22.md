## A day without walking

Slept till nine. Went to the pilgrims' mass at noon, packed in shoulder to shoulder with everyone I'd met along the way — the Irish sisters, the Dutchman with the blisters, the priest with the guitar — and when they swung the great censer down the nave on its rope, trailing smoke over our heads, the whole cathedral went *oh* together like a crowd at fireworks.

## The way, as lines

In the afternoon, in a café, I put the tracks on the laptop and looked at them: a thin wobbly line up the coast of Portugal and into Galicia. Two of the days for now; the rest when I'm home.

## Odds and ends

Bought a scallop-shell pin for Isla and a bottle of Galician *orujo* for Dad, who will pretend not to like it. Posted the card to Ines. Ate octopus, finally, at a long table with the Irish sisters, who toasted "the ones who couldn't come" and looked at me when they said it, as if they knew. Maybe everybody walking has someone.

Home tomorrow.
