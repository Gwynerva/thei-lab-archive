## Over the river

The Minho is the border: Portugal on this side, Spain on that, a wide grey estuary with sandbanks and a mountain on the far bank. There's no bridge on the coastal way, so you wait on a little beach for a man with a boat. He came at nine, took four of us across in ten minutes, and charged us six euros each and a story about a pilgrim who'd tried to swim it.

On the far side it was suddenly an hour later. Spain runs an hour ahead. I'd lost an hour by crossing a river, which as a cartographer I find deeply satisfying.

## Galicia

The waymarks are different here: concrete posts with a blue tile and a yellow shell, and the distance to Santiago cut into them. 172 kilometres at the first one I saw. Then up and over the shoulder of Santa Trega, the mountain from the other bank, and down into A Guarda, its harbour full of little boats and its restaurants full of octopus.

## Alone

Tonight's albergue: twelve bunks, a German couple, a Korean student, and two Irish sisters in their seventies who have walked every route there is. They asked where I'm from and I said Bergen, then Whitby, then both. They said *that's how it goes*.
