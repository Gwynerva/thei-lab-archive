## The stall

In the church hall by nine, Priya carrying the box of notebooks and me carrying the tablecloth and a flask. My half of a trestle table is by the radiator; the other half belongs to a woman who makes earrings out of old spoons. Nana's blue cloth, the notebooks standing up in rows like a little city, and a sign in my best letters:

**Coptic notebooks, sewn by hand**\
A6 £12 · A5 £18

Priya bought the first one at five past nine, before the doors had even opened, so that I'd "have the tin started".

::media[The bingo-card one, which Priya bought before anybody else could]{file="handmade-notebook-2"}

## How it went

The first hour: nothing at all. I rearranged the table four times. The second hour: a woman bought two and said they were too nice to write in. (Please write in them!) Then at lunchtime a run on the map covers: it turns out people love seeing their own street on a notebook. A boy of about ten bought an A6 one, counting the coins out of his own purse while his dad stood back pretending not to help. Fergus came at one, inspected every spine, and bought a marbled one "for a nephew", who I don't think exists.

By three I'd sold out of A6. I sat down behind the table, ate a mince pie and nearly cried.

## Takings

Fourteen sold: ten small, four big. £192 in the biscuit tin. I spent eight pounds of it on a pair of earrings made from a spoon. Fergus, when I told him about Priya saying I was underpricing, said everybody does the first time.
