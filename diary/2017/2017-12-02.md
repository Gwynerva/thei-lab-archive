## The stall

In the church hall by nine, Priya carrying the box of notebooks and me carrying the tablecloth and a flask. Twenty-two other tables: knitted things, candles, jam (not as good as Nana's), wooden bowls, a woman selling tiny ceramic houses. My table was by the radiator, with Nana's blue cloth and the notebooks standing up in rows like a little city, and a sign in my best letters:

**Coptic notebooks, sewn by hand**\
small £8 · big £12 · wonky ones £5

## What sold

Fourteen! **Fourteen notebooks.** The first went at twenty past ten to a woman who turned it over three times and said "You made this?" and I said yes and nearly hugged her. A boy of about nine bought a small checked one with his own pocket money, counting it out in coins. Fergus came at lunchtime, inspected every spine, and bought one of the marbled ones "for a nephew", who I don't think exists.

By four I'd sold six small, six big and both of the wonky ones. Nine left, and one of those is the huge one, which was never really for sale.

## Takings

£130 in the tin (the table was paid for last week), minus two cups of tea and a mince pie each for me and Priya. I'm not going to work out the hourly rate, because it would be about forty pence, and I don't care.
