## The shape of the thing

Sticky notes all over the bedroom wall, and at last the dissertation has a shape. Twelve thousand words, give or take, in six parts:

:::list{counter="lower-roman"}
1. The question — how fast is the cliff at Saltwick going, and is it speeding up?
2. Old maps, 1853–1990 — six surveys and what each one can be trusted for
3. Fieldwork — four days in April, Sam's drone, and a cliff that fell while I watched
4. Point clouds — thirty-one million points and how they were made to line up
5. What I found — the numbers (still being argued with)
6. What I'd do differently — a long chapter, if I'm honest
:::

Roman numerals because Dr Grieve likes them, and because they make it feel like a proper book with a spine.

## The wall

The sticky notes have a system, obviously. Yellow for maps, pink for problems, green for done. On Monday morning there were two green ones. Tonight there are nine, and the pink ones have shrunk to a little cluster around chapter five, like the last snow on a hill.

Callum came in to borrow a phone charger, stood looking at the wall for a full minute and said "Is this a murder?" Fair question. It looks exactly like one.

## What's left

Chapter five, all of it. The abstract, which I'm saving for last, because the abstract is where you have to admit what you actually think. And the acknowledgements, which I've already written four times, because it's the only part that's fun.
