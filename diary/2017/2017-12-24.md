Christmas Eve in Dad's shop until two — the busiest day of his year, everybody buying rope and head torches as presents — then up the 199 steps at dusk with Mum to watch the town lights come on. I counted the steps, like always. 199, like always.

::media[The 199 steps at dusk, the lamp at the top already lit]{file="whitby-steps-1"}

Nana came over from the Bay for the whole of Christmas, and Clare and Dev drove up from Leeds in the evening. Over the trifle Clare tapped her glass and said they've set a date: the 8th of September next year, in York. Mum cried. Dad shook Dev's hand for a very long time. Nana asked Dev whether he could cook; he said a bit; she said that would do.

Then Clare turned to me and asked whether I'd do the paper things — the invitations, the place cards, maybe a map — and I said yes halfway through her sentence.
