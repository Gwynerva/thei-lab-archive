First evening at Lowe Bindery!

## The place

Stockbridge on a Tuesday after dark: the shop lights on, the clock tower, a dog tied up outside the deli. The bindery is down three steps, one long room with a big table under hanging lamps, a cast-iron press in the corner like a small train, and shelves of paper all the way up. It smells of glue and paper and a bit of woodsmoke from the stove.

::media[Stockbridge on the way there, the clock on the old bank]{file="stockbridge-1"}

There are six of us. Fergus Lowe is tall and quiet, with his sleeves rolled up and a grey apron and a lot of tiny paper cuts on his hands. He talks very slowly, and everybody listens very hard, so it works.

## What we did

We folded paper into :hint[signatures]{note="sheets folded in half and nested together: the little bundles of pages that are sewn to make a book"}, which sounds easy, and then Fergus showed us the grain of the paper, which runs one way, like wood, and if you fold against it the fold is fat and cracks. Then a simple pamphlet stitch, three holes, through a single signature. My first book is twelve pages long and I love it more than I've loved anything.

::media[The bone folder Fergus gave each of us to keep]{file="bookbinding-tools-1"}

## Fergus's rules

- Grain long, or the paper will fight you for the rest of its life.
- Measure with a strip of paper, not a ruler. Rulers lie.
- The glue goes on thin. Thinner than that.

Walked home through the dark with the little book in my coat pocket, holding it.
