The number holds. I've checked it every way I can think of — the aerial photos in between, the shrunken 1913 sheet stretched back, every metre of the bay — and the average retreat of the cliff top at Saltwick since 1853 is still **0.3 metres a year.** A metre every three years. A double-decker bus in a lifetime.

But the number I keep looking at is the other one: nine metres gone in the single winter of the 2013 surge. Nine metres! Thirty years of the average in one winter. So it isn't a steady walk backwards, it's a shuffle and then a fall.

Rang Dad at last, now I'm sure. He said "Told you," and then asked if the caravans were safe, and I said not forever.
