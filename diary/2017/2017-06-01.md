June, and a new notebook. This one I made myself, at the bindery, with Fergus's marbled paper inside the covers and the chain stitch up the spine. The first page has my name on it in my best writing and the date, and it opens perfectly flat on the desk, the way he said it would.

The old one is full, all eight months of it, from the day I moved in. I've put it on the shelf next to the 1817 history of Whitby, which seems right.

(If you're reading this on the [diary](/diary/) pages and not in the notebook, you can't feel how the pages lie flat. You'll have to trust me.)

## The summer

It's the dissertation summer now: no lectures, no exams, just the cliff and me. Today I set up a desk in the empty lab by the window, with a plant from the windowsill at the flat and a stone from Saltwick for a paperweight, a lump of grey shale with a fossil shell in it that I picked up off the heap on the last morning of fieldwork. It's a bit of the actual cliff that fell. It seemed right.

Rules for the summer, written on the inside cover:

- a desk by nine, home by six, like a job
- Saturdays are for the Spoke Room, not the cliff
- one notebook a month, at least
- tell Dad the number only when I'm sure of it

Then I spent the afternoon making the second notebook's signatures, because I can.
