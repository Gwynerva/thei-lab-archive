Dad rang, about the tide booklet he sells in the shop, the little yellow one: it came late this year, and he's been copying the tide times off the internet for his customers in biro. "You do computers," he said. "Can't one just work the tides out?"

It can! I've spent three evenings reading. A tide is a sum of waves: one for the moon, one for the sun, more for the moon being nearer or further, for its tilt, for the sun's tilt... dozens, each with its own speed. Every harbour has its own list of how big each wave is and how late it arrives — [the constituents](ref:section:tideline/harmonics#h:Constituents "the list of waves, harbour by harbour") — and if you have that list you can add the waves up for any day you like, into the future, forever.

I want to write the thing that adds them up. For Whitby first. For Dad.
