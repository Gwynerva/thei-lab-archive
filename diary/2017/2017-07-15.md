Priya borrowed her lab-mate's car "for science" and we drove down the coast to St Abb's Head: an hour and a half of A1, then little roads with grass up the middle, then the sea.

## The cliffs

You hear it before you see it — a whole cliff of birds, all shouting at once. Then you reach the edge and the rock face is *moving*. Priya named everything for me, very fast, pointing:

- guillemots, packed shoulder to shoulder on ledges no wider than a bookshelf, like waiters on a break
- razorbills, the same idea with a thicker beak
- kittiwakes, nesting on nothing at all and screaming their own name
- fulmars, gliding past at eye level and looking at us as if we owed them money
- one puffin, which I never saw, so I'm counting it as a rumour

Some of the chicks have already gone. They jump off before they can properly fly, Priya says, and their fathers jump with them and swim them out to sea. She said this very calmly, as if it's a normal way to start your life.

At noon we went out in a little boat from St Abbs harbour to see the cliffs from underneath, under a grey lid of cloud, on water as flat as a plate, with the lighthouse sitting on top like an ornament on a cake.

## The haar

At about three a :hint[haar]{note="sea fog that rolls in off the North Sea on warm days"} came in and the whole headland went white in four minutes. First the lighthouse went, then the sea, then Priya, ten feet away. The birds kept shouting as if nothing had happened. We sat on the grass above the Mire Loch and ate squashed sandwiches inside a cloud.

Drew the headland in the margin on the way home, with the birds as dots. There are a lot of dots.

Three good things:

1. Being inside a cloud with a friend and a sandwich
2. Kittiwakes
3. Not falling off anything
