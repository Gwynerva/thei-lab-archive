Day two. The drone went up!

## Flying

Sam flies standing very still, with the controller and a screen, talking to the drone under his breath like it's a horse. It went up off the grass with a noise like an angry kettle and then it was a white dot over the cliff, going back and forth along its lines, and every two seconds it took a picture. Four flights, about twenty minutes each, between battery changes. A gull did come and look at it. The gull lost interest.

::media[The drone hovering over the cliff top, its camera on its belly]{file="quadcopter-1"}

My job between flights: walk the targets, check none had blown over or been moved, and keep walkers off the bit of path under the flight lines, which mostly meant saying "it's a drone, it's for a dissertation, it's not filming you" to a lot of people with dogs. One man asked if it was looking for treasure. In a way.

## Low water

Full moon today, so a spring tide: the sea went out further than I've seen it for years and the whole shelf at the foot of the cliff was bare and shining, all the way out to the Nab. Dad shut the shop for an hour and came up at low water to watch, in his good boots, and explained, at length, [why the full moon makes the low tide lower](<ref:page:how-tides-work#h:The moon> "Dad's version is shorter than mine"). I wrote it all up properly later. He and Sam got on at once: two men who like equipment.

Missed the bindery tonight, for the first time. Fergus will forgive me for a cliff.
