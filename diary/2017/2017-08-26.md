## Number four, before breakfast

Up at five, Priya at the door with the borrowed car at half past, at the bottom of Ben Chonzie by seven. It's known as the most boring Munro in Scotland: a long grassy lump above Glen Turret, a track most of the way up and a fence to follow to the top. I loved it. Nobody else there, the hills going pink, mountain hares bolting from under our boots with their white bums disappearing into the heather. Top at nine, 931 metres, a cairn, a wind shelter, and the whole of Perthshire.

Number four. Back at the car by eleven.

## The last Saturday

Then straight to Leith, still in my boots, for my last shift at the Spoke Room. I got there at one and fixed exactly two things: a puncture and a squeaky saddle. At four everyone stopped, somebody put the kettle on, and the woman who runs it brought out a cake with a bicycle drawn on it in icing, slightly wonky, which is how you know it was made with love. The honesty tin had a ribbon on it.

They gave me a card signed by everyone (the Dutch bike man wrote "thank you for the brakes", ten months later) and a little spoke key on a string. I made a speech of about eleven words and then had to go and look very hard at the tool wall.

::media[The Frankenbike, finished: faded red, odd wheels, a bell that plays half a tune. Mine]{file="vintage-bicycle-2"}

Ten months of Saturdays. I came in not knowing a cone spanner from an ice-cream cone, and I'm leaving on a bike I built myself.
