## Day one

Walked down Leith Walk at half eight in the brown trousers, with a packed lunch, like a child on the first day of school. Up the stairs of the old warehouse, through the door, and there were twelve desks and eleven people and one empty desk with a sticky note on it saying MIRA in capitals. I nearly cried at the sticky note.

Dougie gave me the tour, which takes four minutes, then sat me down with a cup of tea in somebody else's mug and said:

> We make maps of where the water goes when it rains too much. Councils use them to decide where to build and where not to. Get one wrong and somebody's kitchen floods. So we check everything twice, and then Ines checks it again.

## Ines

Ines sits opposite me. She's from Porto, has been here three years, and has the tidiest computer I've ever seen: four folders on the desktop, all named. She showed me the server, the naming rules (so many rules) and where the good biscuits are hidden. By lunchtime I'd decided I want to be her when I grow up.

---

## What I did

- installed things
- read the house style guide for maps (forty pages, every colour named)
- traced a culvert off an old drainage plan, which felt exactly like the dissertation and made me feel at home
- broke nothing

Walked home along the Shore at half five, past the boats and the pubs and everybody else coming home from work. I am one of them now. I'm someone coming home from work!
