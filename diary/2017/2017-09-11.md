## Day one

Walked down Leith Walk in the brown trousers, with a packed lunch, like a child on the first day of school, and was there at a quarter to nine. Nobody else was. Dougie arrived at twenty past with a bag of croissants and said nobody comes in before nine "except the new ones".

Twelve desks, and mine is the one by the window, with two screens, a sticky note saying MIRA in capitals, and a mug with a sticker on it that says JUNIOR. Dougie said the mug was a joke. It isn't. I nearly cried at the sticky note.

He gave me the tour, which takes four minutes, then sat me down with a cup of tea in the JUNIOR mug and said:

> We make maps of where the water goes when it rains too much. Councils use them to decide where to build and where not to. Get one wrong and somebody's kitchen floods. So we check everything twice, and then Ines checks it again.

## Ines

Ines's desk is pushed up against mine. She's from Porto, talks very fast and very kindly, and has the tidiest computer I've ever seen: four folders on the desktop, all named. She showed me the server, the naming rules (so many rules), where the good mugs are, which radiator works, and where the good biscuits are hidden. By lunchtime I'd decided I want to be her when I grow up.

---

## What I did

- read the house style guide for maps (forty pages, every colour named)
- digitised culverts off old drainage plans, the places where a burn goes under a road in a pipe: forty-three of them
- had two of the forty-three found wrong by Ines, which she said was very good for a first day
- broke nothing
- got put on the kettle rota (Thursdays)

Walked home along the Shore at half five, past the boats and the pubs and everybody else coming home from work. I am one of them now. I'm someone coming home from work!
