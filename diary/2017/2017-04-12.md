I watched the cliff fall.

## Before

Third day, the last flights over the north end of the bay. I was down on the shelf at the foot of the cliff, re-measuring target nine, which had been kicked by a dog. Sam was up on top with the drone. It was a still, grey, ordinary morning, quarter past eleven.

## The fall

First there was a noise like someone pouring gravel out of a lorry, very far off. Then a crack. I looked up and a piece of the cliff about forty metres along from me, the size of a garden shed, came away from the face, slowly at first, and then all at once, and slid down in a fan of grey clay and shale and boulders onto the beach. Dust went up. Every gull on the cliff went up with it, screaming. Then the sea came in round the edge of the new heap as if it had been waiting, and it had.

It lasted maybe five seconds. I stood there with the pole in my hand. I didn't take a photo, I just watched.

::media[A slumped face of clay and shale like the one that came down, the same rock, a mile up the coast]{file="yorkshire-cliffs-1"}

Sam had the drone up and got it from above, all of it, the before and the after. He came down the path at a run to see if I was all right, and I was, I was forty metres away, I was fine. Then we both stood and looked at it and he said "well, that's your data".

In the evening I told Dad. He was quiet for a bit and then he said "how much?", and I said I don't know yet, a few metres at that point, maybe, the drone will say. He said "every winter now", and went out to the shop, even though the shop was closed.

I measured the new edge in my head all night.
