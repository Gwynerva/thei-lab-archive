## The loft

Went up into the loft to put the Christmas decorations away, and behind the box, in its old black case with my name in Tipp-Ex on the lid, was my cello. I haven't touched it since I was sixteen. Opened the case. It smelled of rosin and dust and the loft, and there was the scratch on the front that I always said was shaped like Norway (it is, if you squint and are generous about the fjords).

## The scale

Tuned it, or tried. The A string snapped with a noise like a shot and Mum shouted up the ladder to ask if I was alive. Played a scale on the other three, badly, sitting on a box of old school books with my knees round it, and my fingers remembered more than my head did. Where to put the second finger. How the bow wants to lean. The C string buzzing up through my ribs, which I'd completely forgotten and remembered all at once.

I stopped at sixteen because of exams, and because I was sixteen and everything else seemed more urgent. Neither of those reasons is still here.

## North

Then I did a mad thing and brought it home with me. On three trains. Dad drove it to the station in its own seat, with the seatbelt on, and lifted it onto the train for me as if it were a person.
