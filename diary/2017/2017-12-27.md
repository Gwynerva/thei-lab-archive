## Saltwick

First thing, Dad and I walked out along the cliff to Saltwick, the two of us, to look at the fence post at the top of my cliff. Still there, a little nearer the edge than in April. He looked at it for a long time and said "fast enough", and we stood there a while longer in the wind, not saying anything else, which with Dad is a whole conversation.

## The loft

Then up into the loft to put the Christmas decorations away, and behind the box was a carrier bag of my old cello music: the grade books, the scale book with my teacher's pencil all over it, the Elgar I never finished, and a sonata I was never good enough for and kept anyway.

::file[The sonata from the bag, which I'm going to be good enough for one day]{file="doc-cello" title="Chopin cello sonata"}

I sat on the loft ladder and read through the scale book like a diary. Every page has a date in the corner and a tick or a cross. The last date is in the spring I was sixteen.

## North

Brought the whole bag home with me, to go with the cello in the corner of my room. Three trains. Dad lifted my rucksack onto the train at Whitby as if it were made of glass, and stood on the platform until the train went round the bend.
