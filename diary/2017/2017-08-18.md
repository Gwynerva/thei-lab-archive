## Handed in

Handed in at 15.58, two minutes before the deadline, because the copy shop's binding machine jammed halfway through my second copy and had to be taken apart by a man with a screwdriver while I watched the clock and tried not to lie down on the floor. Then a run up the Bridges to the School office. The woman at the desk stamped it, said "Congratulations," and put it on top of a pile of about forty others, all the same blue. That's it. A year of my life, on top of a pile.

Then I stood on the pavement outside not knowing what to do with my hands.

## What's in it

The cliff at Saltwick has gone back 0.3 metres a year since 1853, on average, and nine metres in one winter. Six old surveys, eleven sets of aerial photos, one drone, thirty-one million points, and two old American reports whose layout I copied shamelessly for my results chapter:

## After

Priya and Callum were waiting at the flat with a cake in the shape of a cliff (Callum) with a toy caravan on the edge (Priya). We ate the caravan first.

Then we took the rest of the cake up Calton Hill and sat on the wall above the Fringe crowds, and I told them the whole thing, chapter by chapter, and they let me. Dr Grieve emailed at six: "Well done. Now rest." I'm going to do exactly as I'm told.
