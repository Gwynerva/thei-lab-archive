The last evening at Lowe Bindery.

I finished my book: six signatures, coptic stitch, grey boards, and the endpapers cut from the sheet of marbled paper Fergus gave me on my birthday, "for when you're good enough". I asked him if I was good enough. He picked it up, opened it flat on the table, ran his thumb down the chain on the spine, and said "aye. Good enough". I've never been so pleased with two words.

::media[Fergus's marbled paper, now the inside covers of the new notebook]{file="marbled-paper-3"}

It's the next notebook for this diary. This one's nearly full anyway, it's got about a week left in it.

## Twelve Tuesdays

What I can do now that I couldn't in March:

- fold with the grain, every time, without thinking
- a pamphlet, a case binding, a slipcase, a coptic book
- sew without stabbing myself (mostly)
- tell good paper from bad by the sound it makes when you flick it

Afterwards all six of us went to the pub on the corner and Fergus came for exactly one half of bitter, then put on his cap, said "keep sewing", and walked off up the hill. The grey cat was in the bindery window as we left, on the warm end of the press, not looking at anybody.

Three good things:

1. "Good enough"
2. the pub with five people I didn't know in March
3. walking home with a book I made, under my arm
