## The hill

The windy tail of a storm all evening, and then at midnight, exactly as promised, the cloud tore off and there were the stars. Priya drove us out past Flotterstone, and we walked up the side of Turnhouse Hill in the dark with red torches, sheep coughing at us from the heather like old men, lay on two foam mats in every jumper we own, and looked up at Orion's club.

## The meteors

The [Orionids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orionids "crumbs of Halley's comet, burning up") are bits of a comet that the Earth drives through every October. We counted **twenty-three** in an hour and a half. Most were tiny scratches, there and gone before you could point. One went right across the sky, green, and left a trail you could see for two seconds afterwards, and we both shouted, and somewhere below us a dog barked back.

## Mine

Priya asked which star is mine. It's in the Whale, low in the south at this time of year, and it was too faint to see tonight. Nana always says it comes and goes but it always comes back. Grandad showed it to her from the deck of a ship, so it's the family's star, really; I just have the name.

Home at three, frozen and happy, with a little map of the sky in the margin and the meteors as arrows.
