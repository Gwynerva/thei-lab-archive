## The hill

The windy tail of a storm all evening, and the last bus out to Flotterstone with a sleeping mat, a flask and two jumpers. I walked up the side of Turnhouse Hill in the dark by red torchlight, sheep coughing at me from the heather like old men, and at eleven, as promised, the cloud tore off and there were the stars. Lay on my back in the heather and looked up at Orion's club.

## The meteors

The [Orionids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orionids "crumbs of Halley's comet, burning up") are bits of a comet that the Earth drives through every October. I counted **twenty-three** in two hours. Most were tiny scratches at the edge of my eye, there and gone. Three were long and bright and left trails, and one broke into two pieces, and I said "Oh!" out loud to nobody, and somewhere below me a dog barked back.

## Mine

Priya picked me up at the reservoir car park at one with the heater on full, and on the drive home asked which star is mine. It's in the Whale, low in the south at this time of year, and too faint to see tonight. Nana always says it comes and goes but it always comes back. Grandad showed it to her from the deck of a ship, so it's the family's star, really; I just have the name.

Frozen to the bone, and happy, with a little map of the sky in the margin and the meteors drawn as arrows.
