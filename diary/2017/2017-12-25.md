## Christmas Day

Nana took charge of the kitchen from a chair by the stove, in her good cardigan and the brooch Grandad brought back from somewhere hot. She approved of the turkey, disapproved of the sprouts ("too early on"), and sent Dad out twice for things we already had.

Clare and Dev were asleep on the sofa together by three, like a pair of cats.

## Presents

I'd made everybody a notebook and a robin. Then the extras:

- Dad got the tide booklet. He turned every page, checked three dates against the printed book from the shop, and said "Close enough." Then he put it in his top pocket and kept it there all day.
- Mum got a big marbled notebook for her garden plans.
- Clare got a fat A5 one "for the wedding lists, volume two".
- Nana got a small checked one, and a jar of her own rowan jelly back as a joke. She laughed for a full minute.
- I got wellies (from Mum, who'd heard about Fife), a head torch (from Dad, obviously), and from Nana a tin of :hint[parkin]{note="Yorkshire gingerbread made with oats and black treacle; better after a week in the tin"} and a needle book she'd sewn, with my initials on it in red.

## The table

Crackers, the bad jokes read out in turn, Dad carving as if it were a precision job, which in his opinion it is. Nana said grace, short, and then said the gravy needed salt, which it did.

---

At dusk Dad and I walked down to the harbour, the two of us, to look at the water. High tide at 16.48, he said, reading it out of his new booklet. It was, give or take.
