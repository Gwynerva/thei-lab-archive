Gave it the moon. Or the biggest bit of the moon: :hint[M2]{note="the principal lunar tide, the moon's pull twice a day, every 12 hours and 25 minutes"}, the largest wave in the sum at nearly every harbour in Britain. Then the sun's, S2. Then three smaller ones. Five waves, added up and plotted for a week of Whitby — and it looks like a tide! It goes up, it goes down, it does springs and neaps.

It's also two hours wrong. This first version is completely naïve: a plain sum, no corrections for the moon's slow eighteen-year wobble, no idea where the moon actually is on the day. The shape is right and the clock is wrong.
