Three more waves today: N2, for the moon being nearer or further, and K1 and O1, the once-a-day ones that make one high water higher than the other. :hint[M2]{note="the principal lunar tide, the moon's pull twice a day, every 12 hours and 25 minutes"} still does most of the work, as it does at nearly every harbour in Britain. Five waves, added up and plotted for a week of Whitby — and it looks like a tide! It goes up, it goes down, it does springs and neaps.

It's also still forty minutes wrong. This first version is completely naïve: a plain sum, no corrections for the moon's slow eighteen-year wobble, nothing for the way the tide trips over itself coming into a shallow harbour. The shape is right and the clock is wrong.
