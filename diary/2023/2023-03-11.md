I went to the school :hint[*loppemarked*]{note="a flea market; every school, choir and brass band in Bergen holds one in spring"} for a lampshade and came home with an accordion.

It was on a trestle table in the gym, between a box of ski boots and another box of ski boots: a red one with cream keys, in a case that smelled of somebody's attic. The man selling it said it was his father's, that his father played it at every wedding in the valley until 1980 and then never again. I asked why. He shrugged. Four hundred kroner. I paid before I'd thought about it, which is how I do most things.

::media[The accordion on the kitchen table, looking more expensive than it was]{file="accordion-1" layout="natural"}

What I know about accordions, as of this evening:

- the right hand is a piano, more or less
- the left hand is twelve buttons, and I know what one of them does
- the bellows leak somewhere near the bottom, so every chord ends in a sigh
- Biscuit is against it

Carried it up the hill in its case like a suitcase of bricks. Jonas met me at the door and said *nei* in a long Bergen way that goes down and then up. Then he asked for a go and played the first line of a waltz, because of course his grandmother taught him, because everyone's grandmother in Norway taught them something.

I don't know why I wanted it. I think I wanted something that was only for fun. The cello has a concert in it, the kayak has a launch date in it, work has a September in it. This has nothing in it at all.
