The deck is on.

Saturday, ten till six, in Jonas's workshop with both heaters roaring and NRK on the radio, which neither of us was listening to. [The deck](ref:section:kayak/deck "seven weeks of evenings") has been seven weeks of evenings — cutting, fitting, cutting again — and today was the part you can't take back.

How it went, more or less:

1. Mixed the epoxy with silica until it was the colour and stiffness of peanut butter. Jonas's word; in Norwegian it's *peanøttsmør*, which is longer than the job.
2. Buttered the sheer clamps all the way round, both sides, bow to stern.
3. Lowered the deck on, four hands and no talking, and it sat down on the hull as if it had always meant to.
4. Straps every hand-width, then copper wire at the ends where the curve fights back.
5. Wiped the squeeze-out before it went off. Missed some. Found it later with my sleeve.

At about three Jonas glued his thumb to the coaming and stood very still saying *nei, nei, nei* while I got it free with a scraper and acetone. He has a small shiny patch on it now where the skin used to be.

When the last strap was tight he walked round her twice and said, "She's a boat now." I said she was a boat before. He said before she was a bowl.

::media[The workshop at the end of the day, half tidied, her under the lamp]{file="boat-workshop-1"}

Home at seven smelling of resin. Biscuit sniffed me and left the room. Seven coats of varnish next, starting in two weeks, and then the water.
