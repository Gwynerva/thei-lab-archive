Bergenstest, the written part. Four hours in a hall at the university with sixty other people and the radiators on full in May.

Reading first: an article about salmon farms and one about whether children should have phones, and I understood both, which three years ago would have looked like witchcraft. Listening: a radio interview with a woman who restores boathouses in Hardanger, and I nearly laughed, because Jonas could have been on the other end of it. Then the writing: two essays by hand, in pencil, with a rubber, like being twelve.

I wrote about Whitby harbour in the first one, the bridge and the shop and Dad's hands, and I think it's the best thing I've written all year, in either language. The second was about cars in cities. I said ban them. I'm not sure I believe it.

Words I couldn't find, in order of panic:

- *barnehage* came back after a minute; nursery, it was there all along
- the word for a gutter, the kind on a roof. I wrote *the rain's road* and hoped
- *tidevann*. I had *flo* and *fjære*, high and low, and blanked on the word for both together, so I wrote *the sea going up and down*, which is at least true

Walked out into sun, which in Bergen in May is a kind of applause. Jonas was at the gate with a coffee and didn't ask how it went, which is the correct thing. Results in June.

One week till the launch.
