Dad is sixty.

We put the sign up at four, Mum and me and a ladder borrowed from the bakery next door, while Dad was out on an errand Mum had invented. Four screws into the old holes. Then we went and stood on the other side of the harbour by the bridge and waited, eating ice creams, like birdwatchers.

He came round the corner at twenty past with the paper under his arm, and stopped. He stood under it for a long time. He didn't look round. Then he went in, and we gave him ten minutes and went over.

He was behind the counter sorting shackles that didn't need sorting. He said, "Scarborough." Mum said, "Bergen, love." He looked at me for a while and said, "You've got the S's right," and then he had to go into the back for a bit to look for something.

The party was in the shop, among the rope and the tins of antifouling: the lifeboat crew, his friends from the trade, Clare and Dev up from Leeds, Clare quiet and on lemonade all night, which I didn't think about until later. Mum had made a cake in the shape of a boat that looked more like a slipper. Dad's speech was one sentence long. "Thank you for coming, the tide's at eleven," and he sat down to the loudest cheer of the night.

We walked home up the hill at ten, everybody's arms linked, and I didn't check my phone once, and it wasn't even hard.
