## Fourteen

The last window went back in at four this afternoon: the eastern gable, up in the loft, the one that looks straight at the glacier. Jonas did it from the ladder, as promised, and I stood in the loft and handed him the pins, and then neither of us knew what to do with our hands.

Fourteen windows since the second of June. A summer.

## What each one needed

- [x] Off its hinges and over to the trestles in the barn, numbered
- [x] The old putty out, with heat and patience, never with force
- [x] The glass out and stacked in order, the cracked panes swapped for old ones from the box
- [x] The frames back to wood where the paint had gone, and fed with linseed oil
- [x] Linseed putty, pressed in with the thumb, and a week to skin over
- [x] Two coats of white linseed paint, a week apart
- [ ] Number nine, Dad's, wants its last coat when the weather turns dry. Next week.

## What they taught me

That an old house tells you the order of things if you let it. That linseed is never in a hurry. That Dad is faster with a putty knife than I will ever be, and that Jonas can stand on a ladder for three hours talking about the weather in Lerwick.

From the road, tonight, the house looks exactly as it did in June. From the inside it is a different house.
