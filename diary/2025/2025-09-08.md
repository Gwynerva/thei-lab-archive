## The way in

The night train to Zugdidi rattled like a drawer of cutlery and I slept anyway. At dawn a minibus, nine of us and a sack of potatoes, four hours up the gorge of the Enguri, past the great dam and its lake the colour of a swimming pool, the driver taking the bends with one hand and crossing himself at every roadside shrine with the other. Nobody spoke. Everybody looked out of the window.

## Mestia

Then Mestia, and the towers: square, grey, tall, with slits at the top, standing between the houses and behind the petrol station and in back gardens. Jonas climbed the one at the museum and came down saying the ladders inside were the best joinery he has seen all year, and he has seen a lot of joinery this year.

## Two maps

After supper I spread the old Soviet sheet from the market on the guesthouse floor beside the new map we are walking with. The towers are in the same places, every one, as if nothing has happened in fifty years. The glaciers are not. On the old sheet the ice comes down the valleys a long way further, in neat blue hatching, to places that on the new map are moraine and birch. I traced the difference with my finger for a long time. Tomorrow we walk.
