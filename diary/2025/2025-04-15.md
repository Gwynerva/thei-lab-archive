## A man on the roof

The roofer came at eight, a wiry man of sixty or so with three ladders on his van and the air of someone who has seen worse. He went up the longest ladder with a coffee in one hand, walked the ridge as though it were a pavement, and spent two hours lifting tiles and tutting.

What he found:

- [x] the tiles mostly sound, "better than the house deserves"
- [ ] about forty cracked or slipped, most of them on the north side
- [ ] the flashing round the chimney rotten through, which is the damp in the north room
- [ ] two battens gone soft over the stairs
- [ ] the gutters "decorative"

## What it means

That the roof can be mended, not replaced. That's the sentence I've been waiting for since November. He'll send a price, and it will be a big one, and it will be smaller than a new roof.

::media[Old tiles and lichen, from the top of his ladder]{file="old-roof-1"}

He said the roof was laid in the twenties with :hint[takstein]{note="roof tiles; these are the old red clay kind"} from a brickworks down the fjord that closed long ago, and that you can't buy tiles like these any more, only take them off other old roofs. Then he looked at the barn and said nothing, which was worse.
