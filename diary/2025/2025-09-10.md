## Zhabeshi to Adishi

Day two. Up through pine forest from Zhabeshi to a long open ridge with the whole Caucasus along one side, snow peaks lined up like teeth, Tetnuldi the whitest. I stopped so often to look that Jonas started timing me. A shepherd's dog walked with us for an hour and then turned back at some line on the ground that only it could see.

Down into Adishi by lunchtime: a handful of houses, three towers, a church, and more cows than people. The guesthouse has a wooden balcony, a stove, and a cat with one ear who sat on my rucksack and would not be moved.

## The river

In the afternoon we went on up the valley without the packs to look at tomorrow's river, and sat for an hour on a boulder under the ice, listening to it crack. The water is grey with ground-up mountain and so loud you have to lean in to talk. Jonas threw a stick in to see how fast it went, watched it vanish, and said nothing, which was the answer.

## The evening

Back for soup, bread, cheese and a jug of something homemade that was mostly courage. The guesthouse mother asked, by pointing at the river and then at us, whether we were taking the horses. We nodded. She looked relieved on our behalf. You can hear the river from the bed.
