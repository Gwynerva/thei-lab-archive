Up the old road through Måbødalen at seven, before the coaches, the river far below the hairpins and the tunnels dripping. Vøringsfossen from the top is less a waterfall than a hole in the ground with a noise coming out of it. Jonas stood on the bridge over the falls with his hands in his pockets and said, "Well," which from him is a sonnet.

Then away from all of it and out onto the plateau, which is the walk the book is really about.

Notes for the page, written on the rock where we ate:

- Park at Fossli, not lower down. The path is better from the top.
- Keep the gorge on your left and your feet well back from it; the edge is not fenced, and it is not kind.
- The cairn is the turn. Miss it and you are walking to Finse.
- The tarn is the lunch. Nobody has ever regretted the tarn.
- If the cloud comes down, go back the way you came. That is :hint[*fjellvett*]{note="the mountain code: turn back in time, tell someone where you are going, and there is no shame in either"}, and it is the only rule up here.

Home on the late ferry, burnt on one side of my face only, which tells you which way I was looking all day.
