## The last day

The easy one: down the valley from Iprari on a dirt road along the river, through hay meadows where whole families were cutting by hand, the scythes going in time. Then a bend, and Ushguli, the towers coming up out of the valley floor in clusters, and above everything Shkhara, white from end to end.

## What I did with it

I drew it. The valley, the river, the four little groups of towers, the church on its knoll, the white line of the ridge, in the margin of the notebook, small, sitting on the guesthouse wall with my boots off. It is the first map I have drawn in weeks that nobody is paying for and nobody will check.

Then I drew the whole trek on the facing page, the way I used to draw the Munros: four days as four short lines between four dots, the river a wiggle across the third line, the pass a little tent shape, and a horse, badly, which Jonas says looks like a table with opinions.

## Supper

Khachapuri again, because it is the law, and a salad of tomatoes that tasted of tomatoes, and a glass of something red from a jug. The guesthouse grandmother asked where we came from, and when Jonas said Norway she nodded and said "Cold," and when I said England she nodded and said "Rain," and we agreed with her on both counts.

## The knee

It held. Four days, every step of them, and I have not dared write that before today in case it heard.
