## Sixty-two

Dad's birthday, the first he has spent out of England since he was nineteen and on a trawler (his version) or a ferry to Rotterdam (Mum's).

Breakfast on the porch at seven, because he wakes at six in any country. His presents:

- a map I drew of Whitby harbour as the 1853 survey has it, with a small red cross on the corner where the chandlery would stand a hundred years later;
- from Jonas, a boat hook he forged and turned in the barn, which Dad weighed in his hand for a full minute without saying anything;
- from Mum, socks. Every year socks. He says he would worry if it stopped.

## On the water

In the afternoon Jonas borrowed a double kayak from the centre in Utne and took Dad out on the fjord, Mum on the porch with her hand over her mouth and the cardiologist's leaflet in her lap. I followed in *Nana June*. Dad has never been in a kayak in his life. He sat bolt upright, paddled as if the water owed him money, and did not stop smiling for two hours. Out by the point he turned round and shouted across to me the only thing Nana ever said about boats:

> Never trust a calm sea.

It was dead calm. We trusted it a little.

## Supper

Sigrid came with a basket of the first cherries and a bottle of her own cider, and made Dad a speech in Norwegian which Jonas translated as "happy birthday" and which was clearly a great deal longer than that. We ate on the porch until the midges came.

Three good things:

1. Dad's face when he weighed the boat hook.
2. Mum and Sigrid doing the washing-up together in two languages and a lot of pointing.
3. The fjord at eleven at night, the colour of an old pewter plate.
