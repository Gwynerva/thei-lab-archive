## Pressing day

Eleven hours of it. The press up the track on the Nesheims' trailer at nine, the mill roaring, Kari's sheep waiting at the fence for the pulp as if they had been told, Jonas on the screw, and the juice coming out cloudy and gold into the carboys, which by the end of the day stood in a row in the cellar like a choir.

What I will do differently next year, written while I still remember:

:::list{counter="lower-roman"}
1. Start picking a week earlier, and pick in the mornings, when the apples are cold.
2. Wash the crates the day before, not at seven on the day.
3. Keep the two nameless trees apart, in their own carboy, to see what they are.
4. More cloths. There are never enough cloths.
5. A chair for Sigrid by the press, because she will not sit down otherwise and should.
:::

## Sigrid

She tasted every carboy with a little glass and the face she uses for reading other people's letters, and said nothing, which Jonas says is the best thing she can say. Then, washing the glass at the outside tap at the end of the day, without looking up:

:::quote[Sigrid, at the tap]{align="center"}
Cider is made in the orchard. In the cellar you can only spoil it.
:::

Two hundred and twelve litres. I wrote it on the barn door in chalk. It is the most of anything I have ever made.
