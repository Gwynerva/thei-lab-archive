## The way here

Fetched them from Bergen on the bus and the ferry, Isla asleep on my lap the whole way over with her fist in my hair, which I did not mind in the least. Clare looked tired in the way only people with two-year-olds look tired, and then she saw the fjord, and looked less tired. The last time I held Isla she was four weeks old and asleep in a hospital blanket in Leeds. Now she has opinions, shoes with lights in them, and a stuffed rabbit called Mira. Clare swears that was Isla's own idea.

## The house

Isla stood in the hall, looked up the stairs, and said "Big." Then she met Biscuit, who allowed himself to be patted on the head once, carefully, and then withdrew to the top of the stairs, where he stayed all evening watching her with great dignity and some alarm.

Clare walked round every room twice, touched the window frames, opened the pantry, looked out of the west window at the fjord for a long time, and said Mum had described it all on the phone and had got it all wrong, because it was better.

## The evening

Fish fingers for Isla, fish soup for us, and Isla asleep in Jonas's old cot by eight, under a quilt, with the window open and the light not going anywhere. Clare and I sat on the porch with a glass each and did not say very much. We didn't need to yet. There is a week.
