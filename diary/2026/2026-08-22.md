Married.

It is very late and the house is full of sleeping people and I want to write down the small things before they go, because the big things will keep.

- Mum, at breakfast, putting Nana's silver brooch in my hand, the one with the anchor, without a word, and closing my fingers over it.
- Priya, up before any of us, with an armful of flowers she had carried over from the market in Bergen, taking over everything nobody had thought of.
- The rain stopping at six as if someone had turned a tap.
- Dad's hand, at the bottom of the row of trees, squeezing mine once, which is the most Dad has ever said about anything.
- Isla, at the end of the words, saying "Again!" very loudly, and everybody laughing, and Jonas laughing hardest.
- Ragnhild at the top of the slope in the shade with a blanket on her knees, holding my face in both her hands and telling me the house was happy.
- The copper ring, which I took off this morning for the first time since Christmas and put in a little box on the kitchen shelf, between the jam jars, where I can see it from the sink.

> Make do and mend.

Nana said it about socks and sheets and broken cups. I think she meant marriages too, the good kind, the kind you keep. Tonight I believe her more than I ever have.
