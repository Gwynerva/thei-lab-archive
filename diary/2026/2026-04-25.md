## Before

I could not eat lunch. Jonas made me eat lunch. We drove in over Kvamskogen with the cello on the back seat in its seat belt, like a passenger, and I tuned it four times in the vestry in Sandviken and it was in tune all four times. Through the vestry door I could hear the violin, then the viola, then the bass, and then my name.

## After

Afterwards, in the church hall, coffee and cake and a great many kind things said in two languages. Anna hugged me so hard my bow arm hurt. The bass player, who went just before me, shook my hand and said nothing about anything, which I appreciated more than I can say.

We drove home in the dark and caught the last ferry. I sat on deck in the cold with my hands in my pockets and the fjord going by black, and I was not thinking about the playing at all, only about the cello, the old school one from Whitby with the scratch shaped like Norway, which spent nine years in its case under my bed and tonight played in a church in the country the scratch is shaped like.

Sigrid rang at eleven to ask. I said it went well. She said, "I know. Anna rang me."
