Two days. I woke at four and lay listening to the house, every room full of people I love, all of them breathing. And I wanted Nana so much I had to go down to the orchard in my dressing gown and stand under the old Gravenstein in the half-light until it passed.

Mum found me there. She didn't ask. She brought two cups of tea and stood beside me and said, after a while, "She'd have had you pinning hems all week, you know." I laughed so hard I spilled the tea. I think that was the idea.

We went back in for breakfast. Dad had made porridge for everybody.
