## The morning

I went down to the boathouse at seven and swam, the first swim of the new winter: nine degrees, the fjord flat, mist going up off it in slow curls. Sixty seconds, counted out loud. Then up the slope with my towel round my shoulders and a mug of coffee, to the desk, to write this. Ten years ago tonight I wrote the first one.

::media[The fjord from above this morning, the islands and the farms along the shore]{file="hardangerfjord-3" layout="stretch"}

## Ten years, in the things I made

I have been going through [all the projects](/projects/) this week, the way you go through a drawer when you move, picking things up and putting them down. What ten years made, more or less in order:

- a year of a course in Edinburgh, which taught me what a layer is and gave me a gown that did not fit;
- a dissertation on how fast the cliffs at Whitby fall into the sea (thirty centimetres a year, nine metres in one bad winter), which made me a cartographer;
- a small library that predicted tides from harmonics, which strangers on three continents used and one of them fixed;
- thirty-four Munros, the last one Ben Nevis, saved for last on purpose;
- the cello, back out of its case after nine years, and never put back;
- a ginger cat, adopted in Edinburgh, flown over the North Sea, now old and on my foot;
- a move to Bergen, with that cat, to a city where it rains more days than not;
- a kayak, built in a borrowed workshop with a man I married last month;
- two hundred and eighty kilometres on foot from Porto to Santiago, both feet intact;
- a house with fourteen windows and forty trees;
- a book of thirty walks, at the printer this week;
- a wedding in an orchard;
- and the swimming, through four winters now, which I would not have believed in 2016.

## What stayed the same

The margins. I drew a little map of the day in the margin of the first page in Leith, the flat and the walk to the university, and I drew one this morning, the boathouse and the steps and the mist. Lists. Counting rain. Nana's sayings, which I have quoted here so often that they have become mine.

---

## What I would tell the girl in Leith

That the lists help, but not in the way she thinks. That it is all right to stop for a while. That Nana was right about nearly everything, and especially about calm seas. That the cliffs at Whitby will still be falling in ten years, and the rain will still be falling in Bergen, and so will she, sometimes, and it is fine: you get up, you make do, you mend.

That it is worth writing down. All of it. Even the days with only one line.

Rain: day one of a new count. Biscuit on my foot. Ten years.
