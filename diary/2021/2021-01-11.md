## Why Norwegian

Dad has always said that if you rowed straight out from the end of Whitby pier and kept going, you'd hit Norway. As a child I took it as a threat. Lately it has started to sound like directions. There's also a job advert from a chart company in Bergen that I keep opening and not applying for, but that's not why. (It's a bit why.)

## The lesson

[The course](https://example.org/norsk-a1 "Mondays and Thursdays, half seven till nine") is online: twelve of us in little squares, and a teacher in Tromsø with a candle on her windowsill and the patience of a glacier. We said our names and where we live. *Jeg heter Mira, og jeg bor i Leith.* A man in Aberdeen is learning because his daughter married a Norwegian and the grandchildren have started answering him in it. A woman in Cardiff wants to read crime novels in the original. I said I make maps, which came out as "I am a map", and nobody corrected me, out of kindness.

New words, lesson one:

- *hei*, hello, and also goodbye, which saves time
- *takk*, thanks; you say it about four times a sentence
- *jeg heter*, I am called
- :hint[*kart*]{note="map; the first word I looked up on my own, the night before"}, which is the one I care about

Two hours afterwards I still had *takk* going round my head like a tune you can't get rid of. Biscuit has been told that he is a *katt*. He took it well.
