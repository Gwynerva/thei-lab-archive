## The flat

An empty flat is louder than a full one. Every step rang. Marta's half went at nine in her cousin's van, across town, and Biscuit has been at the cattery since yesterday, glaring, for his last two weeks in Scotland. Then it was me and the cleaning, and the marks on the walls where nearly five years of things had been: a pale square where the calendar hung, a dent where Callum's bike handlebar met the plaster every morning, the pencil lines on the kitchen door frame where he measured all of us in 2017.

[The flat](ref:project:leith-flat "nearly five years, three flatmates and a cat") was never ours, but it was mine. The hall bookshelf went with the boxes on Thursday, in pieces, though it was built for that hall, to the millimetre, and will look wrong anywhere else. The letting agent said the next tenants will be *delighted*. They'd better be.

## The keys

The agent came at four for the inspection; the keys go through the letterbox in the morning. She checked the oven and the skirting boards and said *lovely* nine times. I walked down the four flights without looking back, and then stood on the pavement and looked back for ten minutes.

## The Shore

One last walk along the Shore in the evening: the river still, people at the tables outside, the swans by the bridge, the smell of chips and seaweed. I bought chips, for the record.

Tonight: Marta's old sofa, and no Biscuit on my feet. Tomorrow at six the airport, Amsterdam, Bergen, and then ten days in a room I've never seen.
