## Up

The first mountain, and the easiest: Fløyen, the one right behind the city with the funicular up its face. I walked, because three separate Norwegians have told me that the funicular *up* is for tourists and the funicular *down* is for people with knees. Out of the white house, up the steps behind Sandviken, along Fjellveien, where the whole city jogs on a Sunday, and then the zigzag path through the trees. Fifty minutes, a good three hundred metres, and a sweat I wasn't proud of.

## The top

And then the city, all of it, laid out like one of Dougie's wall maps: Vågen with the boats in it, Bryggen a row of coloured teeth, the white houses of Sandviken climbing the hill to the left, Nordnes pointing its finger into the fjord, and past them islands and more islands until the sea. Blue sky, for once: the forecast promised cloud, and the forecast was wrong in my favour. There are goats on Fløyen. The council keeps them to eat the undergrowth, and they were eating a leaflet of the walks.

## The seven

Anna says a proper Bergenser has climbed the seven mountains round the city, and that some people do all seven in one day, once a year, which I am choosing not to think about. The seven, as she counts them:

- Fløyen (today)
- Ulriken, the highest
- Løvstakken
- Damsgårdsfjellet
- Lyderhorn
- Sandviksfjellet, the one right behind my house
- Rundemanen

Came down in the funicular after all, with a pushchair, two dogs and a hen party. My knees said thank you.
