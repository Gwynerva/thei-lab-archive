## The barn

An old barn on a farm out at Fana, half an hour on the bus and ten minutes up a muddy track: eight of us on chopping stumps in a circle, a wood stove going, and a teacher with forearms like ship's rope who has carved a spoon a day for twenty years. On a tarpaulin: birch logs, cut green this week, the bark still wet. The whole barn smelled like the inside of a cucumber.

## The axe

First, the axe. You hold it close to the head and chop down along the grain onto the stump, hands always behind the edge, *alltid*, always. Split the log in half, then the half in half, then take the bark off and the pith out, and there is a pale wet board where a tree was. The teacher came round and moved my left hand back two inches without a word, and it was suddenly easy.

## The knife

Then the knife: a short blade on a red handle, sharper than anything in my kitchen.

::media[The red-handled knife they lent me, on the stone step outside the barn at lunch]{file="mora-knife"}

The grips have names. The thumb push. The chest lever, where you hold the knife against your chest and open your shoulders like a door. The one everyone calls *the potato peeler*, which is exactly what it sounds like and the only one I could do on the first try.

## New words

- *spikke*: to carve with a knife, small and slow
- *bjørk*: birch
- *skjekniv*: the spoon knife, the hooked one, for tomorrow
- *flis*: a chip, a shaving; by five the floor was all *flis*

By five o'clock I had a blank, a flat stick with a spoon drawn on it in pencil, and a blister. Tomorrow, the bowl.
