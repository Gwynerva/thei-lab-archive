## The tin

Another box from Dad, bigger and heavier. Inside, Nana's sewing box wrapped in a bath towel, and a toffee tin held shut with an elastic band that broke when I touched it. The tin is full of letters. Forty-one of them, Mum says; she counted them and didn't read them, which is very Mum.

They run from 1952 to 1959, almost all from Arthur, my grandad, whom I remember as a pair of big hands and a smell of pipe smoke and not much else. He wrote them from ships: Rotterdam, Hull, Gothenburg, Bergen, Bergen, Bergen. His ship ran timber and fish back and forth across the North Sea, and Bergen seems to have been the other end of his life.

## Bergen

I read the first six tonight, in order, at the kitchen table, with a pencil behind my ear for no reason. The sixth is from Bergen, in March 1953:

:::quote[Arthur to June, Bergen, March 1953]{align="center"}
It rains here like it means it, and nobody minds. The houses on the quay are painted like a box of sweets.
:::

I have that job advert open in the other tab. I've had it open since December. It is for a chart company in Bergen. I'm writing that down and then I'm going to bed.

## What I'm doing with them

Typing them up, one a night, exactly as written, his spelling and all. They're Nana's, and so they're private: they'll live behind a link and not out in the open. But they deserve to be read by someone other than a tin.
