## A year on the balcony

The weather station has read the balcony every ten minutes for a year today: 52,560 readings, give or take the slug. I sat down with the spreadsheet tonight and a cup of tea, the way you'd sit down with an old friend.

## The numbers

- coldest: −6.2 at five in the morning on the eleventh of February, the week of the snow
- warmest: 27.9 on the twenty-ninth of July last year, with the balcony door wide open
- wettest day: 31.4 millimetres, early in October
- driest stretch: eleven days in April, all east wind
- lowest pressure: 968 hectopascals, in a gale last November that I don't remember at all, because of everything else that November

---

## What it was for

I built it in lockdown because I needed something to watch that changed. It turned out the thing it measured best was me: the weeks I checked it every hour, the weeks I didn't look once. In July it comes down off the rail and into a box, and in September, if the landlord in Bergen says yes to a bracket, it goes up again on the other side of the sea, where the rain gauge will finally earn its keep.\
Readings, 52,560. Days missed, four. Slugs, one.
