## The file

Open elevation data for Arthur's Seat, contours every ten metres, smoothed so the lines don't wobble, then turned into a list of moves for the pen: down here, go there, lift. Four thousand little moves. Getting the settings right took longer than building the frame.

## The first line

Half past ten on a Saturday morning, A3 cartridge paper taped to the bed, Marta and Biscuit both watching. Pen down. The carriage moved off to the right, and the nib drew the 50-metre contour round the foot of the hill in one slow loop, and closed it, and lifted. Eleven seconds. Marta clapped. Biscuit left the room.

Then the 60, the 70, the 80, each loop a little smaller and a little higher, the Crags pinching in on one side and the Lion's Haunch bulging out on the other, until the last little ring at 250, which is the summit, give or take the trig point. Forty minutes for the whole hill. I didn't breathe properly for most of them.

## What went wrong

- The pen ran dry at the 190, so there's a gap like a missing step.
- The paper cockled under the wet ink round the Crags, where the lines bunch up.
- 35 millimetres a second is too fast for a fine nib on the corners. Slower next time.
