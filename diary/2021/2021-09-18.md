## Up Ulriken

Anna picked me up at eight with two *matpakker* and a thermos the size of a fire extinguisher. Ulriken is the highest of the seven, 643 metres, and we went up it the hard way, on a stone staircase that goes on for a thousand steps and then some. At the top we came out above a lid of cloud lying flat over the whole city, the mast sticking up out of it like a periscope, and for ten minutes we just stood there.

## Across

Vidden is the high plateau between Ulriken and Fløyen: fifteen kilometres of rock, heather and small lakes, no trees, cairns to keep you honest. The cloud came up to meet us after half an hour and then came and went all day. Anna taught me *fjellvett*, the mountain code, which is mostly *turn back in time, there's no shame in it* and *tell someone where you're going*, and which she says every Norwegian child learns before they can spell it. We ate the *matpakker* by a tarn in a hole in the cloud, the water so still it had the sky in it twice.

The pictures make it look like a fine day. They're lying. In between them it was a white room with wind in it, and twice the only thing I could see was Anna's red jacket ten metres ahead.

## Down

Six hours, end to end, and then the long way down through the trees to the city with my knees shouting. Anna dropped me at the bottom of my lane and said *første tur*, the first trip, as if it were a thing that goes on a list. It does now.
