## The parcel

The quilt came this morning in a box that once held a microwave, packed by Dad, which means it was wrapped in a clean dust sheet, then bubble wrap, then a bin bag, then a note in capitals on top: THIS WAY UP. IT'S A QUILT, NOT A BOMB.

## What's done

Nana's quilt is a log cabin: strips of cotton laid round a small centre square, light on one side and dark on the other, so that when the blocks go together you get long diagonals of light and shadow right across the bed. Forty-nine blocks, seven by seven. The three layers are tacked together with long white stitches. The quilting itself, the tiny running stitch through all three layers that holds it for good, is done in about a third of it, from the top left corner, in rows, the way she read a book.

- Pieced: all forty-nine blocks
- Tacked: all of it, in white cotton, stitches as long as my thumb
- Quilted:
  - the top two rows, completely
  - the third row, as far as the middle of block 18
  - the rest, not a stitch
- Bound: no; the binding strip is folded in the bag, already cut and pressed

## The needle

The needle was still in block 18, threaded, with about a hand's length of cotton left on it. I haven't moved it. I sat on the floor with the quilt over my knees for most of the afternoon, which Biscuit approved of, and then I folded it back into the box with the needle where she left it, until I know what I'm doing.\
I'll learn. That's the whole plan.
