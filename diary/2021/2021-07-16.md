## The afternoon

Dougie shut the office at three, which in four years he has done for exactly two things: my first flood map going out, and this. Twelve of us round the big table, prosecco in mugs because nobody could find the glasses, and a cake from the bakery on Great Junction Street iced with a very bad map of Norway. They'd put Bergen in Sweden. I didn't say.

## What Dougie said

He made a speech, a short one, standing on a chair, which he says is the only way to get twelve mapmakers to stop talking about rain:

> She came to us able to make a map, and she's leaving able to make the things that make the maps. She still can't make coffee.

Everyone clapped, and I laughed in a way that went wrong in the middle.

## What I said

Not much. Thank you. That I learned more in the junior year than in the whole MSc. That I'll miss the view of the docks from the window and the smell of warm toner. That if anyone comes to Bergen there's a sofa and a cat. I'd written three more things on a card and forgot to look at it.

## After

The pub on the Shore until nine, then home along the Water of Leith with the present under my arm, wrapped in brown paper and string, which I'm not opening until the morning. Four years. The first proper job, and probably the kindest.
