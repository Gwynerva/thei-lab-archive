Up and down [Ben Nevis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Nevis "1,345 metres, and most of them in fog") today: the last of my :hint[Munros]{note="Scottish mountains over 3,000 feet; there are 282 of them, and now I have climbed 34"}, saved for the end on purpose, because if you're going to leave Scotland you should leave from the top of it.

## Up

Out of the hostel at seven, over the footbridge and onto the Mountain Track, which climbs the side of the glen in long zigzags of stone steps that somebody laid by hand, one at a time, for people like us to complain about. Callum, with a bag of rolls from The Wee Oven, and Marta, who is a violinist and claims not to do hills, went up like goats and waited for me at every corner with the tablet. By the halfway lochan we were inside the cloud, and stayed there.

## The top

The summit plateau is a field of grey stones the size of loaves, in fog, with cairns every fifty metres to keep you off the edge of the north face, which drops away into nothing a few steps to your left. Snow in the gullies, in July. The trig point on its big stone plinth, the ruins of the old observatory, a little metal shelter, and about forty people in waterproofs eating sandwiches in a cloud. A man from Dundee took our picture. In it we look like three wet dogs who are extremely pleased with themselves.

## Down

Knees. Three hours of knees. The cloud lifted as we came back past the lochan and Glen Nevis appeared underneath us, impossibly green, a river shining at the bottom of it. In the evening Marta made Callum drive us out to Corpach to look at the mountain from across the loch, and there it was, clear to the top, as if nothing had happened. Then chips, and the long drive home.

Three good things:

1. The fog lifting at the halfway lochan on the way down.
2. Marta singing the whole of the Skye Boat Song at the summit cairn, badly, to applause.
3. Thirty-four.
