## The walk

Twenty minutes along the water from Sandviken in a light rain that didn't seem to mind me: past the boats, past Bryggen, round the end of Vågen where the fish market was setting up, to an old brick warehouse with the company's name on a small brass plate beside the door.

## The office

Second floor, one long room under the beams, twenty-two people and twice as many screens, and on every screen the sea: depths in blues, rocks in black, little numbers everywhere like seeds. Anna met me at the top of the stairs, taller than she is on video, and walked me round, and everyone stood up to shake my hand, which nobody in Leith has ever done.

## The work

Harbour charts: the small harbours of the west coast that the big charts don't bother with, where the fishing boats and the ferries and the summer people go. The survey boat comes back with soundings, millions of them, and we turn them into lines a skipper can trust in the dark. My first harbour is Fedje, an island right out at the edge, where the sea starts.

## Lunch

Lunch is at half past eleven, which my stomach thinks is elevenses, and everyone brings a *matpakke*: slices of bread with *pålegg*, wrapped in the paper from the bread bag. I brought a sandwich in a plastic box and was forgiven. Anna, over hers:

> We speak English with you until Christmas. After Christmas, Norwegian, and we will be very slow and very kind.

Walked home in the rain. Rain: day 9.
