Rain: day 73.

## The night

It started at three in the morning. The house creaked like a ship, which Sverre says it more or less is: built in the 1870s by a ship's carpenter, every joint in it a boat joint. Something metal went bowling down the lane. The weather station's bracket hummed. Biscuit got under the quilt and stayed there.

## The walk to work

Bent double along the road by the water, the rain coming sideways off the fjord and finding the gap between my hood and my collar every time. At Bryggen the sea was slopping over the edge of the quay and the fish market's awnings were all wound in. Half the office worked from home; Anna rang at nine to tell me not to come in, by which time I was in, dripping on the chart of Bulandet.

## The numbers

The station logged all of it: the pressure falling three hectopascals an hour at the worst, down to 968 at four in the afternoon; the wind cups spinning up to 24 metres a second in the gusts before I stopped watching; 41 millimetres of rain in the day. Sverre's barometer, back on its nail by evening, said *storm*, which he says it hasn't said since 1992.

## Nana

All day I kept thinking of her on the harbour wall at Robin Hood's Bay, holding my hand too tightly in a wind half this size.

:::quote[Nana June, on the harbour wall, about 1999]{align="center"}
Never trust a calm sea. It's only thinking.
:::

The calm came at midnight, all at once, like someone shutting a door.
