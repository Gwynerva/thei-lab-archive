## Out

Ten days, and then the door. I walked from Nøstet to Sandviken with the suitcase bumping behind me over the cobbles: round the harbour, past the fish market just opening, along the front of Bryggen with its gables leaning on each other, and on up the road by the water to where the houses turn white. Forty minutes. I stopped every hundred metres to look at something, and twice just to stand in air that nobody else had breathed in a small room.

## The white house

It's at the top of a lane so steep it has steps instead of a pavement: three floors of white-painted boards, a red door, a stone step worn into a dip by a hundred and fifty years of boots. Mine is the top floor, under the roof. Two rooms and a kitchen, sloping ceilings to bang my head on, pine floors that creak in a friendly way, and a kitchen window with a slot of fjord between two roofs. It smells of old wood and green soap. The landlady showed me the stove and left the keys on a ring with a little wooden fish.

## The neighbour

On the stairs, coming up with a shopping bag, a tall old man in a grey cardigan, who put the bag down to shake my hand and said *Haldorsen. Sverre.* Ground floor. Forty years at sea, retired ship's mate, and, he told me before I'd got my key in the door, Bergen's weather isn't as bad as people say, it's worse, but it's honest. He's going to look in on Biscuit when I'm at work. I didn't ask; he'd decided.
