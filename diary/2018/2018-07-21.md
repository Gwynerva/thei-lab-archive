## Getting it home

The joiner at the arch lent us his van and his opinions, and Callum and I carried the bookshelf out of the arch and drove it to the flat at fifteen miles an hour with Callum in the back holding it like a patient on a stretcher. Then the seventy-two steps. I won't describe the seventy-two steps. There was a moment on the third landing when we were wedged, the bookshelf and Callum and I, and nobody could move in any direction, and Priya had to come down and take the top end and say "pivot" a lot.

## In the hall

It stands. It's 1.6 metres tall, five shelves, oak, dovetailed at all four corners, oiled four times, and it fits the narrow end of the hall with exactly the gap I measured in May, and Callum's bike goes past it without touching. I keep walking up and down the hall to look at it.

## What went wrong

1. One dovetail, bottom left, which has a gap you could post a letter through; I filled it with a sliver of oak and some glue and I'll know it's there forever.
2. The fifth shelf, which still needs a mallet.
3. Nothing else. Honestly. I've checked.

---

The joiner, when we carried it out of the arch: "You got there." Three words, and I'll remember them longer than my graduation.
