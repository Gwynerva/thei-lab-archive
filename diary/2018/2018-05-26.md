## The long way west

Five hours in the Kettle from Akureyri to Snæfellsnes, the peninsula on the west that points out into the Atlantic with a glacier on the end of it. Priya slept. I drove and talked to the car, and to the sheep, who stand in the road and look at you the way Mrs Ogilvie looks at a wrong note.

Kirkjufell first, the mountain that's on every postcard, a steep green cone above the sea, with sheep in front of it as though they'd been placed there by the tourist board.

## The orange lighthouse

Then out to the very end, where the lava runs into the sea in black cliffs and a lighthouse stands on them: Svörtuloft, a square tower painted orange, the only orange thing for fifty miles. It's so bright against the black rock and the grey-blue sea that it looks like a mistake, or a joke, or a drawing a child did of a lighthouse. Fulmars were riding the updraught off the cliff below it without moving a feather. We sat on the lava with our backs to the wind and ate the last of the Icelandic chocolate.

::media[Svörtuloft on the black cliffs at the end of the world, the most orange thing I've ever seen]{file="svortuloft" layout="natural"}

I'm going to draw that lighthouse for the rest of my life.

## Last night

A guesthouse in a fishing village; fish soup; the sun at eleven at night still sitting on the sea and refusing to go to bed. Tomorrow the long drive to the airport and home. I'd like to stay about a year.
