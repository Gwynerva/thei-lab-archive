## It's gone

My first flood map went out at four o'clock this afternoon: a surface-water flood map for a small town on the Fife coast, forty-two sheets and a report, emailed to the council in a zip file with a name I checked eleven times. Then nothing happened, which is what happens when you send something important on a Friday.

## What it is

It shows, street by street, where rain goes when there's too much of it — for a ten-year storm, a thirty-year storm, a :hint[two-hundred-year storm]{note="a storm with a half-per-cent chance in any one year, not one that politely waits two hundred years"}. Blue for ankle-deep, darker blue for knee-deep, darkest for the places you'd need a boat. The railway bridge with its hole cut in it. The culvert that was blocked in 1994. The school car park, which is going to be a lake one day, and now somebody knows.

## Afterwards

Dougie bought ice lollies for the whole office from the shop on the corner, which is apparently what happens when someone sends their first map. Twelve adults on the steps outside in the sun, licking orange ice lollies, talking about drains. I am so happy I could cry, and it's too hot to.

Three good things:

1. The map.
2. The lolly.
3. Ines saying "now you're one of us" and then ruining it by saying "poor thing".
