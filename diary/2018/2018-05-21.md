## Jökulsárlón

We came round a bend on the ring road and there it was, without any warning at all: a lake full of icebergs. Not ice. *Icebergs*, blue and white and grey and striped with black ash, the size of buses and churches and houses, some of them, drifting very slowly towards the sea on a tide I could feel tideline wanting to predict.

## The seal

Priya saw it first, of course: a harbour seal among the ice, head up, very calm, like a man in a bath. She narrated its whole life to me in a whisper — what it eats, why it's here (fish come in with the tide), how long it can stay under — and then it sank without a ripple and we didn't see it again. She was happier than I've seen her in a year.

## The one that turned over

While we watched, a berg the size of a bungalow groaned and rolled very slowly over, and the side that came up was a blue I don't have a word for: not sky, not sea, something lit from inside. Its old waterlines were on it in rows. Priya said icebergs turn over when they've melted enough underneath, that it's the shape changing, not anything happening to them. I've been thinking about that all evening.

::media[Jökulsárlón end to end, the glacier coming down behind and the ice heading for the sea]{file="jokulsarlon-5" layout="stretch"}

Down at the beach across the road, small lumps of ice that had made it out to sea lay on the black sand like glass. I picked one up. It was older than the Domesday Book. I put it back.
