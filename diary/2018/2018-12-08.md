## Before

A big cold church off the Kirkgate, with candles in the windows and the heating on, though nobody could tell. Thirty-four of us in black. I ate a banana in the vestry as instructed. The GP had a hip flask of something "for the cellos only", which we did not drink, mostly.

## The concert

The Handel first, the Queen of Sheba arriving at a good trot, and we stayed on the camel the whole way. Then the Elgar. Agnes looked at the cellos before the first bar the way you look at a dog that has been very good and might not be, and then she brought her hands down, and we played it.

I didn't get lost. I want to write that twice. I didn't get lost, not once, and in the slow middle of the second movement there was a long low note under everyone else's tune where I could hear my cello — my school cello, with the Norway scratch — coming up out of the floor of the church and joining the rest of them, and I felt it in my chest and in my knees and in my back teeth. The last time I played in a concert I was fourteen and I wore a borrowed skirt.

Marta's friend recorded the Elgar from the gallery on her phone. It sounds much better than it was, which is the right way round.

## After

Carols with the audience, which in Leith means very loud carols. Then everybody at once in the aisle: Priya and Marta and Ines; Callum, who had come through from Glasgow on the train and was standing at the back in his chef's jacket, having come straight from the café; and in the second row, in a fur hat, Mrs Ogilvie, who shook my hand and said, "Better than I expected," and then, quietly, "much better," and left before I could say anything.
