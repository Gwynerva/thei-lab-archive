## The name

Llanfairpwllgwyngyllgogerychwyrndrobwllllantysiliogogogoch. Fifty-eight letters, if you count like an English person. I wrote it out on the back of a receipt in the car and counted them twice, and Priya made me read it aloud at every roundabout from Bangor onwards. By the station I could do the first half, which is the part everyone does, and the last bit, the "gogogoch", which I said with real conviction.

::media[The sign on the platform, with the pronunciation guide underneath, which was no help at all]{file="llanfair-station-1"}

It means, more or less, *St Mary's church in the hollow of the white hazel near the rapid whirlpool and the church of St Tysilio by the red cave* — a whole map in one word. I'm a cartographer. I think it's the most beautiful place name I've ever seen.

## The lighthouse

In the afternoon we drove out to South Stack, where the lighthouse sits on its own island at the bottom of four hundred steps, and the cliffs were pink with thrift and loud with birds. Priya stood at the railings with her binoculars and narrated, the way she does, every species and what it was having for lunch.

::media[South Stack from the cliff top, the thrift out and the sea behaving]{file="south-stack-1"}

Margins: Anglesey, very small, with the long name written all the way round the coast because it wouldn't fit anywhere else.
