## Arriving

Landed at Keflavík yesterday afternoon into a wind that didn't stop for the passport queue. The hire car is small and white and whistles at ninety kilometres an hour, so it's called the Kettle. We drove east along the bottom of the island in the long light, a road with lava on both sides covered in moss so thick and grey-green it looks like something sleeping, and found the guesthouse at ten at night in what Scotland would call bright afternoon.

## Behind the water

[Seljalandsfoss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljalandsfoss "the one you can walk behind") is a waterfall that drops sixty metres off an old sea cliff, and the cliff is undercut, so a path goes round behind it. You walk into the back of a waterfall. The noise is the whole world; the spray comes at you sideways and upwards and from places spray has no business coming from; and then you're behind it, in a sort of cave of sound, looking out through falling water at green fields and the flat plain going down to the sea.

::media[Behind Seljalandsfoss in the late light, the water framed by the dark, mossy overhang and the plain beyond]{file="seljalandsfoss-1"}

Priya stood there with her hood down and her arms out like it was a shower she'd paid for. I held my phone inside my coat and recorded a minute of it, which sounds like a waterfall, which is what it was.

::audio[A minute of Seljalandsfoss, recorded from inside my coat]{file="sound-waterfall" title="Seljalandsfoss"}

We came out soaked to the knees and laughing, and sat in the Kettle with the heater on full, steaming.
