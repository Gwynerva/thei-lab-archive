## Skógafoss and the black beach

Yesterday Skógafoss, which is wider than Seljalandsfoss and louder and makes a rainbow for anyone who stands in the right place; we stood in the right place for twenty minutes. Then Reynisfjara, the black beach, which is the most beautiful dangerous place I've ever been. Basalt columns stacked like organ pipes against the cliff, sand as black as a stove, and big waves that come much further up the beach than the last one did, without warning. There are signs. People ignore the signs. Nana's voice in my head the whole time:

> Never trust a calm sea.

It wasn't calm, but I didn't trust it either.

## The sandur

Today we crossed the great flat outwash plains east of Vík, where glaciers have dumped their gravel for ten thousand years and rivers wander about over it as they please. Bridges a kilometre long over braided channels with almost nothing in them. It's the flattest place I have ever been and the mountains stand at the edge of it like people waiting to be let in.

In a café in Vík this morning I drew the hall bookshelf on a napkin — the oak arrived yesterday and Callum sent a photo of it leaning against our hall wall like a delivery of planks for a coffin. The napkin is in the map notebook. The bookshelf is better on the napkin than it'll ever be in oak.
