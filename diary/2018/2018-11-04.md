The seven o'clock train to York, then the little one out across the Vale to Scarborough, and the sea at the end of it grey and flat and pretending nothing was wrong. Nana came out of theatre at two, very small in a very big bed, cross about the food before she was even properly awake. She held my hand with her good one and told me, with great satisfaction:

> Sixteen staples, they've put in me. A stitch in time is still a stitch.

Mum laughed for the first time in two days. Dad stood at the window looking at the car park as if it were the sea.

Stayed at Mum and Dad's in Whitby. Couldn't sleep. At about two a curlew was calling somewhere out over the harbour, that long rising call that sounds like a question nobody's going to answer, and I recorded it out of my old bedroom window.
