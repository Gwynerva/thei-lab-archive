## 1.0

tideline 1.0 is out. Easter Monday, a grey sky, a mug of tea gone cold beside the keyboard, and a small Python library that predicts the tide from its harmonics went out into the world, where it will be used by perhaps four people, one of whom is my dad.

It started on [a Saturday in October](ref:diary:2017-10-14 "the first commit") with a single function that added up two cosines. It now knows thirty-seven constituents (though for Whitby I only have four I trust), the moon's 18.6-year wobble, summer time, and the fact that Leith's tide is not a simple curve: in shallow water it rises faster than it falls, which is two waves added together, and which took me most of March.

::media[Two cosines added together, one twice as fast: the lopsided tide of shallow water, and the reason my Leith times were late until last week]{file="tide-diagram-1" layout="natural"}

## What release took

:::list{counter="lower-roman"}
1. Tag it.
2. Write the changelog, which is one line long.
3. Upload it to the package index and watch the progress bar like a kettle.
4. Ring Dad, who said "right", and then, "does it do Scarborough?"
:::

If you want to know how the sums work, the short version is in the [constituents](<ref:section:tideline/harmonics#h:Constituents> "M2, S2 and the rest of the family") — each one a wave with its own speed, all of them added up.
