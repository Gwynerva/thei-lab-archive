Cairn Gorm, number eleven, 1,245 metres, up the long ridge from the car park in wind that leaned on us the whole way. The top is a stony plateau like the surface of somewhere else, boulders and moss and nothing growing taller than your ankle, rounded hills on every side. It felt like the top of the world rather than the top of Scotland, and a weather station on the summit was clocking the wind at sixty miles an hour.
