## The workshop

Took the cello to the luthier Mrs Ogilvie swears by, in a workshop up a close off the Grassmarket that smells of glue, varnish and coffee, in that order. Violins hang from the ceiling on strings like hams in a larder, and there's a cello with no front on it at all on a stand by the window, so you can see right inside, the little bars and blocks, which felt rude to look at. He laid mine on its back on the bench like a patient and went round it with a knuckle, listening.

## The verdict

His list, more or less:

- the bridge: warped, "like a ski jump";
- the soundpost: fallen over, possibly in 2009;
- the strings: older than some of his customers;
- the pegs: slipping, which I knew;
- the seam on the lower bout: open; glue and clamps. He said *clamps* twice.

Two weeks, and less money than I'd feared, though more than I'd hoped. A shop on the south side rented me a cello for the fortnight, a shiny one that sounds as if it went to a better school.

Walking home without it felt like leaving a dog at the vet.
