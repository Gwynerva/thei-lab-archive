## Midges

:hint[Mývatn]{note="Icelandic for midge lake, which is a warning, not a description"} means midge lake. Priya told me this in the car, and I laughed, and Priya said "you'll see", and I saw. They came up off the water in clouds that looked like smoke, and they were in my ears and my hair and my tea. They don't bite, to be fair to them; they're just *there*, everywhere, in enormous numbers. I wore the midge net I'd laughed at for most of the afternoon, and Priya took a picture of me in it that I'll be paying for until one of us dies.

## Everything else

The whole place is boiling. Steam comes out of the ground at Námafjall, where the earth is orange and grey and smells like the worst egg in the world; there's a crater called Hverfjall you can walk round the rim of, a huge bowl of black gravel; and there's a cave, Grjótagjá, with a pool of hot blue water in it under a roof of rock.

In the evening we went to the nature baths and lay in milky-blue water up to our chins while the sun went round the sky instead of down it. Midges can't swim. It was the best hour of the trip.
