## The morning

York in sun, after a week of rain, which Mum took personally as a blessing. Clare got ready at the hotel with Mum and me and the hairdresser and two of her friends from A&E, who talked about a man who'd come in with a teapot stuck on his hand. Clare was calm. Clare is an A&E nurse; nothing that happens at a wedding can frighten her. Mum cried four times before eleven.

## The church

A small church inside the walls. Dad walked Clare in and stood very straight and didn't look at anybody. Dev looked at Clare like she was the sea. I did the reading and my voice held, mostly. Nana sat at the end of the second row in a navy hat and a brooch of Grandad's, and at the vows she nodded once, firmly, as though approving a seam.

## The paper

All of it where it should be: the invitations on the mantelpieces of eighty-five houses, the order of service in everyone's hands, the place cards on the tables with their little drawings, people picking theirs up and showing them to the person next to them. Dev's grandmother found her drawing — a teapot — and laughed until she had to sit down. The present went down very well and Clare cried on it, which was not the effect I had planned on the paint.

## The speeches

Dad's was short, as promised, and mostly about tides, as feared: that Clare had always gone out further than anyone and always come back in. Dev's sister's was funny. Then, at the end of the evening, with the band packing up, Nana beckoned Dev over to table six, held his hand, and told him:

:::quote[Nana, at table six, to the groom]{align="center"}
Never trust a calm sea, love. But you can trust that one.
:::

He told me afterwards it was the best thing anyone had said to him all day, and I believe him.
