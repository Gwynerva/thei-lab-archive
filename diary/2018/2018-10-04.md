## The room

A mapping meetup in a basement bar on the Cowgate: forty-odd people on mismatched chairs, a projector balanced on two boxes of crisps, a man at the bar who wasn't there for the maps and stayed anyway. I was third of three, after a talk on bus timetables and one on drones, which meant forty minutes of sitting with my hands shaking under the table.

::link[The meetup's page, where the slides will go once I've sent them]{url="https://example.org/edinburgh-map-night/october-2018"}

## The talk

Nineteen slides, down from thirty-one. Whitby at low tide and high tide, "how did I know?", and then the idea: that the tide is lots of simple waves added up, each with its own speed — the moon's, the sun's, their interference — and that if you know how big each one is at your harbour, you can add them together for any day you like, forwards or back.

::card[The part I spent longest on, and the part everyone asked about]{to="ref:section:tideline/harmonics#h:Constituents"}

Then the bugs, because people like bugs: the bridge that wasn't, the summer time, the moon's eighteen-year wobble. Then the man in Nova Scotia with his mooring and the students in Chile with their wrong tide curves. I got a laugh on the summer-time bug, a real one, from people who've all done it.

## Afterwards

Questions for ten minutes. Somebody asked if it does storm surges (no, and I said why). Somebody asked if it would work for a lake (the answer is "barely"). And Dr Grieve, my old supervisor, was in the third row, which I hadn't known until she put her hand up and asked a question so kind it was practically a compliment.

A man came up afterwards and said he keeps a boat at Granton and could he use it for that, and I nearly kissed him.

Walked home up the Bridges with my legs buzzing. Priya and Marta had waited up with a bottle of fizz they'd bought for the occasion "whether it went well or not".
