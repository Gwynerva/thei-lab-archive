In March 2019 I bought a second-hand office computer the size of a hardback book for sixty pounds, put it under the desk in my bedroom in Leith, and called it Saltwick. For five and a half years it ran quietly in the dark, first in Leith, then in a cupboard in Sandviken, and did more for me than any computer I've owned.

## What it was for

At first, one thing: [the Munro map](ref:project:munro-map), which had been running since January on an old laptop on the windowsill, too hot to touch by the evening. Then, because it was there:

- backups of everything, including scans of the paper diaries
- the documentation site for [tideline](ref:project:tideline)
- the [weather station's](ref:project:weather-station) database, a reading every ten minutes for two and a half years
- a shared calendar for the flat, which Marta used and nobody else did
- the music I'd ripped from CDs at sixteen, which I listened to more in lockdown than I had in ten years

## What it cost

Sixty pounds, a new disk twice, about four pounds a month in electricity, and a fair number of evenings when something had stopped and I had to find out why instead of doing anything else. I liked those evenings, mostly. A small machine that is entirely yours, that you understand all the way down, is a rare and calming thing.

## The end

On 30 November 2024 I switched it off. Everything on it had moved, one service at a time, to places that didn't need me. The house was coming, and I wanted fewer things in my life that could break at night. More on that in [Switched off](ref:section:home-server/switched-off).
