---
title: The map of all of them
summary: 283 walks, one sheet, every line a day.
---

The working copy was a map of the city on the wall by the door, one pencil line a day, so thick along the river by July that you could not see the river. From June the phone recorded every walk as well, and from the notebook I traced the ones before that. At New Year I put all of them into one map: 283 lines on a plain base of Leith and the north of Edinburgh, each line coloured by its month, from pale green in March to deep blue in December.

What it shows is a small country. Almost every line stays within two miles of the flat. The Water of Leith is a thick dark rope of them; Leith Walk another. Out at the edges are the few long ones, Portobello in August, the Pentlands in May, like the arms of a starfish. In September the lines get short and tangle together near the flat, the knee months. In November they stop for a fortnight, because I was in Whitby, and those walks, to the end of the pier and back, are on a scrap of paper pinned to the corner.

::media[The base I drew them on]{file="map-scotland-2" layout="natural"}

I printed it at A2 and put it on the wall over my desk, and it went to Bergen with me rolled in a tube, and it is on the wall of the landing at Øvre Lid now. People look at it and see a map of Edinburgh. I see a year.
