I never had many users, but the ones I had were the right kind: people who emailed to say what they were doing with it.

- a sea-kayaking club in Tasmania that printed its own tide cards for a sheltered bay with no official station
- a harbour office in Nova Scotia that used it to check its own gauge
- a school in Chile, where a teacher built a lesson around it and sent me photographs of thirty paper tide curves on a classroom wall

In October 2018 I gave [my first talk](ref:event:first-talk) about it, at a mapping meetup in Edinburgh, to about forty people and a plate of samosas. In May 2019 [a stranger fixed my worst bug](ref:event:stranger-fixed-bug): a time-zone mistake that made every prediction an hour out for half the year, in the southern hemisphere only. He sent the fix with a polite note and never wrote again.

:::quote[From the harbour office in Nova Scotia, 2019]{align="center"}
Your library told us the tide was coming in an hour early. Turned out our gauge clock was wrong, not you.
:::
