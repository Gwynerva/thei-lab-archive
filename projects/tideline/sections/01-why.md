---
title: Why a tide library
---

The tide table behind the till at Testwell's Chandlery is the first table of numbers I ever read. Times and heights, a column a day, in tiny print, curling at the corners by March. Dad checks it without looking, the way other people check a clock.

> The tide doesn't care what the book says. But the book's usually right.

That is Dad on the subject, in full.

When I asked as a child where the numbers came from, he said "the Admiralty", as if that explained it. At university I learned that they come from harmonics: from measuring a place's tide for a long time, breaking it into its regular parts and adding the parts back together for any date you like. I wanted to do that myself, from scratch, so that I understood every line of it.

So on a wet Saturday in October 2017, a month into my first job, I made a folder called *tideline* and wrote a function that added two cosines together. It was wrong, but it was a start.
