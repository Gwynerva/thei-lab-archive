The first weave is the view from the landing, in five bands from the bottom: the dark green of the orchard, the grey-blue of the fjord, the far shore, the mountain, and the white of Folgefonna with a strip of sky.

Things I have learned, in the order I learned them:

- Beat the weft down harder than you think. Then harder.
- Leave the weft slack at the edges or the whole thing draws in like a waist. Mine has a waist.
- Raw fleece, washed in the sink with washing-up liquid, gives a grey with more life in it than any skein from a shop.
- A mistake ten rows down is a mistake forever. It is also, after a week, part of the picture.

I stopped in May, when the evenings got light and the orchard needed me, and the weave sat on the frame all summer under a cloth with the glacier half done. Now the dark is back, there is a new warp waiting beside it, and I am finishing the glacier a row at a time after supper. It should be done before the first snow, if the first snow is late, which it never is.
