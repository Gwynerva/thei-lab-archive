The old kitchen door frame was pine, ninety years old at least, straight-grained and as hard as anything I have tried to saw. Jonas cut it into four pieces for me and showed me how to make the corners with half-lap joints, and then went away, which is how he teaches.

It took two weekends:

1. Saw the four sides to length, eighty centimetres by sixty.
2. Cut the half-laps, badly, then again, better.
3. Glue and peg the corners with oak pegs from the loft.
4. Hammer in a row of small nails along the top and the bottom, one centimetre apart, for the warp.
5. Sand it until it was nice to hold.

The warp is cotton seine twine from Testwell's Chandlery, a reel Dad sent in a parcel with a note that said *for the loom, not for nets*. It stands on the floor of the *stova* by the stove, and I sit on a cushion in front of it, which my knees have opinions about.
