The winter of 2025 at Øvre Lid was the first winter in my life with nothing to commute to and nobody to see after four o'clock. The kitchen was a building site, the evenings were long, and my hands, which had been busy all year with putty and tiles and apples, did not know what to do with themselves in the dark.

Nana had a loom once, before I was born: a little upright one that stood in the corner of her front room in Robin Hood's Bay and was sold, Mum thinks, in the seventies. I have one photograph of it and none of anything she wove. When we took the old kitchen out in November, Jonas stood the door frame against the barn wall to burn, and I looked at it and saw four sides of a loom.

So: a frame of old pine, a warp of cotton twine from Dad's shop, wool from Bergen and later from Kari's sheep, and a book from the library in Odda. I am not good at this yet. I like that I am not good at it. It is the first thing in years that I have started without any idea of how it should look at the end.

::media[The frame by the stove, February]{file="tapestry-loom-1" layout="centered"}

What I am weaving is the view from the landing, in bands: the fjord, the far shore, the dark of the mountain, the white of the glacier, the sky. It is coming out lopsided, the way Nana's quilts were lopsided, and the way, Ragnhild says, the windows of this house always were. I have stopped minding.

[Nana's quilt](ref:project:nana-quilt "the other thing I finished for her") is the nearest thing to this I have done before.
