sundays at first. Then Wednesdays as well. Then, by February, most days, nine in the morning, before the city was properly awake.

the regulars: six women, mostly older than Mum, who talk about grandchildren and knee replacements in the water as if they were in a kitchen. The old man in the woolly hat, who has done this every winter for thirty years and still says *brrr* out loud. Me, saying nothing for the first month, then saying a little.

From the notebook, February:

23. Tuesday, five degrees, sleet, two minutes, a cormorant on the buoy
24. Thursday, four degrees, flat calm, three minutes, the coastal steamer going out
25. Sunday, four degrees, snow on the steps, three minutes, the old man sang

:::private
the eleventh swim, in January, I cried in the water and nobody noticed, because everybody's face is wet. That is one of the things the sea is for.
:::

my own rule for the end of the season: the winter is over when the birches on Nordnes come out. On the fourteenth of April they had. Forty-one swims. I went home and slept for two hours in the afternoon, not because I was ill but because I was happy, which was new.
