I started in the worst winter of my life, and I think that is why I started. In December 2023 I had been off work for eight weeks and could not choose between two loaves of bread, and a woman from the pottery class said, come on Sunday. So on Sunday I walked into the sea at Nordnes in sleet, and stayed for forty seconds, and laughed out loud on the steps for the first time in months.

::card[The first one, forty seconds in sleet]{to="ref:event:first-winter-swim#:~:text=forty"}

I have not stopped since. A hundred swims by [the last morning of 2024](ref:event:hundredth-swim), a hundred and eighty-nine by the spring of 2026, and the count goes on.

## The rules

I keep them because I keep rules about everything, and because a count without rules is just a number.

1. The water must be under fifteen degrees, or it does not count. A swim in July is a swim, not a cold swim.
2. In to the shoulders, and breathe out long before anything else.
3. Never alone when the water is under five. Someone on the steps, or Jonas on the rocks with a torch.
4. Minutes, not heroics. One minute for every degree is the most, and I am rarely near it.
5. Write it down: the date, the water, the air, the minutes, one thing I saw.

## What it does

People ask why, and the honest answer is that the cold makes everything else small. For the minute you are in the water there is nothing in your head but the water. No inbox, no roof, no list. Then you come out, and your skin is red and roaring, and for an hour afterwards the world is very clear and very kind, and you could choose between any number of loaves.

> Never trust a calm sea.

Nana said that about boats. I say it about the fjord on a still January morning, black as ink and smooth as glass, and colder than anything that looks so friendly has any right to be.

:::private
What I don't say on the steps: that the cold is the only thing that has ever switched my head off completely, and that I am a little afraid of how much I need it. Tove said that needing something that is good for you is allowed. I am still deciding whether I believe her.
:::

## Two places

Bergen, from December 2023 to the spring of 2025: the steps of the sea bath at Nordnes, a crowd of regulars, an old man in a woolly hat who says *brrr* out loud. Then [the fjord at home](ref:section:cold-water/hardanger), from the rocks below the orchard, mostly alone, with Folgefonna watching. Both are below.
