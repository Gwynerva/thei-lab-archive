From the first of October 2016 to the thirty-first of July 2021 I lived at the top of a tenement off [Leith Walk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leith_Walk "a mile of it, uphill all the way into town"), seventy-two steps up. I counted them on the first day and every day after, the way I counted the 199 steps in Whitby as a child: under my breath, in twos.

Three bedrooms, a kitchen big enough for a table and five people if two of them stood, a bathroom with a window that did not shut, and a living room that got the sun after four. The little bedroom had a skylight, and was mine. If you leaned out of the kitchen window and squinted, there was a strip of the Firth of Forth between two chimneys, grey or blue or silver, and on a clear day Fife.

## Who lived there

- [Priya and Callum](ref:section:leith-flat/priya-and-callum), from the beginning: a marine biologist with plankton in the fridge and a chef with opinions about gravy
- [Marta](ref:section:leith-flat/marta), from September 2018: a violinist who counts out loud and laughs drily
- Biscuit, from February 2019, who outranked all of us within a week
- Me, throughout

## What the flat was

The place I became a grown-up, more or less. I moved in at twenty-four as a student with a rucksack and a bike, and left at twenty-nine with a job, a cat, a cello, a career, thirty-four Munros and a van full of boxes going to Norway. Everything that happened in those five years happened in or near those rooms, and you will find most of it somewhere on [this site](/projects/).

The flat had laws, voted on, binding:

1. Sunday dinner is the law of the flat.
2. Whoever cooks does not wash up.
3. Nothing in the fridge labelled SAMPLES is to be eaten, or even looked at.
4. The sofa lives on the second-floor landing, where it got stuck on the day we moved in, and the neighbours are thanked at Christmas.

The sofa lived on the landing for five years. When I left, it was still there. For all I know it is there now.

::media[Gables by the water at the Shore, on a Sunday walk]{file="leith-shore-4" layout="centered"}

I miss it more than I expected and less than I feared. I miss the sound of the buses on the Walk at night, and the gulls on the roof opposite, and the seventy-two steps, which kept me fitter than any half marathon.
