[Marta moved in](ref:event:marta-moves-in) two days after Callum left: a violinist from the Scratch Orchestra, a data analyst by day, from Kraków by way of Glasgow, with the driest laugh in Leith. She had spotted the scratch on my cello that was shaped like Norway, at my first rehearsal, and I took that as a reference.

For a year it was three of us, Priya, Marta and me. Then [Priya left for Plymouth](ref:event:priya-leaves) in September 2019, and Marta and I decided not to find anyone else. I moved into the big room. The little room with the skylight became the map room, the cello room and, mostly, Biscuit's room.

So it was the two of us, and a cat, for the two hardest years: the first lockdown, when we practised in our rooms with the doors open and played duets badly in the kitchen; the walks; the bread; the cello and the violin through the wall; Christmas 2020 in Leith together instead of at home, a chicken for two and a cracker each.

:::private
The night Nana died, Marta sat on the floor of my room with her back against the bed until four in the morning, and did not say anything, and did not go away. In the morning she made porridge and rang Mum for me, because I could not. I have never thanked her properly. I am thanking her here, where she will not see it, which is very me.
:::

In July 2021 she helped me pack, every mug wrapped in newspaper like a newborn, every box labelled in her handwriting, which is better than mine. On the last morning she stood at the stair door in her dressing gown and waved until the taxi turned the corner. She moved across town that afternoon, with Hamish in his jar.
