---
title: The flat
summary: Seventy-two steps, three bedrooms, one skylight
---

The top floor of a sandstone tenement built in the 1880s, on a side street off Leith Walk, halfway between the town and the Shore. The stair was stone, worn into shallow dips by a hundred and thirty years of feet, and lit by a skylight at the top that turned the whole stairwell green in summer.

The rooms, as they were on the day I left:

- **the kitchen**, the heart of it: a table that seated four, a window with the strip of the Forth, a magnetic strip on the wall where Callum's knives had been, and the fridge, with its history
- **the living room**, which got the sun after four, with the second-hand sofa that did fit up the stair, bought when Callum's went to Glasgow, and Marta's music stand in the corner
- **the big room**, Priya's, then mine from 2019, with two windows over the street
- **the middle room**, Callum's, then Marta's, next to the kitchen
- **the little room** with the skylight, mine for three years and then the map room, the cello room and Biscuit's room
- **the bathroom**, with the window that did not shut, which made baths in January an act of courage

The skylight was the thing. You could lie in bed and watch the clouds come in off the sea, and the gulls on the ridge, and on clear nights a square of stars. I drew it in the margin of the first page of the diary, on the first day, and I have drawn it many times since, from memory, when I could not sleep in other rooms.
