Priya found the flat and therefore had the big room, which nobody disputed. She was doing her PhD on plankton and kept samples in the fridge in little jars labelled SAMPLES. DO NOT EAT. DO NOT EVEN LOOK. Callum had the room next to the kitchen because he was a chef and "needed to be near the hob". I had the little one with the skylight, which was the one I wanted anyway.

What those two years were, in the things I remember first:

- Callum's pasta at eleven at night on the first day, eaten on the floor, because the sofa was still in Morningside
- Sunday dinners, voted into law in the first month: Callum on the roast, Priya on pudding, me on washing up, which was not in the vote I remember
- Burns Night, Callum's haggis, and Priya reciting the address in a Kerala accent better than anybody's in the building
- Pancake Day, the competition, my pancake hitting the ceiling and coming back down, which counts
- the Christmas in 2016 when Callum gave me a fifteen-millimetre spanner for the Spoke Room's tool wall and I nearly cried over a spanner
- Priya and me in Copenhagen, in Iceland, and on Ben Lomond, my first Munro

Callum left on the last day of August 2018 to open [a café in Glasgow](ref:event:wee-oven-opens). We gave him a party and a frying pan with all our names scratched into the handle. He cried; then he made us all breakfast, because he could not leave a kitchen without making something in it.

Priya stayed another year, until her viva and her postdoc in Plymouth. That is in the next section.
