The last day. The removal men had come on the Thursday for the forty-three boxes; Biscuit had gone to the cattery on the Friday, protesting. On the last evening there was nothing left but the sofa on the landing, two suitcases, a sleeping bag, a kettle and me with a bin bag and a cloth.

I walked through every room twice. The marks on the kitchen wall where Callum's knives had been. The hook in the little room where the cello case hung. The rectangle of cleaner paint above the bed where the 1935 map of Leith had been for five years, the one I had taken down on Thursday and wrapped in a towel.

Then, at half past five in the morning, the door locked, the keys through the letterbox, seventy-two steps down for the last time, counting, and [the taxi](ref:event:leaving-leith). I did not look back at the window. I had decided not to, so I didn't.\
I looked back from the corner. Of course I did.
