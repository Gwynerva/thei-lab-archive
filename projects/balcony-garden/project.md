The flat in Leith had a balcony the size of a single bed, facing south-west over the back greens, with a view of other people's washing and, on a clear day, a slice of the Firth of Forth between two chimneys. For four years it held a clothes horse and a dead geranium. In April 2020, with nowhere else to go, it became a garden.

## What I grew

Tomatoes, mainly, in a long planter along the railing, tied to canes with strips of an old T-shirt. Basil on the windowsill, because basil sulks outside in Scotland. Radishes in a window box, far too many, because the packet said "easy" and it was right. Lettuce, which the pigeons ate. Strawberries in a hanging basket, three in total, all eaten by me standing up.

The details are in the sections. The short version is that a balcony garden is mostly watering, and mostly worry, and then one day in July you eat a tomato you grew yourself, in a city, in a pandemic, and it tastes like the sun.

In 2020 the balcony was shared with [the weather station](ref:project:weather-station "readings every ten minutes"), which sat between the tomato canes and recorded, among other things, that I watered them at seven every evening, because the humidity jumped.

## Then Bergen

In Sandviken I had a balcony again, smaller and wetter. I tried again in 2022 with herbs, sugar snaps and twelve tomato plants that were an act of faith. Bergen's rain did what Bergen's rain does. I got a handful of ripe tomatoes, twenty-two green ones and a great deal of very healthy mint.
