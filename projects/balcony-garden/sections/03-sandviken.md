A second go, sown on the kitchen windowsill in April and out on the little balcony of the white house by June: herbs from the market, sugar snaps, and twelve tomatoes from seed, in pots I carried home on the bus from a garden centre in Åsane like babies.

What Bergen taught me about growing things:

1. It rains. Don't water anything, ever.
2. Mint will survive the end of the world.
3. The sun comes round the mountain at eleven and leaves at four, and the plants know it to the minute.

Rain: fifteen days of thirty-one in May. The tomatoes gave me a handful of ripe ones in August and twenty-two green ones at the end of September. I ripened those in a drawer with a banana, which Jonas said was an old wives' tale, and which worked.

The gardens up the hill in Sandviken are full of *ripsbusker*, redcurrant bushes, every one heavy with fruit in July. Next year I'll plant one. Next year I'll have a garden. (I always say that.)
