It rained in the night and stopped at six. I know because I was awake for both, listening to it on the roof over the stairs, which is mended now and did not leak once.

## Morning

When I came down the steps the orchard was hung from tree to tree with strings of little cloth triangles, cut from Nana's scraps: her blue print, the yellow check from the quilt, a strip of the curtain from the cottage. Mum had cut them in Whitby in the spring and Clare had sewn them in Leeds, and they had hung them with Priya before breakfast. I had to go back inside for a minute.

Then Mum and Clare did my hair in the new kitchen, with Isla under the table eating raspberries, while Dad and Dev and Jonas carried chairs. I wore a pale green dress Clare found in Leeds, boots, because it is an orchard, and Nana's brooch, the silver one with the anchor, which Mum had kept since 2020 and gave me at breakfast without saying anything.

## Eleven o'clock

Three from the orchestra, two violins and a viola, played Elgar's serenade, slowly, while Dad walked me down between the trees. Jonas stood under the old Gravenstein by the road in his good shirt and a waistcoat Sigrid had made him wear, looking like a man waiting for a boat. Dad said nothing all the way down and squeezed my hand at the bottom.

The woman from the council read the words in Norwegian and then in English. We said ours. The rings are gold. Isla said "again!" at the end, loudly, and everybody laughed, and that is the moment I will keep.

## Afterwards

- Long tables in the orchard, fish soup and lamb, and the two cakes.
- Speeches: seven, which in Norway is restrained, Jonas says.
- Ragnhild, brought over from Odda by Eirik for three hours, at the top of the slope, receiving people like a queen. She held my face in both her hands and said the house was happy.
- Dancing on the grass when the light went gold.
- Isla asleep on a pile of coats by nine. Biscuit on the porch all day, disapproving, and eating whatever fell.

At midnight we walked down to the bottom gate and looked back up at the house with all fourteen windows lit. The chairs were still in the orchard in the morning, wet, in their rows, facing the tree, and I took a picture of them before anybody could move them.
