Jonas signed us both up in March 2023, when I still said yes to things. He had sailed since he was a boy, on the fjord in his uncle's old boat, but never had a certificate for it, and said he would do the course for the paper and for my company. I think he did it because he could see something coming before I could, and wanted me to have a thing on Saturdays that was not work.

It was. The first summer, the lessons were one of the few things I did not drop, and I am glad of it, though I do not remember much of them except the boom, the words and the knots. The second summer, back on my feet and newly freelance, I remembered everything.

## Why sail, when I have a kayak

The kayak is mine: I built it, I paddle it, and every metre it moves it moves because I made it. Sailing is the opposite. You set the boat up and the wind does the work, and your job is to listen. I needed to learn that more than I needed to learn tacking.

## The words

Half of sailing is vocabulary, and I learned it twice, once in each language:

- :hint[*skjøte*]{note="a sheet: the rope that trims a sail, not a bed sheet, as I told my mother three times"}, the sheet
- *fall*, the halyard
- *baug* and *akter*, bow and stern, which Jonas calls the pointy end and the end with the tea
- *slag*, a tack, and *jibbe*, a gybe, which is what happens when you get it wrong

## Where it went

A certificate in [August 2024](ref:event:sailing-certificate "a kiss, not a collision"). Then two years later the North Sea, which is [its own page](ref:project:shetland). The knots have [a section to themselves](ref:section:sailing/knots), because they deserve one.
