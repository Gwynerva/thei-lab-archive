When everything stopped in March 2020 the cello was one of the things that didn't. With no commute and no orchestra I had more time to practise than I had ever had, and for once I used it: forty minutes most mornings before work, sometimes an hour in the evening, the bedroom door shut and Marta's violin going behind hers.

It was the season of [the prelude](ref:project:bach-prelude), which I had been creeping through bar by bar since the autumn and which, in lockdown, I finally finished, and on the twentieth of June recorded. It was the season of scales in three octaves, which I had been avoiding for two years. And it was the season of playing to nobody, which is a strange and useful thing: no audience, no teacher in the room, only the sound coming back off the wall.

Mrs Ogilvie on the laptop, every Tuesday, half a second behind. Biscuit leaving the room at minute four. The window open in May, and a neighbour across the back green who started to clap at the end of the prelude, which I did not know about for a week.

::audio[The allemande as I was learning it that July, more or less]{file="music-bach-cello-2" title="Allemande, first suite"}

In July we went down to the back green behind Mrs Ogilvie's flat for the first lesson in person since March, and in August the orchestra met again, outdoors, in a car park, forty chairs two metres apart. The cello sounded thin in the open air. It was the best thing I had heard in months.
