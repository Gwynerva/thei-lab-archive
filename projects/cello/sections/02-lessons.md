Eilidh Ogilvie played in an orchestra for thirty-five years and has taught for longer, and I have never once called her anything but Mrs Ogilvie, and never will. She is small and very straight and listens without moving. At [the first lesson](ref:diary:2018-01-16 "twelve years, and then forty minutes of open strings") she heard an open D, a scale and half a minuet, rebuilt my bow hold finger by finger, and said:

> You've been talking for twelve years without breathing.

Then forty minutes of open strings. That was the first month: open strings, scales, no vibrato ("you haven't earned it"). In April she let me have a little. I walked home feeling knighted.

What three and a half years of Tuesdays taught me, in her words, which I wrote in the back of my exercise book:

- Count. Out loud if you have to.
- Slow is not the same as careful.
- The note you just missed is gone. Play the next one.
- The bow does the music. The left hand only does the arithmetic.

In lockdown the lessons went on, on a laptop balanced on a pile of atlases, with her voice arriving half a second after her mouth moved. In July 2020 we had one in her back garden, two metres apart, a blackbird joining in.

The last lesson was on the twenty-ninth of June 2021. She did not make a speech. She gave me a cake of rosin wrapped in a page of an old score and said: "Find an orchestra in Bergen. And count."
