---
title: The Leith Scratch Orchestra
periods:
  - 2018-03-13..2021-06-26
---

A church hall off Great Junction Street, a tea urn, a piano with a cloth over it and about thirty-five chairs in a horseshoe. Anyone could come. People did: a schoolgirl better than all of us, a retired GP called Duncan who counted under his breath, a man of eighty with a viola and a flask, a trombone, two flutes, and Marta, who asked at the [first rehearsal](ref:event:first-rehearsal) whether the scratch on my cello was meant to be Norway, and six months later moved into the flat.

Agnes Muir ran it, and ran it well: brisk, warm, merciless about counting. "Cellos, lovely. Cellos, also: count."

What we played, over three years, roughly in order: the Elgar, a lot of Handel at Christmas, the slow movement of the New World, Holst, and a Dvořák serenade that took us a whole winter. Two concerts a year, in December and June, in the church upstairs, with everyone's family in the pews and a raffle in the interval.

The [first concert](ref:event:first-concert-2018), in December 2018, was my first concert in twelve years. I shook so much in the first piece that my bow bounced, and nobody noticed, because the cellos are in the middle and nobody watches the cellos.

In April 2020 we tried a rehearsal on a video call, forty of us out of sync by seconds, which sounded like a ship's engine room and was the funniest evening of lockdown. My last rehearsal was the twenty-sixth of June 2021. They played me out with the opening of the Elgar, and Duncan, counting under his breath, lost his place for the first time in my hearing and did not apologise.
