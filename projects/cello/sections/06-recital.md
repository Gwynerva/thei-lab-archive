Once a year, in spring, the Strykeorkester gives an evening where anyone who wants to can play something alone. For three springs I said no. In January 2026 I said yes, before I could think, and then had three months to think.

The programme, my part of it: the prelude and the two minuets of Bach's first suite. The prelude because it is the piece I came back for. The minuets because Mrs Ogilvie once said they were the only dances in the suites that a cellist could dance to, and I wanted to end on something you could dance to.

About eighty people in the church, which smells of candles and floor polish. Jonas in the third row. Sigrid beside him, who had come over on the ferry with a cake. Mum and Dad on Jonas's phone, propped on the hymn books. I was fourth, after a violin, a viola and a very good bass.

My bow shook through the first four bars of the prelude so that every note had a little tremble in it, like a voice before crying. Then it stopped, the way it does, and I was inside the piece and not outside it, and the church went away.

:::spoiler
On the way into the pedal point I lost it. Two beats with nothing in my head at all. Then my left hand found the note on its own, the way it has ten thousand times at home, and nobody noticed but me and the bass player, who smiled, and Mrs Ogilvie, who was not there and noticed anyway, in my head. The second minuet, which I had been most afraid of, went without a scratch, and I still do not understand why.
:::

Afterwards I could not remember any of it. Jonas has the recording. I have listened to it once. On the Sunday I did nothing at all, and on the Saturday after, I went back to scales.
