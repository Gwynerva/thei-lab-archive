For my first year in Bergen I did not play in anything. I practised in the flat, quietly, because the walls were thin and Sverre downstairs went to bed at nine, and I told myself I would find an orchestra when my Norwegian was good enough. Then Leif fixed the seam, and I ran out of excuses.

The [audition](ref:event:orchestra-audition) was on a Saturday in August 2022 in the church: a scale, the prelude, and a page of sight-reading of something I later found out was Grieg. Three people behind a table, one of them knitting. I was in by the evening. Back desk, happily, which is still where I sit.

The Strykeorkester is about twenty-five strings and no wind at all, which means that when we play the Holberg Suite we are playing it in the town where Grieg was born, to people who have heard it all their lives, and nobody minds that we are not very good at it. Rehearsals on Tuesdays in the church hall, coffee from a flask in the break, *kake* if it is somebody's birthday, and it is always somebody's birthday.

I stopped going in the autumn of 2023. I could not have counted to four. They kept my chair, and when I came back in the spring of 2024 nobody asked where I had been, which is the kindest thing an orchestra has ever done for me.

In January 2025 we had a film night in the church hall instead of a rehearsal, a projector and a sheet and twenty-five string players arguing about the music in a short animated film about a girl and a dragon. The violas thought the strings were real. The cellos knew better.

Since we moved to Øvre Lid it is two and a half hours each way, bus and ferry and bus, every other Tuesday. Jonas drives me sometimes and sits in the back of the church with a book. It is the one thing from Bergen I would not give up, and I have not had to.
