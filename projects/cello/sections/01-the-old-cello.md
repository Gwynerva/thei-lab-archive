---
title: The old cello
summary: A seventies factory cello from the school cupboard, a scratch like Norway and two repairs
---

It came out of the school's music cupboard when I was eleven and never went back. When the school bought new instruments in my last year, Dad bought this one off them for forty pounds and a promise to collect it, and it went under my bed, and stayed there.

What it is: a full-size cello made in a German factory some time in the seventies, by the label inside, which is half torn and says only the name of the town and a number. Plain maple, a top of spruce with a wide grain, a varnish somewhere between toffee and treacle. Nobody would call it a good instrument. It has a big, slightly woolly voice, a wolf note on the F that growls if you let it, and a scratch on the lower bout, near the C string side, that is the exact shape of Norway, with the long tail going up towards the fingerboard. I noticed that at thirteen. I did not know then that it was a prophecy.

::media[The bridge and the f-holes, after the work in Bergen]{file="cello-4" layout="centered"}

Two repairs in eight years:

1. **Edinburgh, February 2018.** After nine years under a bed it needed its soundpost set back where it belonged, a new bridge and the open seam on the lower bout glued and clamped, and for a fortnight I played a rented cello that was better than mine in every way and that I did not like at all.
2. **Bergen, 2022.** The first winter of Norwegian central heating dried it out and the lower seam opened, so the whole instrument buzzed on the low notes like a wasp in a jar. Leif, a luthier with a workshop up a lane behind Bryggen, closed the seam, reset the bridge, and told me to keep a damp sponge in the case, which I now do, religiously.

It is still the same cello. The scratch is still Norway. And I live in it now, which it seems to approve of.
