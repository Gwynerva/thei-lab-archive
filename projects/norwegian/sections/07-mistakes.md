---
title: Mistakes I'm fond of
summary: Five things I said that I wish I hadn't, and one I'm glad I did
---

You cannot learn a language without saying ridiculous things in it in front of people, and the people, in my experience, remember them longer than you do. These are mine, collected, so that at least I get to tell them first.

1. **Full.** At Anna's at Christmas in 2021, after a great deal of *pinnekjøtt*, I leaned back and said *jeg er full*, meaning I could not eat another thing. It means I am drunk. Anna's father, who is eighty, said *ja, det er jul*, yes, it is Christmas, and poured me another.
2. **The bag.** At the shop, asking for a bag, a *pose*, I said something nearer to *pus*, and asked the young man at the till very politely for a kitten.
3. **Viktig and hyggelig.** Important and pleasant. I get them the wrong way round when I am tired, and so I have told my dentist that his work was very pleasant and told Sigrid that her cake was very important. Both were true.
4. **Ha det bra.** Goodbye, take care. I said it brightly on arriving at a party, for a whole autumn.
5. **Kjempe.** It means giant and goes in front of anything to make it bigger: *kjempefint*, *kjempegodt*. In my first year I put it in front of everything, until Jonas said I sounded like a very enthusiastic eleven-year-old.

:::spoiler
And the one I'm glad of: on the morning of the waffles, ringing to say I was looking forward to the waffles, I said I was looking forward to *him*. He laughed for a long time. Then he said, in English: "Good."
:::
