Monday evenings, six till eight, on a laptop on the kitchen table in Leith, Biscuit on the keyboard whenever I needed to unmute. Twelve of us from nine countries, all with our own reasons for wanting Norwegian: a Spanish nurse with a job in Tromsø, an Indian engineer married to a woman from Stavanger, a retired man in Kent who wanted to read crime novels in the original. The teacher sat in a flat in Oslo with a ficus behind her that I came to love.

What A1 is: the present tense, numbers, the days of the week, how to say where you live and what you do, and that you would like a coffee. The vowels, which are nine and not five. The word order, which puts the verb second whatever happens, like a stubborn guest at a party.

My first list, from the first lesson:

- *hei*, hello, which I already knew
- *ha det*, goodbye, which sounds like a sneeze
- *takk*, thank you, said about forty times a day in Norway
- *kart*, map, which I was very pleased about
- *regn*, rain, which turned out to be the most useful word I would ever learn

In March I got the job. In May I passed the end-of-course test, in a video call, holding up my passport to the camera. Then I packed.
