A real room. After nine months of faces in boxes it was almost too much: a classroom on the hill near the university with radiators that clanked, a whiteboard, coffee in paper cups and fourteen people sitting in rows on Saturday mornings, and [the first Saturday](ref:event:norwegian-class) I could not stop smiling.

The class was half Bergen's new arrivals: a Polish electrician, two Syrian brothers who were engineers, an American who had married a fisherman from Sotra and was furious about it in the nicest way, a Filipino nurse who was better than all of us. The teacher was from Bergen and said *eg* instead of *jeg* when she forgot herself, which was my first warning.

A2 is the past tense, giving reasons, describing things, and writing an email to your landlord about a broken boiler. We all had broken boilers, so we all did very well.

This was also the term I started to understand the radio. One morning in November I realised the newsreader had said something about a storm, and I had understood it without translating, and the storm arrived that afternoon exactly as she had said. I stood at the window watching the rain go sideways and felt, absurdly, that I had made it happen.
