The spring of B1 was the spring of Jonas, which turned out to be the best thing that could have happened to my Norwegian. He spoke English to me for exactly six weeks. Then one evening in March, over the half-stitched hull in his workshop, he said that from now on he would speak only Norwegian to me, because otherwise I would never learn, and because he liked the way I said *ja* when I was concentrating. He has not spoken English to me since, except in Whitby, to Mum and Dad, which I find very hard to watch.

So: B1 in the classroom on Saturdays, and B1 everywhere else, in the workshop, in the car, over waffles. I learned boat words before I learned the words for half the vegetables. I can tell you the Norwegian for an epoxy fillet but had to point at leeks in the shop until May.

The [exam](ref:event:b1-exam) was on the second of June: reading, listening, writing and twenty minutes of talking to two strangers about a picture of a family having a barbecue. I said that in Scotland a barbecue is something you do in a coat. They laughed. I passed.
