---
title: The Bergen dialect
summary: What Sverre meant when he said I had to learn to speak Bergen
---

Nobody warned me. You learn *bokmål* from a textbook, carefully, for a year and a half, and then you arrive in Bergen and everyone is speaking something that sounds like Norwegian played backwards by a man gargling. It is Norwegian. It is just Bergen's.

What is different, as far as I can tell, in the order I noticed it:

:::list{counter="lower-alpha"}
1. **The r.** It is in the throat, not on the tongue, like French, and it is the first thing you hear and the last thing you can do.
2. **The words for I and not.** *Eg* for *jeg*, *ikkje* for *ikke*. Half the sentences anyone says to you begin with one and end with the other.
3. **Two genders, not three.** A book is *en bok* in Bergen and *ei bok* nearly everywhere else, and Bergensers will tell you this is because they are more efficient.
4. **Kossen.** How. As in *kossen går det?*, how's it going, which is said to you twenty times a day and which my textbook did not contain.
5. **Kjekt.** Nice, fun, good, lovely, said of almost anything, from a walk to a sandwich to a funeral.
:::

And the attitude, which is a dialect of its own. There is a saying, said only half as a joke, that someone from Bergen will tell you they are not from Norway, they are from Bergen. After three and a half years I understood it. Now I live in Hardanger, an hour and a ferry away, where they speak something else again, and Sigrid says my Norwegian has *Bergen in it*, and does not mean it as a compliment.
