---
title: Why
---

In December 2020, a month after Nana died, I saw a job advertised at a small chart company in Bergen, read it three times, and closed the tab. In January, in the second lockdown, I signed up for an online class in beginners' Norwegian, and if you had asked me why, I would have said it was for reading hiking maps.

It was not for reading hiking maps. I think I knew I needed to go somewhere, and I wanted to have started before I let myself admit it. In February I opened the tab again and applied.

And then there is the other reason, which is that everyone in Norway speaks English, beautifully, and will switch to it the moment they hear your accent. That is exactly why I wanted to learn. I did not want to live in a country through a glass wall. I wanted to be inside it, even if being inside it meant being slow and wrong and laughed at, which it did, for a year at least.

Nana, who never went anywhere much, used to say you could not know a place until you had been told off in its language. I was told off in Norwegian for the first time on my third day in Bergen, by a woman at a bus stop, about where I was standing. I understood every word. I was delighted.
