Two and a half years, four levels, one test, and a language I now dream in about one night in three, which nobody tells you will happen and which is very strange the first time.

I started in January 2021, before I had a job in Norway or any real plan to go, on a laptop in the flat in Leith in the second lockdown. I finished, if you can finish, in June 2023, when a letter told me I had passed the Bergenstest at B2. In between I moved to Bergen, and the language moved from a subject into the thing I bought bread in.

## The levels

1. [A1, online](ref:section:norwegian/a1), Monday evenings from Leith, twelve faces on a screen
2. [A2](ref:section:norwegian/a2), Saturday mornings in a real room in Bergen
3. [B1](ref:section:norwegian/b1), with an exam in June 2022 and a boatbuilder who refused to speak English to me
4. [The Bergenstest](ref:event:bergenstest), at B2, in May 2023, with the result in June

## How

Lists. Of course lists. Every new word went on a list, the lists went on the fridge, and every Sunday the week's list went into a file. The first year's file is above. My favourites have [a page of their own](<ref:page:norwegian-words#h:Food> "the food words, mostly, and then the weather"), because some words deserve better than a list.

Besides the lists: the radio in the kitchen every morning, understood at first one word in twenty; children's books from the library, which are harder than you think because children's books are full of animals; the weather forecast, which I could follow before anything else because I already knew what the weather was going to be, which was rain; and talking, badly, to anyone who would let me. Shop assistants. Sverre downstairs. Biscuit, who is now, technically, bilingual, in that he ignores me in both.

## What it changed

Everything, slowly. The first time I made a phone call in Norwegian, to the dentist, I had to lie down afterwards. The first time I made a joke in Norwegian and someone laughed, at the joke and not at me, at Anna's kitchen table in the spring of 2022, I walked home taller. Now I teach a [QGIS course](ref:project:qgis-course) half in Norwegian, argue with Sigrid about apples in Norwegian, and still get *hyggelig* and *viktig* the wrong way round when I am tired.

:::quote[Sverre, downstairs, the first winter]{align="center"}
Your Norwegian is very good. Now you must learn to speak Bergen.
:::

He was right. That is [a section of its own](ref:section:norwegian/dialect).
