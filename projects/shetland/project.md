I grew up looking at the North Sea from the wrong side. Whitby harbour faces it, Dad's shop faces it, and for thirty-four years I have stood on the end of the West Pier and looked out at it and known that Norway was over there somewhere. Then I lived in Norway for five years and looked at it from the other side, from Nordnes and the rocks at Herdla, and knew that Whitby was over there. In June 2026 we crossed it, in a boat, under sail, which is the only way to cross something like that properly.

Not to Whitby. That was the first idea and Jonas did the sums: three hundred and fifty miles each way and a fortnight we did not have, two months before a wedding. Shetland is half the distance and, as Jonas pointed out, closer to Bergen than Trondheim is, if you are a gull. So: Bergen to Lerwick, four days on the islands, and home.

## Who and what

Two of us, Jonas and me, in [a borrowed boat](ref:section:shetland/the-boat) of thirty-four feet and nearly fifty years. Jonas has sailed since he was a boy on the fjord in an uncle's boat, and did [the course at the club](ref:project:sailing) with me for the paper and the company. I have two summers of lessons, a certificate on thin card, and a stomach I did not fully trust. We kept three-hour watches: three on, three off, for forty-three hours there and fifty-six back.

## What it was like

Mostly, it was long. People ask about storms and there were none, quite; there was a morning of fog, a day of sun on a sea as flat as a table, a second night with a fresh westerly and a sky that never got properly dark, because in June that far north it doesn't, and on the way home eighteen hours of running before a north-westerly with the sea lifting the stern. The Shetlanders have a word for it, :hint[simmer dim]{note="the midsummer half-light in Shetland, when the sun only just goes under and the sky stays pale all night"}, and I understood it on the second night at two in the morning, steering by a sky the colour of a mussel shell.

When I rang Whitby from Lerwick, Dad said:

> Your Nana would have said never trust a calm sea. I'll say ring your mother.

I rang my mother.

## Why I keep this page

Because I have wanted to do something like this since I was nine and sat on the harbour wall with Dad watching a yacht come in from Holland, and I want to remember that I did it. And because it was the last big thing before the wedding, and in the middle of all the lists for chairs and cake and directions to the orchard, it was a week when the only list was the watch rota, and nothing on it was more urgent than the next three hours.
