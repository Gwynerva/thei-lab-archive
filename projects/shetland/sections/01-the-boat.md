---
title: The boat
summary: Thirty-four feet, nearly fifty years old, and lent to us by a man who has crossed eleven times
---

She has a name, painted on her stern in gold that is mostly gone, and her owner asked me not to put it on the internet, because the last time one of his boats was on the internet it sank. I think he was joking. He is a member of the club, in his seventies, and has crossed to Shetland eleven times; his hip and his wife have decided there will not be a twelfth, and he wanted the boat to go once more while he could watch her leave. So she is *the boat* on this page, and that is that.

What she is: a fibreglass sloop of thirty-four feet, built in Sweden at the end of the seventies, with a long keel, a tiller, a diesel engine that starts on the second try, a gimballed stove with two burners and a saloon panelled in teak gone the colour of tea. Six berths, of which we used two. Charts in a drawer that smells of forty summers.

::media[Masts at the club pontoon on a June evening, ours the third from the left]{file="sailing-yacht-3" layout="centered"}

Jonas spent three weekends in May going over her from the masthead to the bilge, the way he goes over anything that floats: new running rigging, the seacocks, the batteries, a cracked window in the doghouse. I scrubbed the cockpit, labelled every locker in English and Norwegian, and learned where the gas tap was until I could find it with my eyes shut. We gave her back with a full tank, a new fender and a bottle of whisky from Lerwick in the chart table, which her owner said was the correct rate.
