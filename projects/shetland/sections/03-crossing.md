We left the pontoon at 18.00 on the Friday, the owner standing at the end of it with his hands in his pockets, [not waving](ref:event:shetland-departure), and motored out past Nordnes and the sea bath, out through the islands with the sun still high. By ten we had the sails up and the last of Norway was a grey line astern. Then nothing but sea, for a day and a half.

## The first night

Calm, or nearly: a light west-south-westerly, the engine on half the time, the sails slatting. We kept three-hour watches, three on and three off. I tried to sleep in the fo'c'sle on my first watch below, which is like sleeping in a bucket someone is kicking slowly down a hill, and after that I slept on the floor of the saloon wedged between two cushions. I was sick once, neatly, to leeward, at about four, and felt better at once and slightly proud.

## The day

Saturday began in fog, so thick that the bow disappeared, and we took turns up there in our lifejackets, listening for engines and seeing nothing. By noon it lifted all at once, and the sea underneath it was blue and flat as a table. We saw an oil platform on the horizon lit up like a small town, and in the afternoon a pod of white-beaked dolphins came and rode the bow wave for twenty minutes, which is the sort of thing you think will happen and then it does.

## The second night

The westerly came in at dusk, as promised, and we sailed properly for the first time, the boat heeled and going and talking along her hull. It never got dark. At two in the morning the sky to the north was still pale, and I steered by it.

:::spoiler
At three, on my watch, alone in the cockpit, I sang the whole of the Skye Boat Song to the compass, twice, badly. Jonas says he heard every word from his bunk and would not have missed it for anything.
:::

## Landfall

The last lines of the log, in my handwriting, which gets worse as the hours go up:

41. Land, low and grey, not trusted for half an hour
42. Bressay light, white on its point, exactly where the chart said
43. Alongside in Lerwick, forty-three hours. Tea.

Then we stood on the pontoon swaying like two drunks, holding on to each other, laughing at nothing, and went to find chips.
