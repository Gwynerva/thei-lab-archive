Out of Lerwick at seven on the Thursday morning with full tanks, Mum's hat in a dry bag, and a flat calm that had us motoring past Bressay with the sails up and doing nothing. [The way home](ref:event:sailing-home) took fifty-six hours, and the first day of it was so gentle that I read a whole novel in the cockpit.

> Never trust a calm sea.

Nana's line, again. On the second night the wind came round to the north-west and got up, and by morning we were reefed right down and running before a sea that lifted the stern and set it down, for eighteen hours. I was not sick once. I was not frightened either, which surprised me more.

At dawn on the Saturday, a lighthouse on a skerry and the coast behind it, low and dark and then suddenly green, mountains with snow still on the tops, and I made a noise I have not made since I was small.

::media[The first light of Norway, on its skerry]{file="lighthouse-norway-1" layout="natural"}

We tied up at the club in the afternoon. The owner was on the pontoon with his hands in his pockets, exactly where we had left him, and the first thing he did was look at the boat, all of her, slowly, from the bow to the stern. Then he looked at us. By six we were asleep in the car on the ferry, and Biscuit was waiting on the step.
