Three days on land that would not keep still. For the first evening the pavements of Lerwick rose and fell under us, and we walked up Commercial Street like two people on a ferry.

What we did, more or less:

- **Monday**: slept. Showers at the marina, hot and endless. Walked the old town, the stone houses with their feet in the harbour, and a wool shop where I bought a hat with Fair Isle bands on it for Mum and nothing for myself, a discipline I am still proud of. In the evening, a boat out to Mousa, to see the broch and the storm petrels that come back to it in the half-dark.
- **Tuesday**: hired bicycles, and out over the hill on the small roads with the sea on both sides at once. [Ponies on the hill road](ref:event:shetland-ponies), eleven of them, who stood across it and would not move for a bicycle of any nationality.
- **Wednesday**: the bus to the very south end, and out along [Sumburgh Head](ref:event:sumburgh-puffins "and the puffins"), where the puffins stand on the edge of the cliff a few feet from you and look as surprised to see you as you are to see them.

People in the pubs asked where we had come from, and when we said Bergen they nodded as if that were the most natural thing in the world, which in Shetland, I suppose, it is.
