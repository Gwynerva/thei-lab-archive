Two months of lists, which as anyone who knows me knows is my favourite kind of month. Wedding lists on the left of the kitchen table, boat lists on the right, and Biscuit asleep across the middle where they met.

The boat list, as it stood the night before we left:

- [x] liferaft serviced, the certificate taped inside the locker
- [x] lifejackets, harnesses and jackstays, one each and a spare
- [x] charts: the Norwegian ones for getting out of the skerries, the Admiralty ones for Shetland
- [x] radio licence (mine, a Saturday in April in a classroom that smelt of floor polish)
- [x] three practice nights out on the fjord in May, watches and all
- [x] food for five days, cooked and frozen, labelled
- [x] tell Mum before we leave, not after
- [ ] learn to sleep when it is my turn to sleep

The last one I never managed.

The weather took the most time and taught me the most. For six weeks I read the forecasts every morning and evening the way I used to read tide tables, and drew the pressure charts in my notebook in pencil, highs and lows and the lines between them, until I could see a window coming three days off. Jonas trusts his nose. I trust the Norwegian Meteorological Institute, and so in the end did he.

::link[The forecasts, every six hours, for two months]{url="https://www.met.no"}

The window came for the twelfth: light westerlies, backing south-west and freshening on the second day, nothing over force five. We took it.
