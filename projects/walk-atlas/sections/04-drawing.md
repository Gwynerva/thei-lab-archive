Six months at the window, one map a week, more or less, with the kitchen being demolished and rebuilt in the next room for most of it.

How each map was made:

1. The walk's height data, cut to the page, plotted by the pen plotter as faint pencil contours.
2. The path, streams, walls and buildings in ink, from my notebook sketches and the GPS track.
3. Watercolour: greens and greys for the land, one blue for water, one warm colour for the path.
4. The names, by hand, last of all, with a ruler for the baselines.
5. A scan, and a check of every junction against the notebook.

The first snow came on 15 November, and the window went white for three days. On one of those Sundays, the snow so thick the fjord disappeared, we watched a film about a girl, a dog and the spirits of the mountains, which felt like a film about our view.

By the end of April I had thirty maps, a sore wrist and a stack of failed sheets the height of a cat. The failed sheets lit the stove for most of May.
