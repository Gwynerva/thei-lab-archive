*Thirty Walks Around the Fjord* is a walking atlas of Hardanger: thirty walks within a day of our house, each with a map drawn by hand, a page of directions and a few lines about what you'll see. It is published by *Forlaget Fjellstue*, a small publisher in Bergen with four people and a cat, in Norwegian and English in one volume. It is at the printer as I write this.

## How it began

At the Christmas market in 2024 a woman bought a print of [my Sandviken map](ref:section:illustrated-maps/sandviken) and asked whether I'd ever thought of drawing walks for a book. She turned out to be its publisher. We met in January in a café by the fish market; by the spring we had a plan; on [10 June 2025](ref:event:atlas-contract) I signed.

:::quote[From her first letter, January 2025]{align="center"}
We don't need another guidebook with photographs. We need one that looks like somebody walked it and drew it, because somebody did.
:::

## The walks

Thirty, from an hour along the shore below the house to a long day up to the edge of the glacier. I walked every one between April and October 2025, most of them twice: once to learn it, once to check it. Jonas came on eleven, Biscuit on none, a sheep on part of one. The list is in [Walking them all](ref:section:walk-atlas/walking).

## The maps

Every map started as faint pencil contours, drawn on watercolour paper by [the plotter](ref:project:pen-plotter) I built in Leith. Over them I drew the paths, the streams, the farms and the views in ink, then colour, then the names, in the lettering I'd spent a spring learning. Thirty maps took six months, most of them at [one of the fourteen windows](<ref:section:farmhouse/the-house#h:Fourteen windows> "the one facing Folgefonna, in the room that is now the study").

## Now

The proofs came in May 2026 and took the summer, with a wedding in the middle of them. On 15 September the files went to the printer, and the printed sheets are due any day. After that come the binding, the boxes, and a small launch at the publisher's office, with apple juice from our own trees.
