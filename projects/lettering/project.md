From February to May 2024 I went to a lettering course on Tuesday evenings at a print workshop in Bergen: fourteen evenings, eight of us round a long table, pens, brushes and a great deal of paper. It was the first thing I did for myself after the winter I stopped, and it was the right one.

## Why lettering

Maps are full of words, and on a hand-drawn map the words are half the drawing. For years I'd drawn maps by hand and then typed the names on in the computer, and they always looked pasted on, like labels on jars. I wanted the names to belong to the map. I also wanted, though I didn't say so at the time, something slow that I couldn't do wrong in a way that mattered.

Dad's sign had started it. In 2023 I repainted the old sign over [his shop](ref:project:boatyard-sign) for his sixtieth, and halfway through the second coat I realised I had no idea how to make a letter, only how to copy one.

## What I learned

- that a letter is a set of strokes in an order, not a shape to be filled
- that spacing matters more than the letters themselves
- that my hand shakes less in the evening than the morning
- [the map hand](<ref:section:lettering/alphabets#h:The map hand> "the one I use now"), a plain upright alphabet that reads at two millimetres high

Everything I've drawn since has my letters on it: the prints, the commissions and every name in [the atlas](ref:project:walk-atlas), all 1,140 of them, which I counted, once, on a bad night, to make myself feel better about my wrist.
