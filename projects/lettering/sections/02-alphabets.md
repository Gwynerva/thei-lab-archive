The alphabets I learned on the course, in order, and what each one was for:

:::list{counter="upper-alpha"}
1. **Roman capitals.** The oldest, the hardest, the most beautiful. For titles.
2. **An italic hand.** Quick and slanted, for notes in margins and the odd place name that wants to lean.
3. **A sans-serif hand.** Plain and even, the letters of signposts. For roads and paths.
4. **My own.** Built from the third, for maps.
:::

### The map hand

My map hand is upright, narrow, with a tall :hint[x-height]{note="The height of the small letters, like x, a and n, compared with the capitals. Taller reads better when small."} and open counters, so that an *e* and an *o* don't fill in when the ink spreads. It works at two millimetres. It has one flourish, on the capital *R*, which I allow myself because of the rowan.

Water names go in italic, slanted, in blue, the old way. Hill names go in capitals, spaced wide. Farm names go in my map hand, and the smaller the farm, the more carefully I letter it, because nobody else ever will.

### What I practise

Every morning, before work, five minutes: one line of capitals, one line of the map hand, one word. Today's word was *frost*. It came out well.
