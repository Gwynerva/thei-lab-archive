Every December since 2017, bar one, I've cut a linocut and printed my Christmas cards from it. It started because I was a student and cards were expensive, and it carried on because it turned out to be the most Christmassy evening of the year: the radio on, the kitchen floor covered in newspaper, ink on my elbows, and forty damp cards drying on the radiators, the bookshelf and the cat.

## The blocks

- **2017**: a robin on a railing, cut in Leith with borrowed gouges
- **2018**: the West Pier lighthouse at Whitby
- **2019**: Biscuit asleep on a map, his first Christmas with me
- **2020**: a loaf, because that was the year
- **2021**: the gables of Bryggen, my first Christmas in Bergen
- **2022**: a kayak upside down on its strongback
- **2023**: nothing; I never cut the block
- **2024**: one apple on a bare branch
- **2025**: the house at Øvre Lid, which nearly finished me

The verse has changed less than the pictures. The first one went inside forty cards in 2017, and I've kept the shape of it ever since, swapping the place:

:::quote[Inside the first card, 2017]{align="center"}
Robin on the railing,\
frost on the stair,\
Merry Christmas from Leith,\
wish you were here.
:::

## The list

The list started at twenty-three and peaked at fifty-one in 2022, when I had Bergen people as well as Scottish ones and Whitby ones. Nana June got the first card off the floor every year until 2019. The last one she had was the cat on a map, and she kept it on her mantelpiece next to the clock until the spring. Since then the first card goes to Mum, who pretends not to know it's a tradition.
