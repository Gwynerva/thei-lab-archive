[Sandviken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandviken,_Bergen) is the old part of Bergen that climbs the hill north of the harbour: white wooden houses packed up a steep slope, lanes too narrow for cars, stone steps between the gardens, and the mountain going straight up behind. From August 2021 to March 2025 I lived there, in two flats three streets apart.

## The first flat

The top floor of a white house built in the 1870s, two rooms and a kitchen under a roof that ticked as it warmed, a window on the harbour, and a neighbour downstairs, Sverre, a retired ship's mate who taught me Bergen weather and Bergen Norwegian and gave Biscuit sardines on Sundays. I lived there alone with the cat for two years, which is [its own section](ref:section:sandviken-flat/alone).

## The second

In August 2023 Jonas moved in, and we moved together, three streets up the same hill, to a bigger flat in a white house with a blue door and a kitchen you could turn round in. That flat saw the worst autumn of my life and the best spring, and a great deal of rain. That is [the other section](ref:section:sandviken-flat/two-of-us).

## What I miss

I started counting what I would miss in November 2024, four months before we left, which is a bad habit. The list grew:

- the bench at the end of Nordnes and the coastal steamer going out
- the swims, and the old man in the woolly hat
- the fish market at six in the morning
- the bakery on the corner, who said *det vanlige?* from our second month
- the sound of the city at night, which is like the sea
- the rain. Yes. The rain.

What I took: sixty-one boxes, a cat, a cello, a waffle iron and a brass barometer that still says rain, and in Hardanger, unlike Bergen, is sometimes wrong.
