The first night after quarantine I slept in the flat with a bed, a table, two chairs and nothing else, because the boxes from Leith were still on a ship somewhere in the North Sea. Biscuit came two days later. The boxes came the week after that. Then it was the two of us, Biscuit and me, in two rooms over the harbour, for two years.

What alone was like:

- rain, counted. *Rain: day 12.* *Rain: day 19.* In October 2022, twenty-one days of it in a row
- the radio in Norwegian in the kitchen every morning, understood one word in twenty and then one in two
- the gingerbread town at Christmas, the first 17 May, the bonfire at midsummer, each one a first and written down
- Sverre downstairs, sardines on Sundays, weather every morning
- cello, quietly, because the walls were thin
- and from January 2022, Jonas, who came for waffles, and came back, and by the summer had a toothbrush in the bathroom and opinions about the stove

:::private
The first winter I was lonelier than I told anybody. Mum asked on Sundays and I said it was fine, and it was, mostly, but there were evenings in November when it was dark at four and raining and I sat in the kitchen with Biscuit and could not think of one person in the country I could ring. I am writing it down because by the next winter there were several, and I want to remember that it changed.
:::

In July 2023 Jonas and I signed for the bigger flat. I packed the first flat into boxes in a weekend, with a list on the fridge and Biscuit in the empty bath.
