Maps of places drawn the way you remember them rather than the way they are measured: a neighbourhood, a walk, a valley, with the streets in ink, the colour in watercolour and the names in my own hand. Printed on heavy paper, signed, sold in the shop, at the Christmas market and, more and more, made to order.

## How it started

In the summer of 2024, a few weeks after leaving Nordvåg, I walked every street in Sandviken with a sketchbook, because I was about to stop living there one day and wanted to keep it. The map took six weeks. A friend asked for a print of it, then a friend of hers, and on [14 September](ref:event:first-print-sold) a stranger paid for one, which is the moment a drawing becomes a print.

## What they look like

- an ink line, slightly wobbly, on purpose
- watercolour washes, never more than five colours to a map
- hand lettering, which I spent a spring learning properly ([lettering for maps](ref:project:lettering))
- small drawings of the things that make a place: the bakery, the bench with the view, the house with the blue door, the cat that sits on the wall
- north at the top, a scale bar in a corner, and somewhere a tiny rowan tree, which is my signature

## Where they go

Most go to people who live in the place, or used to. Some go to people who are leaving, which I understand best. The [prices](ref:page:prints) are on their own page, and the story of the first market is in [The Christmas market](ref:section:illustrated-maps/christmas-market).

The prints are also why there is [an atlas](ref:project:walk-atlas): its publisher bought the seven mountains map at the market in December 2024, and we met the following January. The maps that came out of that are bigger, slower and more careful, but they started here, with one street and a box of paints.
