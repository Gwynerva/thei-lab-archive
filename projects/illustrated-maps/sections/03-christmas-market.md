Two days at [the Christmas market](ref:event:christmas-market-2024), at a trestle table in a wooden hut two metres wide, between a man selling wool socks and a woman selling reindeer sausage, warm for the first hour and cold for the rest.

What I took: sixty prints of six maps, a card reader, a flask, two pairs of socks each. Jonas took the till on Sunday so I could talk to people, which is the actual job at a market, it turns out. Everybody wanted to find their own street. Three people found their childhood house. One man found his grandmother's, and stood at the table for twenty minutes telling me about her.

Sold: 41 prints. Left: 19, mostly of the seven mountains, which people liked looking at and didn't want to own.

One of the buyers, on the Sunday afternoon, was a quiet man with a grey beard who bought the seven mountains map and asked for my card, twice. I gave it to him and forgot him. In January he rang to ask whether I had ever thought about drawing walks for a book. I said yes, all the time.
