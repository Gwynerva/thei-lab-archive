Two days at [the Christmas market](ref:event:christmas-market-2024), at a trestle table between a man selling knitted hats and a woman selling honey, in a hall that was warm for the first hour and cold for the rest.

What I took: sixty prints of four maps, a card reader, a flask, two pairs of socks each. Jonas took the till on Sunday so I could talk to people, which is the actual job at a market, it turns out. Everybody wanted to find their own street. Three people found their childhood house. One man found his grandmother's, and stood at the table for twenty minutes telling me about her.

Sold: 41 prints. Left: 19, mostly of a walk on Ulriken that people liked looking at and didn't want to own.

One of the buyers, on the Saturday afternoon, was a quiet woman who bought the Sandviken map and asked whether I had ever thought about drawing walks for a book. I said yes, all the time. She gave me a card from a small publisher. I put it in the cash box and found it again on New Year's Day.
