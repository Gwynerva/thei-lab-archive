An online course in [QGIS](https://qgis.org), the free mapping software, for people who need maps in their work and have never made one. Eight Monday evenings, seven till nine, Norwegian time. Twelve students a term, usually; fourteen once, which was too many, and I'll never do it again, and I did it again this autumn.

## Who comes

Planners, ecologists, journalists, a parish council clerk from Cumbria, a fruit farmer from the other side of the fjord, two archaeologists, a woman mapping her village's old footpaths for a book, a man mapping every bench in his town for no reason he would admit to. Most have used a map for years and never been allowed to touch one.

## How it works

Each student brings a question on the first evening, something they need to know about a place. By the eighth evening each of them leaves with one finished map that answers it. Everything in between is in service of that map. The week-by-week plan is on [the syllabus page](ref:page:qgis-syllabus), and how I arrived at it is in [The syllabus](ref:section:qgis-course/syllabus).

I teach the way I was taught at Edinburgh: show it once, let them break it, then ask how sure they are. Dr Grieve's green ink lives on in the comments I write on their maps, though I type mine, in green, which nobody has yet noticed.

## So far

- autumn 2025 and spring 2026, from the farmhouse

It is the part of freelance work I would do for nothing, which is why I'm careful to charge for it.
