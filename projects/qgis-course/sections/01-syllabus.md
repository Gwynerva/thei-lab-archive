---
title: The syllabus
summary: Eight evenings, each one a step towards one map.
---

The course has changed a little every term, but its shape hasn't. The first two evenings are about getting comfortable:

1. Opening QGIS without fear. Layers, the map canvas, panning around your own street.
2. Data: where it comes from, what a coordinate reference system is, and why your points are in the sea off Ghana.

Then the middle of the course, where the map actually gets made:

3. Styling: colour, line, symbol. The first evening anyone says "oh".
4. Elevation: contours and hillshade from open height data, everybody's favourite.
5. Analysis: buffers, overlays, counting things inside other things.
6. The print layout: legend, scale bar, north arrow, title, and the space around them.

And the end, which is about saying it clearly:

7. Critique. Everyone shows their draft; everyone else asks how sure they are.
8. The finished maps, and a glass of something, each in our own kitchen.

The detail of each evening, with exercises and data, is on the [link-only syllabus page](ref:page:qgis-syllabus "for students, but nobody minds if you look").
