Up at six in a hotel in York, Clare's dress hanging on the wardrobe door like a ghost, and every piece of paper I'd made laid out on the bed for a last count. Two place cards missing. Found them in my coat pocket at the door.

The day itself is somebody else's to write; Clare's, mostly. For me it was the moment in the hall when people found their names, picked up the card, turned it over to look at the rosemary, and put it in a pocket or a handbag to take home. Nobody threw one away. I checked the tables afterwards, which is a sad thing to do at a wedding, and I don't care.

Nana danced, on the legs she had then, before November. Dev cried; Clare didn't, until the end. Dad made a speech of nine words:

> She's brilliant. He's brilliant. Look after each other. Cheers.
