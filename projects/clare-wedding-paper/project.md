My sister got married on 8 September 2018, in York, to Dev Mistry, who is a physiotherapist and the calmest man I've ever met. In May she rang to say, "You're good with paper, aren't you?" and I said yes before I'd heard the rest of the sentence. The rest of the sentence was eighty-four invitations, eighty-four place cards, a table plan and an order of service.

## What I made

- 84 invitations, lettered by hand, with a printed insert for the boring parts (directions, the hotel, the gift list)
- 84 place cards, folded, each with a name and a tiny drawn sprig of rosemary
- one table plan on a board as big as our kitchen table, which is how I know how big our kitchen table is
- 90 orders of service, printed, with a lettered cover

## How it went

Better than I deserved. I'm not a calligrapher. I can do a decent [italic hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italic_script "the one I can actually do"), a passable round hand, and a copperplate that looks like a spider fell in the ink. So the invitations were italic, in dark green ink, on a heavy cream card from a print shop off Leith Walk that still had its old type cases on the wall. The place cards were a looser, quicker hand, because by August speed mattered more than beauty.

Clare's only instructions were "not pink" and "nothing with doves". Dev's only instruction was "whatever Clare says". Mum's instructions ran to two pages and were mostly ignored, lovingly.

The paper things are the part of a wedding that nobody notices unless they're wrong. Nobody noticed. Clare framed her invitation, and it hangs in their hall in Leeds, slightly crooked, which I've decided is deliberate.

Eight years later I did all of it again for [my own wedding](ref:project:our-wedding "the orchard, August 2026"), and found that I'd forgotten nothing, especially the cramp.
