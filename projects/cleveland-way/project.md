In July 2019 Dad and I walked the coast half of the Cleveland Way: Saltburn to Filey, about eighty-five kilometres along the top of the cliffs of North Yorkshire, in six days on the calendar and five by Dad's count, because the day to Robin Hood's Bay "doesn't count, it's just going to your Nana's". He shut the shop for the week, which he had not done since 1998. Mum drove our bags from bed to bed and met us most evenings with sandwiches and opinions about our sunburn.

It was Dad's idea. He said that I had spent two years measuring how fast the cliffs were falling into the sea and had never walked along the top of all of them, and that he would like to before they did.

## The days

1. [Saltburn to Whitby](ref:section:cleveland-way/saltburn-whitby), two days, past Boulby and Staithes.
2. [Whitby to Robin Hood's Bay](ref:section:cleveland-way/whitby-bay), past Saltwick, to Nana's.
3. [Robin Hood's Bay to Scarborough](ref:section:cleveland-way/to-scarborough), two days, by Ravenscar.
4. [To Filey Brigg](ref:section:cleveland-way/filey), the end.

## Dad

Dad walks like he sails, steadily, without fuss, looking at the weather. He knows the name of every boat in every harbour on that coast and the story of half of them. He told me more in six days than in the twenty-seven years before, mostly about the sea and partly, in the evenings, about his own father, whom I never met. On the fourth day he said:

> You could have stayed here, you know. You'd have been fine.

And then, before I could answer: "But you'd have been bored." He was right on both counts.
