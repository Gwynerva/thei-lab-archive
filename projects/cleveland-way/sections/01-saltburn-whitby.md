## Friday

The train to Saltburn, then down the cliff lift for luck and back up the steps, because Dad said a walk should start at the sea. Then east along the top: Huntcliff, the old ironstone country, and then Boulby, the highest cliffs on the east coast of England, two hundred metres straight down to the rock. Dad stood at the edge with his hands in his pockets and said "that's a long way to fall", which is the most Yorkshire sentence ever spoken.

We came down into Staithes in the evening, steep red roofs crowded round a harbour like people round a fire, and slept in a guest house with two single beds and seagulls on the windowsill.

## Saturday

Staithes to Runswick Bay along the cliff, down to the beach and across the sand at low tide, then on through Kettleness and down at last to Sandsend, where Dad lay down in the long grass at the top of the beach and fell asleep instantly, like a dog. Then the last three miles along the sands into Whitby, with the abbey getting bigger, over the bridge, and home to a pie in Mum's oven. From Saltburn to our own front door.

Signpost at Runswick, which I copied into my notebook:

Cleveland Way\
Whitby 9 miles\
Saltburn 11

We were exactly in the middle of something. That felt right.
