## Ravenscar and on

Nana waved us off from her door at eight in her dressing gown. Along the beach at low tide to Boggle Hole, then up the long climb out of the Bay to [Ravenscar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenscar,_North_Yorkshire "the town that never was"): in Victorian times somebody laid out streets and drains for a seaside resort on the cliff top, and nobody came, and the streets are still there, in the grass, going nowhere. There is an old railway platform with no railway. I made Dad stand on it and wave at a train.

::media[The old station at Ravenscar]{file="ravenscar-1" layout="centered"}

Then cliff after cliff, down into wooded gullies and up again, seals hauled out on the rocks far below, Hayburn Wyke with its waterfall straight onto the beach, and a room over a pub at Cloughton. Dad's feet, which he has never once mentioned in my lifetime, were mentioned. His knees, he says, are fine.

## Scarborough

The short day: nine kilometres into Scarborough, past the castle on its headland, done by lunch. Fish and chips on the harbour wall, and an afternoon with nothing to do but watch the tide come in, which Dad and I can do for longer than most people.
