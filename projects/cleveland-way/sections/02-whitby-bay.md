The short day, the one I had been waiting for: out of Whitby past the abbey, along the top of the East Cliff, and down the coast to Nana's.

It goes straight past Saltwick Bay, my dissertation cliffs. I had measured them from maps and photographs and a drone, and I had stood under them with a tape, but I had never walked along the top with someone who was not working. Dad wanted to see where the edge had been in 1853. I showed him: about twenty metres out, in the air, over the beach. He looked at the air for a while.

::media[Black Nab from the cliff]{file="saltwick-bay-4" layout="natural"}

Then Robin Hood's Bay, and Nana waiting at the top of the lane with opinions about our boots, and then down the steep lane, past the rowan tree, into her front room, where she had made a curd tart and a pot of tea strong enough to stand a spoon in. She asked whether we had walked all the way, and when we said yes, said "well, there's buses".

She was ninety that summer. We stayed the night. She showed Dad the letters from my grandfather, the ones from the 1950s, and Dad, who never sits still, sat still for two hours.

[My dissertation](ref:project:whitby-erosion "the cliffs, measured") is about the cliffs we walked along that morning.
