title: Filey Brigg
periods:
  - 2019-07-17

The last day, out of Scarborough along the shore and then up onto the cliffs, gentler now, red clay instead of rock, and in the early afternoon the end: Filey Brigg, a long reef of rock running out into the North Sea like a finger pointing at nothing.

There is a stone that marks the end of the Cleveland Way. We sat on it. Dad took out a flask and two tin cups, which he had carried all the way from Saltburn without telling me, and poured us each a tot of rum, which is what you do, he said, at the end of a voyage.

We walked out on the Brigg as far as the tide let us, and stood there with the sea on three sides. Dad said "Well. That's that, then," and nothing else for a long time.

::media[Filey Brigg from the cliff]{file="filey-brigg-1" layout="stretch"}

That night I drew the whole coast from memory on the back of the guesthouse's breakfast menu, Saltburn to Filey, every headland in order, with a dotted line and two stick figures. I got it nearly right. Dad took it home, and it is on the wall of the shop.
