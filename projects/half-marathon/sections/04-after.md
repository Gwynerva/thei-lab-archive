---
title: After
---

I kept running, which surprised everyone, me most. Not for anything: no more races, no plan on the fridge. Two or three times a week, at six, along the river. It was the most reliable hour of the week, the one where nothing was asked of me but the next mile.

In the first lockdown it became the hour, the one time each day I was allowed out, and I ran instead of walking more often than not, round Holyrood Park and up the hill, faster and further than I had trained for the race, because there was nothing else to spend myself on.

And on the twelfth of September 2020, coming down the path off Arthur's Seat on a Saturday morning, I put my foot on a loose stone and [the knee went](ref:project:knee). That was the end of running. I have walked a great deal since: thirty-four Munros, a Camino, sixty kilometres across Svaneti. I have not run again, apart from for the last ferry, and I do not miss it as much as I thought I would.

I miss the six o'clock river, a little. And the scones.
