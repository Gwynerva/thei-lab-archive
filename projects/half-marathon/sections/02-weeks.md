The first week I ran for twenty minutes without stopping and had to sit on a bench on the Links afterwards with my head between my knees while a man walking a pug asked if I needed an ambulance.

The weeks after that blur together, as they should. The notebook has a line for each one, and the middle of the summer reads like this:

9. Long run 9 miles, Leith to Cramond and back. Knees fine. Everything else not.
10. Holiday week, the Cleveland Way with Dad. Eighty miles of walking. Counted it.
11. Long run 10 miles in the rain. Ran past Callum's old flat for no reason.
12. Long run 11 miles. Hail on the prom. In August.
13. Long run 12 miles: the whole Water of Leith walkway, Balerno to the Shore, downhill all the way, which is cheating and which I recommend.

Week fourteen I ran the full distance once, slowly, alone, on a Sunday in September, round and round Holyrood Park, and did not tell anybody in case it was a fluke. Weeks fifteen and sixteen were for resting, and for panicking, which the rule on the fridge said not to do.

Total, from the spreadsheet: sixty-one runs, three hundred and four miles, two pairs of socks worn through and one toenail I would rather not discuss.
