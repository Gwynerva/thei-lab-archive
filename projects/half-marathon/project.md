I am not a runner. I want that on the record. I am a walker who, in the summer she was twenty-seven, ran a half marathon, and then kept running for a year out of something between habit and stubbornness, and then stopped, not by choice.

It began in May 2019, the week I was promoted, at the leaving do of a colleague who had run every half marathon in Scotland and said, at about eleven at night, that anybody could do it. I said I couldn't. He said that was what everybody said. By midnight I had entered the Glasgow half, on my phone, in the pub, and I woke up the next day with a confirmation email and no memory of the details.

So, from the seventeenth of June: [a plan](ref:section:half-marathon/plan), sixteen weeks of it, four runs a week, from barely round Leith Links to thirteen miles.

## What it was like

Mostly, it was early. I ran before work, at six, along the Water of Leith or down to the shore, while the city was still rubbing its eyes. I hated the first twenty minutes of every run all summer and loved the last ten. I learned that my body, which I had always thought of as a thing that carried my head about, could be asked to do something hard and do it.

I ran through the Cleveland Way with Dad in July by not running at all and walking eighty miles instead, which I decided counted. I ran in the heat, in the rain and once in hail, in August, which is Scotland for you.

## The race

The sixth of October. [All of it is here](ref:section:half-marathon/race), and the time is in the title of [the day itself](ref:event:half-marathon-race). It was under two hours, which was the only number I cared about, by a minute and nineteen seconds, which I will tell anyone who stands still long enough.
