For ten months of Saturdays, from my second week in Edinburgh until the week before I started my first job, I fixed bikes in a basement off Leith Walk. *The Spoke Room* was a co-op: anyone could bring a bike in and either pay us to fix it or borrow the tools and fix it themselves while one of us hovered. The volunteers got tea, biscuits and twenty pounds a day out of the tin, which in 2016 was my food budget.

The basement was damp. In winter you could see your breath until about eleven. The light came from one high window at pavement level, so you worked by the legs of people walking past, and by a string of bulbs somebody had rescued from a skip.

## What I learned there

- how to true a wheel by ear, listening for the spoke that pings flat
- that most brakes are fine and just need somebody to care about them for ten minutes
- the difference between a bike that is worn out and one that is only neglected
- patience, a little, with people and with cones and bearings

I wasn't a mechanic when I started. I'd had one puncture kit and a lot of confidence. By the summer I was the one people asked about old three-speed hubs, which says more about the other volunteers' age than about my skill.

Then, on 20 June 2017, somebody took my own bike from the stair at the flat, the lock cut clean. I took it more personally than I should have. Three years later, in lockdown, I rebuilt [a 1984 Raleigh](ref:project:raleigh) out of a skip with everything I'd learned in that basement, and I thought about the Spoke Room the whole time.

The rest of that year is under [all projects](/projects/), the MSc especially.
