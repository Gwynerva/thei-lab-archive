My niece Isla was born on the 2nd of March 2024, in Leeds, and I met her at the end of that month: a small, cross, wonderful person with Clare's chin and Dev's eyebrows, who fell asleep on my chest on the second day and made me forget how to move. On the plane home I started drawing a cat.

## The book

It's called *The Cat Who Slept on Maps*. It's thirty-two pages, square, twenty centimetres a side, painted in watercolour and lettered by hand, and it's about a ginger cat called Biscuit (of course) who falls asleep on a different map every night and wakes up in the place on the map: the harbour at Whitby, the top of Arthur's Seat, the fish market in Bergen, an orchard above a fjord. Every morning he comes home to a girl called Isla, who asks him where he's been.

It's the first thing I ever made for a child, and the first thing since 2022 that I made because I wanted to rather than because I ought to. I drew it through the spring and summer of 2024, the year I went back to work, left work, walked to Santiago and went freelance, and the book came with me through all of it. Two of the pages were painted in hostels in Portugal, on a pad on my knee.

## Binding

I bound it in November with the coptic stitch I learned at Lowe Bindery in 2017, so that it opens flat on the floor for a small person lying on her stomach. The covers are board wrapped in blue cloth, with the title lettered in white on the front in the alphabet I'd learned on the [lettering course](ref:project:lettering) in the spring.

One copy. Posted on the 10th of December, insured, with a card that said *For Isla, from Auntie Mira and Biscuit. Read it lying down.*

Clare sent a video at Christmas: Isla, nine months old, sitting on the floor with the book open in front of her, slapping the page with the cat on it and shouting. It's the best review I'll ever get.
