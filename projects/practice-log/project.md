Three weeks after my first cello lesson in twelve years, Mrs Ogilvie told me to keep a record of my practice. She meant a notebook. I am a person who, given a notebook, will draw a map in the margin and forget what the notebook was for, so instead I wrote a script.

It was small: about forty lines of Python that asked me three questions every evening, wrote the answers to a file, and drew a bar for every day. I ran it for sixty days, from the fifth of February to the fifth of April 2018, and then stopped, on purpose, because I had found out what I wanted to know.

What I wanted to know was whether I was actually practising, or just feeling guilty about not practising, which at twenty-five I found hard to tell apart. The answer was: mostly practising, about half an hour a day, more than I thought on bad days and less than I thought on good ones.

## Was it worth it

Looking back from 2026, I think the script was a crutch, and a good one. The cello was new again and hard, and every evening I was afraid it would turn out I had given it up at sixteen for a reason. The bars on the screen were proof that I was doing the work, even on the nights when the work sounded awful. After sixty days I did not need the proof. I knew.

I still keep a log, now and then, in the margins of the diary: minutes, what, how it went. No script. But the three questions are the same ones, because they were the right ones.

The cello itself has [its own page](<ref:project:cello#h:Why the cello> "and why I went back to it"). This is only the arithmetic.
