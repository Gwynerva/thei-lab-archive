---
title: The script
summary: Three questions, a file and a row of bars
---

I wrote it on the evening of the fourth of February 2018, at the kitchen table, while Callum made soup and asked why it was taking me longer to write a program about practising than it would take to practise.

What it did, every evening when I typed its name:

1. Asked *how many minutes?* and would not take an answer that was not a number.
2. Asked *what did you work on?* and offered a list: bowing, scales, the piece, the orchestra part, other.
3. Asked *how did it go?* and took anything at all.
4. Added a line to a file with the date and the three answers.
5. Drew the last fourteen days as a row of bars, one for each day, the height of the minutes.

That was all. No graphs, no streaks, no badges, nothing that would make me feel bad on purpose. A day without practice was simply a day without a bar, a gap in the row, and the gaps did their own work.

The *how did it go?* column was meant for moods and turned into a diary of its own: *better*, *awful*, *sore fingers*, *the rented cello hates me*, *the neighbours knocked*, *nearly music*. When I read it now it is the best part of the file.

The script lives in a folder that has moved from laptop to laptop for eight years, and it still runs. I tried it in 2026, for old times' sake, and typed in twenty minutes of scales and *fine*. It drew one bar, alone, eight years after the others.
