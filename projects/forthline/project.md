*Forthline Mapping* was twelve people in two rooms above a marine electronics shop on the Shore in Leith. Dougie Mair founded it in a spare bedroom in 2009 and moved it to the Shore when he got his first council contract. I joined on 11 September 2017, three weeks after handing in my dissertation, and left on 16 July 2021 to move to Norway.

## What we made

Surface-water flood maps, mostly for councils in Scotland and the north of England. Not rivers bursting their banks, which everyone pictures, but the other kind: what happens when it rains harder than the drains can take, and the water goes where the ground tells it to. Into basements, under railway bridges, down the one street that sits a little lower than all the others.

A flood map like that is a model of rain on a surface, run for storms of different sizes, and drawn so that a planner, an engineer and a worried resident can all read it.

:::quote[Dougie, on my second day]{align="center"}
A flood map is a promise that somebody will read it at three in the morning with water coming under the door. Make it readable at three in the morning.
:::

## What I did

- **2017–2018:** the junior year. Digitising drains, cleaning elevation data, colouring in.
- **2018:** [my first flood map](ref:section:forthline/first-flood-map), delivered in a heatwave.
- **2019–2021:** developer. I wrote the pipeline that turned a model run into finished map sheets, then the tools the rest of the team used every day. I spoke about it at a conference in Amsterdam, with my hands shaking.

## The people

Dougie, who wore the same blue jumper every Friday and remembered everyone's birthdays. Ines from Porto, who sat opposite me for three years and taught me to swear in Portuguese and to name my variables properly. Ten others I am still in touch with, more or less.

## Looking back

It was a good first job: small enough that everything I did mattered, kind enough that my mistakes didn't. I had my evenings, and I spent them on [tideline](ref:project:tideline), which grew straight out of the work. When I left, the whole office walked down to the Shore with a cake iced as a contour map of Arthur's Seat. Dougie cut it at the summit.
