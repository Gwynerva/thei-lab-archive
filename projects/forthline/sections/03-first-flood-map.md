My first flood map from start to finish was for a small town in the Borders with a river, two burns and a high street that floods every few summers. Eight weeks: building the surface, setting the drain capacities, running the model for five storm sizes, then drawing it all into map sheets for the council.

The hardest part wasn't the modelling. It was the legend. How do you say "this street has a one in thirty chance each year of water above your ankles" so that someone believes it and isn't frightened? I made nine versions. The one that went out uses five blues, one sentence and a little drawing of a welly at the right depth.

It was [delivered on 29 June](ref:event:first-flood-map), in the middle of [the summer it didn't rain](ref:event:heatwave-2018). I emailed it from the office with all the windows open and the whole of Leith smelling of hot tar. Dougie said it was the most Scottish thing he had ever seen: a flood map, delivered in a drought, by an Englishwoman.
