Nana June's sewing box sat on the floor by her chair for as long as I can remember: a wooden box on four short legs, with a lid that opened in two halves and trays that swung up and out on brass arms, like a bridge opening for a ship. As a child I thought it was the cleverest object in the world. I still do.

It came to Leith with the quilt after the funeral, in the boot of Dad's car. One side had split along the grain where the glue had given up, the lining was in tatters, and one of the brass arms was held on with a twist of fuse wire, which was very Nana.

## What I did

In February and March 2021, in the second lockdown, I took it apart, glued and clamped the split, cleaned the brass, re-lined the trays with green baize and put it back together. It took five weeks of weekends. Most of that time went not on the wood but on the contents: sorting seventy years of thread, buttons, pins and other people's mending.

## What I didn't do

I didn't sand it. I didn't refinish it. The lid is worn pale in two places where her hands went to open it, and I wasn't going to take those away.

It lives at Øvre Lid now, by the stove, with my thread in it and some of hers. When Isla is old enough she can open the trays and watch the arms swing out, and I'll tell her it's the cleverest object in the world.
