[The first appointment](ref:event:first-physio) was on a Thursday at a clinic on Leith Walk, ten minutes from the flat, which on crutches was twenty-five. Morag Fleming: small, grey-haired, sleeves rolled up, thumbs like a mechanic's. She pressed the knee in six places and asked me to tell her when it hurt, and I told her five times out of six, and she nodded as if I had given the right answers in an exam.

Then she drew arrows on a diagram of a knee in biro and told me how it would go. Weeks for the swelling. Months for the strength. A year before I would stop thinking about it. She was exactly right about all three.

Every week at first, then every fortnight. In the second lockdown, in January, on a laptop on the floor of my bedroom, Morag's face in the corner of the screen saying *lower, slower* while I did step-ups on a pile of atlases. By then I was walking every day again, three miles, then five, then up Calton Hill without stopping.

The last appointment was the twenty-sixth of February 2021. She watched me hop on the bad leg twenty times, balance on it with my eyes shut, and squat to the floor and up again. Then she said:

> You're discharged. Go and climb something.

I climbed Ben Nevis four months later. I sent her a photograph from the top. She did not reply, which I think is a physiotherapist's way of saying well done.
