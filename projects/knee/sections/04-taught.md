---
title: What it taught me
---

That the body keeps its own time and will not be hurried by a deadline, a summer, or a list.

That *slow* and *careful* are not the same thing, which Mrs Ogilvie had told me about the cello two years before and which I had to learn again in my leg.

That most of getting better is boring. Ten seconds, ten times, every day, for months. Nothing to post, nothing to show anyone, no single day when it gets better. Then one day you walk up a hill without thinking about it, and only at the top do you notice you didn't think about it.

That you can lose something you took for granted, the running, and be sad about it, and then find you do not need it as much as you thought. I walk now. I walked to Santiago. Walking is enough.

And that asking for help is allowed: from a stranger from Aberdeen on a steep path, from Marta with a taxi, from Clare on the phone, from Morag and her thumbs. I was bad at it in 2020. I was worse at it in 2023, and that is [another story](ref:project:burnout). I am a little better now.
