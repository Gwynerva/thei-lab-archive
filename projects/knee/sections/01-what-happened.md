A Saturday, clear and cool. I ran up Arthur's Seat from the flat, as I had most Saturdays that summer, and touched the trig point, and turned round and ran down the steep path on the east side towards Dunsapie Loch, which I had been told a dozen times not to run down.

Halfway, a stone moved under my right foot. The foot went one way and the knee went the other, inward, and there was a pop. Then I was sitting on the path with no memory of sitting down, and the knee was hot, and I could not straighten it.

I hopped and hobbled the rest of the way down with my arm round the shoulders of a stranger, a student from Aberdeen who would not let me do it alone and whose name I never asked, which I regret. Marta came with a taxi. Clare, on the phone from Leeds, in her nurse voice: ice, rest, raise it, and go and get it looked at, and stop saying you're fine.

The minor injuries unit, a long wait, a young doctor who bent it in every direction while I made noises. A sprain of the ligament on the inside of the knee, probably a small tear in the cartilage, crutches for a fortnight and a referral to a physio.

[The day the knee went](ref:event:knee-goes#:~:text=knee), as I wrote it that night, is shorter and has more swearing in it.
