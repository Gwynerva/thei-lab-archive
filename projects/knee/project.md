This page is for me, and for anyone I send it to who has just done the same thing and wants to know whether it gets better. It does. Slowly, and not in a straight line, and only if you do the boring exercises, but it does.

## What happened

On a Saturday morning in September 2020 I was running down the path off Arthur's Seat, too fast, the way I had been running all that lockdown summer, and put my right foot on a stone that moved. The knee went inward and there was a pop I felt more than heard. I sat down on the path. A man with a spaniel asked if I was all right, and I said yes, and I was not. [The rest of that day](ref:section:knee/what-happened) is its own section.

What it was, in the end: a sprained ligament on the inside of the knee, and a small tear in the cartilage, the meniscus, which is the rubbery cushion between the bones. Nothing that needed surgery. Everything that needed time.

## What happened next

Five months of physiotherapy with Morag Fleming, a small woman with very strong thumbs who has seen every kind of knee and was unimpressed by mine. A page of exercises, which I still do, less often than I should. A winter of walking, and then of walking further, and then the hills again.

## What it means now

In July 2021 I climbed Ben Nevis, the last of my thirty-four Munros, on the knee, and it held. In 2024 I walked from Porto to Santiago on it, and it held. In 2025 I carried a pack across Svaneti for four days, and it held. It aches before rain, which in Hardanger means it aches, and it does not like stairs first thing in the morning, which in a house of 1891 is unfortunate.

But it is mine, and it works, and I know it better than I know my left one. That is something.

[What it taught me](ref:section:knee/taught) is the last section, and the one I would send.
