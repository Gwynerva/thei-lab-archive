## Reykjavík

We landed at eleven at night, in daylight, into a wind that did not stop for ten days, collected Snowy, and drove into a city of coloured roofs that would not go dark. In the morning, before we left, we went up the tower of the big church and saw the whole of it, and the mountains across the bay with snow still on them. Breakfast was a hot dog from a stand, which everyone told us to have, and which was very good.

## Waterfalls

The next morning we drove east along a flat green plain with the mountains on our left, and they started: water falling off the edge of the land every few kilometres, as if the country had too much of it. At Seljalandsfoss you can walk round behind the fall, on a wet path, inside the noise. We did it twice. Then Skógafoss, which is wide and so loud you have to shout, with a rainbow in the spray as if someone had arranged it.

## The black beach

Reynisfjara: black sand, basalt columns like organ pipes, and sea stacks out in the surf. There are signs everywhere about the waves, and they are right. One came up the beach much further than the others while we were looking at the columns, and we ran, and Priya lost a glove to it. The sea kept it.
