In May 2018 Priya and I hired a small white car at Keflavík, which Priya named Snowy before we were out of the car park, and drove it round Iceland anticlockwise in ten days. [Route 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Route_1_%28Iceland%29), the ring road, is about thirteen hundred kilometres. We did nearly two thousand, because of detours, and because of one wrong turn near Egilsstaðir that I will not discuss.

I had wanted to go since I was fourteen and saw a photograph of a glacier lagoon in a geography textbook. Priya wanted to see puffins and was disappointed, because it was too early in the year and we never found one. Between us we saw nearly everything else.

## The route

1. Reykjavík, and the [south coast](ref:section:iceland/south): waterfalls, black sand, the church at Vík.
2. [Jökulsárlón](ref:section:iceland/glacier-lagoon), the lagoon full of icebergs.
3. [The east fjords](ref:section:iceland/east-fjords), and the fog.
4. [Mývatn](ref:section:iceland/myvatn), the steam, and the midges.
5. [Snæfellsnes](ref:section:iceland/west), the lighthouse, and home.

## What I learned

That a map of Iceland lies to you about distances, because the road is never where the straight line is. That you can be cold and sunburnt at the same time. That Priya sings in the car, all of it, every song, and that after the fourth day I started to join in. That light at midnight makes you stupid in a good way. That a country can be almost empty and still feel full.

And that my job is the right job: I spent half the trip looking at the landscape and thinking *how would you map that*, the lava fields especially, and came home and asked Dougie whether Forthline had ever done anything with lava. It had not. It still has not.
