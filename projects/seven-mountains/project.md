Bergen sits between seven mountains, more or less. People argue about which seven, the way people in Edinburgh argue about which seven hills; the list I used is the one Mr Haldorsen downstairs gave me in my first week, written on the back of an envelope, and if he says those are the seven, those are the seven. He was a ship's mate for forty years. He is not often wrong about where things are.

They are the :hint[*byfjellene*]{note="the city mountains: the ones you can walk up from your own front door"}, and that is the best thing about them. In Scotland a hill was a day: a bus, a car, a long drive, a walk in. Here you leave the flat, turn left, and you are going up.

## One at a time

1. [Fløyen](ref:section:seven-mountains/floyen), August 2021, a week after I arrived
2. [Ulriken](ref:section:seven-mountains/ulriken), across Vidden, in a cloud
3. [Løvstakken](ref:section:seven-mountains/lovstakken), on my own, in October

Then a winter of rain, a kayak course, and a man who builds boats, and in 2022 the rest:

4. [Damsgårdsfjellet](ref:section:seven-mountains/damsgardsfjellet), in April
5. [Lyderhorn](ref:section:seven-mountains/lyderhorn), in July, with a swim
6. [Sandviksfjellet](ref:section:seven-mountains/sandviksfjellet), in August, the one above our roof
7. [Rundemanen](ref:section:seven-mountains/rundemanen), in October, in sleet

And then, two years later, at the end of the worst year and the start of a better one, [all seven in a day](ref:section:seven-mountains/all-seven).

## What Mr Haldorsen said

When I told him, in my first week, that I meant to climb them all, he looked at my Scottish boots, then at the sky, then at me.

:::quote[Sverre Haldorsen, on the stairs, August 2021]{align="center"}
The mountains will wait. The weather will not. Go when it is good, and when it is not, go anyway, but take a hat.
:::

I took a hat. I have taken a hat ever since.

## What they are for

For the first year they were how I learned the city: from the top of each I could see where I lived, where I worked, the harbour, the way the weather came in from the sea. I drew a map of Bergen with the seven around it like the numbers on a clock and filled each one in as I climbed it, the way I filled in [the Munros](ref:project:munros "thirty-four of them, in Scotland").

Later, in 2023, I stopped climbing anything. In 2024 they were how I started again.
