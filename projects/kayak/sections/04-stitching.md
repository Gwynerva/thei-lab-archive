Back from Lofoten with the northern lights still in my eyes, and straight into stitching.

How it goes:

- drill holes along the edge of each panel, every ten centimetres, two millimetres in from the edge
- cut copper wire into short lengths, a few hundred of them
- line up two panels edge to edge and stitch them together with a twist of wire through each pair of holes
  - twist with pliers, not too tight, or the wire snaps
  - not too loose, or the seam gapes
  - every twist the same way, so you can find them again
- work from the middle out, both sides at once, so the hull stays straight

Four panels a side, keel to sheer. On the third evening the panels stopped being flat and folded up into the air round the forms, and there it was, a boat, hanging in the light from the workshop windows. I stood back and said a word in English that Jonas has now learned.

Six weeks. Four hundred and twelve stitches. Copper on my fingers every night, green in the creases of my hands where no soap would reach.
