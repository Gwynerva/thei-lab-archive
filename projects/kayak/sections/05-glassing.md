The summer went by with the hull sitting stitched and glued on the strongback, the wires cut out and the seams filleted, waiting, while I learned how to be in Norway in summer, went to Hardanger and met Jonas's mother.

September was supposed to be glassing. Then Dad's heart, on the 5th, and a week in Whitby: a stent, a hospital car park, Mum being brave, Dad being terse. "I'm fine. Go and build your boat." So I came back and built my boat.

Glassing: a sheet of fine glass cloth laid over the hull dry, smoothed with your hands until it drapes into every curve, then epoxy squeegeed into it until the cloth goes clear and the wood glows through like honey in a jar. Three coats to fill the weave. Then sanding, sanding, sanding.

1. 25 September: [cloth on the hull](ref:event:glassing-begins "the day she went shiny").
2. 2 October: the first fill coat, and the rain starts.
3. 15 October: the last fill coat. *Glass on.*

It rained for twenty-one days without stopping, the workshop smelled of resin and woodsmoke, and the epoxy took twice as long to cure in the damp. November was sanding with masks on and the radio up.
