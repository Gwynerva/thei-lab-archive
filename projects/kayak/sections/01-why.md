---
title: Why build one
summary: A cold fjord, a borrowed kayak, and the offer of a corner of a workshop
---

January 2022, written the week after the course.

Two days on the water at Hjellestad, in a borrowed plastic sea kayak, in drysuits, at minus three. My hands hurt. My face hurt. I fell in once, on purpose, for the rescue practice, and the cold went through me like a bell.

And still. Out on the fjord, with the paddle going in and out and the hills white on both sides, it was the quietest my head has been since I moved here. Five months of Norwegian verbs and harbour charts and rain, and then, suddenly, nothing but water and breath.

Why build one instead of buying one:

- because a good sea kayak costs more than I earn in a month
- because I've carved spoons all winter and my hands want something bigger
- because Dad has mended boats all his life and I've never made one
- because the instructor said it would be "not hard, only long"
- because he offered me a corner of his workshop, and I said yes very quickly, and then went home and sat on the floor and said "oh no" out loud to Biscuit

His name is Jonas. He builds wooden boats and teaches people to paddle them. He has sawdust in his eyebrows permanently.
