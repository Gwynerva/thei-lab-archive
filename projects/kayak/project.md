I built a sea kayak between January 2022 and May 2023, in a boat workshop in Bergen, with a great deal of help from the man who taught the course where I first sat in one. I married him this summer, which is a story for [another page](ref:project:our-wedding). This one is about the boat.

She's five metres twenty long and fifty-six centimetres wide, built of 4 mm okoume plywood, stitched together with copper wire, glued with epoxy, sheathed in glass cloth and varnished seven times. She weighs nineteen kilos. She's called *Nana June*, after my grandmother, who never saw her and would have said she was too narrow to be safe, and then asked to have a go.

## Why

Because of [the course in January 2022](ref:event:kayak-course "two days on a cold fjord, and Jonas"), mostly. I sat in a borrowed plastic kayak on a cold fjord and the world went quiet in a way it hadn't since I moved to Norway. Because I'd spent the autumn carving spoons and wanted something bigger. Because my dad has mended boats all his life and I'd never built one. And because the instructor, when I asked how hard it would be to make one, said "not hard, only long", and offered me a corner of his workshop. I knew perfectly well that wasn't only about boats.

## Plans

The design is a stitch-and-glue sea kayak from a set of published plans. You cut the panels out of thin plywood, drill along the edges every ten centimetres, and stitch the panels together with twists of copper wire until the hull takes its shape in the air like a paper boat. Then you glue the seams from inside with thickened epoxy, cut the wires out, and cover the whole thing in glass cloth. No moulds, no steam, no ribs. It's the way to build a boat in somebody else's workshop in your evenings.

What the plans asked for:

- [x] four sheets of 4 mm okoume, marine grade
- [x] two kilos of copper wire
- [x] epoxy, about twelve litres in the end
- [x] glass cloth for the hull and the deck
- [x] varnish, more than the plans said
- [x] a strongback, a cockpit coaming, hatches, a seat, footrests and a great deal of patience

## The launch

We put her in the water on [the 20th of May 2023](ref:event:kayak-launch#:~:text=water) at a slipway near the workshop, at nine in the morning, in sunshine, which in Bergen counts as a miracle all by itself. Jonas, his mother Sigrid, Anna from work, Sverre from downstairs, and Mum and Dad on a video call on my phone in a freezer bag. I poured a spoonful of rowan jelly, made from Nana's recipe and stirred into a jug of fjord water, over the bow, because champagne seemed wrong and rowan seemed right. Then I got in and pushed off, and she floated level and true, and I paddled out into the harbour and didn't want to come back.

That spring was already going wrong at work, and by the autumn I'd stopped altogether. I didn't paddle much that year after the launch. But she was there, in the boathouse, finished. Something was finished.
