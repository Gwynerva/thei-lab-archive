## The average

Comparing every pair of dates, the cliff top at Saltwick has retreated on average **0.31 m a year since 1853**, give or take 0.08. Over 164 years that is roughly fifty metres: the width of a football pitch's penalty area, as Dad put it, which helped nobody.

## Nine metres

The average hides how it really happens. Most years almost nothing moves. Then one winter takes a bite. Between the aerial photographs of 2012 and 2014, one stretch south of the Nab lost nine metres, most likely in the tidal surge of December 2013. The drone survey confirmed the scar; the fall I watched in April 2017 took another two metres off the same stretch.

## Faster?

Probably slightly faster since about 1950, though the old maps are too uncertain to be sure. I wrote that sentence eleven ways.

:::quote[Dr Grieve, in green ink, on draft four]{align="center"}
How sure are you? Say it in the number, not in the adjectives.
:::

So the final version gives the rates with their errors and no adjectives at all. It got the dissertation prize in the department, which I didn't know existed until I'd won it.
