Three months of turning photographs into a cliff. The recipe, as I finally got it working:

1. Throw away the blurred photos (about a fifth of them, mostly from the windy Tuesday).
2. Match features between overlapping pictures until the software knows where each camera was.
3. Tie the whole thing to the thirty-eight control points so it sits in real coordinates.
4. Build a dense point cloud: thirty-one million points, each with a colour and a height.
5. Clean it. Remove seagulls, one dog and a man in a red jacket who walked through four flights.
6. Cut a cliff-top line out of it that can be compared with the 1853 pen line.

Step five took a week. Step six took a month, because "the top of the cliff" turns out to be a matter of opinion when the grass curls over the edge.

::media[What a point cloud looks like when it works. This one is a street in San Francisco; mine was a cliff, and for most of June it looked like porridge.]{file="lidar-1" layout="natural"}

By the end of July the cliff in the computer matched the cliff on the ground to about eight centimetres. I printed the number and stuck it on the fridge, where Callum drew a small cliff falling off it.
