---
title: Old maps, 1853–1990
---

The oldest evidence I had was paper. Six editions of the large-scale survey covered Saltwick between 1853 and 1990, and on each one somebody had drawn the top of the cliff with a pen.

1. 1853, the first six-inch survey: beautiful, and wrong in places by up to twelve metres
2. 1893, the first revision at a larger scale
3. 1913
4. 1928
5. 1952, the first one after the war
6. 1990, the last one drawn before everything went digital

Each sheet had to be :hint[georeferenced]{note="Pinned to real coordinates by matching points that haven't moved since: church towers, field corners, the abbey itself."} before I could compare it with anything. That took most of February. An old map lies to you in at least three ways:

:::list{counter="lower-roman"}
1. the paper has shrunk or stretched since it was printed, and not evenly
2. the surveyor drew either the top of the cliff or the bottom of the slope, and doesn't say which
3. the sheets don't quite meet at their edges, so the coast takes a small step sideways at every join
:::

I measured all three, as far as I could, and put the error on every line. Two years later I went back to the same maps for their own sake; that is [the old maps of the coast](ref:project:old-maps-yorkshire), and its [1853 survey](ref:section:old-maps-yorkshire/the-1853-survey) in particular.
