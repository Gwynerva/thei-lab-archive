In November 2016 Dad and I walked from the West Cliff out to Saltwick Bay on a grey Saturday. He stopped by a fence post hanging over the edge on its own wire and said that when he was a boy it had been a field back from the edge. Not literally, he admitted later. But the path had moved, and the caravan park had moved, and the cliff was still going. I asked him how fast. He said "fast enough", which is not a number.

So this became my dissertation: **how fast is the cliff at Saltwick retreating, and has it sped up?**

## How I measured it

Three kinds of evidence, each a different century's way of looking at the same edge:

1. **Old maps.** Six editions of the large-scale survey, from 1853 to 1990, each with a cliff line I could trace and compare. More in [the old maps](ref:section:whitby-erosion/old-maps).
2. **Aerial photographs.** Eleven flights between 1946 and 2014, rectified one by one, very slowly.
3. **A drone survey.** Four days in April 2017 with the department's technician, Sam, and his drone, turning about fourteen hundred photographs into a model of the cliff today.

::media[Saltwick Nab from the north, the stretch I measured, on the last morning of fieldwork]{file="saltwick-nab-1" layout="stretch"}

## What I found

On average the cliff top has retreated about **0.3 m a year** since 1853, with an uncertainty I spent a whole chapter on. That rate hides a lot. Most years almost nothing moves; then a single winter takes metres at once. Between the photographs of 2012 and 2014 one stretch lost [nine metres](<ref:section:whitby-erosion/results#:~:text=nine metres> "the worst stretch, measured three ways"), most of it, as far as anyone can tell, in the surge of December 2013.

Has it sped up? Probably a little, since about 1950. Not as much as the headlines say, more than the council's old figure. I was careful about that sentence for weeks.

## Looking back

It was the first thing I made that anyone else used: the council's coastal officer asked for my shapefiles, and I sent them with a very long README. Every Christmas since, Dad and I walk out to Saltwick and look at where the fence post was. It went in the winter of 2019. We stood there a while, and he said "fast enough" again, and this time I knew what he meant.
