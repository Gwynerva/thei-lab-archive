In April 2024, back at work half time after the winter I stopped, I handed in my notice at Nordvåg without another job to go to. On 1 July I registered as a sole trader, an *enkeltpersonforetak*, which is a long word for a short thing: me, a laptop, a business account and a promise to the tax office.

## What I do

Three kinds of work, in roughly equal thirds when a year goes well:

1. **Maps for people who need one.** Walking maps for tourist offices, a harbour plan for a sailing club, location maps for reports, a map of a family's farm for a seventieth birthday. Some in software, some by hand.
2. **Teaching.** An online course in QGIS, eight evenings a term, which turned out to be the part I love most. That is [Teaching QGIS online](ref:project:qgis-course).
3. **Prints and the book.** [Illustrated maps](ref:project:illustrated-maps) of neighbourhoods and walks, and since 2025 [a walking atlas of Hardanger](ref:project:walk-atlas) for a small publisher in Bergen.

## How it is going

Better than I feared and less than I hoped, which Mum says is the definition of a job. The first winter was thin. The second was full. Since we moved to the house in 2025 I have worked at a kitchen table that was, for five months, the only table in the kitchen, and then, while the kitchen was being rebuilt, a plank across two trestles in the bedroom.

I work about thirty hours a week, which I choose, and I stop when the light goes off the far side of the fjord, which in December is half past two. I am still learning how to say no to work I would enjoy, because there is always more of it than there are hours, and I have been the person who forgot that once already.

## What I've learned

- the client who wants it "quickly and cheaply" always wants it good as well
- a third of everything goes into the tax account the day it arrives, no exceptions
- teaching what you know makes you know it properly
- the best work comes through people who have seen the last work

I miss colleagues, and lunch at half eleven with everyone, and the gulls on the window. I don't miss much else.
