The [first client](ref:event:first-client) wrote on 15 July: a tourist office on an island south of Bergen that wanted a hand-drawn map of its walking routes for a leaflet. Six walks, one map, one folded sheet. I said yes before I had worked out what to charge, and charged too little, and did it anyway, and it is still the leaflet they hand out.

The rest of that first half year:

- **August:** a map of the walks round a cabin village, for its owners' association
- **September:** quality checks on soundings for a survey firm, which was Nordvåg's work done at home, at my own hours, for twice the rate
  - I said I would never do chart work again; this is why one shouldn't say never
- **October:** the first QGIS course, twelve students, which became its own thing
- **November:** a location map for an environmental report, eleven versions
- **December:** a map of a family's farm for a grandfather's eightieth, hand-painted, delivered on the 20th, the last job of the year

By Christmas I had earned about half of what I'd earned at Nordvåg in the same months, and I was twice as tired in a completely different way: the good way, from making things.
