The orchard came with the house: forty trees on the slope below it, most of them older than anyone who has picked from them in my time. Ragnhild sold the apples for forty years. In her last years nobody pruned them, and when we came they were tall and tangled and full of moss, and still, every one of them, flowering.

Sigrid's advice, the first week, was to do nothing.

> Watch them for a year. A tree will tell you what it wants if you are not standing on it with a saw.

So we watched. We cut out only the dead wood that first spring, and in May the [blossom](ref:section:cider/blossom-2025) came like snow going the wrong way, and in October we had more apples than two people and their friends could eat in a lifetime. Hardanger has made cider since the monks, and our neighbours below, Eirik and Kari, have a press. The rest followed.

## The year of an orchard

- **March.** Prune, on a dry day, with Sigrid standing over us, for the tree we want in ten years.
- **May.** Blossom. Worry about frost. Lie awake listening for it.
- **Summer.** Mow under the trees, twice. Thin the fruit, which feels like a crime.
- **September.** Pick the early ones and eat them in the tree.
- **October.** Pick everything else. Press. Smell of apples for a week.
- **January.** Bottle.

It is the first thing I have ever made that runs on a year instead of a weekend, and it suits me better than I expected. You cannot hurry a tree. You cannot even hurry the yeast.

The [first pressing](ref:event:cider-pressing-2025) gave us 212 litres; the second, this October, more. What it tastes like is in [the tasting notes](ref:section:cider/tasting "honest ones"). Sigrid says it is very good for a first year, which from Sigrid is a medal.
