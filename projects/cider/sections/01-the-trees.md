---
title: The trees
summary: Forty trees, three kinds of apple we know, two pears pretending to be apples, and two that nobody can name.
---

I mapped the orchard in our first May, of course, on squared paper, every tree a circle with a number in it. Then, on the day of the blossom, I walked it with Sigrid and wrote in the names.

- **Gravenstein**, twenty-two trees, the old ones
  - the row along the road, which Ragnhild's father planted the year she was confirmed
  - the rest up the slope towards the house, two of them more moss than tree, and still fruiting
- **Aroma**, nine, planted in the eighties along the track
- **Summerred**, five, early, red, gone to the birds if you are not quick
- **Pears**, two, by the woodshed, old, hard as stones until one week in October, pretending to be apples
- **Nobody knows**, two, at the barn's north corner

Twenty-two and nine and five and two and two: forty.

The two nameless trees are the ones I love best. They flower late, a deep pink, and their apples are small, yellow-green and very sour, and they make the cider better. Sigrid has walked round them twice and said "hm" both times, and was put out, which is the closest I have ever seen her come to not knowing something.
