We picked for a fortnight, in the evenings and at weekends, into every box, crate and bucket in the house. On the fourth of October Eirik and Kari brought their press up the track on a trailer and set it up on the barn bridge, and everybody we know in Hardanger came, and some people we did not know, and Sigrid, who was in charge.

How it goes:

1. Wash the apples in a bath of cold water in the yard.
2. Mill them in the electric mill, which is loud and which Biscuit hated.
3. Build the pulp into a stack of cloths and racks in the press.
4. Turn the screw. Turn it again. Watch the juice come.
5. Pour the juice into carboys, with a funnel and a prayer.
6. Feed the dry pulp to Kari's sheep, who think it is Christmas.

By the scales we had 389 kilos, three crates of them Sigrid's own Discovery, brought as a present. By my notebook that made 214 litres of juice. In the nine carboys in the cellar, at the end, there were 212. Two litres went on the floor of the barn, and the barn still smells of it a year later.
