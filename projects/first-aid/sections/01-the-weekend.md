title: The weekend

  - 2019-03-16..2019-03-17

A village hall with a stage, a smell of floor polish and school dinners, twelve of us on plastic chairs and an instructor called by everybody, including himself, *the Major*, though he had never been one. Twenty years in mountain rescue. A beard you could lose a glove in.

Saturday was the hall: the order of things, again and again, until it was in our hands and not our heads.

1. **Danger.** Is the hill still trying to kill anyone? Yourself first.
2. **Response.** Talk to them. Squeeze a shoulder.
3. **Airway.** Open it.
4. **Breathing.** Look, listen, feel, ten seconds.
5. **Circulation.** Find the bleeding and stop it.

Then bandages, splints out of rolled-up sleeping mats and walking poles, a sling out of a jacket, and how to wrap someone in a survival bag so that they are a warm parcel and not a cold one.

Sunday was the hill. We went up into the snow behind the hall in pairs and found, every half an hour, a *casualty*: a member of the course lying in the heather with a label round their neck saying what was wrong. A broken ankle. A head injury, who answered every question with "I'm fine, what day is it?" Hypothermia, who was in fact quite cold by then. I splinted an ankle with a map case and two tent pegs and was told it was "inventive", which I chose to take as praise.

Home on the bus, knackered, with a certificate and a cold.
