Week one: centring. Week two: centring. Week three: centring, and one cylinder that stood up for nearly a minute.

Nobody warns you that throwing is ninety per cent centring. You drop a lump of clay on the wheel, wet it, and lean your whole weight into it with your elbows braced, until it stops wobbling and spins true, smooth as a stone in a river. Until it's centred, nothing else works. Once it's centred, everything is possible and most of it goes wrong anyway.

What I learned that term, roughly in order:

1. wedge the clay first, or the air bubbles will find you
2. wet hands, but not a swamp
3. slow wheel for opening, fast wheel for centring
4. leave the pot until it's :hint[leather-hard]{note="dry enough to hold its shape and be handled, still damp enough to trim and carve; it feels like cheese"} before you trim it
5. don't name anything until it's out of the kiln

The class was twelve of us. A retired dentist who was annoyingly good. Two students. A woman who only made bowls for her dog. The teacher, who said "lovely" about everything and meant it about nothing until it actually was lovely, and then she said it differently.
