I've tried to learn to throw pots twice, four years apart, and both times it was really about something else.

## Leith, 2019

The first time was an evening class in a studio in Leith, Tuesdays from September 2019, twelve of us in aprons at twelve wheels. It was about Priya leaving. She moved to Plymouth on the first of September, and two days later I was sitting at a potter's wheel with my elbows clamped to my knees, trying to centre a lump of clay and failing for two hours. The flat was quieter than I could stand. The studio was loud and wet and nobody there knew me.

I made one thing worth keeping that term: [the lopsided mug](ref:event:lopsided-mug "19 November 2019"), which leans about eight degrees to the east and holds exactly one cup of tea. I've used it every day since, in three countries.

Then 2020 happened, the studio closed, I moved to Norway, and for four years I didn't touch clay.

## Bergen, 2023

The second time was a Tuesday class in Bergen from November 2023. It was about the burnout, though I couldn't have said so then. I was on sick leave, and somebody suggested I find something for my hands that wasn't a keyboard. The studio was in a basement near Møhlenpris. The teacher spoke Bergen dialect fast and kindly, and I understood about half and did the other half by watching.

:::private
Tove's idea, of course. I didn't tell anyone at the studio why I had my Tuesdays free.
:::

Clay was the right thing. You can't throw a pot and think about anything else. If your mind wanders, the pot wanders, and then it collapses, and you laugh, or I did, eventually, around December.

## What I have to show for it

Eleven pots that survived the kiln, from two cities: the mug, three bowls, a jug that doesn't pour, five small cups for coffee, and a vase for Sigrid that she uses for cutlery. Not much for two courses. But I'm not sure pottery was ever really about the pots.
