Sixteen weeks, one Monday at a time, in the bedroom in Leith with the skylight, the laptop on the chest of drawers and the rock kit on the sill.

What we did, roughly by month:

- **April**: minerals, which are the letters, and rocks, which are the words. Scratching things with a penny and a nail to see how hard they were. The kitchen knife was declared off limits by Marta.
- **May**: volcanoes, and Edinburgh's in particular. I walked round Salisbury Crags with the lecture notes on my phone and found the place where the hot rock meets the layers it pushed into, and stood there touching it for so long a dog came to see what I was doing.
- **June**: sediments, layers, fossils, time. Deep time, which made lockdown feel very short.
- **July**: the coast of Yorkshire, which the lecturer chose as an example without knowing that one of his students had grown up on it. I sent a screenshot of an ammonite from Robin Hood's Bay to Mum, who showed it to Nana, who said she had a bucket of those.

The last essay was on why Whitby's cliffs fall down, fifteen hundred words, which I could have written in my sleep and nearly did. Results came on the thirtieth of July, a Thursday. I went for a celebratory walk up Calton Hill and found, on the way, three bits of sandstone in a garden wall that I could now name.
