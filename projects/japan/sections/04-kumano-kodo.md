The Kumano Kodo is a network of old pilgrim roads through the mountains of the Kii peninsula, walked for a thousand years by emperors and farmers to the three great shrines of Kumano. I walked the best-known piece, the Nakahechi, from Takijiri to Hongū, in four days.

1. **Takijiri to Takahara.** Short and steep, straight up from the river into the cedars. A small village on a ridge, terraces, mist in the valley below, and an inn where I was the only guest.
2. **Takahara to Chikatsuyu.** Forest all day. Little stone statues by the path, the old resting places of the pilgrims, each with a sign and a stamp. Moss on everything.
3. **Chikatsuyu to Hosshinmon.** The long day. Rain in the morning, steam coming off the forest in the afternoon. I did not see another walker until three.
4. **Hosshinmon to Hongū.** Downhill, through tea fields and then forest again, and out at the top of the stone steps of the shrine, with white banners on both sides, which I walked down very slowly.

The path is waymarked with small stone posts and wooden signs, and you could not get lost if you tried. I did try, once, on purpose, to see what the forest was like off the path. It was like the path, but darker, and I went back.
