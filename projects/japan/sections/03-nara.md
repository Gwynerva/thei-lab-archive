The deer of Nara bow. I had read this and not believed it. You buy a packet of deer crackers from a stall, and a deer walks up to you and dips its head, and you bow back, and it bows again, and then it eats the crackers, the paper band and, if you are not careful, your map.

There are more than a thousand of them, wandering the park and the temples and the pavements like people who live there, which they do. A doe and her fawn followed me all the way to the Great Buddha Hall and waited outside while I went in.

The Buddha is enormous, dark bronze in a dark wooden hall, and you feel very small, which I think is also the point.
