## Blossom

I arrived on the first full day of blossom, which was luck, and which everyone in Kyoto seemed to have arrived for as well. In Maruyama Park the big weeping cherry was lit in the evening, pale pink against black, propped up on wooden crutches like an old man, with people sitting under it on blue tarpaulins eating and drinking and singing. I sat on a wall with a rice ball and watched a man of about eighty stand under it with his hands behind his back for an hour.

## Before dawn

On my third morning I got up at half past four, took the first train south in the dark, and went up the mountain through the tunnels of orange gates while it got light. Thousands of gates, each given by somebody, with their names painted on in black. Foxes in stone with red bibs. Nobody else for the first hour except a man sweeping.

## The rest

Temples, more temples, a rock garden where I sat for an hour trying to see all fifteen stones at once and could not, which is the point. The river in the evening, with people on the banks. A tiny bar with six seats and a cat.
