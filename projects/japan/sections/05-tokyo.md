After four days of forest and one of the falls at Nachi, Tokyo was like walking into a loud room. I spent the first afternoon in it the way I had spent mornings in the forest: walking, without a plan, turning left when I felt like it. That evening I ate yakitori under a railway bridge with salarymen, and a man in a grey suit taught me to say "one more" and then made me say it.

The last evening I went up a tower at dusk and watched the whole city switch its lights on, from the near streets out to the edge, until it was lights to the horizon in every direction, with Tokyo Tower off to one side like a lit match. Then ramen, from a shop where you buy a ticket from a machine and press the wrong button.

::media[Tokyo at night, from up high]{file="tokyo-skyline-2" layout="stretch"}

I flew home the next day with a stamp book, a fox charm and a cold. Biscuit ignored me for exactly one evening, as a punishment, and then slept on my head.
