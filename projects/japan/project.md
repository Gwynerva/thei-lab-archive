I went to Japan on my own in April 2019, for two weeks, because I was twenty-seven and had never travelled anywhere alone, and because I had read about a pilgrim road through the mountains of the Kii peninsula and could not stop thinking about it.

Biscuit had been with us for eight weeks. Marta looked after him and sent me a photograph every morning, which arrived in my evening, of a ginger cat asleep on something of mine.

## The shape of it

1. [Kyoto](ref:section:japan/kyoto), four days, in cherry blossom.
2. [Nara](ref:section:japan/nara), one day, with the deer.
3. [The Kumano Kodo](ref:section:japan/kumano-kodo), four days on foot, Takijiri to Hongū.
4. [Tokyo](ref:section:japan/tokyo), two days, and a last night.

In between, trains. Japanese trains are the most restful places I have ever been. I would like to live on one.

::media[The forest path on the Kumano Kodo]{file="kumano-kodo-2" layout="stretch"}

## Alone

I was worried about being lonely, and I was, a bit, on the second evening in Kyoto, eating noodles at a counter with eleven other people eating noodles alone, all of us facing the wall. By the fourth evening I liked it. On the Kumano Kodo I barely spoke for four days except to say good morning and thank you, and I came out of the forest at Hongū feeling as if I had been rinsed.

:::quote[What the woman at the inn in Takahara said, when I asked if I would get lost]{align="center"}
The road has had a thousand years to make itself clear. Follow it.
:::

## What came home with me

A fox charm from Fushimi Inari that hangs on my bag. A stamp book with a stamp from every shrine on the Kumano Kodo. A taste for pickles at breakfast that nobody in Leith shares. And the walking: five years later I walked [another old pilgrim road](ref:project:camino#h:Stages "Porto to Santiago, 2024"), and I thought about the cedars every day of it.

What I ate is [its own page](ref:section:japan/food), because it had to be.
