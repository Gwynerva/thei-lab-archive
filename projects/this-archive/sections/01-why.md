---
title: Why I write it down
summary: Nine false starts, then one that took
---

I had started a diary about nine times before. Bits of my life were in the margins of lecture notes, on the backs of envelopes, in three different apps and in a Year 10 notebook with a horse on it. In October 2016, new city, new course, new flat, I decided to do it properly, in one place, and I made rules on the first page so that I could not wriggle out of them:

:::list{counter="lower-roman"}
1. Short is fine. One line is fine.
2. No tearing out pages, not even the embarrassing ones.
3. A little map in the margin when something happens somewhere.
4. Weather counts as news.
:::

I have broken the second rule twice, both in 2017, both about boys, and I regret neither.

Why it took this time, when it never had: because of the first rule. All the other diaries died because I thought every day had to be an essay, and when it couldn't be, I stopped. Nana said a stitch in time is still a stitch, and I decided that applied here too: one line is still a line. Most days in this archive are one line. Some are three pages. Both count.

And why I keep it, now, ten years on: because of Nana's letters, which I read after she died and which told me who she was at twenty-two, in her own words, and not who I had decided she was. I want that for myself. Not for anyone else. A way of not making myself up afterwards.
