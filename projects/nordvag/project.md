*Nordvåg Kart AS* makes nautical charts of small harbours on the west coast of Norway: the fishing harbours, guest marinas and ferry quays that the big official charts show at a scale too small to steer by. I worked there from 16 August 2021 to 31 May 2024, in an office on the top floor of an old warehouse across Vågen from Bryggen, with gulls on the window ledge and a view that never once got ordinary.

## The work

A harbour chart starts as a survey: a boat goes back and forth over the harbour with an echo sounder, and comes back with millions of depths. We turned those into charts a skipper could trust: soundings, depth contours, the rocks, the leading lights, the depth alongside every pontoon. I wrote the tools that checked the soundings, then the tools that drew them, then, increasingly, the tools that let everyone else draw them faster.

My team lead was Anna Solheim, who interviewed me over a video call in March 2021 with a cat walking across her keyboard, and who became, slowly, then all at once, a friend. Christmas 2021 was at her family's, with *pinnekjøtt* and her father's long speech, which I didn't understand a word of and enjoyed enormously.

## The release

In 2023 we moved every chart to a new system at once. It was the right decision and it went on for seven months, and [the last month of it](ref:event:release-month) was September. I said yes to everything. I don't think anyone asked me to.

## After

In October 2023 I went on sick leave, and stayed on it until the middle of January. In 2024 I came [back half time](ref:event:back-to-work), handed in [my notice](ref:event:notice) in April and left at the end of May with a card signed by everyone and a framed chart of the harbour below my old flat.

I don't regret the job. I learned the coast from the sea side, which almost nobody who lives here does, and I can still read a harbour from a chart before I've seen it. What I regret is the year I forgot how to stop. That part is mine to keep, and most of it is not written here.
