What a harbour chart has to tell you, roughly in the order a skipper looks:

1. how deep the water is, everywhere a boat could go
2. where the rocks are, especially the ones that only show at low water
3. where the lights are, and what they do at night: their colour, their rhythm, the sector they cover
4. where you can tie up, and how deep it is alongside
5. everything else: the fuel berth, the slipway, the speed limit, the cable you must not anchor on

Nordvåg made charts of the harbours too small for the national series to show in detail. Most of them had fewer than a hundred houses. Some had a shop. One had a shop, a church and a café that opened on Saturdays only if the owner's boat was in.

::media[A chart of somewhere else, but every symbol on it means what ours meant]{file="nautical-chart-3" layout="centered"}

My part was the software underneath: checking each survey for impossible depths, drawing the contours, placing the soundings so they didn't sit on top of each other. A sounding in the wrong place is a lie told to a boat. I took that seriously from the first week, maybe too seriously by the end.
