Biscuit has more paperwork than I do, and he has never once been grateful for it.

1. **The fifteenth of June.** The vet: microchip read (it beeped; he did not), rabies jab, the first form in green ink.
2. **Twenty-one days.** The waiting time after the jab before he is allowed to fly.
3. **July.** The crate, big enough to stand up and turn round in, left open in the living room as furniture. He slept in it. He was also sick in it.
4. **The thirtieth of July.** To the pet-transport people's cattery, yowling down four flights like a small ginger siren. The worst hour of the whole move.
5. **The thirteenth of August.** The health certificate, the last stamp, the flight: Edinburgh to Amsterdam to Bergen, in a heated hold.
6. **The thirteenth.** A cargo shed at the far end of Flesland, a man in a hi-vis vest checking his chip, and a crate with live-animal stickers on it and a very angry cat inside. *Han er sint*, said the man. He was.

He did not speak to me for an hour. Then he found the one patch of sun on the floor of the new flat and lay down in it as if he had paid for it.
