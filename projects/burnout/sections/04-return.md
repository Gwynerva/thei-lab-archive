[Back to work](ref:event:back-to-work) on the fifteenth of January, four hours a day, nine till one, with a plan signed by Anna, the occupational health nurse and me. A postcard of Whitby Abbey propped against my monitor that nobody would own up to.

The rule, which was Tove's: go home when you said you would. I broke it twice in the first week and then not again.

By March, six hours. By April I was nearly myself in the mornings, and in the afternoons I sat at my desk and looked at the harbour charts I had loved for two and a half years and understood, without any drama, that I was not going to stay. It was not the people. Anna was kind all the way through, and still is. It was that I could not go back to the place I had been when it happened and pretend it was a different place.

[I handed in my notice](ref:event:notice) on the twelfth of April. Anna said she had been expecting it since January, and then she said she would miss me, and then she asked if I would make her a map.

Last day the thirty-first of May. Cake, a card signed by everyone, a framed chart of the harbour at Sandviken that someone had redrawn by hand with a little cat on the quay. I walked home the long way.\
Seven days later I was in Porto, at the start of the Camino.
