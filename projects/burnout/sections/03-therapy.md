Tove: older than I expected, reading glasses on a cord, a room above a bakery in the centre that always smelled of cardamom. Two chairs, a plant, a box of tissues placed exactly where you would need it. Tuesdays, at ten in the morning until I went back to work and at four after that, from the twenty-fourth of October 2023 to the end of May 2024.

At [the first session](ref:event:first-therapy) I told her about the release, in order, like a report. Then about the kayak. Then about Nana, which I had not meant to. She said very little. At the end she said that I talked about everything I had made as if it were a debt I was still paying off.

Things she said that I wrote down afterwards, on the bus:

1. *You are allowed to be tired before you have finished.*
2. *What would you say to Clare, if she told you this?*
3. *Rest is not the reward for the work. It is part of the work.*
4. *Grief does not go away because you were busy when it came.*

The fourth one took until January. We spent most of December on Nana: the lockdown, the hospital I could not visit, the funeral of twenty people in a cold chapel that I watched on a laptop on the floor of the flat in Leith, the year after when I did everything except be sad. One Tuesday I cried for most of the hour, and Tove let me, and passed the box, and at the end said, *there she is*, and I did not know if she meant Nana or me.

What I did not expect: that it would be practical. Lists, of course. A plan for the return to work, written down. A rule about going home when I said I would. And a question to ask myself every evening, which I still ask: :hint[*what did you do today that was only for you?*]{note="most days in October the answer was nothing; by May it was usually the swim"}

Twenty-eight sessions. The last one, in May, she shook my hand and said I knew where she was. I do.
