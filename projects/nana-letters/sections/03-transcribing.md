I started in Leith in February 2021, in the lockdown, and finished in Bergen in November 2022, a week before the binding. Forty-one letters, about forty thousand words, typed with the letter on a book stand beside the laptop and a magnifying glass for his worst pages.

The rules I made on the first evening and kept to the end:

1. Every word as written, spelling and all. His *tommorow* stays; so does her *Wensday* (once, in 1958, which she would hate me to tell you).
2. Crossings-out as crossings-out: *I have ~~missed you~~ been thinking of you*, which tells you more than either.
3. Margins in square brackets, with what is drawn there: a ship, a rose bush, a map of the garden.
4. Nothing left out, in the typescript. What goes in the book is a different question.

::file[One letter, typed out exactly: Arthur, 1953, two days out of Bergen]{file="letter-1953" title="A letter of 1953"}

Some evenings I did half a letter. Some evenings in the Bergen rain I did two. In the move I did none at all for three months, and when I came back to it, in the autumn of 2021 in the flat in Sandviken, I found I had stopped in the middle of 1954 with Arthur in a storm off Newfoundland, and had to wait the whole summer to know he got through it, which I already knew, because I exist.

The quilt and the letters went together that year. Her stitches in the evenings, her handwriting at weekends. I finished the quilt on the anniversary, and the letters the next winter, and both of them felt less like finishing a project than like sitting with her.
