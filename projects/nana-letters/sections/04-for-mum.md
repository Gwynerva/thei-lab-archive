I bound it in Bergen in the first three weeks of December, in the evenings after work: the typescript printed on cream paper, sewn in sections on tapes, a case of grey board covered in dark blue cloth, and in a little window cut in the front, a square of Nana's fabric from the scraps left over from the quilt, faded pink with a sprig of something on it. On the spine, in gold letters I pressed one at a time with a borrowed type holder: *June & Arthur, 1951–1959*.

Christmas Eve in Whitby, the year Jonas met everybody. After tea, when Dad had gone to sleep in his chair and Jonas was being taught dominoes by Clare, I gave it to Mum.

She did not open it for a long time. She held it in her lap and put her hand flat on the square of fabric. Then she opened it at the first page, read the first letter, closed it, and said, *that's her*. Then she went into the kitchen and did the washing up for half an hour with the door shut.

On Boxing Day she read to us from it, the bit about the gale and the rhubarb, and Dad laughed in the right places and Jonas understood about half and laughed in the other places, and it was the best Christmas we had had since 2019.
