The 1957 letters are in Mum's book with pieces missing: the pages about Fremantle, and one note. They are in the typescript, and in the tin, and here.

In the spring of 1957 Arthur was offered a job ashore in Fremantle, at the port, with a house, and he wanted to take it. He wrote to June about the sun, the wages, the oranges, the beaches, the schools for the children they did not yet have. He wrote about it in every letter for four months. She did not answer that part of any of them. She wrote about the weather and her mother's legs.

Then, in August, one note from her, short, one side only, folded inside her Sunday letter, the only thing in the whole tin written in pencil:

> If you want Australia you can have it, Arthur.\
> You can't have it and me both. I'm not leaving the Bay.\
> J.

He did not write for five weeks. Then a letter from Port Said that begins, *Well then*, and talks about the weather, and ends, *Home when this contract's done, love, spring of fifty-nine, for good, if they'll have me at the yard.* And he came home, and they had Mum six years later, and he worked at the boatyard in Whitby until he retired, and as far as anyone knows they never talked about Australia again.

I did not put those pages in the book because Mum does not need to know that her father nearly went to Australia without her mother, or that her mother let him choose. Or perhaps because I did not want her to read that last line, *if they'll have me*, and think of him being sad. Or perhaps because I am not sure what I think, and I did not want to hand her something I had not understood.

Jonas says I should give them to her one day. I think he is right. Not yet.
