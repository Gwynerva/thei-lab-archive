---
title: The tin
summary: A biscuit tin with a stagecoach on the lid, and a hundred and eighty-six letters inside
---

A square tin, red and gold once, with a stagecoach in the snow on the lid and a dent in one corner where somebody dropped it on a stone floor. It smells of paper and, faintly, of the shortbread it was bought with, at Christmas 1960, Mum thinks.

Inside: nine bundles, one for each year from 1951 to 1959, each tied with a different colour of sewing thread, because Nana never threw thread away. The thinnest bundle is 1951, eleven letters, when they were courting and he was on a coaster in the North Sea. The fattest is 1956, thirty-one letters, the year he was away for ten months on a ship to Australia and back.

What else was in the tin:

- a photograph of a ship at a quay, with *Fremantle 56* on the back in his writing
- a pressed rowan leaf, brown and thin as tissue
- three stamps from Aden, loose
- a ribbon from her wedding dress
- a card in his writing that says only *for June, for the wonderful one*

The envelopes are the best part, almost. Stamps from Montevideo, Durban, Colombo, Port Said. Postmarks I had to look up. Her address on his, written in a hand that gets steadier every year as he learns to write at sea: Mrs J. Dunn, the lane, Robin Hood's Bay, Yorkshire, England, as if the postman might not know.

Mum had never opened it. She said it felt like reading over her parents' shoulders. I said that was exactly what it was, and that I would be careful, and I was.
