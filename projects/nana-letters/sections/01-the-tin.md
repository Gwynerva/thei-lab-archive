---
title: The tin
summary: A toffee tin with a stagecoach on the lid, and forty-one letters inside
---

A square tin, red and gold once, with a stagecoach in the snow on the lid and a dent in one corner where somebody dropped it on a stone floor. It smells of paper and, faintly, of the toffee it was bought with, at Christmas 1960, Mum thinks.

Inside: eight bundles, one for each year from 1952 to 1959, each tied with a different colour of sewing thread, because Nana never threw thread away. The thinnest bundle is 1959, two letters, the year he came home for good. The fattest is 1956, nine letters, the year he was away for ten months on a ship to Australia and back.

What else was in the tin:

- a photograph of a ship at a quay, with *Fremantle 56* on the back in his writing
- a pressed rowan leaf, brown and thin as tissue
- three stamps from Aden, loose
- a ribbon from her wedding dress
- a card in his writing that says only *for June, for the wonderful one*

The envelopes are the best part, almost. Stamps from Montevideo, Durban, Colombo, Port Said. Postmarks I had to look up. Her address on his, written in a hand that gets steadier every year as he learns to write at sea: Mrs J. Dunn, the lane, Robin Hood's Bay, Yorkshire, England, as if the postman might not know.

Mum had counted them and never read them. She said it felt like reading over her parents' shoulders. I said that was exactly what it was, and that I would be careful, and I was.
