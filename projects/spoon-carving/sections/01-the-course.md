Saturday. Bus to the end of the line, then a walk up a farm track in the drizzle to a red barn with smoke coming out of a pipe in the roof. Six of us round a woodstove: a retired nurse, two students, a father and his teenage son who didn't want to be there and by lunchtime didn't want to leave, and me.

New words, day one:

- *øks*: axe
- *kniv*: knife
- *skje*: spoon
- *spon*: shavings, which were everywhere, in my hair, in my boots, in my sandwiches
- *flis*: a splinter (learned the practical way)

> Let the wood tell you where the spoon is. Then argue with it.

That was the teacher, at the start. By the end of Saturday I had a lump of birch the rough shape of a spoon and two plasters.

Sunday: the knife work and the bowl. The hook knife is the hardest tool I've ever held. It curls the wood out of the bowl in thin rolls if you do it right, and tears it if you don't. I tore it. Then I didn't. At four o'clock I had a spoon: a lumpy, lopsided spoon that holds soup.

Rain on the walk back to the bus: heavy. Didn't care.
