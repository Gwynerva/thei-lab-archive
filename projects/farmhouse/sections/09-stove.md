The stove came from a farm sale in Kinsarvik in August: black cast iron, older than Ragnhild, with a little door that opens with a hook and a flat top that takes a kettle. It took four of us to get it up the steps of the *stova*. Then it stood there, cold, for two months, while the chimney waited for its liner and the liner waited for a man.

On the eighteenth of October the man had been, the sweep had signed the paper, and at half past six in the evening Jonas laid the fire the way his grandfather taught him, small sticks over birch bark, and handed me the matches. It caught at once. The iron ticked as it warmed. Biscuit came in from the hall and lay down in front of it as if he had been waiting his whole life.

::audio[The first fire, recorded on my phone on the floor in front of it]{file="sound-fire-1" title="First fire"}

We sat up until midnight feeding it. The *stova* went from eight degrees to twenty-one, by the thermometer on Sverre's old barometer. I wrote the date on the pantry door, under Ragnhild's snow dates, in pencil: *stove lit*.
