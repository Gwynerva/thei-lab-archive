The rule was one window at a time, so that the house was never open to more than one room's worth of weather. The first came out on the second of June; the last went back on the twenty-ninth of August. Thirteen weeks, fourteen windows, and more tins of linseed paint than I want to count.

## How

For each window:

1. Lift the casements off their hinges and carry them to the trestles in the barn.
2. Soften the old putty with a heat gun, gently, and dig it out. Lose your patience. Find it again.
3. Take out the panes and stack them in the order they came out. The glass is older than any of us, and there is no more like it but a few old panes in a box in the barn.
4. Scrape and sand the frames back to wood, cut out the rot, scarf in new wood where it must be, and feed everything with raw linseed oil.
5. Prime, set the glass back on a bed of putty, pin it, and putty again.
6. Three coats of linseed paint, white, a week apart.
7. Hang them back and swear at the hinges.

Every pane was caulked with linseed putty by hand, pressed in with my thumb and smoothed with an old table knife of Ragnhild's that is exactly the right shape. By the end my right thumb had a hard pad on it like a cellist's.

## Help

Mum and Dad came for a week in July. Dad was meant to be resting, and was found on the Wednesday on a ladder at the pantry window with Jonas holding the bottom, *only looking*. After that we gave him the trestles in the barn and a chair, and he did the putty on windows six to nine sitting down, as if they were boats that had come into the shop in a bad state: silently, with his glasses on his forehead and the radio on. Mum made jam. We had scaffolding up for the upstairs windows for two weeks, and on the last evening of the visit the four of us sat on the porch until half past eleven in a light that would not go, and Dad said it was a good house. That is the most he has said about any house.

Window fourteen, the eastern gable, went in last. [The first one out](ref:event:first-window "the pantry window, because nobody would see if we ruined it") seems a very long time ago.
