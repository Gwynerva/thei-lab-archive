A Saturday, grey, the fjord the colour of a slate roof. Jonas had had the key since August, in its envelope, and had not used it: he wanted us to open the door the first time together. Sigrid came too, in the front of the van, because she said she would know the turning. She did not.

What I wrote down that evening on the ferry back, in the order I noticed it:

1. The smell: cold wood, apples, a little damp.
2. The clock in the *stova*, stopped at twenty past four.
3. Fourteen windows. I counted twice.
4. The view from the landing, which made me sit down on the top stair.
5. The barn, which made Jonas go quiet the way he goes quiet in front of an old boat.
6. Nothing works. Not the sink, not the cooker, not the light in the pantry.

In the kitchen I said out loud what I had been thinking since the gate, that it was too much, and Sigrid put her hand on the wall and said the thing about houses needing people who stay, and went out to the trees. Driving down in the dark Jonas said "Well?" and I said "Well." In our house that means yes.
