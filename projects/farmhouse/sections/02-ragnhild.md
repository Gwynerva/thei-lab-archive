---
title: Who lived here
summary: Lars who built it, and Ragnhild, who was born in it and gave it to us.
---

The beam over the cellar door says *L. B. 1891*. Lars Brekke built the house and planted the first apple trees; his son built the woodshed and planted more; his granddaughter Ragnhild was born upstairs, behind the third window from the left, in 1934, the youngest of four, and lived here until the spring of 2024. Her brothers went to Bergen and to sea. She stayed. When her father died she ran the orchard on her own and sold the apples down at the quay, and for forty years she was the one in the valley who knew when the frost would come.

What she left in the house:

- the pantry door, with a calendar from 1987 on the back and, on the front, the day of the first snow pencilled for every winter from 1952 to 2023
- the clock in the *stova*, which started the moment I wound it
- her father's tools in the loft, oiled and wrapped in newspaper from 1972
- a drawer of string, sorted by thickness, and forty years of seed packets labelled in pencil
- a wedding photograph of two people nobody can name
- a jar of something in the cellar that Jonas will not let me open

She gave Jonas the key in August 2024, in an envelope, and asked for rent, not a sale; a price later, if we stay. I went to see her in Odda on the twenty-sixth of April 2025, with Jonas and a drawing of the house from the bottom of the orchard. She is ninety, small and very straight, and she asked about the roof before she asked about us.

> "Is the north end still leaking?" "Yes." "Good. Then it's still the same house."

She told us which trees are the oldest (the row of Gravensteins by the road, which her father planted the year she was confirmed), that the barn lost its roof one winter and was given a new one in 1931, that the well never once ran dry, not even in 1947, and that the house has a ghost who is only a draught from the cellar. She does not want to come back to visit. She said it would be like visiting a man who has married again. But she wants pictures, printed, on paper, and she gets them.

[That afternoon in Odda](ref:event:visiting-ragnhild "April 2025") is the best one I have had in this whole project.
