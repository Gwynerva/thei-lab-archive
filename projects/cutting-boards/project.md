The hall bookshelf left a pile of oak offcuts in the cupboard by the front door, and they sat there all autumn looking at me. In November 2018 I had no money for presents and a plane that wanted using, so everyone got a chopping board.

Five boards from the offcuts, each planed flat by hand, the edges rounded with a rasp and sandpaper, and finished with food-safe oil until the grain went the colour of honey. One of them, Callum's, is end grain, glued up from a chequerboard of little blocks, which took more evenings than the other four together and was worth every one.

It was a strange November. Nana fell on the 3rd, and for a few weeks the planing was the only thing I could do in the evenings that stopped me ringing Mum every hour. You can't worry and plane at the same time; the plane won't let you. By the time the boards were oiled she was home and complaining, and her board went to Robin Hood's Bay with me at Christmas, wrapped in a tea towel.

Where they are now:

- Mum's is in the kitchen in Whitby, stained purple by years of jam.
- Dad's is in the shop, where he cuts rope on it, which was not the plan.
- Clare and Dev's is in Leeds, "too nice to use", apparently.
- Nana's came back to me in 2021, with her sewing box and her quilt. I use it every day.
- Callum's has been in the kitchen of [his café](https://example.org/wee-oven) in Glasgow since the week before Christmas 2018, when I took it through on the train.
