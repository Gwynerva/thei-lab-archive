Someone stole my bike from the stair in June 2017, and for three years I didn't get another one. Partly money, partly stubbornness, mostly a feeling that if I bought a new bike I'd be agreeing with whoever took the old one that bikes are things you can just replace.

Then on the 2nd of May 2020, on my daily walk, there was a skip outside a tenement off Leith Walk where someone was clearing out a flat, and sticking out of the top of it was the back wheel of a red road bike.

## The bike

A Raleigh, made in 1984 according to the serial number stamped under the bottom bracket, which a forum of very kind men with a lot of time on their hands helped me decode. A steel frame in what was once bright red and is now a sort of faded strawberry, twelve gears, levers on the down tube, drop handlebars with the bar tape rotted to string. Rust on everything that could rust. Two flat tyres that had been flat since about 1995.

The frame was straight, though, and that's the only thing that matters. Everything else on a bike can be replaced. The frame is the bike.

## The work

I'd learned the basics fixing other people's bikes at [the Spoke Room](ref:project:spoke-room "Saturdays in a damp basement, 2016–2017"), so I knew what I was looking at. I didn't know how long it would take with everything shut. Parts came in the post, slowly, from three different shops, and the balcony became a workshop for two months: the bike upside down on newspaper, Biscuit supervising from the window, and a radio on anything that wasn't the news.

I kept the paint. I thought about respraying her and decided I liked the fade, and the scratches, and the sticker of a bike shop in Nottingham that has probably been closed for thirty years. Restoring something doesn't have to mean pretending it's new.

## Riding

[The first ride](ref:event:raleigh-first-ride#:~:text=ride) was on the 18th of July, along the Water of Leith path, out to Balerno and back. I rode her every week after that until I left Scotland, and then I sold her for twenty pounds to a student at the Spoke Room, who promised to keep the sticker. Somebody in Leith is riding a forty-year-old Raleigh, and it's partly mine.
