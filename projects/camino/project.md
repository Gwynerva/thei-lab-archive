In June 2024, a week after I left Nordvåg, I walked from Porto to Santiago de Compostela along the coast: the Camino Portugués, the coastal way, about 280 kilometres in fifteen days, with Ines for the first of them and alone, or not quite alone, for the rest.

I am not religious. I walked it because I had walked an old pilgrim road once before, [the Kumano Kodo](ref:section:japan/kumano-kodo "Japan, 2019"), and had come out of the forest feeling rinsed, and because I needed rinsing. Why I needed it is on [another page](ref:section:camino/why).

## Stages

Fifteen days, as I wrote them in the notebook, each with a little map in the margin.

### Portugal

1. **7 June.** Bergen to Porto. Ines at the airport with a sign.
2. **8 June.** Porto: the first stamp at the cathedral, and a day by the river.
3. **9 June.** Porto to Vila do Conde, the sea on our left, with Ines.
4. **10 June.** Vila do Conde to Esposende, with Ines.
5. **11 June.** Esposende to Viana do Castelo, in the rain.
6. **12 June.** Viana do Castelo to Caminha, at the mouth of the Minho.

### Galicia

7. **13 June.** Across the Minho in a small open boat, to A Guarda.
8. **14 June.** A Guarda to Baiona, the sea all the way, the best day.
9. **15 June.** Baiona to Vigo, the long grey edge of the city.
10. **16 June.** Vigo to Redondela.
11. **17 June.** Redondela to Pontevedra.
12. **18 June.** Pontevedra to Caldas de Reis, among vines.
13. **19 June.** Caldas de Reis to Padrón, past the stone poet.
14. **20 June.** A slow day, feet up in the morning, a few kilometres in the afternoon.
15. **21 June.** The last kilometres, and an hour lying on the square.

## What the days were like

Up at six, coffee standing at a bar, walk until two, wash the socks, find the stamp, eat the menu of the day, sleep in a room full of strangers breathing. Yellow arrows on everything: walls, kerbs, lamp posts, a rock in a field, the back of a road sign. After a week I stopped seeing them as directions and started seeing them as company.

> A good pair of boots is half the road. The other half is not complaining.

Nana said that about the Cleveland Way, which she never walked. I thought about it on every hill in Galicia, and complained anyway, and the boots were new, which Ines had told me was a mistake. [Feet](ref:section:camino/feet) has the details, for the strong-stomached.

::link[The Portuguese Way, the coastal variant, for anyone thinking of it]{url="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Way"}

The [arrival in Santiago](ref:event:camino-santiago#:~:text=Obradoiro "the square, and an hour on it") is in the events, with the Compostela that has my name in Latin. When I got home I went freelance, and I think the second thing came out of the first, somewhere around Vigo.
