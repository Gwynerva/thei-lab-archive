---
title: Feet
summary: The blister called Galway, and everything else I learned below the ankle.
---

The boots were new. Ines said that was a mistake, and Ines was right, and by Viana I had a blister on my left heel the shape of Ireland. I called it Galway, before I had met anyone from Galway, which made the introductions in Baiona awkward.

What I learned, for anyone setting out:

- **Boots**
  - wear them in for a month, not a fortnight
  - half a size bigger than you think; your feet swell by the third day
- **Socks**
  - two pairs, thin under thick, changed at lunch
  - wash them every evening, hang them on the outside of the pack to dry
- **Blisters**
  - stop at the first hot spot, not the second
  - the plasters with the gel in them, and a needle and a lighter, which Ines wielded on an albergue bunk at Viana with a great deal of commentary, and which I will not describe
- **Everything else**
  - feet in cold water at the end of every day, a fountain if there is one
  - nobody at an albergue minds what your feet look like; everybody's look like that

Galway healed by Pontevedra. The boots are fine now, of course. They have walked most of the thirty walks of the atlas since, and they are by the door at Øvre Lid, and they have a shell on a string tied to the laces that I am not taking off.
