Caldas to Padrón on the nineteenth, past the stone poet under his great tree. The twentieth slowly: feet in a basin in the morning, socks on a line, peppers fried in oil for lunch, and only a few kilometres in the afternoon, like a sensible person.

On the twenty-first the last kilometres, as slowly as I could walk them. Out before six, in the dark and the fog, through eucalyptus woods that smelled of cough sweets, then suburbs, then the old town, then the passage under the archbishop's palace with a piper playing in it, and then the square opened out all at once in front of me, the towers, the stairs, people lying on the stones with their packs for pillows. I lay down too. An hour.

::media[The cathedral at night, the last evening]{file="santiago-cathedral-4" layout="stretch"}

On the twenty-second, the pilgrims' Mass at noon, which I went to as a cartographer goes to a church, to see how it was made, and where the great censer swung down the nave on its rope and the whole cathedral went *oh* together. Octopus in the evening at a long table with people I had met on the road. The next morning the airport, and Bergen, and Biscuit at home, who had not noticed I was gone.
