The ferry across the Minho was not running, so a man with a small open boat took pilgrims across five at a time, and halfway over the clocks went forward an hour and all five of us looked at our phones at once. Then sand, pines, the first yellow arrow in Galicia, and Monte Santa Trega standing over everything.

Galicia is green and granite. Every garden has a *hórreo*, a stone granary on legs like a small chapel for maize. Vines grow on granite posts over the paths so that you walk through tunnels of leaves. It rained less than in Portugal and I minded less when it did.

- **A Guarda to Baiona.** The best day: the coast road, the sea all the way, rocks, gulls, and Baiona's harbour full of yachts at the end of it, where I met a woman from Galway at the albergue who became the person I walked beside, on and off, to the end.
- **Baiona to Vigo.** The long grey edge of a city, which nobody warns you about.
- **Vigo to Redondela.** Above the ría, on an old water channel through the woods.
- **Redondela to Pontevedra.** A medieval bridge, a lot of eucalyptus, and a town of granite squares.
- **Pontevedra to Caldas de Reis.** Vines, and a hot spring in the square of Caldas where you put your feet in the public fountain, and everybody does.

::media[The long hórreo at Lira]{file="horreo-2" layout="centered"}

Somewhere around Vigo I put something down that I had been carrying since the autumn, and did not go back for it. I do not know what to call it. I just noticed, on the way into Redondela, that my shoulders were lower.
