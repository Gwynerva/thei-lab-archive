I learned to bind books on twelve Tuesday evenings in the spring of 2017, in a basement in Stockbridge that smelled of wheat paste and wet coats. Fergus Lowe ran the course at his bindery with the radio on low and a tin of shortbread that was opened every week and offered never. By May I could sew a book. By December I had sewn about fifty and sold fourteen of them at a craft fair to people I'd never met, which still feels like a card trick.

## Why coptic

There are a hundred ways to hold pages together. The coptic stitch is the one that shows: the thread runs across the bare spine in little chains, the book opens flat, and you can see exactly how it was made. No glue, no cloth to hide behind. I liked that it was honest, and I liked even more that it needed almost nothing: a needle, waxed thread, an awl, a bone folder and a kitchen table.

[The first evening](ref:event:first-bindery-evening "where it started") I sewed a pamphlet so crooked it wouldn't close. Fergus held it up to the light, said "aye, well", and handed it back. That was the whole of his teaching method, and it worked.

### What I made that year

- pocket notebooks in A6, for lists and for maps in the margins
- a few in A5, for the cello notes I wasn't writing yet
- one square one on purpose, after two square ones by accident
- a sketchbook of heavy watercolour paper that never quite closed

The covers were board wrapped in whatever I could find: wallpaper samples, a gingham shirt of Callum's (with permission, eventually), old Ordnance Survey sheets from the box under the counter at the Spoke Room. A map cover is still my favourite. You open the book and Arthur's Seat is under your thumb. In the autumn of 2018 I started [marbling my own](ref:project:marbled-paper), which made the covers prettier and the bathroom much worse.

## Looking back

The notebooks taught me more than binding. They were the first thing I made that strangers paid for, and the first craft I kept at after the course ended. Fergus closed the bindery in 2022 and wrote to tell me so: a proper letter, folded in three, on paper he'd made himself. Seven years after the first Tuesday I used the same stitch for [a book for my niece](ref:project:isla-book), and my hands remembered it before I did.

If you want the long history, [the Wikipedia article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coptic_binding "Egypt, about the second century, and still going") is better than anything I could write. The short version is in the sections below.
