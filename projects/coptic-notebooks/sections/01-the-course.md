Tuesdays are bindery nights now! Half six till nine in Fergus Lowe's basement off the main street in Stockbridge, eight of us round one long table: two retired teachers, a man who rebinds old hymn books for fun, a girl from the art college, me, and three people I only ever knew by their aprons.

What we did, more or less:

1. a pamphlet stitch (mine wouldn't shut)
2. a single section with a soft cover
3. several sections sewn onto tapes
4. **coptic**, at last, in week four
5. a case binding, which I hated
6. everything again, but tidier

> Measure twice, fold once, and never trust a ruler you haven't met.

That's Fergus, every single week, usually while confiscating someone's blunt scalpel. He doesn't do praise. The best you get is "aye, well", which I got twice and have written down both times.

I missed the Tuesday of [fieldwork week](ref:event:whitby-fieldwork "four days on the cliffs at Whitby") and had to do the case binding at home as a punishment. Knackered most weeks, dissertation all day and paste and thread all evening, but it was easily the best three hours of the week.
