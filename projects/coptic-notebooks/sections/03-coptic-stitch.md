---
title: The coptic stitch
---

The coptic stitch sews each section of paper to the next with a chain of loops across the bare spine, and the first and last sections straight onto the covers. No glue, no spine, no cloth. The book opens completely flat, which is why it's perfect for maps and drawing, and you can see every stitch you made, which is why it's slightly terrifying.

How I do it, after twelve Tuesdays and a lot of unpicking:

:::list{counter="lower-alpha"}
1. Fold the sections, five sheets each, and press them under the bricks overnight.
2. Punch the stations with a template so every hole lines up: four for A6, six for A5.
3. Sew the first section to the front cover, going out and back through each hole.
4. Add the next section and link it to the one below with a loop at every station.
5. At the head and tail, drop down and catch the row below with a :hint[kettle stitch]{note="a small loop that locks each section to the one before it, top and bottom; nobody agrees why it's called that"}.
6. Sew the last section to the back cover, tie off inside, and say something rude to the needle.
:::

The tension is everything. Too tight and the spine bows; too loose and the book flops open like a tired dog. Fergus says it takes fifty books to get it right. I'm on about thirty.
