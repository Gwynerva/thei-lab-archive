---
title: The old sign
summary: Painted by Dad when he took the shop on, and faded by every winter since
---

I grew up under that sign. I learned to read on it, more or less: Mum says the first word I ever read out loud by myself was CHANDLERY, which I doubt, but I like it.

What it was like by 2023, when it came out of Mum's parcel in Bergen:

- the board: three planks, one split along the grain at the left end and screwed back together, twice
- the ground: once a bright orange, now the colour of weak tea
- the letters: black, half of them gone back to grey wood, about twenty centimetres high, in a tall, slightly squashed hand that is entirely Dad's
- the wobbles: the R in CHANDLERY leans back; the S in TESTWELL'S is fatter at the top than the bottom; the apostrophe is a little black comma
- the right-hand end: a gull had been standing on it for about thirty years, and it showed

::media[An old painted sign, the way the weather wears them]{file="sign-painting-1" layout="natural"}

Dad says he painted it "in a weekend, with a pot of yacht paint and a borrowed brush". It lasted three decades. That's the standard I was up against.
