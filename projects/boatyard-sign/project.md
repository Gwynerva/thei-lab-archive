Testwell's Chandlery has stood on the harbour at Whitby since before I was born: a narrow shop with rope coiled in the window, a workshop at the back that smells of tar and two-stroke, and over the door a long wooden sign that my dad painted himself, by hand, in black letters on orange, when he took the shop on. By 2023 the orange had gone pale and half the letters had gone back to grey wood. You could read it if you already knew what it said.

Dad was turning sixty in July. He doesn't want presents. He says so every year, and every year we ignore him.

## The plan

Mum took the sign down while Dad was at a heart check in Middlesbrough, told him it had gone to a sign-writer in Scarborough, and sent it to me in Bergen by courier. In Jonas's workshop I stripped it back to the wood and lettered it again by hand over five weeks of evenings, copying Dad's letters as closely as I could, wobbles and all, because it's his sign and his letters, and I wanted it to look like the old sign on the day it first went up, not like a new one.

The sign says, as it always has:

:::quote[Over the shop door, Whitby]{align="center"}
TESTWELL'S CHANDLERY\
ROPES · REPAIRS · CHARTS
:::

I flew over with it in a ski bag, wrapped in two duvets, which needed a long conversation at the desk about skiing in July, and Mum picked me up from the bus. On the afternoon of [his birthday](ref:event:dad-60 "8 July 2023"), while Dad was out, Mum and I put it back up over the door, and when he came round the corner it was there.

He looked at it for a long time and said, "Aye. That'll do." Later he said, "You've got the R wrong." I hadn't; his R is wrong, and I copied it. He knew. That's why he said it.

He goes out every morning now before the shop opens and stands under it for a minute, Mum says, and comes back in without a word.
