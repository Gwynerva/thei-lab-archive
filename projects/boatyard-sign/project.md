Testwell's Chandlery has stood on the harbour at Whitby since before I was born: a narrow shop with rope coiled in the window, a workshop at the back that smells of tar and two-stroke, and over the door a long wooden sign that my dad painted himself, by hand, in white and red on dark blue, when he took the shop on. By 2023 the blue had gone grey, the red had gone pink, and the white had mostly gone. You could read it if you already knew what it said.

Dad was turning sixty in July. He doesn't want presents. He says so every year, and every year we ignore him.

## The plan

Mum measured the old sign with a tape while Dad was out, photographed it in sections, and sent me everything. I built a new board in Bergen, in Jonas's workshop, from three planks of larch tongued and grooved together, the same size to the centimetre. Then I lettered it by hand over five weekends, copying Dad's letters as closely as I could, wobbles and all, because it's his sign and his letters, and I wanted it to look like the old sign on the day it first went up, not like a new one.

The sign says, as it always has:

:::quote[Over the shop door, Whitby]{align="center"}
TESTWELL'S CHANDLERY\
Rope · Repairs · Tide Tables
:::

I flew over with it wrapped in a duvet as checked luggage, which cost extra and needed a long conversation at the desk, and Clare picked me up at the airport. On the morning of [his birthday](ref:event:dad-60 "8 July 2023") Dad came down to open the shop and the new sign was already up, and the old one was leaning against the wall beside the door.

He looked at it for a long time. Then he said, "You've got the R wrong." I hadn't; his R is wrong, and I copied it. He knew. That's why he said it.

The old sign is in the workshop at the back now, on the wall, where he can see it from the bench.
