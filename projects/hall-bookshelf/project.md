For two years the books in the flat lived on the hall floor, in towers against the wall, sorted by height because that was the only way they'd stand. Priya's marine biology, Callum's cookbooks, my maps and the bindery's offcuts. Every time somebody came in with a bike the towers went over like dominoes.

In May 2018 I decided to build a bookshelf, by hand, with joints, because buying one would have been sensible and I'd had enough of sensible. Dougie at work, who makes furniture in his garage at weekends, lent me a tenon saw and two chisels and said, "Do the dovetails. Nobody does the dovetails any more." So I did the dovetails.

## What it is

A tall, narrow shelf, 180 centimetres by 60 by 22, five shelves, in oak. The carcase is held together at the corners with through dovetails, the shelves sit in housings, and there isn't a screw in it. It is not perfectly square. If you put a marble on the third shelf it rolls gently to the left and stops at the cookbooks, which feels right.

## Where it was made

On the back green, mostly, on a folding workbench, in the longest dry spell Edinburgh had had in years. When it rained I worked on the landing with the door open and the neighbours stepping over the sawhorse. The shavings went into the stair's compost bin, and the dust got into everything I owned.

It stood in the hall for three years, holding about four hundred books and, from 2019, a cat. When I left Leith in 2021 it went to Bergen in pieces with the boxes, and it has been put back together in every hall since, never quite fitting and always full.
