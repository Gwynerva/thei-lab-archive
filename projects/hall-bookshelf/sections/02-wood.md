Saturday at a timber yard out past Seafield, which smells like a church and a sawmill at once. I'd come for pine. I left with oak, because the man there laid a board of each on the trestle and said, "Pine's for shelves you'll throw away." Sold.

Six boards of European oak, :hint[quarter-sawn]{note="cut so the growth rings run up and down through the board, which keeps it flatter and shows the silver flecks"} where I could afford it and plain-sawn where I couldn't, planed all round at the yard because I don't own a thicknesser and never will.

Then two weeks of letting it sit in the hall to get used to the flat, as you're supposed to, while I tripped over it every morning. Marked out on 2 June, cut to length on the back green with a borrowed saw, and the ends planed square with a jack plane I'd bought second-hand from a man in Musselburgh who had forty of them.
