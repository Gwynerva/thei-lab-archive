Five weeks of evenings on the back green. Four corners, five tails on each, and every tail sawn, chopped and pared by hand.

How a dovetail goes, the way I do it now:

1. Mark the baseline all round with a cutting gauge.
2. Mark the tails with a bevel at one in seven, because oak is hard.
3. Saw on the waste side of the line. *Waste side.* Always.
4. Chop out the waste with a chisel, halfway from each face.
5. Use the tails to mark the pins on the other board, and repeat.
6. Tap together. Weep. Pare. Tap together again.

The first corner took nine evenings and has a gap you could post a letter through. The fourth took two and was tight enough that I had to clamp it to get it home. The heatwave arrived halfway through and the oak moved in the heat, so the back corner has a sliver of veneer glued into it that I'll show to nobody.
