Nana June taught me to knit when I was seven, on a pair of her old needles with the numbers worn off, sitting on the step of her cottage in Robin Hood's Bay. I knitted a scarf for a teddy bear and then nothing at all for twenty years.

In April 2020, two weeks into the first lockdown, I rang her and said I wanted to knit a sweater. She said, "About time," and posted me the needles. The same ones. They arrived wrapped in a page of the local paper with a note in her handwriting that said *Cast on loose, pull tight.*

## The sweater

It's a plain raglan in a raspberry-red wool, knitted in the round from the bottom up, with one cable running up the middle of the front. One cable, because a whole sweater of cables was too ambitious, and none at all seemed cowardly. I picked the wool from [a small shop in Leith](https://example.org/yarn-shop-leith "they posted wool all through lockdown") and had them send a sample card first, so I could see the red in daylight.

The body took all summer. I knitted on the balcony in the evenings, on video calls with the orchestra, on the sofa through every film I'd ever meant to watch. The sleeves took the autumn and two attempts, because the first one came out long enough for an orangutan.

## November

Nana went into hospital on the 10th of November. I was halfway up the second sleeve. She died on the 21st. I didn't knit for a fortnight, and then I did, because the sweater was the last thing she and I had been doing together, even with two hundred miles between us, and I couldn't leave it with one arm.

> Make do and mend.

She said it about everything. I finished the sweater on the 19th of December and wore it on Christmas Day in Leith, with Marta, instead of in Whitby, and rang Mum, and we didn't say much.

It's too warm for indoors in Norway and not warm enough for outdoors, so I wear it in between: on the ferry, in the barn, on the first cold evening of every autumn. The cuffs have gone thin. I'll mend them.

The needles are in the drawer with Nana's sewing things, where they belong.
