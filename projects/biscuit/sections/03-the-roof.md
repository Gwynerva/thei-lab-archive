The skylight in the little room in Leith, which by then was the map room and Biscuit's room, was supposed to stay shut. The woman from the rescue centre had said so, and for a year and a half it did. Then on a mild Saturday in October 2020, with the radiators on and the room stuffy, I opened it a crack to air the room, went to make a cup of tea, and came back to an empty map chest and a ginger tail disappearing upwards.

He spent the afternoon on the roof. Four floors up, on the slates, on top of a Leith tenement, sitting on the chimney stack with his paws tucked under him, watching gulls at eye level for the first time in his life and clearly having the best day he had ever had.

I spent the afternoon with my head and shoulders out of the skylight, saying his name in a voice that got higher and higher, and shaking the treat tin, which he ignored. Marta rang the landlord, who said to ring the fire brigade, who said, kindly, that cats come down. A neighbour across the back green watched the whole thing from her window with a mug of tea.

He came down at dusk, when the gulls went to bed. Walked down the slates as if he did it every day, dropped through the skylight on to the map chest, and asked for supper.
