Moving a cat to Norway turned out to be a project with its own checklist, so I made it one:

- [x] a microchip, which he already had, and which the vet found after some searching in his neck
- [x] a rabies jab on [the fifteenth of June](ref:event:biscuit-passport), and then the twenty-one days you have to wait after it before he can travel
- [x] his papers, a health certificate from the vet in the last days before he flew, which is what everyone still calls his passport, and he does not care either way
- [x] a crate of the right size, labelled for an international flight with LIVE ANIMAL and THIS WAY UP and his name in my handwriting
- [x] a pet transport company, because I had to go first, quarantine for ten days, and he could not come into an empty flat
- [ ] explain any of this to Biscuit

So he went to the pet transport people's cattery the day before I left, yowling like a small ginger siren all the way down the stair, which was the worst part of the whole move. He stayed there for a fortnight while I sat out my quarantine in Bergen, and they sent me a photograph of him every evening, always on the same blanket, always looking at the door.

On the thirteenth of August he flew, Edinburgh to Amsterdam to Bergen, in a heated hold. I waited at a cargo shed at the far end of the airport for two hours, and then they brought the crate out on a trolley and he was inside it, flat to the floor, ears back, furious. In the taxi he did not speak to me at all. In the flat he walked round every room with his belly low, sat in the empty bath for a while, and then lay down in the one patch of sun by the window, and from there, an hour later, watched his first Norwegian gull.
