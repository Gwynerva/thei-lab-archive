The first client paid on the twentieth of August 2024, for a map of a cycle route, and I printed the bank statement and stuck it on the fridge, which Jonas photographed and sent to his mother.

Since then, money comes in like weather. A good month is a commission, two prints, a course cohort and an invoice paid on time. A bad month is everything late at once and the roof leaking. The first winter I panicked every bad month. The second I made a rule: I keep the next three months in a separate account, and I do not count the rest.

Where it comes from now, roughly, in a year:

- the atlas, an advance in two parts and later, I hope, a little more
- the QGIS course, two terms a year, twelve students each
- the illustrated maps, prints and commissions, which are small and steady
- map work for clients, which is large and not steady at all

Where it goes: the rent to Ragnhild's family, which counts towards the house; the house itself, which eats money the way Biscuit eats butter; and tax, which in Norway as a sole trader you pay in advance, four times a year, on a guess, which suits me, because I like guessing about numbers.

Nine months of savings now, not eleven. The cards are still face down in the drawer. I look on the first of the month. Mostly.
