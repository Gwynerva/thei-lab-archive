A salary in kroner looks enormous until you buy something. A beer in Bergen, the first week, was a hundred and twenty kroner, and I did the sum in pounds and put it back. A deposit on the first flat was three months' rent, in a special account, which took most of what was left of *one day*.

But it was a good salary, and a fair one, and after the first year I was saving more than I ever had in Leith. The spreadsheet, rebuilt in kroner in January 2022, has a new column called WAFFLES, because that is what Jonas and I spent our money on that spring.

From August 2023 we shared the rent of the bigger flat, and Jonas, who earns less than I did, insisted on paying half. I insisted on paying more. We settled on half, and I paid for the food, and the spreadsheet made it come out about even.

Then the autumn of 2023. I was on sick leave for three months, and in Norway sick pay is the whole salary, from the first day, which I had not known and which I was deeply, Britishly uncomfortable about. I opened the spreadsheet every day that autumn, as if it might have changed. It had not. The money was fine. I was not.

When I handed in my notice in April 2024 I had eleven months of savings, if nothing came in and we ate a great many lentils.
