This page is private, and it is for me. I keep it because money is the one thing in my life I find hard to look at, and I have learned, at some cost, that the things I find hard to look at are the ones I should write down.

## The short history

- **Leith, 2017–2021.** The first salary, the first spreadsheet, a third of a rent, a bus pass I never used, and a savings account that grew by a little every month until it was enough to move a cat to Norway.
- **Bergen, 2021–2024.** A salary in kroner that looked enormous until I bought a beer. Rent alone, then shared. Savings that grew faster, until the autumn of 2023, when I learned that Norwegian sick pay is very good and Norwegian guilt about taking it is very bad.
- **Freelance, 2024 onwards.** Money that comes in like weather: some months a river, some months a dry bed. Eleven months of savings at the start, and a rule about not counting them.
- **The house, 2025 onwards.** Not a mortgage. Rent to Ragnhild's family that counts towards the price.

## How the house works

Because I will forget the details, and because it frightens me less written down:

1. We rent Øvre Lid from Ragnhild's family for five years, from April 2025, and every krone of the rent counts towards the price they asked.
2. After five years we choose: a mortgage for what is left, or five more years of renting on the same terms, after which it is ours.
3. The roof, the windows, the well and everything else are ours to mend from the first day. **That is the part that frightens me.**

We will most likely take the second way, and in 2035 the house will be ours, and I will be forty-three.

## What I have learned

That a spreadsheet does not make you richer but does make you calmer. That FOOD and OTHER FOOD are both necessary columns. That ~~saving~~ not spending is a skill and I am bad at it, except on myself. That Jonas paying more than half the rent in 2024 mattered to me more than it did to him, and that he was right that it did not matter. That I should look at the cards on the first of the month and not at three in the morning.
