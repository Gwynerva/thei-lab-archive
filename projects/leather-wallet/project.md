My old wallet died at Waverley in February 2017: the stitching went along the bottom and a fortnight of coins went down the platform. A sensible person buys a new one. I bought a bag of leather offcuts from a saddler online, a pair of blunt needles and a pot of beeswax, and spent three weeks making one.

It's a plain bifold, eleven centimetres by nine when it's closed, with two card pockets on each side and a coin pocket that was a mistake (more about that in the pattern). The leather is veg-tanned, about two millimetres thick, and it has gone from the colour of a digestive biscuit to the colour of strong tea. Nine years in a back pocket will do that.

## What it taught me

- that a stitch you can't see is still a stitch, and the inside matters
- that leather keeps every mark, including the ones you didn't mean
- that I like things that take a long time and then last a long time

The stitching is all saddle stitch: two needles on one thread, passing through the same hole from opposite sides, so that if one stitch breaks the rest still hold. Nana approved. She says *a stitch in time is still a stitch*, which isn't quite the proverb but is better.

I stitched one side of the card pockets on [the weekend in Copenhagen](ref:event:copenhagen) with Priya, in a café by Nyhavn, while she read and pretended not to know me. The wallet came home finished on 4 March. The coin pocket has been re-stitched twice since. Everything else is as I made it.
