We had not been away since the house. A year and a half of windows and roofs and a kitchen with no kitchen in it, and in the summer of 2025 Jonas looked at me across the scaffold boards and said we should go somewhere with no houses at all. I said I knew just the place. It turned out to be full of houses, very old ones, each with a stone tower beside it, and he has not let me forget it.

Svaneti is the high valley on the southern side of the Caucasus, in the north-west corner of Georgia, where the Enguri runs down from the glaciers. The classic walk goes from Mestia to Ushguli in four days, sleeping in village guesthouses, and that is what we did, in the second week of September, when the hay was in and the first snow had not yet come down to the passes.

## The shape of it

1. [Tbilisi](ref:section:svaneti/tbilisi), two days, for the baths and the bread and a hymn I still hum
2. The night train to Zugdidi, then a minibus up the gorge to [Mestia](ref:section:svaneti/mestia)
3. [Four days on foot](ref:section:svaneti/trek "Zhabeshi, Adishi, Iprari, Ushguli"): Zhabeshi, Adishi, Iprari, Ushguli
4. A jeep back, one more glacier, and home to the fjord

## Why on foot

Because it is how I learn a place. A map tells you where things are; walking tells you how far apart they are, which is a different fact and the one that matters. By the end of the second day I could have drawn the valley from memory, and on the plane home I did, on the back of the boarding passes.

And because of the knee. Five years after it went on Arthur's Seat I still think of it before any long walk, and I wanted to give it something hard to do and see what it said. It said nothing at all for sixty kilometres, which is the best thing a knee can say.

:::quote[The woman who kept the guesthouse in Ushguli, through Jonas's translation app]{align="center"}
Eight months of winter. The road closes. I would not live anywhere else.
:::

## The food

We ate :hint[khachapuri]{note="bread baked round a great deal of cheese; in Svaneti there is also kubdari, the same idea with spiced meat inside"} every day for ten days and I would do it again tomorrow. Jonas came home a convert to tarragon lemonade and a Svan salt with garlic and herbs in it, of which he bought half a kilo in Mestia and now puts on everything, including, once, porridge.

## The map

I drew it, of course: the four days on one sheet, the villages as little towers, the river crossing as a horse with a question mark over it. It is pinned above my desk beside the atlas proofs, and it is the only map in the room with a horse on it.
