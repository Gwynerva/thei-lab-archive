We landed after midnight on the Friday, by way of Istanbul and a sandwich I would rather not remember, and a taxi driver with a picture of his grandchildren on the dashboard took us up a hill to a guesthouse with a vine over the door. The air at one in the morning was warmer than a Hardanger afternoon.

Saturday was [the whole day in the old town](ref:event:tbilisi "the baths, the clock tower, the hymn"): Narikala, the baths, the leaning clock tower, the bread, and the singing in the church, which I have listened to every week since.

On Sunday we walked up into the botanical garden in the gorge behind the fortress, because Jonas wanted to see what grows where it never freezes. A walnut tree with its first yellow leaves. Figs nobody was picking. A waterfall in the middle of a city.

::media[Under the walnut tree in the botanical garden, the first yellow leaves]{file="video-tbilisi" layout="natural"}

Then the market for socks, plasters and :hint[churchkhela]{note="walnuts on a string, dipped again and again in thickened grape juice until they look like candles; the best trail food I know"}, a last supper of dumplings, and the night train to Zugdidi from the big station a little before ten. Four bunks, clean sheets, a conductor who brought tea in glasses. I slept like a stone and woke up in a different country: green, wet, the mountains somewhere behind the cloud.
