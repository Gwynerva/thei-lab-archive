### Tuesday: Mestia to Zhabeshi

Up out of the town past the little airstrip, up again through birch and long grass to a ridge where the whole range stood up in front of us at once: Tetnuldi white and sharp to the east, the glaciers, the Mulkhra valley green below. Lunch on the ridge, cheese and bread and an apple from home that had come all the way from our orchard. Down to Zhabeshi in the evening, legs shaking, and a guesthouse with a dog who slept across our door.

### Wednesday: Zhabeshi to Adishi

A short day over a shoulder of hill and down to Adishi, a village of a few houses, three towers and a great many cows. In the afternoon we walked up to look at the glacier and [the river below it](ref:event:adishi-crossing), which we would have to cross in the morning.

### Thursday: Adishi to Iprari

The crossing first. Men with horses wait on the near bank in the early morning, and for a few :hint[lari]{note="Georgian money; the horseman asked fifteen each and was worth it"} they carry you over the grey braided channels of meltwater, which come up to the horse's belly and would have come up to mine. Then the long climb to the Chkhutnieri pass, about two thousand seven hundred metres, the glacier behind us smaller every time I turned round. At the top, a meadow and a wooden cross and everything we had walked on one side and everything we had not on the other. Down through birch into Iprari, where the guesthouse mother gave us bean soup, looked at my face and gave me a second bowl.

### Friday: Iprari to Ushguli

The easiest day and the one I will remember longest: a dirt road along the river, the valley widening, and the towers of Ushguli coming up out of it one by one. [Ushguli](ref:event:ushguli) is its own page.

What it came to, in my notebook, the evening we arrived: four days, four villages, sixty kilometres, about four thousand metres up and nearly as many down, one river, no blisters. The knee held.
