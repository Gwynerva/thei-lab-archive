I decided on Ben Lomond, in 2017, that Ben Nevis would be the last, whatever the number was when I got there. It turned out to be thirty-four, and four weeks before I left Scotland.

Callum drove up from Glasgow with rolls from The Wee Oven, Marta came because one mountain a year is plenty and this was it, and we went up the tourist path from the visitor centre at seven, because it was the end and I did not need to prove anything. Zigzags, the halfway lochan, the long stony switchbacks where people stop talking, and the fog closing in at a thousand metres and never letting us out. The knee, strapped, held. I counted the steep bits in Norwegian.

At the top you could see about thirty metres: the old observatory walls, the trig point, cairns coming out of the grey one at a time, a lot of people in a lot of colours, and somebody's dog in a jumper.

I touched the trig point (rule one, near enough). I ate the tablet (rule five). I did not cry, which surprised me, and then on the way down, at the lochan, I did.
