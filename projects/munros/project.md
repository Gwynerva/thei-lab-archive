A Munro is a Scottish mountain over three thousand feet, 914 metres and a bit. There are 282 of them, according to the list that counts, and people collect them the way other people collect stamps, which is why it is called bagging. I climbed thirty-four between May 2017 and July 2021, which is about twelve per cent, which is not very many, which I am fine with.

I started with a Hogmanay resolution I did not take seriously: climb a Munro. Priya took it seriously for me, and in May we got the bus to Balloch and went up Ben Lomond with half of Glasgow. At the top, in a wind that nearly took my hat, she said "one down, 281 to go", and I laughed, and that was that.

::media[Ben Lomond at sunset, the first one]{file="ben-lomond-1" layout="stretch"}

## How it went

- [2017](ref:section:munros/2017): six, in a student's boots, mostly with Priya.
- [2018](ref:section:munros/2018): seven more, with Glencoe and the Cairngorms and Schiehallion for number ten.
- [2019](ref:section:munros/2019): nine, the best year, Ben Lawers for number twenty.
- [2020](ref:section:munros/2020): five in a strange summer after lockdown, and then the knee.
- 2021: seven, the last of them in the spring before I left, and [Ben Nevis](ref:section:munros/ben-nevis), saved for last on purpose.

The [whole list](ref:section:munros/the-list) is there with dates, and the [rules](ref:section:munros/the-rules) I set myself on the second one.

## What I took from it

A habit of looking at contour lines and seeing a day. A packing list I still use in Norway (it is [a file](ref:section:munros/the-list "at the bottom") if you want it). A respect for weather that I did not have before, which Bergen later finished off. Friends I had made in the rain. And the knowledge that I am slow and steady and get there, which is a useful thing to know about yourself before you are thirty.

I never cared about the number the way some people do. I cared about the days.

### The lochan below Ben Lawers

One day I will write the whole of the evening Marta and I came off Ben Lawers late, in the last of the light, and stopped by a little lochan to fill the bottles. Marta saw something in the water before I did.

:::spoiler
Marta, who does not believe in anything she cannot measure, swears to this day it was a kelpie: a horse made of water, standing in the shallows, waiting for somebody foolish enough to climb on its back. It was a pony that had got through a fence. Probably. We walked down very fast all the same.
:::
