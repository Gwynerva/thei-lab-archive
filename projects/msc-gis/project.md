I arrived in Edinburgh in September 2016 with two suitcases, a bike with a buckled back wheel and a vague idea that maps could be a job. A year later I had a master's degree, an offer from a mapping company in Leith and a habit of seeing everything as layers. This is the year in between.

## What it was

A one-year MSc at the University of Edinburgh: two semesters of taught modules, then a summer on a dissertation. About thirty of us, from eleven countries, in a computer lab that smelled of radiator dust and instant coffee. The modules, roughly:

- spatial data and databases, the one that made everything else make sense
- remote sensing, which was mostly arguing with satellites
- programming for GIS, in Python, my first real code
- cartography and visualisation, where I was happiest
- fieldwork and surveying, including a weekend in the Cairngorms with a receiver on a pole

The dissertation was a question about my own coast: [how fast is Whitby falling into the sea?](ref:project:whitby-erosion "the dissertation, from the question to the numbers")

## Looking back

I was twenty-four, living in a top-floor flat off Leith Walk with Priya and Callum, fixing bikes on Saturdays at [the Spoke Room](ref:project:spoke-room) for tea and a little money, and writing a diary every night in a notebook (it is all in [the diary](/diary/) now). I said "brilliant" about everything. I was knackered most of the time and did not notice.

What I kept from that year is less the software, which has all changed since, than a way of asking things. Where is it? How do you know? How sure are you? Dr Grieve, my supervisor, wrote the last of those three in the margin of every draft I gave her, in green ink, until I started asking it myself.\
I still hear it in green.

The degree came with distinction, which I mention only because Dad made me promise to. The :hint[gown]{note="Hired, and made for somebody a foot taller. The hood slid off backwards every time I bowed."} did not fit, and that is in all the photos too.
