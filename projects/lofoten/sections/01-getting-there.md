Bergen to Bodø in the evening on a small plane that bounced, the lights of little places strung out along the coast below. Priya came in from Oslo an hour later, from Plymouth the long way round, and we hugged in arrivals until a man asked whether we needed help. More than two years since we had stood in the same room. A night in a hotel by the harbour with the wind banging something metal until morning.

In the morning the car ferry across the Vestfjord to Moskenes, three and a half hours, the hire car down in the hold and us up on deck in a long slow swell, watching the islands come up out of the sea: a wall of white teeth on the horizon, getting taller and taller. Priya was fine. I was fine until the café opened and I smelled the sausages.

We drove off at Moskenes and along to our cabin at Sakrisøy, a bridge away from Reine, in the dark, very slowly, the road white, not knowing what was round us, only that it was big and close.
