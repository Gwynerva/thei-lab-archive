We opened the curtains on the first morning and there it was: every peak white to the waterline, red cabins on black rock with snow on their roofs, the water a green-grey I had no name for, and not a breath of wind.

We walked out to Hamnøy and back along the road with the bridges, and my fingers stopped working after twenty minutes. The second day the fog came in and took the mountains away, and we drove to the end of the road at Å and stood at the end of Norway looking at nothing.

Both nights we stood in car parks staring at the forecast and then at the sky and then at the forecast, and both nights it was cloud. Priya kept saying that the sky did not owe us anything. I kept checking.
