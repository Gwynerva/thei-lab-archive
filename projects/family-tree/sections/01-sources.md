---
title: Sources
summary: Where every name on the tree came from
---

Where the names came from, roughly in order of how much I trust them:

- **Parish registers**: baptisms, marriages and burials, the backbone of everything before 1837. Copies of the registers for Fylingdales, the parish the Bay is in, and for Whitby, read on microfilm in the county archive and later as scans online.
- **Civil registration**: births, marriages and deaths from 1837, ordered as certificates when I could not work something out any other way. Seven certificates at about eleven pounds each, which I would rather not total.
- **The censuses**: every ten years from 1841, who was in each house on one night, with their ages, often wrong, and their jobs, sometimes wonderful (*fisherman's widow, keeps a mangle*).
- **Gravestones** in the churchyards at Fylingdales and Whitby, photographed on the Christmas walk with Dad in 2019, some of them too worn to read.
- **The family Bible**, in Mum's spare room, with births written in the front in five different hands from 1849.
- **Nana**, on the phone, every Sunday from November to March, who knew everybody's nickname and nobody's date of birth.

:::spoiler
The marriage of 1868 that joins us to the Dunns of Staithes took place in March. The first child was baptised in June. Nana, when I told her, said *well, it was a cold winter*, and changed the subject to the price of fish.
:::

What I learned about sources: that every one of them lies a little, and that the truth is usually where three of them nearly agree.
