---
title: Size and ox gall
summary: The two liquids that make it work, and the one that smells
---

Marbling is two liquids having an argument, and you referee.

:::list{counter="upper-roman"}
1. **The size.** Water thickened with :hint[carrageenan]{note="a gum from a red seaweed, Irish moss or carrageen, the same stuff that sets some puddings"}, whisked the night before and left so the bubbles rise. Too thin and the colours sink; too thick and they won't spread. It should run off a spoon like thin cream.
2. **The colour.** Paint thinned until it floats. Acrylics work; gouache works better and costs more.
3. **The gall.** A drop or two of ox gall in each jar breaks the surface tension, so the paint spreads instead of sitting in a bead. More gall, wider drops. It smells like a farmyard on a hot day, and you get used to it, mostly.
:::

The paper needs treating as well: a wipe of alum solution the night before, so the colour bites. Forget the alum and the whole pattern slides off into the sink when you rinse it, which I did with the best sheet I'd ever made.
