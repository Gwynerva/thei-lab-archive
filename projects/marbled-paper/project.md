Marbling is the most magic thing I've ever done with paper, and I've done a fair amount. You thicken a tray of water with seaweed, drop colour on it so it floats, comb the colour into patterns, lay a sheet of paper on top, and lift it off with the pattern on it. Every sheet is a one-off. You can't make the same one twice even if you try, and I've tried.

I learned at [a Saturday workshop](ref:event:marbling-workshop "20 October 2018, in a print studio in the Old Town") in October 2018, with seven strangers in aprons and a teacher who talked about colour the way other people talk about weather. Then I spent the winter doing it at home, which is a terrible idea in a flat with one bathroom and two flatmates.

## Why I wanted it

For the covers. The [coptic notebooks](ref:project:coptic-notebooks) had been wearing maps and wallpaper for a year, and I wanted endpapers like the ones in old books: feathers and stones and peacock eyes, in colours that don't exist anywhere else. Bought marbled paper costs a fortune. Made marbled paper costs a bathroom.

## What it took

- a tray from a builders' merchant, the kind for mixing plaster
- carrageen powder for the size, and a whisk
- a bottle of ox gall, which is exactly what it sounds like
- paints thinned with water, in twelve jam jars
- combs made from strips of card and dressmaking pins
- alum, to prepare the paper the night before
- a great deal of newspaper and a very understanding Marta

By January 2019 I had a box of about sixty good sheets and a bath with a faint blue ring that no cleaner could shift. The good sheets went onto notebooks for two years. The ring is probably still there.

[Paper marbling on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_marbling) has the history. The sections below have my mistakes.
