---
title: The pedal point
summary: The passage near the end, where the open D rings under everything
---

Near the end of the prelude there is a passage where the music climbs and climbs and the open D string keeps sounding under every figure, the same note again and again, the bow crossing back to it after each new note on the string above. It is called a pedal point, after the organ, where the organist holds a note down with a foot and the harmony goes on above it. On the cello there is no foot. There is only your bow arm, going back and forth across two strings faster than you can think.

It took me from January to April 2020. In the notebook:

- January: could not play it slowly.
- February: could play it slowly, could not play it in time.
- March: lockdown. Played it for an hour a day. The neighbours across the back green must have known it as well as I did.
- April: could play it in time, could not make it sound like music.

What made it music, in the end, was something Mrs Ogilvie said over the laptop: that the D is not a note you play but a floor you stand on, and the tune is everything else. After that I stopped attacking the D, and let it ring, and the passage started to climb by itself, the way it is supposed to, towards the top and the pause and the long slide home.
