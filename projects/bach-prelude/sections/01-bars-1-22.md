The first twenty-two bars are the part everyone knows: the open G, the rocking up through the chord and back, again and again with one note changing each time, so that the harmony moves underneath while the shape stays the same. It looks easy on the page. It is not.

What took the time, in my practice notes:

- **String crossings.** The bow goes back and forth across three strings in every beat, and the elbow has to lead it, not the wrist. My wrist did not agree for six weeks.
- **Evenness.** Sixteen notes in a bar, and every one the same length and the same weight. My fourth finger was always late and always quiet.
- **The open G.** Ringing under everything if you let it. Mrs Ogilvie said to let it.

Practice log, 14 November: twenty minutes on bars nine and ten, the same two bars, over and over, like a dog with a stick. Twenty-five minutes on scales. Biscuit asleep on the case.

By Christmas I had twenty-two pencil ticks and could play the first part at seventy-two on a good night. Mrs Ogilvie listened to it in the last lesson before the holidays, said "better", and gave me two more bars for January.
