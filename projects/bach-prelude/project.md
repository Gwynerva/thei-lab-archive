Everyone knows it, even people who think they don't. It is in adverts for cars and banks and in every film where somebody is about to look out of a window and think about their life. Two and a half minutes of G major, a rocking figure of broken chords that goes round and round and climbs and falls and comes home. Every cellist learns it. Most of us learn it too early, the way I did at fifteen, and play the notes without ever playing the piece.

In October 2019 I decided to learn it properly. I was twenty-seven, two years back on the cello, and Mrs Ogilvie had just said, in passing, that I was ready for it, which from her is a medal.

## How

Slowly. That was the whole method, and it was hers:

1. Two bars a week, no more, however impatient.
2. A metronome at sixty, then sixty-six, then seventy-two, and no faster until the end of the winter.
3. A pencil tick in the score for every bar I could play three times running without a mistake.
4. No playing it through from the beginning "to see how it's going". Ever.

I broke the last rule about once a week. It always went badly.

## When

Through the autumn of 2019, alongside a pottery class and a half marathon's worth of tired legs; then through the winter; then through a lockdown, when there was nothing else to do in the evenings and the prelude was there, waiting, in its tick-marked score on the music stand by the window.

The [first twenty-two bars](ref:section:bach-prelude/bars-1-22) took the autumn. [The long passage near the end](ref:section:bach-prelude/the-pedal) took the spring. And on the twentieth of June 2020, I [recorded it](ref:section:bach-prelude/recorded) on my phone, propped on a pile of books, in fourteen takes.

## Afterwards

It has stayed. I play it most weeks, not for practice but the way you might walk round a garden you made. In April 2026 I played it in a church in Sandviken, at [the recital](ref:section:cello/recital "with the two minuets after it"), to eighty people, and it was the same piece I had ticked through bar by bar in Leith, and I was not the same player, and both of those things were true at once.
