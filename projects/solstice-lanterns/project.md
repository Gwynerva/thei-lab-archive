In Bergen in December the sun rises at a quarter to ten and sets at a quarter to four, when it bothers to come out at all, which in December 2022 it mostly didn't. On the shortest day there are under six hours of daylight. Up north they have none, and I'm told this is supposed to make us feel better.

At home the shortest day was always Nana's. She put a candle in the window at Robin Hood's Bay "to show the sun the way back". In my first Bergen winter I forgot. In my second I wanted to do something properly, and properly, for me, means paper.

## Twelve lanterns

I made twelve paper lanterns at the kitchen table over ten evenings, folded from heavy tracing paper and red mulberry paper, each with a battery tea light inside, because Jonas, a man who builds wooden boats, has a professional fear of open flames. On the solstice we carried them up the hill behind the house with the neighbours, hung them in the birches at the viewpoint above Sandviken, and stood there in the dark at five in the afternoon looking down at the city lights and the black water.

Somebody brought *gløgg* in a flask. Somebody else brought a speaker and played something with bells in it. It was cold and wet, and one of the lanterns blew away down the hill and was found the next morning by a dog walker, who hung it on a fence.

There's more about [the walk itself](ref:event:solstice-walk "Lanterns on the solstice") with the rest of that December. We've hung the same twelve every year since, mended, with a candle in the window as well, for Nana.
