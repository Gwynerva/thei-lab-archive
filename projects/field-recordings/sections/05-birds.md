In March 2024 I was back at work for four hours a day and back on my feet for most of the rest, and one evening Jonas came in from the boat workshop and said the oystercatchers were back on the shore at Herdla. I had heard them from the kayak the summer before without really listening. I went the next Saturday, with new batteries.

:hint[*Tjeld*]{note="the oystercatcher, Norway's national bird, voted for by people who have clearly never tried to sleep near one"}, in Norwegian. Black and white with a bill like a carrot, and a call like a squeaky gate being opened by an excited child. They were everywhere on the stones, running and piping, and I sat on a rock with the recorder in my lap for an hour and got all of it, and when I played it back in the car I laughed out loud.

::audio[Oystercatchers on the shore at Herdla]{file="sound-oystercatcher" title="Tjeld"}

After that, birds all spring:

- a curlew on the marsh at Herdla at dusk, the loneliest sound I know, and the one I play when I miss the moors above Whitby
- a robin singing at the top of the rowan behind the house, sounding exactly like a British robin, which is somehow comforting
- a blackbird at four in the morning in May, outside the bedroom window in Sandviken, when the sky is already light and the city is not

::audio[The blackbird at four in the morning, May]{file="sound-blackbird" title="Blackbird"}

Getting up at four to record a blackbird is, Jonas says, the most hopeful thing he has ever watched anyone do. It was. That is why I did it.
