Bergen is a city you hear before you see. You come out of the station in the dark and it is already there: rain on the glass roof, gulls, a bell somewhere up the hill, the hiss of a bus on wet cobbles, and under all of it the harbour, a slow knocking of boats against the quay. I lived there for three and a half years, and for two of them I walked about with a small recorder in my coat pocket and tried to keep some of it.

It began in October 2022, in [the twenty-one days of rain](ref:event:rain-streak "October 2022, the rain that would not stop"). The week before it began I had bought a recorder, a small grey thing with two microphones crossed on top, and on the sixth day I stood it on the windowsill under the roof and pressed the red button. That was [the first recording](ref:event:rain-recorded), and the rest followed the seasons: the rain all autumn, the church bells in the winter, the harbour in the spring, and after a long silence, the birds.

## What I learned to listen to

The first thing a recorder teaches you is how loud everything is. Fridges. Ventilation. Your own breath, your own coat, your own stomach. You stand in a beautiful place with your headphones on and hear, mostly, a lorry three streets away. The second thing it teaches you is to stand still, for a long time, and wait for the lorry to go.

I learned that rain sounds different on every surface: on the tin roof of the bike shed, on an umbrella, on the leaves of the rowan in the back yard, on the fjord itself, where it makes a sound like frying. I learned that the bells of the four churches I could hear from the flat are not in tune with each other and that this is the best thing about them. I learned that gulls have about nine different things to say and that most of them are complaints.

## Why I stopped

I stopped in the summer of 2023, when I stopped most things, and I did not pick the recorder up again until the March after, when the oystercatchers came back to the shore at Herdla and I wanted to keep their noise more than I wanted to stay in bed. The birds are the last section. They are the recordings I am proudest of, because they were the hardest to go out and get.

## Where they are

All of them are on [a map](ref:section:field-recordings/sound-map), a dot for each place I stood, built out of the old code from [the Munro map](ref:project:munro-map). The weather for each day came from [the Norwegian Meteorological Institute](https://www.met.no), because I could not help myself.
