A day in the county archive in Northallerton, booked three weeks ahead, the train via Darlington with a flask and two pencils (ink is not allowed, and they check).

What I'd ordered up, and what I got:

- [x] an estate plan of a farm above Robin Hood's Bay, about 1780, with every field named and coloured
- [x] the tithe map of the parish, enormous, on a table of its own
- [x] a coastguard's sketch of the cliff at Saltwick, about 1870, found by luck in a box of letters
- [ ] the harbour commissioners' plans of the piers (on loan to an exhibition, of course)

The estate plan was the best thing I saw all year. Somebody had painted the sea along the bottom edge in three strokes of blue, and each field had a name in copperplate: *Long Close*, *Far Wandale*, *Cliff Field*. Cliff Field is not there any more. It is on the beach.

::card[The day itself, from the diary's side]{to="ref:event:county-archive#:~:text=archive"}

I copied the field names into my notebook and phoned Nana from the platform at Northallerton to read them to her. She knew two of them.
