title: Where to look
---

A list for anyone who wants to do the same for their own bit of coast, in the order I'd look now:

:::list{counter="upper-roman"}
1. The national libraries' online map collections. The Ordnance Survey's old six-inch and 25-inch sheets for the whole of England are on [the National Library of Scotland's map site](https://maps.nls.uk "free, and the best thing on the internet"), which is a sentence I still find funny.
2. Wikimedia Commons, for scanned charts and county maps in the public domain.
3. Your county archive, for estate plans, enclosure maps and anything hand-drawn. Book ahead. Bring pencils.
4. The local museum's library. Whitby's has a reading room upstairs with a radiator that ticks and a view of the park.
5. Old books about the town. A history of Whitby printed in 1817 had a list of field names that matched an estate plan exactly.
:::

::link[The online collection of old Ordnance Survey maps I used most]{url="https://maps.nls.uk"}

And one rule I learned the slow way: write down where every image came from *the moment you save it*. I spent two evenings in December finding the source of a chart I'd downloaded in August.
