---
title: The 1853 survey
summary: The first six-inch sheet of Whitby's coast, and everything on it.
---

The first large-scale Ordnance Survey of the coast around Whitby was surveyed around 1850 and published as six-inch sheets in the years after. For the dissertation I'd used it as a line: the cliff top in 1853. This time I read the whole sheet, slowly, with a magnifying glass, the way you'd read a letter from someone you'd never met.

## What is on it

- the alum works at Saltwick, already in ruins and labelled so
- every field boundary, hedge and stile on the cliff path
- the coastguard station, with a flagstaff drawn as a tiny cross
- a footpath to the beach at Saltwick that doesn't exist any more because the cliff it ran down doesn't
- the spot heights, in feet, written in a hand so small and regular it looks printed

## Nana's lane

Further down the coast the sheet shows Robin Hood's Bay house by house. Nana's lane is there, climbing from the dock, and her cottage, and in the garden a dot that might be the rowan tree. It can't be the same tree, she said; rowans don't live that long. But somebody planted a tree in that garden in 1850 and somebody kept planting one, and that seemed to her about the same thing.

## How wrong it is

By up to twelve metres, in places, which I measured in 2017 and forgave in 2019. The surveyors were drawing a crumbling coast from the top in bad weather with chains and a theodolite. I've tried it with a GPS receiver and a drone and been wrong by eight centimetres, and I had it easy.
