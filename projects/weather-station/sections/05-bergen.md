The station had never seen weather like it. In Leith the bucket clicked a few hundred times a month. In Sandviken it clicked a few hundred times a *day*.

My downstairs neighbour, Mr Haldorsen, a retired ship's mate, took a professional interest. Every morning he tapped the barometer on his hall wall and compared it with my phone, and every morning he was closer to the real forecast than I was.

::media[Mr Haldorsen's barometer, tapped every morning at seven]{file="barometer-1" layout="natural"}

He taught me Bergen weather the way Dad taught me tides: by pointing.

> When you can see Askøy, it is going to rain. When you can't see Askøy, it is raining.

Things the station learned that year:

- that a rain gauge on a window ledge measures mostly the wall above it
- *sludd*: sleet, the worst word in the language
- that in Bergen the pressure falls on a Friday afternoon out of spite
- that 230 days with rain in a year is not a joke, even when you are making one
