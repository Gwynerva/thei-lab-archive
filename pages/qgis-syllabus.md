For students of [my online QGIS course](ref:project:qgis-course). If you've been sent this link, you're on the list, and welcome.

Eight Monday evenings, seven till nine, Norwegian time, online. Each evening has a short talk, a long exercise and a little homework. By the last one, everyone has made one finished map of a place they care about, and printed it out.

## The eight evenings

1. **Where things are.** What GIS is; installing QGIS; layers, and what a layer is.
   - homework: choose your place
2. **Getting data in.** Shapefiles, GeoPackages, spreadsheets with coordinates in them, OpenStreetMap, national map services.
   - homework: find three datasets for your place
3. **Making it look like something.** Symbols, colours, categories; why rainbow colour ramps are a crime. The week people either fall for the software or give up on it.
4. **Asking a map questions.** Selections, filters, buffers, joins.
   - homework: one question, answered with a map
5. **Projections.** Why the map looks squashed, and what to do about it. It used to be week two, and broke people; now everyone already has a map they care about when we get here.
6. **Height and words.** Elevation models, hillshade and contours, then labels, fonts and halos. The evening everyone goes quiet, and my favourite.
7. **The layout.** Title, legend, scale bar, north arrow, credits.
   - homework: a first draft of your map, sent to me by Friday
8. **Show and tell.** Everyone shows their map for five minutes. I bring cake, in spirit.

## What you need

- a computer less than about eight years old, and a mouse (a trackpad will make you cry in week four)
- QGIS, the long-term release; I'll send the link
- two hours a week for the homework
- a place you know well enough to argue with the data about it

## What you don't need

- ~~Any experience.~~ Most people start with none.
- Money for software. It's free.
- To be good at drawing. I'll teach you to cheat.
