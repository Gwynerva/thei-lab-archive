People ask what I draw with, so here is all of it, more or less, as of the autumn of 2026. Nothing here is sponsored. Most of it was bought second-hand, or borrowed and never given back.

## Drawing

- A fountain pen with a fine nib, filled with waterproof black ink. It's the only pen I own that I'd go back into a burning house for.
- Fineliners, 0.1 and 0.3, for contours and the lettering on small maps.
- A pocket watercolour box of twelve pans, plus two tubes I buy separately: a grey-blue for fjords and a green that is almost exactly a Hardanger hillside in June.
- Hot-pressed cotton paper, 300 g/m², because cheaper paper cockles the moment it sees a wet brush.
- A soft pencil, a hard pencil, and a kneaded eraser that has become, over the years, the shape of a small grey dog.

## Maps and code

- [QGIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/QGIS "free, open, and the thing I teach"), every day, for everything that starts life as data.
- Python, for the jobs QGIS can't do or I'm too impatient to click through. [tideline](ref:project:tideline) taught me most of what I know about it.
- A plain text editor, and a terminal I keep open at the bottom of the screen like a kitchen drawer.
- ~~A drawing tablet.~~ I bought one in 2019 and used it for three weeks. Paper won.

## The plotter

[The pen plotter](ref:project:pen-plotter) I built in Leith in the spring of 2021: two stepper motors, a servo and a fountain pen held in a wooden clothes peg. It came to Norway in a shoebox and sits on the shelf above the desk. These days it draws faint pencil contours on watercolour paper, hundreds of them, and I ink over them by hand, because my hand would wobble by the fortieth.

## The cello

A 1970s German factory cello with a scratch on the front shaped like Norway (it is, whatever Jonas says), a bow rehaired in Bergen, and rosin, of which I have far too much because people keep giving it to me for Christmas.
