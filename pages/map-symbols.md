Every map I draw by hand has a legend in the corner, and the same handful of private symbols turns up on all of them, from the margins of my school French book to [the atlas](ref:project:walk-atlas). Some are proper cartography. Most aren't. Here they are, so you can read my maps without having to ask.

:::list{counter="upper-roman"}
1. **A dot in a circle**: a bench with a view. Filled in, the bench is dry, or has a roof.
2. **A small cup**: somewhere you can get a cup of tea. With steam lines: the tea is good.
3. **A triangle**: a summit, as on any map. A triangle with a flag: a summit I've stood on.
4. **A wavy line under a path**: wet feet, guaranteed.
5. **Two short strokes across a path**: a gate. Three strokes: a gate that sticks.
6. **A star**: come back here.
7. **A tiny cat**: a cat lives here and will want to be stroked. Biscuit is on every map of Sandviken and every map of Øvre Lid.
8. **A crossed-out umbrella**: shelter from the rain. Rare on maps of Bergen; I've looked.
:::

## Where they come from

The proper ones I learned at Edinburgh, from the maps of the [Ordnance Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordnance_Survey) and from an American government handbook for land surveyors, which has a symbol for almost everything, including several kinds of marsh.

## The rule

A symbol has to be drawable in under two seconds with a 0.1 pen, and readable by someone who has never met me. The tiny cat fails the second test. It stays.
