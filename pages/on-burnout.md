I wrote this in October 2024, a year after I stopped. I've shared the link with a few people who asked, and with one or two who didn't ask but, I thought, needed it. It isn't advice. It's what happened.

## The autumn

In September 2023 we shipped [the release](ref:event:release-month) we'd been building at Nordvåg for most of a year. I'd been working evenings since the spring and weekends since June, and telling myself, and Jonas, that it would all calm down in October.

On Monday 9 October I sat down at my desk, opened the laptop, and couldn't read the screen. Not couldn't see it: couldn't *read* it. The words were there, and they didn't go in. I closed the laptop and went to the doctor, who said one word, *utbrent*, burnt out, and signed me off for a month, and then another, and then another. I didn't go back until January.

## What stopping is like

People imagine it's a sort of holiday. It isn't. For the first weeks I couldn't choose between two kinds of bread in the shop, and once I left the basket on the floor and walked out without either. I couldn't read more than a page. I couldn't draw, which frightened me more than anything, because drawing is what I do when everything else is too much.

What I could do, it turned out, was very small things, slowly. Make tea. Feed the cat. Walk to the end of the road and back. Sit on the steps and watch the weather come in over Askøy. I'd always thought of small things as what you do on the way to the big ones. That autumn they were all there was, and they were enough to be going on with.

## What helped

Not in order of importance, because I couldn't tell you the order:

- Sleep, as much as my body wanted, without arguing with it.
- Someone to talk to every week, who was paid to listen and was good at it.
- [Clay](ref:event:pottery-again "a Tuesday evening class, from November 2023"). My hands had something to do that didn't matter. A bowl that collapses on the wheel is a bowl that collapses, and nobody's harbour chart is wrong because of it.
- [Cold water](ref:event:first-winter-swim). On 3 December a woman from the pottery class took me to the sea at Nordnes. Seven degrees, in sleet. For about forty seconds I couldn't think about anything at all, and then I laughed out loud for the first time in weeks.
- Walking without a destination, and without a map, which for me is like walking without shoes.
- Jonas, who never once asked me when I'd be better.

## Going back, and going

I went back [half time in January](ref:event:back-to-work), to a desk by the window and a manager who had read up on it. It was kind, and it was careful, and by March I knew. In April I [handed in my notice](ref:event:notice). Not because Nordvåg was a bad place to work. Because I'd learned I couldn't do that kind of work in that kind of way, and I'd rather do less of something I could keep on doing.

## What I'd say

If you're reading this because you're where I was: stop sooner than you think you need to. The work will survive without you for a while. It turns out it survives without you altogether, which is humbling, and then a relief.

:::quote[Nana June]{align="center"}
A stitch in time is still a stitch.
:::

She meant darning. I've decided she meant this too: it's never too late to stop and mend something, but it's a great deal easier early.

:::private
What I haven't told anyone but Jonas. In the first week of November 2023 I sat on the bathroom floor for most of an afternoon and couldn't get up: not because anything hurt, but because I couldn't think of a reason good enough to stand. Biscuit came and sat on my feet. Then Jonas came home. That's all. I'm writing it down here so that I never pretend, later, that it was smaller than it was.
