When I started [the kayak](<ref:project:kayak#h:Plans> "plans, plywood and far too much reading") in Jonas's workshop in February 2022, I didn't know what half the words meant, in either language. Jonas taught me most of them in Norwegian, so I learned them twice. The underlined ones have a longer story behind them.

## Building

- **Strongback**: the long, dead-straight beam a hull is built on. Ours was two planed spruce boards on trestles, and :hint[it was the hardest part]{note="Everything after it is only as straight as the strongback. Jonas spent an afternoon with a string line and wouldn't let me start until it was right to the millimetre."}.
- **Stitch and glue**: the method. You cut the panels, drill holes along their edges, :hint[stitch them together]{note="With about three hundred twists of 1 mm copper wire. My fingertips were green for a month."}, glue the seams from inside, then pull the wire out.
- **Okoume**: the plywood, a light reddish African hardwood in 4 mm sheets. It must be *marine* plywood, with waterproof glue, or it comes apart like a wet biscuit.
- **Scarf joint**: a long, sloping joint that glues two sheets end to end into one, because nobody sells plywood five metres long.
- **Fillet**: a curved bead of thickened epoxy along the inside of a seam, :hint[smoothed with a spoon]{note="A plastic teaspoon, rounded end, dipped in methylated spirit. The best tool in the workshop, and it cost nothing."}.
- **Chine**: the line where the bottom panel meets the side.
- **Sheer**: the top edge of the hull, where it meets the deck. The **sheer clamp** is the strip of pine glued along it to give the deck something to hold on to.
- **Glassing**: covering the hull in fibreglass cloth and epoxy. The cloth goes clear as it's wetted out, and the wood comes up underneath like a photograph developing.
- **Bulkhead**: a wall inside the hull that makes a watertight compartment, so the boat floats even when the cockpit is full of water.
- **Coaming**: the raised rim round the cockpit that the spray deck fits over. Ours is three laminated rings.
- **Fairing**: filling and sanding until the hull is smooth to the hand. You do this until you hate it, and then for another week.
- **Varnish**: seven coats. [The seventh](ref:event:seventh-coat) went on on 22 April 2023, and I cried a bit, for no reason at all.

## On the water

- **Rocker**: how much the keel curves up at the ends. More rocker, the boat turns easily; less, and it runs straight.
- **Skeg**: a small fin at the stern that you drop to stop the wind turning you round.
- **Edging**: tilting the kayak on purpose, with your hips, to turn it.
- **Brace**: slapping the paddle flat on the water to stop yourself going over. :hint[A low brace]{note="Jonas had me practise it fifty times in the harbour at Sandviken one evening in May, with the whole quay watching."} saves you; a high brace saves you dramatically.
- **Wet exit**: getting out upside down. Pull the loop on the spray deck, push the boat away, come up spluttering.
- **Ferry glide**: crossing a current at an angle so it carries you sideways to where you meant to go. Like most good ideas, it feels like cheating.

## In Norwegian

*kajakk* (kayak), *åre* (paddle), *spant* (frame), *kjøl* (keel), *skrog* (hull), *lakk* (varnish). And *båtbygger*, boatbuilder, which is what Jonas is and what I am, as he puts it, *nesten*: almost.
