I started learning Norwegian [on a laptop in Leith in January 2021](ref:event:first-norwegian-lesson), passed [the Bergenstest](ref:event:bergenstest) two and a half years later, and still learn a new word most weeks, usually from Sigrid, usually in a tone of mild surprise that I didn't know it already. These are the ones I love. Some have no English word at all; some have one, but the Norwegian is better.

## Everyday

- *kos*: cosiness, but also something you *do*, and a whole attitude to November. *Koselig* is the adjective, and it can describe a candle, a person or a Tuesday.
- *dugnad*: when everyone in the building turns up on a Saturday morning to wash the stairwell and rake the leaves, unpaid and without complaint, followed by waffles. :hint[Nobody organises a dugnad.]{note="Somebody does, obviously. You just never find out who."}
- *takk for sist*: "thanks for last time", said the next time you meet, about whatever you did last time, even if it was nothing.
- *uff*: the sound of something going slightly wrong. *Uff da* if it's worse.
- *hyttetur*: a trip to the cabin, which is less a holiday than a national duty.
- *forelsket*: the first, giddy part of being in love. I learned it in February 2022 and used it far too often.

## Food

- *pålegg*: anything that goes on a slice of bread: cheese, ham, jam, fish paste from a tube. There's no English word because there's no English need.
- *matpakke*: the packed lunch. Slices of bread with *pålegg*, stacked, with a sheet of paper between each, eaten at your desk at half past eleven. A whole culture in a lunchbox.
- *brunost*: brown cheese, sweet and fudgy, cut with a cheese slicer, which Norway also invented. Biscuit would sell me for it.
- *skillingsbolle*: the Bergen cinnamon bun, the size of a saucer.
- *pinnekjøtt*: salted, dried lamb ribs, steamed over birch sticks at Christmas. [I first ate it at Anna's](ref:event:christmas-2021) and was converted on the spot.
- *utepils*: the first beer drunk outside in spring. It's a food word, but really it's a weather word: see below.

## Weather

- *regnvær*: rain weather. In Bergen, then: weather.
- *pøsregn*: pouring rain, as from a bucket, which is what *å pøse* means.
- *kjipt*: rotten, grey, miserable. Sverre's word for most of January.
- *yr*: fine drizzle, the kind that doesn't fall so much as hang about. It's also the name of [the forecast everyone checks](https://www.met.no "the Norwegian Meteorological Institute, who make it"), which tells you something.
- *sludd*: sleet. There is a great deal of it on the seventh mountain, mostly when I'm on it.
- *fjellvett*: mountain sense, and the nine rules of it that every Norwegian child learns. Turn back in time; there's no shame in turning back.

:::quote[Every Norwegian, at least once a winter]{align="center"}
*Det finnes ikke dårlig vær, bare dårlige klær.* There's no bad weather, only bad clothes.
:::

To which Sverre, who was a ship's mate for forty years, always added: *men det finnes dårlige båter*. But there are bad boats.
