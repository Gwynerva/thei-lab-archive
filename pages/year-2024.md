*Written on New Year's Eve, 2024, at the kitchen table in Sandviken, after the hundredth swim, with Mum and Dad safely home in Whitby after their first Norwegian Christmas.*

This was the year I turned round. It began with me going back to a job I'd stopped being able to do, and it ended with an agreement for a house we can't quite afford, in a place we'd been to twice. In between, I walked to Santiago. I'll try to put it in order. The days themselves are in [the diary](/diary/).

## Going back

I went back to Nordvåg [half time](ref:event:back-to-work) on 15 January. Everyone was kind. By March I knew it wasn't going to work, and not because of them, and on 12 April I [handed in my notice](ref:event:notice). I've written about that autumn and what came after [on another page](ref:page:on-burnout), for anyone who needs it.

## Isla

[Isla Mistry was born](ref:event:isla-born) on 2 March, in Leeds, to Clare and Dev. I [met her](ref:event:meeting-isla) at Easter: four weeks old, suspicious of the ceiling light, with Nana's chin. She fell asleep on my chest and I didn't move for an hour and forty minutes. Dad timed it. That summer I started [a picture book for her](ref:project:isla-book), about a cat who lives on a map, and posted it in December, bound by hand.

## The Camino

On 2 June I walked [all seven of Bergen's mountains in one day](ref:event:seven-in-a-day), as a sort of test, and passed. Five days later I was in Porto with Ines, my old colleague from Forthline, at the start of [the Camino Portugués](ref:project:camino): the coastal way, 280 km in fifteen days, along the Atlantic and then inland through Galicia to Santiago. Ines walked the first days with me and then went home, and I went on alone, which was what I'd wanted, and harder than I'd wanted.

The stages are [in the project](<ref:project:camino#h:Stages> "all fifteen of them, with the blisters"). I reached [Santiago](ref:event:camino-santiago) on 21 June and lay on the stones of the square for an hour and couldn't think of a single thing I had to do next. That was the point.

## Freelance

I [registered the business](ref:event:freelance-registered) on 1 July and had [a client](ref:event:first-client) by the 15th: a trail map for a tourist board, which paid for August. In September [the first print sold](ref:event:first-print-sold), a map of Sandviken, to a stranger, for 450 kroner. In October the first cohort of [my QGIS course](ref:project:qgis-course) logged on, twelve faces in little squares. In December I sold forty-one prints at [the Christmas market](ref:event:christmas-market-2024) and couldn't feel my feet.

## The house

On 9 November Jonas and I took the ferry to Utne to look at [a house](ref:event:house-viewing) that his great-aunt Ragnhild had lived in all her life and couldn't manage any more: Øvre Lid, built in 1891, fourteen windows, forty apple trees, a barn with swallows' nests in it, and a view down the fjord to the end of the world. It needed everything. We knew before we'd reached the gate.

:::quote[Sigrid, on the step, after the viewing]{align="center"}
The house is old and the roof is bad and you are both mad. Good. It needs mad people.
:::

## Three good things

1. Isla's chin.
2. The square in Santiago.
3. [The hundredth swim](ref:event:hundredth-swim), this morning at nine, at Nordnes, four degrees, a pink sky over Løvstakken, and nobody counting but me.
