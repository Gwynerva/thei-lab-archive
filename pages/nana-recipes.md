Nana June made these in her kitchen at Robin Hood's Bay, up the steep lane, with the back door open and the radio on and me on a stool "helping". She never weighed anything the same way twice. These are as near as I can get, from her cards, from Mum and from memory. Only family has the link to this page, so if you're reading it, hello: you're family, or near enough.

## Rowan jelly

From the rowan at the bottom of her garden, every September; in 2017 [she let me do the stirring](ref:event:rowan-jelly "and told me off for it the whole time").

::file[Her card, copied out, spelling and all]{file="rowan-jelly" title="Rowan jelly, as Nana wrote it"}

You need:

- 1.4 kg rowan berries, stalks off (Nana: 3 lb)
- 900 g crab apples or sharp cooking apples, chopped, cores and all
- 1.1 litres water
- sugar, 450 g to every 600 ml of juice

1. Simmer the berries and apples in the water until soft, about half an hour.
2. Tip it all into a jelly bag and let it drip overnight.
3. Measure the juice, warm it, add the sugar and stir until it's gone.
4. Boil hard. After about ten minutes, test: a drop on a cold saucer should wrinkle when you push it.
5. Pot it hot, into hot jars.

> Do not squeeze the bag. It goes cloudy, and then everybody knows.

Good with lamb. Better with cheese. Best on toast at eleven at night after a long drive north.

## Parkin

For Bonfire Night, always, and it has to be made days ahead or it isn't parkin, it's cake.

- 225 g medium oatmeal, 110 g self-raising flour, a teaspoon of ground ginger, a pinch of salt
- 110 g each of butter, black treacle, golden syrup and dark brown sugar
- an egg and two tablespoons of milk

Warm the butter, treacle, syrup and sugar together until they're one thing. Stir them into the dry things, then the egg and the milk. Pour into a lined square tin and bake at 140 °C for about an hour and a quarter. Then into a tin with a lid, and **leave it alone for three days**. It goes sticky. That's the point.

## Curd tart

The Yorkshire one, with currants and nutmeg, which Nana made for Easter and for funerals.

- shortcrust for a 20 cm tin
- 225 g curd cheese (Nana made her own, from milk and a lemon)
- 50 g butter, 50 g sugar, two eggs, a handful of currants
- nutmeg, grated thickly over the top

Blind-bake the case. Beat the butter and sugar, then the eggs, then the curd and the currants. Fill, cover with nutmeg, and bake at 180 °C for about thirty-five minutes, until just set and barely brown.

:::spoiler
Her secret, which she told me when I was eleven and made me swear not to tell: a spoonful of the rowan jelly, melted, brushed over the pastry before the filling goes in. I'm only telling family.
:::
