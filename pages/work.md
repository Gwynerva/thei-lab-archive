The short version: I'm a cartographer. I make maps for people who need them, teach people to make their own, and draw a few for the pleasure of it. The longer version, newest first.

## Since 2024: freelance

Since July 2024 I've worked for myself, first from the flat in Sandviken and now from [Øvre Lid](ref:project:farmhouse), at a desk in the room with the best window. [Going freelance](ref:project:freelance) turned out to mean three kinds of work:

:::list{counter="lower-roman"}
1. **Maps for clients**: trail maps for a tourist board, a flood-risk layer for a small municipality, an illustrated map for the walls of a hotel.
2. **Teaching**: [an online course in QGIS](ref:project:qgis-course), eight evenings a term, twelve students at a time, since October 2024.
3. **Drawing**: [illustrated map prints](ref:page:prints), and *Thirty Walks Around the Fjord*, [a walking atlas of Hardanger](ref:project:walk-atlas) for Forlaget Fjellstue in Bergen, at the printer as I write.
:::

## 2021–2024: Nordvåg Kart, Bergen

Nautical charts of small harbours on the west coast of Norway, in a team of nine. I was the one from abroad who asked why everything was done the way it was done, which was useful for about a year and tiring for everyone after that. I worked on the data model behind [the big release](ref:section:nordvag/big-release) of 2023, and on the harbour surveys before it. The rest of that story is [in the project](ref:project:nordvag "including the part where I stopped"); I left in May 2024, in good order, with cake.

## 2017–2021: Forthline Mapping, Leith

Four years at a twelve-person company in Leith making surface-water flood maps for Scottish councils. I came in as a junior who knew a bit of Python and left as the developer who looked after the tools everyone else used. [My first flood map](ref:event:first-flood-map) went out in June 2018, and I still check, whenever it rains hard on the Forth, whether it was right.

## 2016–2017: the degree

An [MSc in Geographic Information Science](ref:project:msc-gis) at the University of Edinburgh, with distinction, and a dissertation on [how fast the cliffs at Saltwick Bay are retreating](ref:project:whitby-erosion): about 0.3 m a year on average, and nine metres in one bad winter.

## What I'm good at

- turning a heap of data into a map somebody can read in ten seconds
- explaining that map to the people who will make decisions with it
- Python, QGIS, PostGIS, and the usual tools around them
- lettering by hand, in English and in Norwegian
- standing in the rain long enough to check that the ground agrees with the map
