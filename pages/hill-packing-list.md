Written on a card in Leith in 2017, after I climbed [Ben Lomond](ref:event:ben-lomond) in jeans; revised after [a weekend of wilderness first aid](ref:event:first-aid-weekend) in 2019; and translated, more or less, when I moved to the country of [fjellvett](<ref:page:norwegian-words#h:Weather> "the nine rules of mountain sense"). Print it. Tick it at the door. Then look at the sky, and tick it again.

## The bag

- [ ] Waterproof jacket **and** trousers
  - [ ] even if the forecast says dry
  - [ ] *especially* if the forecast says dry
- [ ] A warm layer, a hat and gloves, even in July
- [ ] Map and compass
  - [ ] in a bag that keeps the map dry
  - [ ] and the knowledge of how to use them: the phone is not a map
- [ ] Head torch, spare batteries
- [ ] A litre and a half of water, and a flask of tea
- [ ] Food
  - [ ] cheese, oatcakes and a bar of tablet, in Scotland
  - [ ] a [*matpakke*](ref:page:norwegian-words#:~:text=matpakke "the packed lunch, a whole culture") and a chocolate bar with a wafer in it, in Norway
- [ ] First aid kit, with blister plasters
- [ ] Midge net, May to September in Scotland; in Norway, the *knott* in July are just as bad
- [ ] Bothy bag
- [ ] Phone, charged, in a pocket that zips

## Before you go

:::list{counter="lower-alpha"}
1. Tell somebody where you're going and when you'll be back.
2. Check the forecast, then the mountain forecast, which is a different thing.
3. Decide now where you'll turn round, and keep to it.
:::

The third I only learned properly in Norway. The first two I learned the hard way, which is how everybody learns them.
