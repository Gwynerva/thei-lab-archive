summary: Letters to Nana June, written after she died on 21 November 2020; nobody reads them but me.

Nana wrote letters all her life and kept every one she was sent. After she died I found I couldn't stop writing to her. So I haven't.

### 3 December 2020

Dear Nana,

Twenty people in a cold chapel, which was all they'd let in, and half the Bay standing outside in the lane in the rain with their hoods up. Mum read the poem you chose years ago and kept in the sewing box. Dad carried you, with Dev and two men from the lifeboat, and didn't say anything, which is what Dad is for. I read what you said at your ninetieth, and everyone laughed, the way you'd have wanted, and then I couldn't do the rest. I'd come down on the train from Edinburgh with a sandwich I didn't eat.

I'm going to finish your quilt. I don't know how yet. I'll learn.

Love,\
Mira

### 21 November 2021

Dear Nana,

[It's finished.](ref:event:quilt-finished) A year to the day, in a flat in Bergen, in the rain, with Biscuit asleep on the corner I was trying to stitch. The label on the back has your name and mine, and the years, in your blue thread. It's on my bed now. It's far too warm for the flat and I don't care.

I live in Norway now, by the way. You always said I'd end up across the North Sea, like Grandad. I still don't know how you knew.

Love,\
Mira

### 20 May 2023

Dear Nana,

There's a boat with your name on it. Five metres of plywood and seven coats of varnish, and she's called [*Nana June*](ref:event:kayak-launch), in your sort of letters, on both sides of the bow. She floated. She went straight. Jonas cried, which he denies.

> Never trust a calm sea.

I didn't. It was flat as a plate all afternoon and I kept looking over my shoulder for the wave. You'd have laughed at me.

Love,\
Mira

### 2 March 2024

Dear Nana,

Clare had a little girl this morning. Isla. Seven pounds and an ounce. I haven't met her yet; I've had a rotten winter and I've only just gone back to work. I'll fly over at the end of the month.

She's got your chin. Clare sent a photo and it was the first thing I saw.

### 22 August 2026

Dear Nana,

I got married today, in an orchard in Hardanger, under apple trees older than you'd be, to a man who builds boats. You'd have liked him. You'd have told him off about his boots and then given him the biggest slice.

I wore your brooch, the silver one with the anchor. Mum gave it to me at breakfast without saying a word. And your thimble, on a ribbon round my wrist, under my sleeve, where only I knew.

:::quote[Grandad, writing to you from a ship, 14 March 1953]{align="center"}
It will come back bright, it always does.
:::

He was writing about a star. I've decided he meant everything.

Love, always,\
Mira
