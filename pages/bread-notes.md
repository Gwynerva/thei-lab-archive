These notes started in the spring of 2020, when everyone in the world began a sourdough starter at once and I was no exception. Mine was called [Hamish](ref:event:hamish-born "born on 29 March 2020, in a jam jar"). He stayed behind in Scotland when I moved, which was right for both of us, and in Bergen I started again with rye from the corner shop and never gave the new one a name. These notes are for both of them.

## Hamish's diet

- Feed him once a day if he lives on the counter, once a week if he lives in the fridge.
- 50 g starter, 50 g flour, 50 g water: half rye, half strong white, if you can.
- He's ready when he has doubled and smells of yoghurt and apples, not nail varnish. Nail varnish means hungry.
- He did not like the windowsill in Leith in July. His successor does not like the shelf above the stove at Øvre Lid in January.

## The loaf

[The loaf that finally worked](ref:section:sourdough/the-loaf), on 23 May 2020, is still my everyday bread:

1. 100 g active starter
2. 375 g water, warm from the tap
3. 500 g flour, 450 strong white and 50 wholemeal
4. 10 g salt, added after the rest
5. one cold night in the fridge

That's 75% :hint[hydration]{note="Water as a share of the flour, by weight: 375 g of water to 500 g of flour is 75%. I don't count the starter's own water, which annoys proper bakers."}, about as wet as I can manage without the dough managing me.

## Timings

The day before:

- **9.00** feed the starter
- **14.00** mix starter, water and flour, then leave it half an hour
- **14.30** salt in, squeezed through with wet hands
- **15.00 to 17.00** four sets of stretch-and-folds, half an hour apart
- **17.00 to 20.00** leave it covered until it has grown by half
- **20.00** shape it, into a floured basket, into the fridge

The next morning: oven to 250 °C with the pot inside for forty-five minutes; bake twenty minutes with the lid on and twenty-five with it off. Wait an hour before cutting. I have never once waited an hour before cutting.

## Things that went wrong

- ~~More starter makes it rise faster.~~ It makes it sour and flat.
- ~~Any flour will do.~~ Plain flour gives you a pancake with ambitions.
- Wet dough on a wooden board: you'll be scraping it off for a week.
- Never prove the basket on a radiator. Ask me how I know.
