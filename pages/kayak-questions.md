Ever since *Nana June* went into the water in May 2023, people on the quay have asked the same eight questions. Here they are, answered.

### How long did it take?

Fifteen months, from [the strongback](ref:event:strongback) in February 2022 to [the launch](ref:event:kayak-launch) on 20 May 2023. Perhaps three hundred hours of real work; the rest was waiting for epoxy to cure and varnish to dry, and a good deal of standing about in the workshop looking at it.

### Did you really build it yourself?

Yes, in Jonas's workshop in Bergen, which was the other good thing to come out of [a kayak course in January 2022](ref:event:kayak-course "where I met both the idea and Jonas"). He drilled none of the holes and twisted none of the wire. He did, twice, stop me gluing something on backwards.

### What's it made of?

Okoume plywood, 4 mm thick, stitched with copper wire, glued with epoxy, glassed inside and out, and varnished seven times. [The glossary](ref:page:boat-words) has all the words.

::file[The cut list, for anyone thinking of building one]{file="kayak-cut-list" title="Kayak cut list"}

### How heavy is it?

Nineteen kilos. I can carry it to the water on my shoulder, badly, for about forty metres.

### What did it cost?

Less than a new plastic one, and more than I'll ever tell Dad.

### Can I borrow the plans?

They aren't mine to lend: it's a published design, and the designer deserves the money. [The project](<ref:project:kayak#h:Plans> "including what I'd change next time") says which one it is.

::link[Where to buy the plans]{url="https://example.org/kayak-plans"}

### Why is it called *Nana June*?

After my grandmother, June Dunn, who died in November 2020, and who said "never trust a calm sea" so often that it's painted, in very small letters, inside the cockpit. [The launch](<ref:project:kayak#h:The launch> "the day she floated") has the rest of the story.

### Has it ever leaked?

:::spoiler
Once, nearly. In August 2023 I [put it on a rock at Herdla](ref:event:herdla-rock) at low water, in perfect weather, entirely my own fault: a long scrape along the keel and a crack in the glass you could put a fingernail in. It came home on the roof of the car and spent three weeks being mended. The repair is the slightly paler stripe along the keel, and I'm fond of it now.
:::
