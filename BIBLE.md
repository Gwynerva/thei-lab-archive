# The bible: Rowan Ellery, 2016–2026

The life this archive writes down. For authors only: no reader parses it. Ids
named here (projects, sections, events, pages, tags) are the ones the archive
uses; a change of an id here is a change there.

Everything is invented. Rowan, her family, her friends, her employers, her
teachers, the farm and every business named here are fictional. Places are
real and public (towns, mountains, streets, festivals, storms with public
names); nothing private of anyone real is written. No famous person appears.

## 1. Rowan

**Rowan Ellery** (she/her), born **1992-03-14** in Whitby, North Yorkshire.
Named after the rowan tree in her grandmother's garden at Robin Hood's Bay.
Pi Day birthday, which she brings up more often than anyone wants.

Cartographer and GIS developer; maker of small things by hand. Curious,
stubborn, funny in a dry way, prone to taking on one project too many. Not a
natural athlete but walks everywhere. Keeps lists. Draws maps in the margins
of everything. Writes British English (colour, metres, organise, mum).

Online handle: `rowan`. Slogan (from 2024): *Maps, small boats and things made
by hand.* Earlier slogan (2016–2023, only in the diary's memory): *Mostly
maps.*

What runs through all ten years:

- **Maps.** She thinks in maps: every trip gets a hand-drawn one; her diary
  mentions grid references, contours, tides.
- **The sea and the weather.** Whitby harbour as a child, the Firth of Forth,
  the North Sea crossed at last in 2026. She counts rain days.
- **Making things by hand**, learned from Nana June ("make do and mend").
- **The cello**, abandoned at sixteen, taken up again at twenty-five.
- **Biscuit**, the ginger cat, from 2019.
- **Family across the North Sea** after 2021: phone calls with Mum on Sunday
  evenings, Christmases in Whitby.

## 4. The writing voice

The diary is the same person over ten years; the voice moves with her.

| Years | Voice |
| --- | --- |
| **2016–2017**, the student | quick, chatty, exclamation marks, lists and checklists, "brilliant", "knackered", jokes at her own expense; short entries most days, a long one after anything new; the odd typo |
| **2018–2019**, the young professional | longer and more confident; travel written as small essays with `##` headings; dry humour; notes on code and cello practice |
| **2020**, lockdown | daily, short, often numbered ("Day 41."); weather, bread, the walk, Biscuit; introspective; after 2020-11-21 almost nothing, then a few spare lines |
| **2021–2022**, the newcomer | observational and sensory; Norwegian words in italics (*kos*, *utepils*, *dugnad*), lists of new words, rain counted; warmer and lighter once Jonas appears |
| **2023**, burnout | sparse; fragments; lowercase first words; no headings; a lot inside `:::private` |
| **2024–2026**, settled | calm, measured, longer reflective entries with headings, quotes from Nana and from Sigrid; renovation notes as checklists; English with Norwegian mixed in naturally; gratitude without sugar |

Recurring habits in the texts:

- **Three good things** — a short numbered list at the end of a day, from 2017;
  it fades in 2021 and returns in 2024.
- **Rain count** — "Rain: day 12." in Bergen.
- **Nana says** — Nana June's sayings, quoted: "Make do and mend", "A stitch
  in time is still a stitch", "Never trust a calm sea".
- **New words** — Norwegian words with their meaning, 2021–2023.
- **Margins** — she mentions drawing a little map of the day.
- **Practice log** — cello minutes, now and then.
