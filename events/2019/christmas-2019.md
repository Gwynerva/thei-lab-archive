Home for Christmas. The train down the coast in the dark on the 23rd, Dad at the station with the van, the chandlery smelling of rope and paraffin the way it has for thirty years.

Christmas Day at Mum and Dad's, all of us, and Nana brought over from the Bay in the van, in her good coat with the brooch on it. She sat at the head of the table and told Dev how to carve. I gave everyone a cat card and a jar of rowan jelly made from her recipe, and Nana tasted it and said, "Bit stiff. Did you skimp on the sugar?" I had not skimped on the sugar.

She told the story of Christmas 1957, when the pudding caught fire, and so did Grandad Arthur's sleeve, twice.

:::private
She told it twice, an hour apart, in the same words. Mum caught my eye across the room. Nobody said anything. She's ninety. It's allowed.
:::

---

Boxing Day: up the 199 steps to the abbey in a gale, the harbour lights below, Dad pointing at the piers and telling me what the tide was doing. The rest of the time: the sofa, the leftovers, and the cello. I played Nana the first bars of the prelude, and she said it was "very nice, very sad".

Back on the train on the 27th. Biscuit, who was minded by Marta, has forgiven nobody.
