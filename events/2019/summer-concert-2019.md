The summer concert. Elgar's Serenade for Strings, a bit of Holst, and a medley of sea songs at the end, because Agnes thinks every concert should finish with the audience allowed to clap along.

Second desk of the cellos this time, which means people can see me. I've practised the Elgar every evening for a month: forty minutes, timer on, Biscuit on the score. In the end I played nearly all the notes and most of them at the right moment. There's a passage in the first movement where the cellos climb quietly underneath everyone else, and for eight bars I forgot I was being looked at and just played.

::audio[The first movement, as it ought to go (not us)]{file="music-strings-1" title="Elgar, Serenade for Strings"}

Mrs Ogilvie came, sat at the back, and afterwards said "better", which from her is a standing ovation. Mum rang to ask how it went and I talked for twenty minutes without stopping. At sixteen I'd never have believed I'd come back to this. ([Why the cello](<ref:project:cello#h:Why the cello>), all over again.)
