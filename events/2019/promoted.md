Dougie called me into the glass box this morning, which usually means a council has rung about a map. Instead he said that from today I'm a GIS developer, not a ~~junior GIS analyst~~, that I'll be looking after the flood model's tools rather than just running them, and that he'd "been meaning to say for a while". Then he asked if I wanted a coffee, as if nothing had happened.

What changes: I get the Python. All of it. The scripts that turn rainfall and terrain into surface-water maps, which until now lived in folders with names like *final-v3-REAL*. I get to rewrite them properly, with tests. I've been doing that on the side for a year; now it's allowed.

What doesn't change: the desk by the radiator, the kettle rota, Ines stealing my good pen.

:::private
The money: a bit more than I'd hoped, a bit less than I'd asked for in my head. Enough to stop doing the sums at the end of every month. I'm not writing the number here.
:::

Ines made me a paper crown out of a misprinted flood map. I wore it till lunch.
