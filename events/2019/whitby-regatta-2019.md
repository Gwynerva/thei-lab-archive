Back to Whitby for regatta weekend, my first in years. The harbour was full: the old crews racing their rowing boats, the swimming race across the harbour, a tall ship in for the weekend, and the whole town on the piers with chips.

Dad had the chandlery open from seven and sold more rope by ten than he does in a week. Then he shut it, put a sign on the door (*Gone to the regatta. Back when it's over.*), and we stood on the West Pier for the racing. He knew the crews. He knew their fathers. He explained the tide to a family from Leeds who had only asked where the toilets were.

At four the Red Arrows came in low over the abbey, and the whole harbour went quiet and then roared.

He still brings up [the Brigg](ref:event:cleveland-way-walk#:~:text=Filey) at every chance; I think the walk got into him. Tonight, over fish and chips on the steps, he said, "Next summer, the moors half." I said yes before he could change his mind.
