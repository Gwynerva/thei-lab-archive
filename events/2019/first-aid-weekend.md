Sixteen of us in a scout hall at the foot of the Pentlands, one gas heater, and an instructor who'd been in mountain rescue for twenty years and told every story ending first. ("He was fine. But let me tell you about his leg.")

Saturday was theory and bandages. Sunday was outside, in sleet, finding "casualties" in the heather and doing all of it with cold hands. I splinted a man's forearm with a walking pole and a rolled-up map, which is the most me thing I have ever done. Then I was the casualty: twenty minutes in a ditch with a pretend broken ankle, getting properly cold, which taught me more than all the slides.

What's in my hill bag since:

- [x] a proper first aid kit, not a tin of plasters
- [x] a foil blanket *and* a bivvy bag
- [x] tough-cut scissors
- [x] a pencil and a waterproof notebook, for times and pulses
- [ ] actually practising the triangular bandage

The certificate is on the fridge, under the cat calendar.
