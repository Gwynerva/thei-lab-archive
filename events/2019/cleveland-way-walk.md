Dad's idea. He's walked bits of the Cleveland Way all his life and never the coast in one go, and at Christmas he said, "If not now, when? I'll be fifty-six." So we did the coast half: Saltburn to Filey, about eighty-five kilometres, six days on the calendar and five by Dad's count, because the day from Whitby to the Bay "doesn't count, it's just going to your Nana's".

## Saltburn to Whitby

We started at the pier at Saltburn in a sea fret, Dad with his rucksack from 1987 and me with mine from last year, and walked into the fret for two days. Cliffs, a great many steps, Staithes squeezed into its ravine, Runswick and its red roofs, the long beach at Sandsend. Dad named every boat in every harbour and told me who'd owned it before.

On the second afternoon the fret lifted at Sandsend, and there was the abbey on its cliff, and the piers, and home.

## Whitby to the Bay

The short day. Out past the abbey and along the clifftop to Robin Hood's Bay, eleven kilometres, with the dissertation's cliffs under our feet the whole way. I showed Dad where the fence posts had gone over the edge since 2017. He said, "Told you." He did tell me.

Nana was waiting at the top of the lane with a pot of tea and opinions about our boots.

## The Bay to Scarborough

The long bit. Ravenscar, the town that was going to be a resort and never was, its roads laid out for houses nobody built. Then the long cliff path to Scarborough, Dad slowing on the climbs and pretending to look at the view. I pretended to believe him.

## Filey Brigg

The last morning, Scarborough to Filey, and the end of the trail at the Brigg: a long reef of rock running out into the sea, as if the coast doesn't want to stop. We walked out on it at low tide as far as we could go, and Dad stood at the end with his hands in his pockets for a long time and said, "Well. That's that, then," which is the most emotional thing he's said since Clare's wedding.

## What I learned about Dad

1. He can name every boat between Saltburn and Filey.
2. He sings old sea songs when he thinks nobody can hear.
3. His knees are fine. His feet are not.
4. He's proud of me. He didn't tell me; he told the man in the chip shop at Filey.

That night I drew the whole coast on the back of the guesthouse's breakfast menu, every bay and nab, with a dotted line and two stick figures. Dad has taken it home for the wall of the shop.
