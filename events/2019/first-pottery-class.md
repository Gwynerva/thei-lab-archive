First pottery class: ten Tuesdays at a studio in Abbeyhill, ten of us in aprons, and a teacher who has been throwing pots for forty years and makes it look like stirring tea.

You start by *centring*: pressing a lump of wet clay into the middle of the spinning wheel until it runs true. She did it in ten seconds. I tried eleven times, and eleven times the clay went off-centre, wobbled, and either flew off the wheel or slumped into something like a cowpat. Eleven-nil to the wheel.

::media[The wheel, between disasters]{file="potters-wheel-1"}

On the twelfth go, for about four seconds, it ran true under my hands, smooth and still while it spun, and I understood why people do this. Then I sneezed.

I went home with clay under every nail and up one sleeve to the elbow, and Biscuit sniffed me for a full minute as if I'd been unfaithful. Next week: opening the clay out. The teacher says that by November we'll each have made a mug. I've seen my cowpats. We'll see.
