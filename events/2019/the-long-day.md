It started at breakfast, as these things do. I said the longest day of the year was wasted on people who work, and Priya said I couldn't get up Arthur's Seat and back three times between breakfast and teatime, and I said of course I could, and Marta, who should know better, wrote the terms on the fridge whiteboard: *one fish supper, with a pickled egg*.

So I took the day off. (Dougie: "What for?" Me: "A bet." Dougie: "Fair enough.")

## Three times up

The route: down Leith Walk, across the top of town, into the park at Holyrood, up the steep way under the Crags, touch the trig point on the summit, and back the same way. About eight and a half kilometres a lap.

::media[The route, three times over, in pencil on the city]{file="map-scotland-2"}

- **The first** at half seven, in cool air, with the city still yawning. Fifty-two minutes to the top. Smug.
- **The second** at half eleven, hot, with three hundred tourists and a piper on the summit rocks. Fifty-eight minutes.
- **The third** at four. Everything hurt. I stopped by the loch to look at the swans for longer than swans need looking at. An hour and a quarter, and I touched the trig point with both hands, like someone reaching a lifeboat.

::media[Edinburgh from the Crags, on the second way up]{file="arthurs-seat-3"}

Home by half six. Then a bath, for an hour, with the door open so I could follow the argument in the kitchen about whether it counted. Priya: the third lap was "more of a stagger". Marta, the judge: a stagger is a kind of walk. Priya conceded, with a shake of the head, and promised the fish supper "next week".

## On the roof

Then we took a bag of chips up through the skylight onto the flat bit of the roof, the three of us and a blanket, and watched the sky over the Forth go gold, then pink, then a deep clear blue that never quite turns black in June. The gulls went quiet at about ten. At nearly eleven, the first star.

I've lived in this city nearly three years and never seen it from up there at that hour. From the roof you can see the whole route, the hill dark against the last of the light. My legs are made of wood. Worth it. (The rest is in [the diary that night](ref:diary:2019-06-21 "written half asleep").)
