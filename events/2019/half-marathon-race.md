Twenty-one point one kilometres through Glasgow: over the river, round the parks, and down the long straight by the Clyde to the finish on Glasgow Green. Sixteen weeks of training for this, three runs a week since June, along the Water of Leith in the rain, round Inverleith Park, and one terrible long run in Fife I don't talk about.

My target was two hours. I'd never have said it out loud, but I wrote it on my wrist in biro: ~~2.05~~ 1.59.

Kilometres one to ten felt easy, which the plan had warned me about. Eleven to sixteen were fine. Seventeen to nineteen were the worst stretch of road I've ever been on, though there was nothing wrong with the road. At twenty I saw Marta and Callum, who'd come with a sign that said GO ON MIRA, NOBODY'S WATCHING, and I laughed so hard I got a stitch and ran the last kilometre with it.

Official time: **1\:58\:41**. Under two hours by one minute and nineteen seconds, which on paper is nothing and in my legs is everything.

Callum fed us at The Wee Oven afterwards, enormous plates. I couldn't get down the stairs to the toilet. Tonight I'm on the sofa with a bag of frozen peas on each knee, and for the first time in sixteen weeks the plan says nothing at all.
