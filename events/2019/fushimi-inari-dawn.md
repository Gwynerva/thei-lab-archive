Alarm at half four. The first train south, half empty, and at the shrine by quarter past five, with the sky only just going grey.

Everyone has seen the photographs of the orange gates. What the photographs don't give you is the sound: nothing. Your own feet on the stones, a crow, water somewhere. Thousands of gates, each one given by a business or a family, the names painted in black down the posts, and the tunnel of them climbing and splitting and climbing again all the way up the hill.

The foxes are everywhere: stone ones, the shrine's messengers, in red bibs, with keys or scrolls in their mouths. I started counting them and gave up at sixty.

::media[One of the foxes, in his bib]{file="fushimi-inari-4"}

At the top there's no view to speak of, which felt right somehow. On the way down the sun came through the gates sideways and turned the whole path orange, and by eight the first tour groups were coming up. I went for breakfast, smug as a cat.
