Nana June is ninety. All of us came: Mum and Dad, Clare and Dev from Leeds, me on the train from Edinburgh with a cake tin on my knee for two and a half hours. Twelve people in a cottage built for four.

She'd been told not to do anything. She'd made three trays of parkin and a curd tart "just in case". After the hip last winter we'd all worried she'd be frail; she walked down the lane to the slipway on her own and told us off for hovering.

Nine candles, one for each decade, because ninety would have set off the smoke alarm. Dad made a speech of about eleven words. Clare cried. Then somebody put the radio on, and Nana danced with Dev in the kitchen, slowly, to something from 1955, and for a minute nobody could look at anybody.

When I asked her what ninety felt like, she said:

:::quote[Nana, at ninety]{align="center"}
Same as seventy, only everybody's nicer to you.
:::

She gave me the rowan jelly recipe again, written out in her big round hand, with a line at the bottom: *Don't skimp on the sugar. Never trust a calm sea.* I don't know what the second part has to do with jelly. I'm keeping it anyway.
