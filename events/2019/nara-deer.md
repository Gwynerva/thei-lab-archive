Day trip to Nara. I'd read that the deer bow, and assumed it was one of those things people say. It isn't. You buy a stack of the special crackers, a deer comes over and bows its head, quite formally, you bow back, and then it headbutts you in the thigh until you hand over the rest.

::media[The crackers, in their paper band, briefly mine]{file="nara-deer-4" layout="natural"}

There are over a thousand of them and they have the run of the town. One ate the corner of my paper map of the temples while I was reading it. I'd marked my route in pencil. It ate the route.

The Great Buddha Hall at Tōdai-ji is the biggest wooden building I've ever stood in, and the Buddha inside is so large my neck hurt. There's a pillar with a hole in its base the size of the Buddha's nostril, and children crawl through it for luck. I didn't try. Adults, I noticed, get stuck.

Back to Kyoto on the evening train with deer slobber on my jeans. Tomorrow, south, to the mountains.
