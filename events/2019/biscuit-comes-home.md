I went to the rescue centre to *look*. Marta came to make sure I only looked. We both know how that went.

## The rescue centre

Two long rows of pens, and most of the cats in them asleep or making a point of ignoring us. Then, halfway down the left-hand row, a ginger tom put a paw through the wire and patted my sleeve, twice, the way you'd test a melon.

The card on his pen said *Mr Biscuits*. About three years old. Found in a bin store in Gorgie in November. "Good with people. Opinions about other cats." Neutered, chipped, and one bad tooth they were "keeping an eye on".

The forms took longer than the decision. The home check was last week: the woman from the centre looked at the windows, the stairs, the four flights up, and said, "He'll be fine. They like a view."

I've wanted a cat since I was six. Mum sneezes at them, Dad says cats are for people without boats, and student flats don't allow them. The landlord here said yes in a one-line email, which is the nicest thing he has ever done.

## The journey

Home in a taxi in a cardboard carrier, complaining the whole length of Leith Walk in a low, steady voice, like a man on hold to the council.

## The first eight hours

- He went under the bed for two hours.
- He came out for chicken.
- He inspected every room, and the bath twice.
- He found the Ordnance Survey sheets on my desk and lay down on sheet 66, Edinburgh, as if he'd ordered it.
- He sat on the kitchen windowsill and watched the gulls with his mouth open, making a noise like a dripping tap.

He's dropped the "Mr" and the "s". He's Biscuit. Marta says he looks more like a ginger nut; I say that's what a biscuit is.
