The interview with Nordvåg Kart was at ten, on a video call from the box room, with the bed folded up and the Munro map on the wall behind me so I'd look like someone who goes outside.

Three of them: Anna, who would be my team lead and who laughed in the right places; a man who asked about projections and nothing else; and someone from the office who never turned her camera on.

They'd set me a task last week: an old harbour chart of a little place north of Bergen, scanned, and could I turn it into something a modern chart could use. I showed them how I'd lined it up with the coastline, where the soundings didn't agree, and the rock I thought had moved (it hadn't; the old surveyor had). Anna said, "Most people don't notice that rock."

Then they asked about [tideline](ref:project:tideline), and I talked for far too long about harmonic constituents. Nobody stopped me.

They'll tell me in two weeks. Afterwards I walked to the Shore and back and couldn't remember a word I'd said.
