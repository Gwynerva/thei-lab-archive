The first proper storm. Mr Haldorsen (Sverre, he says, Sverre, but I can't yet) knocked at six to warn me. He'd been watching his barometer drop all afternoon. He tapped it for me, and the needle jumped down again, and he nodded like a man whose old friend had just confirmed something. "*I natt blir det uvær*," he said. Tonight there will be bad weather. Then, in English: "Tie down what you love."

I took the herbs in off the window ledge. Biscuit took himself under the bed.

At about ten it arrived. The wind came off the fjord and leaned on the whole house, and the house, being wood, leaned back and creaked like a ship. The rain went sideways, then upwards. The window rattled in its frame until I stuffed a sock in the gap. I lay awake listening, the way I listened to Storm Dennis from my old room in Whitby, but this was closer and louder and more personal.

::media[His barometer, which he trusts more than any forecast]{file="barometer-1"}

In the morning: branches all down the lane, a bin lid in our garden that isn't ours, and the neighbours' trampoline upside down two gardens along. Mr Haldorsen was already out in his boots, picking things up. "Only a small storm," he said. "Wait for January."
