## The flat, emptied

The removal men came on Thursday and took forty-three boxes, the cello in its hard case, the hall bookshelf in pieces and the framed map from Forthline, and drove them all away to a ship. Since then the flat has been an echo with a kettle in it.

Marta helped me with the last of it. She wrapped every mug in newspaper like it was a newborn, labelled the boxes in better handwriting than mine, and made me sit down when I started on the skirting boards for the third time. "You are not leaving them a museum," she said. "You are leaving them a flat."

Biscuit went to the pet transport people's cattery yesterday, yowling like a small ginger siren all the way down four flights. He flies after my quarantine. What's left in the flat: two suitcases, a sleeping bag, a toothbrush and a pint of milk.

## Hamish stays

Yesterday I also handed Hamish over to Marta, in his jar with the rubber band that marks how far he rose at his last feed. He's sixteen months old. He's made something like two hundred loaves. A sourdough starter could fly to Norway in a suitcase, in theory, but I couldn't face explaining him at the border, so he's staying in Leith, where he was born, and Marta has promised to feed him on Tuesdays and Fridays and send photos.

I may have said goodbye to a jar of flour and water with more feeling than is normal.

## The last walk

Last night, late, I walked the old route: down Leith Walk, along the Shore, over the bridge and back along the Water of Leith, past the bench where I sat through most of 2020. The swans were there. The Shore was full of people drinking outside on a warm night, and I stood by the water and listened for a while: English, Scots, Polish, somebody laughing.

## Tomorrow morning

The taxi comes at half five. I'll lock the door, post the keys through the letterbox and carry the suitcases down for the last time. Marta, who moves across town in the afternoon, has promised to stand at the stair door in her dressing gown and wave until we turn the corner.

::card[The flat on its last day, for the record]{to="ref:section:leith-flat/empty"}

This time tomorrow I'll be on the plane, and below me the Forth, the three bridges, the whole of Fife, and then cloud. Four years and ten months in one flat. I don't know what I am if I'm not someone from Leith. I suppose I'm about to find out.
