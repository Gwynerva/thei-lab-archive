A year today. I'd planned it, more or less: the last of the hand quilting on the last corner, so that it would be finished on the day. I sat by the window in Sandviken with the quilt over my knees and Biscuit on top of the quilt, and did the last row, and tied it off, and cut the thread.

She'd pieced the whole top before she got ill: stars on cream, red and blue and yellow, out of forty years of scraps. Some of the fabric I know. The blue with white dots was a dress of Mum's. The yellow was the kitchen curtains at the Bay. I only had to do the quilting, which sounds small and took ten months of evenings, through a lockdown and a move across the North Sea.

:::quote[Nana June, every time I tried to rush]{align="center"}
A stitch in time is still a stitch.
:::

[The quilt](ref:project:nana-quilt "nearly a year of evenings") is on the bed now. It's too warm for the flat and I don't care.

:::private
I stitched a label on the back, in the corner, in her blue thread: *June Dunn pieced it, 1929–2020. Mira finished it, Bergen, 2021.* Then I sat on the floor and cried for an hour, properly, for the first time since the funeral. It was good. I needed it.
:::

I rang Mum afterwards. She'd been to the grave with flowers. We didn't say much. We didn't need to.
