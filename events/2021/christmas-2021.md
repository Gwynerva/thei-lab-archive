The first Christmas that isn't Whitby and isn't Leith. Anna wouldn't hear of me spending it alone, so I spent Christmas Eve with her family, in a house in Fana with five generations of candlesticks.

In Norway the day is the 24th. The church bells ring Christmas in at five, and then there's dinner, which in this family is :hint[pinnekjøtt]{note="salted, dried lamb ribs, soaked for a day and steamed over sticks of birch for hours"} with mashed swede and potatoes, and something to drink that I was told was not optional. The ribs come out tender and salty and you eat them with your fingers.

Then *riskrem*, cold rice pudding folded with cream, with red berry sauce and one almond hidden in the bowl. Whoever finds it wins a marzipan pig. Anna's youngest nephew found it, and everyone pretended to be surprised.

Then everyone holds hands and walks round the Christmas tree, singing. I didn't know the words. I hummed. Nobody minded.

I rang Mum and Dad from the hall. Dad asked what the lamb had done to deserve it. Mum asked if we'd had crackers, and I said they don't have crackers here, and she said, "Well, that's just sad."

New words for [the food list](<ref:page:norwegian-words#h:Food> "words I eat"): *pinnekjøtt*, *riskrem*, *mandelgave* (the almond prize), *julebrus* (Christmas fizzy pop, bright red).
