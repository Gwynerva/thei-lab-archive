Last day at Forthline. Four years, nearly to the week, since I walked in with a new notebook and no idea what a surface-water flood model was.

We had it in the yard behind the office, because half the team still won't sit in a room together, which is fair. Dougie made a speech from an upturned crate. He said I came to Forthline as a junior who asked too many questions and was leaving as a developer who answered them, and that the flood maps of three councils have my fingerprints on them, which he meant nicely. Then he said Norway was very lucky and sat down quickly before anyone could see his face.

Ines sent a video from Porto: her balcony, a glass of something, and "*Boa sorte*, you mad woman".

They gave me a present wrapped in an old flood map: a framed print of a 1935 pictorial map of Edinburgh and Leith, with our street on it, tiny, and the Shore, and the docks.

::media[Edinburgh and Leith in 1935; the office would be just off the bottom of the docks]{file="old-map-scotland-1"}

I cleared my desk into one box. Four years shouldn't fit in one box. They did.
