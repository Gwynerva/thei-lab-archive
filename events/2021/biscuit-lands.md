He's here. Biscuit flew from Edinburgh to Bergen by way of Amsterdam, in the hold, in his crate with the live-animal stickers on it, and I collected him from a cargo shed at the far end of the airport, where a man in a hi-vis vest checked his papers and his chip and said, "*Han er sint.*" He's angry. He was.

He didn't speak to me in the taxi. He didn't speak to me for the first hour in the new flat. He walked round every room with his belly low to the floor, sniffed every corner, sat in the empty bath for a while, and then found the one patch of sun on the floorboards by the window and lay down in it as if he'd paid for it.

The flat is in a white wooden house in Sandviken, up a steep lane, with a window that looks over the harbour. It has a bed, a table, two chairs and, as of today, a cat. Everything else is on a ship.

At ten tonight he got up on the bed and purred like an outboard motor. I think that means he forgives me. Or that he's decided this is his flat now and I can stay.
