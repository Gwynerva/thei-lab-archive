A weekend course in spoon carving at a craft centre outside the city: a barn with a wood stove, eight of us at chopping blocks, and a teacher who has been making spoons for forty years and talks about birch the way some people talk about wine.

Saturday: the axe. You split a length of green birch down the middle, then hew away everything that isn't a spoon. I was terrified of it for an hour, and then I wasn't, and that was the best feeling I've had since I got here.

Sunday: the knife. A small red-handled one, sharp enough to shave with (don't). The grips have names: the thumb push, the chest lever, the one that looks like peeling a potato towards yourself and is fine if you do it right. I did it right most of the time. One plaster.

New words: *sløyd*, crafts in wood, the whole idea of making useful things by hand; *bjørk*, birch; *skje*, spoon; *tollekniv*, the knife.
