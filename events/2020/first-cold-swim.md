Walk 152 ended in the sea.

For weeks I'd watched them from the prom: a group of women, mostly older than Mum, who meet by the old groyne at eight every morning and walk into the Forth as if it were a bath. This morning one of them caught me looking and said, "Coming in, then?" And I'd brought a towel. Just in case.

Fourteen degrees, they said. It felt like four. The first ten seconds are a sort of scream you don't let out; then your breath comes back, and your skin goes from burning to tingling, and then, and this is the part nobody believes, you feel wonderful. I lasted six minutes. They stayed in for twenty and talked about their grandchildren the whole time.

Out, shaking, unable to do up my own buttons. One of them poured me tea from a flask with a slug of something in it.

I'm going back on Saturday. I've been told to buy a bobble hat.
