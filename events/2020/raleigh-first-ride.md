Walk 117 was a ride, which I've decided counts. The Raleigh rode.

Ten weeks ago it was in a skip on Easter Road, rusted solid, two flat tyres, the saddle split, a frame number that says 1984 and a colour that was once red. Since then: stripped to the last bearing on the balcony, every part soaked, scrubbed and greased, the three-speed hub taken apart on a tea towel and put back together with no parts left over (a first; [the Spoke Room](ref:project:spoke-room) taught me something after all). New tyres, new cables, new brake blocks, and a second-hand saddle from a man in Musselburgh.

Today: Leith to Portobello along the front and back, eighteen kilometres. The gears change with a clunk you can feel in your teeth. The brakes work, in a leisurely sort of way. A woman at the lights said, "My dad had one of those." Everyone's dad had one of those.

I'm absurdly proud of it. It isn't fast, or light, or beautiful. But in May it was rubbish in a skip, and now it's a bike.
