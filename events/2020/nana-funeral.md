The funeral. Twenty people allowed in, so twenty of us in a cold chapel, masks on, a seat between each household; and outside in the lane perhaps fifty more, neighbours from the Bay, standing in the rain with their hoods up because they couldn't come in.

Mum read the poem Nana chose years ago and kept in the sewing box. Dad read nothing; he carried her, with Dev and two men from the lifeboat. I read what she said at her ninetieth, because it was the most her thing I knew:

> Same as seventy, only everybody's nicer to you.

People laughed, which is what she'd have wanted. Then I couldn't do the rest.

::card[Ninety, and dancing in the kitchen]{to="ref:event:nana-90#:~:text=kitchen"}

No wake. You can't, now. We stood in the lane afterwards, two metres apart, and everyone said how she'd made their curtains, or their wedding dress, or their child's costume for the nativity in 1974.

We put a sprig of rowan on the coffin, from her tree. No berries left this late. Just the twigs.
