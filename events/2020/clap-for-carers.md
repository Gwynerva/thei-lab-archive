Day 4. At eight o'clock everyone came to their windows and clapped for the NHS. The whole street, both sides, four floors up: someone banging a pan, someone on the top floor opposite with a trumpet. I've never seen most of these people before, and I've lived across from them for three years.

I was clapping for Clare. She's on nights in A&E in Leeds this week, in a mask that leaves marks on her nose, and she texted at four this morning: *it's not too bad yet. yet.*

I clapped till my hands stung, and then I cried a bit at the window, and Marta passed me a tea towel, which was the nearest thing.

Rang Clare at eight the next morning, after her shift. She was eating cereal out of the box in her car. She'd heard about the clapping. She said, "That's nice. Tell them to stay in."
