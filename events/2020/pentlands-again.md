Walk 67. The first day of being allowed a few miles from home, and I was on the bus to the edge of the Pentlands by eight, with a flask, a squashed sandwich, and nobody else on the top deck.

Up Allermuir, then along the ridge. Skylarks going straight up out of the heather and hanging there, singing as if they were being paid. Sheep. A wind with nothing in it but air. I'd forgotten what it's like to see further than a street in any direction: the whole city laid out behind me, the Forth, Fife, and to the south hill after hill after hill, and not a building on any of them.

::media[Looking off the Pentlands, the fields going on for ever]{file="pentland-hills-3"}

I sat on the top for an hour and didn't do anything. I didn't take many pictures. I think I'd been holding my breath since March.

Home by four, sunburnt down one side of my face. Biscuit sniffed my boots for a long time. They smelled of somewhere else.
