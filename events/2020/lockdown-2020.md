Day 1. The announcement on the telly at half eight, and then that was that. Stay at home. One walk a day. Marta and I sat on the sofa afterwards and didn't say anything for a long while, and then she said, "Well. Good thing we like each other."

This is the whole of it, written at the end, out of sixty-seven days of the diary.

## The rules we made

The government's rules were simple. Ours took longer:

- One walk a day each, at different times, so each of us gets the flat to herself for an hour.
- Work at the kitchen table (me) and at the desk in her room (her). No video calls in the kitchen after six.
- Cello practice four till five; violin five till six. Biscuit's opinion not sought.
- Sunday: a proper dinner, a tablecloth, and clothes with buttons.
- Nobody reads the news after nine at night.

We broke the last one most nights.

## What the days were like

Day 9. Every day is Tuesday.

Day 18. Clapped for the nurses again at the window. Rang Clare afterwards; she sounded like somebody at the bottom of a well.

Day 22. The walk: down to the Shore, along the river to Canonmills, back up the hill. The same walk every day, and every day something new on it: a heron, a cherry flowering behind a railing, a man playing the trumpet out of a window to nobody.

::media[The bridge at Canonmills, on the walk]{file="leith-river-2"}

Day 34. Mum on the phone, steady as ever. Nana on the phone, less steady, but full of the war: "We've done this before, love. You just get on."

Day 41. Bread, the walk, the cello, Biscuit, bed. I think I'm happy, mostly, and then I feel guilty about that.

Day 50. I haven't touched another person in fifty days, except Marta, twice, by accident, in the hall.

Day 58. Warm. Sat on the balcony with my feet in a washing-up bowl of cold water and called it the seaside.

## What kept us going

- [Hamish](ref:event:hamish-born), who needed feeding twice a day whether we felt like it or not.
- The walk. (There's a whole project for it now, in among [all projects](/projects/).)
- The orchestra on a laptop on Tuesday nights, however bad it sounded.
- Biscuit, who thinks this is the best thing that has ever happened.
- The Sunday phone call to Whitby.

---

Day 67. From tomorrow we can meet one other household outdoors, a few miles from home. I'm going to the Pentlands. I'm going to see a hill that isn't Arthur's Seat through a window.

I don't know how to end this. It hasn't ended, really. But the first part has.
