Mývatn means "midge lake". I thought this was a joke, the kind of name Vikings gave places to keep visitors away. It is not a joke.

They come off the water in clouds whenever the wind drops, and while they don't bite (unlike Skye's, which bite with intent), they go in your ears, your nose, your eyes and your mouth, and they hum. We bought head nets at the petrol station along with half the other tourists, and walked round looking like a convention of beekeepers.

What we saw through the nets:

- Námafjall, where the ground steams and bubbles and smells of eggs, and the earth is orange and yellow and grey like a burnt cake
- Hverfjall, a crater a kilometre across, black gravel inside, whose rim we walked in a gale that blew the midges away (thank you, gale)
- Grjótagjá, a little cave with a pool of clear blue water inside, far too hot to swim in

::media[Inside Hverfjall, where the wind blew the midges away]{file="myvatn-2"}

In the evening we sat in the nature baths up to our chins in milky blue water, with no midges at all, because of the sulphur. Best hour of the trip so far.
