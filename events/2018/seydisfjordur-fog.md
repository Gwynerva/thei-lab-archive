Over the pass from Egilsstaðir this morning, up through snow still metres deep at the roadside in late May, and then down, in hairpins, into Seyðisfjörður, which was full to the brim with fog.

I mean full. The fjord is long and narrow, with mountains a thousand metres high on both sides, and we could see none of them. Just the road, the first wooden houses, the harbour wall, and a white wall of nothing beyond. A ship's horn somewhere out in it, very low. Gulls appearing and disappearing.

::media[What the fjord looked like, more or less, all day]{file="fog-2"}

The town is small and bright: houses in red and yellow and green, a pale blue church at the end of a street painted in rainbow stripes, a bakery with very good cinnamon buns. We sat in the bakery for two hours waiting for the fog to lift. It did not lift. It thickened slightly, out of spite.

So, no view. But I've never been anywhere so quiet. I keep thinking about living somewhere like that, at the end of a fjord, with the weather coming and going past the window. Probably it's just the cinnamon buns.
