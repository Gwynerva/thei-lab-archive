The first named storm of the season, Ali, came through Edinburgh like a lorry through a market.

The tenement across the street has been in scaffolding since June, sheeted in green netting, and all morning the wind got under the netting and filled it like a sail, and the whole frame hummed and banged: a low organ note with percussion. Marta stood at the window with her violin and tried to find the note. Somewhere around a B flat, she says, and the poles were out of tune with each other.

At work the power flickered three times, the trees on the Links were losing branches, and at two Dougie sent everyone home before the trains stopped. Half the trains stopped anyway.

By evening it had blown itself out. The scaffolding stayed up, against all my expectations. Somebody's trampoline was in the middle of Easter Road.
