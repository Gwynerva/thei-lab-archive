I gave a talk! Twelve minutes, at the mapping meetup that happens upstairs in a pub near the university on the first Thursday of the month, about [tideline](ref:project:tideline): how you predict a tide with nothing but a list of numbers and a lot of cosines.

About forty people. Pints. One dog. I had seventeen slides and practised them nine times on Marta, who now understands tides better than anyone else in the orchestra.

It went like this. The moon pulls, the sun pulls, the Earth spins underneath them, and each of those motions puts its own wave into the sea. Every wave has its speed, and every harbour has its own height and timing for it: [the constituents](<ref:section:tideline/harmonics#h:Constituents> "the list, with the main ones explained"). Add them up and the tide falls out.

::media[The slide that got a laugh: the bulges, following the moon]{file="tide-animation-1" layout="natural"}

Three questions. Two I could answer. The third, about the Bay of Fundy, I answered with "I don't know, but I'll find out", which I'm told is the correct answer and which I hated saying.

Afterwards a man from a boatyard in Granton asked if he could use tideline for his slipway. Yes! Yes, you can. My first user who isn't my dad.
