Mrs Ogilvie decided I was ready for "other people" and sent me to the Leith Scratch Orchestra, which rehearses on Tuesday nights in a church hall with a tea urn and a radiator that knocks in waltz time.

It's run by Agnes Muir, who conducts in a fleece and has the patience of a saint and the ears of a bat. Anyone can come. There were about forty of us: a retired surgeon on the double bass, two schoolgirls leading the violins, a viola player who apologised after every passage. I sat at the back desk of the cellos beside a woman who has sat at that same desk for nineteen years, and who whispered bar numbers to me like a prompter.

We read through the first movement of Elgar's Serenade for Strings. So much for no Elgar. I was lost by bar twelve. I came back in at bar thirty with great confidence in the wrong place. By the third time through I was playing perhaps half the notes, roughly where they belonged, and when the whole room swelled up together in the middle I felt it in my ribs.

::audio[The Serenade played properly, which is what we're aiming at for December]{file="music-strings-1" title="Serenade for Strings"}

Forty-five minutes on the first page when I got home. The neighbours are going to hate Elgar by Christmas.
