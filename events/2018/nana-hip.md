Mum rang at seven on Saturday morning, which Mum never does. Nana had fallen in her kitchen in the night, reaching for a tin on the top shelf, and lain on the floor till the neighbour came round at six with her paper. A broken hip.

I got the first train down. She was in a bed on the ward in Scarborough with her hair done (she'd asked the nurse), looking very small and very cross, and the first thing she said to me was "don't you dare cry, it's only a hip".

They pinned it on the Sunday. She came round from the anaesthetic asking for a cup of tea and her glasses, in that order.

I stayed four days, slept at Mum and Dad's, and went in every afternoon. We did the crossword. She told the nurses that I make maps, and books, and that I'm "very clever but can't boil an egg", which is unfair and true.

The physio had her walking with a frame within a week. She went home on the 20th, to a rail by the back door and a stool by the cooker that she says she won't use. She'll use it.

I keep thinking about her on that floor in the dark for five hours, alone. She says she sang hymns to pass the time.
