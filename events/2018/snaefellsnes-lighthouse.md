The second-last day, and the far west: the tip of the Snæfellsnes peninsula, where a glacier sits on top of a volcano and the land ends in black lava cliffs over a heaving sea.

At the very end stands Svörtuloft lighthouse, a square tower painted the brightest orange I've ever seen on a building, standing on the black rock like a traffic cone someone left on the moon. It's orange so that ships can see it against the snow in winter. I'd like everything to be that clear about what it's for.

We walked along the cliff edge from it for an hour, with fulmars hanging in the updraught at eye level, so close you could see them adjust their wings by a single feather.

::media[Kirkjufell on the drive back, waterfall and all]{file="kirkjufell-1"}

On the way back east we stopped at Kirkjufell, the mountain everyone photographs, and photographed it, and a farmer drove past and waved with the patience of a man who sees two hundred people a day photographing his mountain.

Home tomorrow. I've filled a notebook with a map of every day. The last page is the whole island with our route in red, round and round, like a lap of honour.
