My big sister is married.

## The paper things

At Christmas Clare asked me to "do the paper things", and I said yes before she'd finished the sentence, and then spent every spare evening from May to August finding out what that meant:

:::list{counter="lower-roman"}
1. Ninety invitations, printed on thick cream card at a letterpress workshop in Leith, with a little linocut of the Minster at the top.
2. Ninety-four place cards (four late additions, one feud), lettered with a dip pen in copperplate, which I learned from a library book and a great deal of wasted card.
3. An order of service, folded and saddle-stitched with blue thread to match the bridesmaids.
4. A map, from the church to the reception, hand-drawn, with the city walls, the river, and Dad's favourite pub marked in case of emergencies.
:::

::media[One of the nib tests, from the week I nearly gave up on copperplate]{file="fountain-pen"}

## The day

Hot and bright, a September day like an apology for the summer we'd had. The church was cool inside and smelled of lilies, old stone and other people's best perfume. Clare in a dress with sleeves and pockets, because she's practical. Dev crying from the moment she appeared at the church door. Mum crying from about an hour before that. Dad walked her down the aisle in a suit he'd had altered, his tie straight for once, and I saw him check the tide times on his phone just before, out of pure habit, and put it away.

Nana came, in a wheelchair for the distance, in a hat with a feather that was bigger than she is.

I stood at the front with the other bridesmaid, Dev's sister, held the flowers, and did not cry until the vows, which I believe is a family record.

## The speech I didn't make

I wasn't asked to give a speech. I wrote one anyway, the week before, in the margins of the seating plan. It's mostly about how Clare taught me to ride a bike on the West Cliff by letting go without telling me. I'll give it to her one day.

---

At the reception I counted six people who picked up their place card, turned it over, and put it in a pocket. Clare says that's the highest compliment paper can get. The map was used by exactly one guest, Dad, to get from the church to the pub.
