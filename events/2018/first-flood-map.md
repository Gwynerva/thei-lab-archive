My first flood map went out today. Mine: I built the model, ran it, checked it, drew it, and my initials are in the corner of every sheet in very small letters, which Dougie says is tradition and Ines says is so they know who to blame.

It's a surface-water map for a small town in Fife: what happens when a storm drops more rain in an hour than the drains can carry, and where the water goes when it can't go down. Six weeks of work. Culverts, kerbs, the height of every road from lidar, a model that runs overnight on the big machine, and three storms, from the kind you get every few years to the kind you get once in two hundred.

The blue on the map is where people's kitchens would be wet. I kept thinking about that. A real street, a real house, someone who'll look at it one day and see her own back door.

We delivered it at four. The council said thank you. Dougie bought a cake shaped like a raindrop, which in this weather felt like a dare.
