I've started counting dry days, the way some people count sheep. Edinburgh has turned a colour I didn't know it had: the Meadows are straw, the Links are dust, the Pentlands are brown as a paper bag, and the tar on Leith Walk goes soft by two in the afternoon.

Everybody is out. Pale Scottish legs that haven't seen daylight since 2013, on every bit of grass. Half the city goes to Portobello after work and stands in the sea up to the knees, as one, sighing.

The flat is a top-floor oven. We sleep with every window open, the gulls start at four, and nobody minds. Callum made gazpacho. Priya measured the sea at Portobello with a proper thermometer, for fun: seventeen degrees, "practically tropical".

The irony is that at work I'm modelling where water goes in a flood. I'm drawing maps of rain in a city where the last rain is a rumour.

I'll miss it when it's gone. I'll complain about it until then.
