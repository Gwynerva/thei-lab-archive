Took the cello on the bus to a luthier's workshop up a close off the Grassmarket, recommended by Mrs Ogilvie in the tone of someone recommending a good dentist.

The luthier, a quiet man in a cardigan with sawdust on it, laid the cello on a padded bench and went over it with his fingertips like a doctor. Open seam on the lower bout: glue and clamps. Bridge: warped, replace. Soundpost: "wandered". Strings: older than some of his customers. Then he tapped the front, listened, and said it was "a decent German factory instrument, seventies, honest". I felt absurdly proud of it.

::media[Through the f-holes, under the new strings, a fortnight later]{file="cello-4"}

Two weeks without it. I've been practising my bow hold on a pencil in meetings, which Ines has noticed and is too kind to mention more than once a day.

:::private
The bill: £185. The bridge alone was £60. I paid it out of the savings pot labelled ICELAND and moved the trip back a week in my head.
:::

Collected it on the 17th. It sounds like a different instrument. It sounds like it wants to be played.
