Priya had two days of seabird counting to help with at South Stack, on the far western tip of Anglesey, and asked if I wanted to come and carry the flask. I did. We drove down from Edinburgh on Friday night, seven hours, and slept in a B&B in Holyhead where the landlady had a framed photograph of every cat she has ever owned.

## The sign

On Saturday morning, on the way out, we stopped at the station. You have to. The village is called, in full,

Llanfairpwllgwyngyllgogerychwyrndrobwllllantysiliogogogoch

which is fifty-eight letters, and the sign on the platform has it all on one long board with the pronunciation underneath, in case you're feeling brave. We were feeling brave. We were wrong.

It means, roughly, *St Mary's church in the hollow of the white hazel, near the fierce whirlpool, and the church of St Tysilio by the red cave*. The story goes that it was stretched to that length in the 1860s to make the trains stop and the tourists get off, which, judging by the car park, still works.

::media[The station building, the whole name across its front]{file="llanfair-station-2"}

As a cartographer I have feelings about this. The full name fits on no map at any scale I'd want to use. The Ordnance Survey gives it as Llanfairpwllgwyngyll and moves on, and so, mostly, does everybody who lives there. But I like that the long version exists, a whole walk from the church to the cave folded into one word, like a route written down by someone who didn't trust you to find it.

## South Stack

Then the real reason we came: South Stack, a lighthouse on its own little island off the cliffs, four hundred steps down and a bridge across. Priya counted razorbills with a clicker in each hand, muttering. I counted steps, and puffins (four), and choughs (two, red-legged, showing off).

::media[South Stack from the clifftop, with the thrift out]{file="south-stack-1"}

The wind came straight off the Irish Sea, and the birds hung in it at eye level without moving a feather. We ate our sandwiches with our backs against the wall of an old stone hut at the top, out of the wind, and watched the ferry come in to Holyhead.

## Saying it

By Sunday afternoon Priya could say the whole name in one breath, at speed, like a spell. I can manage the first half and the "gogogoch" at the end, with a sort of hopeful mumble in the middle where the whirlpool is.

We drove home on Sunday through the rain, practising. Seven hours. We had a great deal of practice.
