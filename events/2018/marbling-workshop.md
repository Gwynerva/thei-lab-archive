A day's workshop in marbling at a print studio in town. I've been binding notebooks for eighteen months with covers I bought; it's time to make my own.

How it works, which I still find slightly magical: you fill a shallow tray with :hint[size]{note="water thickened with carrageen moss, so the colours float on it instead of sinking"} and drop colours on it from a brush, and they spread into rings on the surface. A drop of ox gall in the paint makes it spread further. Then you drag a stylus through, or a comb, and the rings pull into veins and feathers and swirls. Then you lay a sheet of paper on top, very gently, lift it, and the pattern is on the paper, the tray is clear, and you can never make that exact pattern again.

My first six were mud. The seventh was a blue-and-black stone pattern I'm unreasonably proud of. By the end I'd done a feathered one that almost looked deliberate.
