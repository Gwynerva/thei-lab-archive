## Getting there

We landed at Keflavík last night at eleven, in daylight, which my body refused to accept, and picked up a small white hire car that Priya immediately named Snowy. The plan: ten days, all the way round the ring road, anticlockwise. Today was the first proper day of [the south coast](ref:section:iceland/south "the first four days"), east out of Reykjavík past lava fields covered in moss as thick as a duvet, until Seljalandsfoss appeared on the left: a white line falling sixty metres off a green cliff.

## The path behind

The thing about Seljalandsfoss is that the cliff behind it is undercut, so a path goes right round the back of the fall. You walk into the spray, up some wet steps, and then you're behind it, in a cave of noise, looking out through a curtain of falling water at the flat green plain and the sea beyond.

I had a new waterproof jacket. It turns out "waterproof" is a claim, not a promise. By the far side I was wet to the vest, Priya was laughing so much she had to sit down on a rock, and I didn't care in the slightest, because I was *behind a waterfall*.

## Skógafoss, for comparison

Half an hour further east, Skógafoss: wider, louder, a wall of water you can walk right up to but not behind. There were rainbows in the spray all afternoon. We climbed the steps beside it to the top, 527 of them (I counted, of course), and looked down the river to the sea.

## What I'm learning

Iceland is a country made of weather. In one afternoon we had sun, hail, a rainbow, a squall that rocked the car on its springs, and sun again. Priya says it's Scotland with the volume turned up. I'd say it's Scotland if Scotland had been made last week and nobody had tidied up yet. Every lay-by on the road has a view that would be famous anywhere else, and nobody has even put up a sign.

Tonight, a guesthouse near Vík. My jacket is steaming on the radiator, I've eaten an enormous bowl of lamb soup, and I've drawn today's map in the notebook: a coast road, two waterfalls, and a small wet stick figure behind one of them. Tomorrow, the black beach.
