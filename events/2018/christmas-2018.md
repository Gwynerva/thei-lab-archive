Christmas at home, and Nana walked from the car to the front door on her own, with a stick, and wouldn't let anyone take her arm. Seven weeks after the hip. Dad timed it. Mum cried. Of course.

Presents this year were wood. I made [five cutting boards](ref:project:cutting-boards "five boards, five people, one splinter") in the evenings of November and December, oak and cherry, oiled till they glowed. Dad's has a groove for the juice. Mum's has a hole for hanging. Nana's is small and light enough for her wrist. Clare and Dev got one between them, as a married couple, which they pretended to find romantic.

And the cards: [this year's linocut](ref:section:linocut-cards/lighthouse) is the West Pier lighthouse, cut from a photo I took in October. Forty of them, printed on the kitchen floor in Leith with Marta holding the ink tray.

::media[The two lights at the harbour mouth, the photo I cut the block from]{file="whitby-pier-2"}

Boxing Day, a slow walk on the West Cliff, with Nana in the car at the whalebones, flask in hand and window down, supervising.

Three good things:

1. Nana, walking.
2. Dad using his board before lunch, for the carrots.
3. Everyone home.
