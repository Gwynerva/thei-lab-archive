Twelve years ago, at sixteen, I put the cello in its case under my bed in Whitby and decided I was finished with it. Too many exams, too much Elgar, not enough talent. In October Mum sent it up in the back of Dad's van, and it has leaned in the corner ever since, looking at me.

Tonight I took it to a lesson.

Mrs Ogilvie (Eilidh Ogilvie, but I will never be able to call her Eilidh) played in an orchestra for thirty-five years and teaches from a front room in Stockbridge full of music stands and one very old dog. She looked the cello over. She found the scratch on the front that I've always said is shaped like Norway, and agreed, which I took as a good sign. Then she found the open seam and the leaning bridge, and said the word "luthier" in a tone I didn't love.

Then she had me play an open C string for ten minutes. Bow arm loose, weight from the back, sit like I mean it. My first notes in twelve years sounded like a fridge. By the end, a slightly better fridge.

Practice: open strings, ten minutes a day, the scale of C, and no Elgar "for the foreseeable".

I walked home along the Water of Leith with the cello on my back like a beetle, grinning at strangers. [Why the cello at all](<ref:project:cello#h:Why the cello> "the long answer, from the beginning") is a longer story. The short one: I missed it.
