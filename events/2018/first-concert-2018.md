My first concert in twelve years. The last one I played in, I was fourteen, in the school hall in Whitby, and I made a mess of the Elgar and swore never again.

Tonight, the Leith Scratch Orchestra's winter concert in a church on the Kirkgate, two hundred people or so, and Elgar's Serenade for Strings: the very piece I was lost in at bar twelve in March.

Mum and Dad came up on the train and sat in the second row. Dad wore the graduation tie. Mum had a tissue ready before we'd even tuned.

I was not lost at bar twelve. I was briefly lost in the second movement and found again by the woman next to me tapping the bar number with her bow. Marta, two desks in front, turned her head about a centimetre at the end of the slow movement, which is the violinist's way of saying "that was lovely". And when the last chord came, and Agnes held it, and held it, and let it go, I felt the whole room breathe out.

Encore: a carol, with the audience singing along, slightly faster than us.

Afterwards Dad said "that was grand" four times and nothing else, which is the most he's ever said about music.
