It stands! The bookshelf for the hall, which I started in May with a sketch on squared paper and an ambition well beyond my skill, is finished, oiled and full of books.

Oak, from a timber yard in Granton, cut to length by a man who looked at my drawing and said "dovetails? By hand?" in a voice that suggested I reconsider. I did not reconsider. I cut eight practice corners in the back room of the Spoke Room in June, ruined five, and the last three were good enough.

::media[The plane, the shavings, and the evening I finally got the top flat]{file="hand-plane-1"}

Three coats of oil this week, a day between each, the whole flat smelling of linseed. This morning Callum and I carried it into the hall, stood it against the wall, and loaded it with every book that's been piled on the floor since 2016. It didn't wobble. It didn't fall. I checked it with a spirit level: level to the bubble.

Priya patted it and said "good shelf". Callum, who leaves in six weeks, said it was a shame he wouldn't see it full for longer. That was the only sad thing about today.
