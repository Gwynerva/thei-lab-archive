Bergen has a reputation. Everyone told me before I came: it rains two hundred days a year. I thought they were exaggerating, the way people in Whitby exaggerate about the wind. They weren't.

On the 3rd of October it started raining, and it didn't stop.

## Counting

I've counted rain days since I got here, in the back of my notebook: Rain: day 1, day 2, a tick for each. Usually the count breaks after four or five, a dry day sneaks in, and I start again.

This time it didn't break. On day three, as a joke, I started writing a small app to count it for me from the balcony gauge, because I am who I am (that's [the rain counter](ref:project:rain-counter), a story of its own). By day ten I was saying the number out loud at breakfast. By day fourteen people at work were asking me for it in the morning instead of saying hello. Mr Haldorsen was unimpressed. In the winter of 2006, he said, it rained here eighty-five days in a row. "Twenty-one," he said, and patted my arm.

## What rain is like here

It isn't like Leith rain, which comes in on the wind, does its business and leaves. Bergen rain lives here. It sits on the mountains and comes down the slopes as if it's going home. It isn't even stormy, mostly. Nothing like [last November](ref:event:first-bergen-storm#:~:text=trampoline). Just rain, patient and endless.

Norwegian has more words for it than English, and I've collected them all month for [the weather list](<ref:page:norwegian-words#h:Weather> "my favourite words"):

:::list{counter="lower-roman"}
1. *yr*: fine drizzle, almost mist, the kind that soaks you without seeming to fall
2. *duskregn*: drizzle, a little heavier
3. *regnbyger*: showers, which here means a ten-minute break between rains
4. *styrtregn*: a downpour, the gutters roaring
5. *sludd*: sleet, rain that is thinking about becoming snow
6. *øsregn*: rain by the bucket, from *øse*, to bail out a boat
:::

I bought proper waterproof trousers. I stopped carrying an umbrella, because the wind here turns umbrellas inside out, and the bins in town are full of dead ones on a gusty day. I learned the saying everybody quotes at you: *det finnes ikke dårlig vær, bare dårlige klær*. There's no bad weather, only bad clothes.

## The last three days

19. Sideways, all day. The lane was a river. Biscuit refused to go out and looked at me as if it were my fault.
20. A fine grey *yr* from morning to night, so soft you could barely see it, and I was soaked through by the time I got to work.
21. A downpour at dawn, a pause, then rain again until midnight, steady and patient. I went out at ten at night and stood in it, just to be there for it.

## The day it stopped

On the 24th I woke up to silence and lay there wondering what was wrong. Then I realised: nothing was dripping. I opened the curtains on a blue sky, washed clean, every mountain sharp, every roof shining, and the whole of Sandviken came out on to its steps in the sun, blinking.
