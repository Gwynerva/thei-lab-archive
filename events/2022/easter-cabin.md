*Påske*: Easter, when the whole of Norway goes to the mountains, sits in the sun against a cabin wall, eats oranges and reads crime novels. The crime novels have a name, *påskekrim*. Even the milk cartons have crime stories on them.

Jonas's family has a cabin up on Kvamskogen, an hour and a bit from town: two rooms, a wood stove, an outhouse, snow to the windowsills. His mother lends it out like a library book. We had it for five days.

I packed:

- for skiing
  - borrowed skis, boots and poles (all Anna's, all slightly too big)
  - three pairs of gloves, because Jonas said three
- for not skiing
  - two crime novels in Norwegian from the library, of which I read eleven pages
  - Nana's thimble, out of habit
  - the cello's practice mute, but not the cello, which was pointless

Cross-country skiing, it turns out, looks like walking and isn't. Day one I fell over forty times. Day two, thirty. Day three I fell over going *uphill*, which Jonas says he has never seen anyone do. Day four I went eight kilometres without falling and got a round of applause from a family of strangers.

In the evenings: the stove, waffles, cards by candlelight, a great deal of *kos*. Not much reading.
