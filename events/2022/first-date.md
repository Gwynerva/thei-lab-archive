Saturday in the workshop again: cutting out the forms, the plywood shapes that will hold the curve of the hull, and sanding their edges until Jonas stopped running his thumb along them and frowning.

At four, when the light went, he took an old cast-iron waffle iron down from a shelf. His grandmother's. Heart-shaped, five hearts to a round. He made the batter in a bowl he had clearly also used for epoxy at some point (washed, he swears), and cooked them on the wood stove, turning the iron by its long handles. We ate them with brown cheese and *rømme* and strawberry jam, sitting on upturned buckets by the stove with the radio on.

We talked until eight. About boats, mostly, and then about not boats: his mother's orchard in Hardanger, Dad's shop, Nana, how he ended up building boats, how I ended up here.

He walked me to the bus. It was raining, of course. I'm not sure whether that was a date.

It was a date. I've decided it was a date.
