The festival is on: two weeks of concerts all over the city, in halls and churches and down on the fish market. Jonas had two tickets for a string quartet in an old stone church on Saturday evening, with the light still coming in through the high windows at nine.

Four young players from somewhere in central Europe, whose names I didn't catch. Haydn first, which was cheerful, and then the Mozart quartet in D minor, and in the slow movement the cellist played a line so quietly that the whole church leaned in to hear it. The rain started on the roof halfway through. Nobody minded. It sounded like part of it.

I've hardly played since I came. The cello stands in the corner of the flat in its case like a piece of furniture. Watching her, I wanted it back so badly my hands hurt.

::audio[The slow movement they played, from a recording I found afterwards; theirs was better]{file="music-quartet-1" title="Andante, quartet in D minor"}

Walking home, I told Jonas about the Leith Scratch Orchestra, about Mrs Ogilvie, about [my first concert in twelve years](ref:event:first-concert-2018). He said there's a string orchestra in Sandviken that rehearses in the school on Tuesday nights. "You should ask," he said. I might.
