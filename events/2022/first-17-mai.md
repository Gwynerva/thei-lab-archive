*Syttende mai*, Norway's national day, and I had no idea. I thought: a parade, some flags. It isn't a parade. It's the whole country in its best clothes, out on the streets from early morning until late, shouting *hurra*.

At eight the :hint[buekorps]{note="Bergen's old neighbourhood boys' brigades, which march with drums and wooden crossbows, each with its own uniform"} came drumming down our lane, small boys in caps with wooden crossbows on their shoulders, the drums going like a heartbeat, and I stood on the steps in my dressing gown with my mouth open.

Then the children's parade through the centre: every school in the city, every child with a flag, brass bands, two hours of it. Women in *bunad*, the embroidered folk costumes, each from a different valley, silver brooches flashing; men in *bunad* too. Mr Haldorsen in a three-piece suit with a ribbon on the lapel. I had a ribbon as well, which Anna pinned on me at the office yesterday so I'd "look less like a tourist".

Lunch was a hot dog wrapped in a waffle. Then ice cream. Then another hot dog. On the 17th, apparently, you eat whatever a child would choose.

::media[Hot dog, waffle: today's national dish]{file="waffles-2"}

*Gratulerer med dagen*, everyone said to everyone: happy birthday, to the country. I said it back about three hundred times. My voice has gone.
