An audition. At thirty. For an amateur string orchestra that rehearses in a school in Sandviken on Tuesday nights. I haven't been this nervous since my first rehearsal with the Scratch Orchestra, four years ago.

I asked in June, after the festival concert. The conductor wrote back: come in August, play something you love and something you don't, and we'll do some sight-reading. So all summer I practised with the window open, and the whole lane heard every wrong note.

Something I love: the first part of the Bach prelude, the one I learned in Leith and [recorded in lockdown](ref:event:prelude-recorded). Something I don't: a page of three-octave scales, which I never will.

The hall smelled of floor polish. The conductor, a small woman with reading glasses on a chain, sat at a desk with the section leader beside her, and they both smiled, which was worse. My bow shook for the first four bars. Then the cello took over, the way it does, scratch shaped like Norway and all, and I forgot them.

::audio[The prelude the way it sounds in my head, not the way it sounded today]{file="music-bach-cello-1" title="Prelude, first suite"}

Sight-reading: a viola part, by mistake, in the wrong clef. I got through it.

They rang at six. I'm in. Back desk, inside, which means I turn the pages. First rehearsal on Tuesday.
