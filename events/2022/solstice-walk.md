The shortest day: the sun up at ten and gone by three, if you could see it, which we couldn't.

I've made twelve paper lanterns over the last fortnight, at the kitchen table in the evenings, out of thin paper, willow strips and glue, each one a different shape: stars, a boat, a fish, a moon, and a house with fourteen tiny windows I cut out with a scalpel. Little battery candles inside, because I'm not walking twelve open flames through a wooden neighbourhood.

At five, in the dark, we walked them up the lane: me and Jonas, Mr Haldorsen with the boat, the two little girls from across the road with a star each, their parents, and a man from the end house who just joined in. Twelve lights going up the hill in the drizzle, round the bend, up to the viewpoint, where we stood with the whole city lit up below us and the lanterns bobbing.

Nobody made a speech. Somebody said, "*Nå snur det.*" Now it turns. From tomorrow the days get longer.
