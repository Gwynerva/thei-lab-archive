Two nights of cloud. Two nights of standing in car parks staring at a forecast app, then at the sky, then at the app. Priya kept saying, "It's not a performance, Mira, it doesn't owe you anything," and I kept saying I know, and checking again.

Tonight, at about ten, the cloud cleared from the west.

## How it started

At first I thought it was cloud lit up by a town: a pale greyish arc low over the mountains to the north. Then it brightened. Then it went green, the green you only otherwise see in a highlighter pen, and it started to move.

That's the thing nobody tells you properly. It moves. Not slowly, like cloud. It ripples, it folds over itself like a curtain in a draught, it pours down from overhead in bands, it fades in one place and flares in another. For a few minutes right overhead it went pink at the edges and moved so fast that I ducked, which Priya will never let me forget.

We lay on the bonnet of the hire car in every layer we owned and watched it for two hours. We didn't say much. Priya said "oh" about forty times. I said "oh" about the same.

## Why it moves

Priya, who is a scientist and can't help herself, explained it on the bonnet: particles from the sun caught in the earth's magnetic field, pouring down near the poles and making the thin air glow, green from oxygen lower down, red and pink higher up. I've read [the explanation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora "for when I want it explained again") twice since. I understand it. It doesn't help. It still looks as if the sky is alive.

## The star

At about midnight, when it had faded to a soft green haze, I told Priya the story of my name. Grandad Arthur, on the deck of a ship one night in the fifties, pointing Nana to a star in the Whale: a star that brightens and fades over most of a year, and disappears, and comes back. Mira. She told me the story when I was small, and never told it the same way twice, except for one sentence.

:::quote[Nana June, about the star I'm named after]{align="center"}
The wonderful one. It comes and goes, but it always comes back.
:::

Priya was quiet for a while, and then she said, "That's you and the cello," and then, "That's you and everything," and I didn't trust myself to answer.

We drove back to the cabin at one with the heater on full. I couldn't sleep. I kept getting up to look out of the window.
