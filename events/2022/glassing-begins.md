Glassing day. The hull has been stitched with copper wire and filleted along its seams since the spring, and now it gets its skin: a layer of fibreglass cloth laid over the whole outside, wetted through with epoxy until the cloth disappears and the wood shows through as if it were under water.

We laid the cloth on dry first and smoothed it down with our palms, from the middle out, like making a bed with a very expensive sheet. Then Jonas mixed the epoxy in small batches, two parts resin to one of hardener, stirred for exactly a minute by the clock, and I spread it with a plastic squeegee, working it into the weave. Too little and the cloth goes white. Too much and it runs and sags. You have about twenty minutes before it starts to go off.

It took five hours. My arms are dead. The workshop smells of resin, a sweet chemical smell that gets into your hair and your dreams.

And the plywood under the glass: I wasn't ready for it. The okoume has gone a deep, glowing honey colour, every line of the grain showing, and when the light from the window hits it, it looks alive. Jonas walked round it twice and said, "*Nå er det en båt.*" Now it's a boat.
