A letter from Fergus today: real paper, a real stamp, his slanting handwriting on the bindery's headed paper. He's closing Lowe Bindery at the end of the month. He's past seventy, his eyes aren't what they were, the rent has gone up, and, he writes, "the world wants its notebooks cheaper than I can sew them".

The presses are going to a college. And inside the letter, wrapped in a sheet of marbled paper, blue and gold, the last from the last tray, was his old bone folder from the bench. "For whatever you make next," he wrote. "Use the paper for the endpapers."

He ends:

> Keep your stitches even and your knots small. You were the only student I ever had who unpicked her own work without being told.\
> Yours, with best wishes to the cat,\
> Fergus

::media[What he sent: the bone folder, worn pale by forty years of his hand]{file="bookbinding-tools-1"}

I sat on the steps with it for a long time. I learned to bind books in that room on Tuesday nights in 2017, with rain on the skylight and the smell of glue. It feels as if someone has taken a room out of a house I used to live in.

I'll write back properly, on paper, tomorrow.
