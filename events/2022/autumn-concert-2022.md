The autumn concert, in the church in Sandviken, my first with the *Strykeorkester*. Three months of Tuesdays in the school hall, learning the music and the words for the music: *opptakt*, upbeat; *nedstrøk*, down-bow; *fra begynnelsen*, from the top, said by the conductor about a hundred times a night.

The programme: a suite by Grieg, because this is Bergen and it's the law, and Elgar's Serenade for Strings, which I've loved since I first heard it at sixteen.

The church was full. Mr Haldorsen in the front pew in his suit. Anna and half the office. Jonas in the second row, too tall, with his knees against the pew in front.

In the slow movement of the Elgar there's a place where the cellos carry the tune for a few bars, quietly, under everything. My hands didn't shake. I looked up from the music for a moment and the whole church was listening, and for the length of those bars I was nowhere but there.

::audio[The first movement of the Elgar, in a recording far better than us]{file="music-strings-1" title="Serenade for Strings"}

Afterwards, coffee and cake in the church hall, as after every concert in every country. A woman I'd never met told me "*du spilte så fint*", you played so beautifully, and I said *takk* and didn't explain that she meant all twelve of us.
