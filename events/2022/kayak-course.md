I signed up in December, because of my resolution, and because I wanted to be on the water here instead of always looking at it. Beginners' weekend, two days, all kit provided. "January?" said Anna. "You are brave or stupid." I'm finding out which.

## Saturday, in a pool

Day one was in a swimming pool on the other side of town, eight of us in borrowed kayaks bumping into each other under the lifeguard's eye. First the wet exit: you capsize on purpose, hang upside down for a moment in the blue silence, pull the loop on the spray deck and slide out. I came up spluttering and laughing, with water in places water shouldn't be.

Then strokes: forward, sweep, draw, and the low brace, where you lean on the water as if it were a table. My table kept collapsing.

The instructor is a boatbuilder, it turns out. Jonas. Tall, beard, patient, with a voice that doesn't rise even when someone hits him with a paddle (me, once, sorry). He has a way of watching what your hands do and then saying one sentence that fixes it. "Push with the top hand, don't pull with the bottom." It worked at once. I was irrationally pleased.

## Sunday, on the sea

Day two we went out on the fjord from a little beach west of the city: water at four degrees, air at two, flat calm, a sky the colour of pewter. Dry suits, three layers underneath, mitts on the paddles. We paddled along the shore for two hours, past boathouses and rocks with weed like hair, and a seal watched us from a skerry with total contempt.

::media[Kayaks on a Norwegian shore, the way I'll always picture that beach now]{file="kayak-fjord-1"}

I've never been so close to the water. In Whitby we always looked down on it, from the pier, from the cliffs, from Dad's shop. Here you're level with it. You feel every ripple through the seat.

## Jonas

At the break, on the beach, with coffee out of a flask, I said something about Dad's boat shop and how I'd always wanted to build a boat, ever since I was small, sanding things in the back of the chandlery. Jonas said he builds them. Wooden ones. Kayaks mostly, in a workshop in town where he teaches.

"Then build one," he said. Just like that, as if it were a sandwich. "I have a workshop. You bring the plywood."

I laughed. He didn't. I think he meant it.

On the way back I capsized once, on my own, for no reason at all, and he towed my kayak in while I swam the last bit in a dry suit, which is like swimming in a bin bag. Everyone clapped.
