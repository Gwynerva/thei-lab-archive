*Sankthansaften*, Midsummer Eve: bonfires all along the fjord, on beaches and rocks and in back gardens, on the night the sun barely sets.

Ours was on the rocks below the old battery, built over a week by the neighbours out of driftwood, pallets, a broken chair and a Christmas tree somebody had kept since January for exactly this. Jonas, a professional with wood, was put in charge of lighting it. It wouldn't light. Mr Haldorsen watched him for ten minutes with his hands in his pockets, then lit it with one match and a page of newspaper, and said nothing, which was worse than saying something.

Then it went up properly, sparks over the water, children running round it, somebody's grandmother singing. Boats came in close to watch. The sky never got darker than a deep blue. At one in the morning I could still read the sign on the battery wall.

Walked home up the lane smelling of smoke, at what felt like four in the afternoon and was in fact half past one.
