We drove to :hint[Hardanger]{note="the fjord and the district around it, south-east of Bergen, known for its fruit orchards"} to see the blossom, as all of western Norway does in mid-May. I'd heard about it so often I expected to be disappointed.

I wasn't. On every slope along the fjord that faces the sun there are orchards, apple and pear and cherry and plum, and for about two weeks a year they're all in flower at once, white and pale pink above the green water, with snow still on the mountains above. The air smells of it. The bees are out of their minds.

And there was Sigrid, Jonas's mother, at the farm near Utne: small, brown from the sun, hands like tree roots, in a man's jumper with holes at the elbows. She shook my hand, looked at me hard, and took me out to the trees to show me which ones were good and which were "lazy".

> An apple tree is like a man. If you don't cut it back hard every winter, it grows tall and lazy and gives you nothing.

She said it in Norwegian, slowly, so I'd understand, and then looked at Jonas. Jonas looked at the sky.

Up the hill above the farm there's an old white house with more windows than I could count, where Jonas's great-aunt lives. "She's eighty-eight," Sigrid said. "She'll outlive us all."

::media[One branch, up close]{file="hardanger-fruit-2"}

We drove home with a box of last year's cider in the boot. I'm to come back in October, Sigrid says, for the apples.
