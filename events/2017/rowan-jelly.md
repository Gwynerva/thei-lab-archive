Down to Robin Hood's Bay for the weekend, because Nana rang to say the rowan was "dripping with berries" and she needed young knees on the ladder.

So I picked, and she supervised. Three pounds of berries off the tree in her garden, stalks off one by one at the kitchen table while she told me about every jar of jelly she's made since 1951. Then crab apples from the lane, chopped with the cores in, and the whole lot simmered till the kitchen smelled sour and red and wonderful.

The jelly bag hung all night off an upturned stool between two chairs, dripping into a bowl. I was not allowed to squeeze it. Nobody is allowed to squeeze it.

> Make do and mend, and never squeeze the bag.

She says the first half is her mother's and the second half is hers.

Sunday morning we boiled the juice with the sugar, did the wrinkle test on a cold saucer, and potted eight jars. She sent me home with four, and the card, which I've copied out exactly as she wrote it, capitals and all.
