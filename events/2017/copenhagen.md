Priya had a reading week, I had a reading week, and flights from Edinburgh were thirty-eight pounds each. So: Copenhagen! My first trip abroad in two years.

## Friday

Landed at lunchtime, Metro into town, dumped our bags at the hostel and walked straight to Nyhavn, because you have to. It's exactly like the pictures and also much colder than the pictures. We had hot chocolate in a bar with a blanket on every chair, which is apparently normal here. Of course it is.

## The bikes

Saturday we hired bikes, and oh. Oh! Cycle lanes as wide as a road, with their own kerbs and their own traffic lights, and everybody on a bike: men in suits, grannies, mums with three children in a box at the front. Nobody in Lycra. Nobody shouting. I nearly cried at a junction.

We rode out to the Little Mermaid (tiny, crowded, fine), round the lakes, through Christiania (no photos), and over a bridge that's only for bikes and people. Priya kept stopping to look at ducks in the harbour. Marine biologists, honestly.

In the afternoon we climbed the Round Tower, which has no stairs, only a brick ramp spiralling up inside, wide enough for a horse and cart (a king once rode up it, apparently). From the top the whole city lies flat around you: green copper spires, red roofs, the harbour, the bridge to Sweden faint in the haze. I drew it on a napkin. It's the best map I've made all year and it has a coffee ring on it.

Dinner was open sandwiches, piled so high you eat them with a knife and fork: herring, egg, prawns, a lot of dill. Priya had three. I had two and regretted not having three.

## Sunday

Rain. Proper rain. We went to the national museum and spent four hours with the Vikings and a bog body, then ate open sandwiches at the airport that cost twelve pounds each and were worth it.

## What I'd tell myself

- Pack:
  - a proper waterproof, not the cute one
  - gloves, two pairs (one will be lost)
  - a padlock for the hostel locker
- Cycle everywhere. Walk the rest.
- Don't convert prices in your head. It only hurts.

Came home with a fridge magnet, a cold, and a very strong opinion about cycle lanes in Edinburgh, which I have since shared with everyone at the Spoke Room, twice.
