First night of [the bookbinding course](ref:section:coptic-notebooks/the-course)! Lowe Bindery is a tiny workshop in Stockbridge with a big iron press in the window and a bell on the door. Fergus Lowe runs it. He is about seventy, wears a brown apron, and says "aye, well" before everything, including "aye, well, that's wrong".

There are six of us round one table. Tonight we folded paper. That's it. Two hours of folding paper. And it was brilliant, because there's a right way: with the grain, with a bone folder, one firm stroke, never two.

::media[My bone folder, my precious]{file="bookbinding-tools-1"}

Then we gathered our folded sheets into a :hint[signature]{note="a few sheets folded together and sewn as one; nothing to do with your name"} and Fergus showed us how to pierce the sewing holes with an awl through a little card template. Next week: sewing.

He gave us a reading list. The first thing on it is over a hundred years old and he says it's still the best.

::file[The first thing on Fergus's reading list]{file="doc-bookbinding-1" title="Bookbinding, and the care of books"}

I came home and folded every scrap of paper in the flat. Priya has asked me to stop.
