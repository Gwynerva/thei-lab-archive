I've been staring at this cliff for five months, in photos and maps and point clouds that don't exist yet, and today I watched it fall.

We were packing the drone away at about four when there was a crack like somebody dropping a plank in an empty hall. I looked up, and a slab of the clay above the rock, maybe the size of a car, leaned out from the face, slowly, almost politely, and slid. Then the dust, and a long grinding rattle as it broke up on the ledges below, and then nothing at all except the sea.

Sam said a word I won't write down. I didn't say anything. My hands were shaking.

Nobody was below, thank goodness, and the path at the top was fine. But there's a fresh brown scar now where yesterday there was grass, and that scar is going into my dissertation with a date and a time: 16.07.

I rang Dad. He said "aye, she does that". The sea is a she, to Dad.
