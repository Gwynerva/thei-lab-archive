We left Lerwick in a flat calm on the eighteenth, motoring out past Bressay with the sails up and doing nothing, the sea like grey silk all the way to Norway.

> Never trust a calm sea.

Nana said it every time we went down to the harbour, and she was married to a man who went to sea, so she knew. The calm lasted a day. On the second night the wind came round to the north-west and got up, and up, and by morning we were reefed down to a handkerchief and running before a following sea that lifted the stern and set it down, lifted and set down, for eighteen hours. Grey water everywhere, and green water once, over the cockpit and down my neck. Jonas helmed most of it and sang, badly, to keep himself awake.

I was not frightened, which surprised me. I was tired and wet and very busy, and somewhere in the second night, clipped on in the cockpit with a mug of tea I could not drink, I thought: I am crossing it. The sea I have looked at from Whitby my whole life. Not over it. On it.

We raised the Norwegian coast at dawn on the twentieth, the islands low and dark and then suddenly green, were tied up in Bergen by the afternoon, and asleep in the car on the ferry by six.
