## Five months

[Taking the old one out](<ref:event:kitchen-demolition#h:What came out> "the list of what went, in November") took a day and a half. Putting a new one in took from the fourth of November to this morning, the day after the equinox, which Jonas says is a coincidence and I say is not.

Most of it was not dramatic. It was evenings. Pipes in December, which a plumber from Kinsarvik did while explaining at length why the last plumber had been wrong, in about 1974. Electrics in January. The floor in February: the old boards lifted and numbered in pencil on the back, the joists under them doubled where they had to be, and every board laid back in its own place, the way you put a book back on its shelf. Then oil, three coats, a day between.

In between, the atlas. I drew walks twenty-one to twenty-six at a trestle table in the parlour, in fingerless gloves, while Jonas cut tiles in the snow outside the window and the plumber's radio played in the next room. I have never been so behind on everything and so content about it.

::media[The boards, back down, with my feet for scale]{file="wooden-floor-1"}

## What went in

Jonas built the cupboards in the barn, in birch, the way he would build a boat: no screw anywhere a screw would show, every edge rounded so it will not catch your hip. The worktop is oak from a sawmill in Kvam, oiled like a deck. The sink is a deep white one, salvaged from a farm at Kinsarvik, that you could bath a small dog in.

- The old range stays in the corner, blacked and polished, and works. It is now the warmest place in the house, and Biscuit's.
- The pantry shelves stayed in the pantry. I won.
- One wall is mine, with a long shelf for the jam jars that came out of the old cupboard in November, because everything in this house comes round again.

## The first meal

Sigrid came, and the Nesheims, and we ate fish soup at the new table under the west window, in the evening light Ragnhild asked us to keep. I think we kept it. Kari asked what it had all cost and Jonas said *enough*, and poured her more cider.

Afterwards I washed up in a sink that drains into a pipe and not a bucket, and stood there with my hands in the hot water for much longer than washing up takes, watching the fjord go dark.

Make do and mend, Nana said. We did both. It took five months, and it is the best room I have ever stood in.
