## The morning

It rained in the night, hard, and I lay awake listening to it on the roof (over the stairs too, mended now) and thinking, well, that is that. At six it stopped. At seven the sun came over the mountain behind us and lit the fjord, every apple in the orchard was wet and shining, and Folgefonna was out across the water as if it had been booked.

Mum and Clare and Priya hung the bunting in the orchard before breakfast, rows of it looped from tree to tree, made from the scraps in Nana's sewing box: her blue print, the yellow check from the quilt, a strip of the curtain from the cottage at Robin Hood's Bay. Mum cut the triangles in Whitby in the spring and Clare sewed them on her machine in Leeds. I did not know until I came down the steps and saw it.

## Under the Gravenstein

We were married at eleven under the old Gravenstein by the road, the one Ragnhild's father planted, by a woman from the council who did it in Norwegian and then again in English for Dad, who had asked her to. There were forty-one of us on the slope. Jonas wore his good shirt and a waistcoat Sigrid had made him wear. I wore Nana's brooch, and a dress, and boots, because it is an orchard.

Three from the orchestra played, two violins and a viola, Elgar's serenade, slowly, while I walked down between the trees with Dad. He said nothing all the way down, and squeezed my hand at the bottom, which is the most Dad has ever said about anything.

The rings are gold. The copper one is in a little box on the kitchen shelf, between the jam jars.

## The rest of the day

Long tables in the orchard. Fish soup and lamb and Sigrid's cake and Anna's cake, which were in competition and both won. Ragnhild, brought over from Odda by Eirik for three hours, sat at the top of the slope in the shade with a blanket over her knees, receiving people like a queen. She held my face in both her hands and said the house was happy. I believe her.

:::quote[Priya's toast, the end of it]{align="center"}
She draws a map of everywhere she goes. I think this is the first place she has drawn without needing to know the way out.
:::

Dancing on the grass when the light went gold. Isla asleep on a pile of coats by nine. Biscuit on the porch all day, watching it with great disapproval and eating whatever fell.

At midnight Jonas and I walked down to the bottom gate and looked back up at the house with every one of its fourteen windows lit, and the bunting moving a little in the dark between the trees.
