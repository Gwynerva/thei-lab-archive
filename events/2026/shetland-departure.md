We slipped the lines at six in the evening with the forecast as good as a forecast for the North Sea in June ever is: west-south-west, three to four, easing. Two of us aboard, Jonas and me, food for a week, and, for once, no cello.

Out past Nordnes, with a man in the sea bath on the point waving. Out past the last islands. A cruise ship leaving the harbour behind us gave three blasts on its horn, which I chose to take personally.

::audio[Three blasts as it passed, which I am choosing to believe were for us]{file="sound-ship-horn" title="The horn"}

I have crossed the North Sea a dozen times in aeroplanes, looking down at it. I stood at the end of the pier in Whitby looking east at it for my whole childhood. Tonight I am on it, at five knots, on watch from midnight, and Shetland is a hundred and ninety nautical miles of it away.

Dad rang as we cleared the coast. He asked about the barometer and the reefing lines and whether I had a knife on me. Then he said, *your Nana would have loved this*, and had to go.
