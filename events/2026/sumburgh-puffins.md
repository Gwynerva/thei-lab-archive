Down to the very south end on the bus, and out along the head to the lighthouse in a wind that made talking optional.

And then puffins. Dozens of them on the grassy lip of the cliffs, a few metres from the path, standing about in pairs like small men in dinner jackets waiting for a bus that is late. Some came in from the sea at speed, orange feet out, landing as if they had never done it before. Some had beakfuls of grass for their burrows and looked very busy about it.

::media[One with a beakful, who stood still for exactly one picture]{file="puffin-3"}

Below them, on the ledges, rows of guillemots and the odd razorbill, and the noise of the whole colony coming up the cliff on the wind.

::audio[The colony from the wall, the gulls loudest of all]{file="sound-gulls-1" title="Sumburgh Head"}

Jonas, who has paddled under the bird cliffs at Runde more than once and is not easily impressed by a puffin, sat on the wall for an hour and said nothing at all. I count that.
