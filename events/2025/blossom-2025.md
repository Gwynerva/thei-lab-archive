It happened in two days. On Saturday the trees were sticks with green tips; on Monday morning the whole slope below the house was white, the fjord turquoise underneath it, and I stood on the porch with my coffee going cold and could not think of anything to do except stand there.

Sigrid came up in the afternoon to walk the rows with me and name the trees. Twenty-two Gravensteins, nine Aroma, five Summerred, two old pear trees pretending to be apples, and two that nobody, not even Sigrid, can name. She was put out about those two.

:::quote[Sigrid, on pruning, secateurs in hand]{align="center"}
You prune for the tree you want in ten years, not the one you have. The tree will forgive you. The apples will not, if you are timid.
:::
