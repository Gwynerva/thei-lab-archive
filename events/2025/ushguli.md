## The last two days

After the river the trek stopped being a walk and became a place we lived in for a while. The two days I have not written down yet, for the record:

3. Adishi to Iprari, over the Chkhutnieri pass, after the horses. A long climb in sun, the glacier behind us smaller every time I turned round, then a meadow at the top with everything we had walked on one side and everything we had not on the other. Down through birch and a smell of hay into Iprari, where the guesthouse mother gave us soup and a look of pity.
4. Iprari to Ushguli, the easiest day and the one I will remember: a dirt road along the river, the valley opening, and the towers of Ushguli coming up out of it one by one like people standing to see who has arrived.

## Ushguli

It is said to be one of the highest places in Europe where people live all year. It looks it. Four hamlets of stone houses and towers on a green valley floor, the slope behind them bare, and at the end of the valley, filling it, Shkhara: a ridge of snow and ice so big it does not look far away, only large.

We arrived at three, dropped the packs and walked up the valley towards the glacier until our legs said no. Cows on the track. A boy on a horse with no saddle. Hay drying on wooden frames. A church on a knoll with a wall round it and frescoes inside, faded to the colours of the hills.

## What I want to keep

Not the view; there are photographs of the view. The things around it.

- The weight of the pack coming off on the guesthouse step, and how light I felt for an hour after, like walking on the moon.
- Jonas asleep at seven in the evening with his boots still on.
- The woman at the guesthouse, who has lived here all her life, saying that the winter is eight months long and the road closes and she would not live anywhere else. I believed her. I know what it is to look at a mountain every morning and need it.
- Four days, four villages, sixty kilometres, one river, no blisters. The knee held.

Tomorrow the jeep back to Mestia, and one more walk, to the other glacier. Then home, to fourteen windows and a roof I have not thought about for a whole week.
