The cards went out on the sixth: a linocut of the house with all fourteen windows lit, which took three evenings to cut and one to print, and which Mum has already put on her mantelpiece next to the abbey.

On the twenty-third Jonas brought a tree in from the slope above the well, a little crooked, which is how we like them. On the twenty-fourth Sigrid came with the pinnekjøtt she had been steaming since morning in her own kitchen, and finished it on ours, on the old range in the corner, its first proper job in years. Eirik fetched Ragnhild from Odda for the afternoon. She sat by the parlour stove in her good cardigan, looked round the room and said it was warmer than in 1950, which I think was praise.

At five the bells rang down in Utne for *julaften*, and we ate, and Whitby rang on the laptop: Mum and Dad in paper hats, Clare and Dev, and Isla, who held up a drawing of a cat and said *Bis-kit* very loudly, which made Biscuit leave the room.

*God jul*, everyone. I am writing this on the twenty-sixth by the stove, in the afternoon dark, full of things.

:::private
And on the twenty-fourth, after the dishes, in the orchard. I will write it down properly somewhere only I can read it. For now, one word: yes.
:::
