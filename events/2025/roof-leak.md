Three days of rain off the North Sea, the proper autumn kind, and at two in the morning a sound on the stairs like a clock that had lost its rhythm. Drip. Drip. Pause. Drip.

The water was coming through the ceiling over the turn of the stairs, exactly where the roofer had looked in April and said nothing. We put the jam pan under it, then the washing-up bowl, then both. Biscuit sat on the top step and watched the drops with professional interest until nearly four.

::media[The stairs, with the washing-up bowl in its new home]{file="staircase"}

In the morning Jonas went up into the loft with a torch and came down grey with dust and said one word in Norwegian I will not write here. Battens gone, tiles slipped, and the board underneath soft enough to push a thumb into. He has nailed a tarpaulin over it from inside, and the roofer will come when the rain stops, which in Hardanger in October may be never.

:::private
What he quoted for the whole stairs side is more than we have until the atlas money comes in the spring. I did the sums twice at the table and then went and stood in the orchard in the rain for a while, which did not change the sums but helped.
:::

Rain: day 3. I had stopped counting when we left Bergen. I have started again.
