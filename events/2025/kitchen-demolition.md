Everything we own in the barn, everything left in plastic sheeting, and at eight on Monday morning Jonas handed me a crowbar and said I should have the first go, since it was my idea.

It was my idea. It came out faster than seven months of living with it had led me to expect.

## What came out

- [x] the cupboard doors held shut with a bent nail each
- [x] the cooker that has not cooked since about 2009
- [x] the sink and its bucket, which I will not miss
- [x] three layers of lino, the bottom one brown and orange squares that Ragnhild chose in 1974 and was proud of
- [x] a wall cupboard full of jam jars, all empty, all clean, all with lids
- [ ] the old range in the corner, which stays, because Ragnhild asked, and because it is beautiful
- [ ] the pantry shelves, still under discussion

Under the last of the lino there are boards, wide pine ones, the floor of 1891, grey and dry and sound. We stood and looked at them for a long time.

By Tuesday afternoon the kitchen was a bare room with a range in the corner and a lot of dust, and we were cooking on the camping stove again, like in April. It feels like going backwards. Jonas says it is the only way forwards in an old house: take it back to the boards and start from what was true.
