Eirik and Kari Nesheim brought their press up on a trailer at nine, a wooden screw press older than both of them together, and by ten the orchard was full: the Nesheims, Sigrid with three crates and her opinions, Anna and her husband over from Bergen on the early ferry, two neighbours I had only ever waved at, and a great many wasps.

A proper *dugnad*. Everyone picks, someone washes, someone feeds the mill, and the strongest person available turns the screw. That was Jonas, for six hours, with breaks for coffee and waffles.

The Gravensteins went in first, then the Aroma, then a little of everything else, and the juice came out cloudy and gold and tasting of the whole summer at once. Sigrid tasted from every carboy, frowned, and said nothing, which Jonas says is the highest praise she has.

In the end: **212 litres** from forty trees, in nine carboys in the cellar. Now we wait. Sigrid says it will be ready when it is ready, and that if I open a carboy to look, she will know.
