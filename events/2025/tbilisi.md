A whole day in Tbilisi before the mountains, and we spent it on foot, which is how I learn a city.

Up to Narikala in the morning heat for the view: the city laid out like a map someone has crumpled, the Kura in its gorge, the domes of the bathhouses in Abanotubani like loaves set out to rise. Down by the steps to the baths, for an hour in water that smells of eggs and leaves your skin feeling new. Past the clock tower by the puppet theatre, which leans as if it has heard something interesting.

Lunch was the round khachapuri, quartered, with lemon tea. Jonas, who has opinions about cheese, had no opinions afterwards, only another one.

::media[Lunch, and the end of Jonas's opinions about cheese]{file="khachapuri-1"}

Late in the afternoon we went into a small church to get out of the sun, and a few people at the front were singing. Three voices, then more, the parts leaning on each other the way the old balconies over the gorge lean on their beams. I stood at the back and did not move until it was over. A woman told us afterwards what it was.

::audio[Shen Khar Venakhi, Thou Art a Vineyard: the hymn they were singing]{file="music-georgian" title="Thou art a vineyard"}

Tomorrow night, the train to Zugdidi, and then the mountains.
