Jonas drove us up to Odda to see Ragnhild, with a bag of the first rhubarb and a drawing I had made of the house from the bottom of the orchard. She is ninety and sits very straight. She held the drawing at arm's length for a long time, then pointed at the third window from the left on the upper floor and said that was where she was born.

She told us things the house will never tell us itself. That the barn was rebuilt in 1931 after a winter took its roof. That her father planted the row of Gravensteins by the road the year she was confirmed. That the well never once ran dry, not even in 1947.

> Houses are like old people. They do not mind being mended, as long as you ask them first.

I asked her what she wanted us to keep. She thought about it, and said the clock, and the lilac by the door, and the way the light comes into the kitchen in March. I told her we would do our best with the light.

On the way home I wrote it all down for [who lived here](ref:section:farmhouse/ragnhild "her ninety years, and the ones before"), before I could lose the order she said it in.
