The roofer came over from Kinsarvik at eight, a man of few words and three ladders, and spent two hours walking the ridge of our house as if it were a pavement. I watched from the orchard with my heart somewhere near my throat. Jonas went up after him, because of course he did.

What he found, in the order he said it:

- the tiles are the old clay ones, and most of them are good for another fifty years
- the battens under them are not, on the north side
- the flashing round the chimney is "a hope rather than a plan"
- over the stairs, something he did not like the look of and would not say more about

He will patch the north side in May, before the walking season and his holiday. The rest, he said, will hold one more winter. Then, after a pause long enough for the fjord to change colour: *kanskje*. Maybe.

I wrote it all down in the house notebook and drew the roof from above, the bad bits hatched. It looks like a map of a country with a difficult border.
