Walk seventeen of the atlas, and the one people will buy the book for, so it has to be right.

We left the car at Fossli at eight, before the coaches, and took the path along the edge of the gorge with the falls below us on the left, water dropping a hundred and sixty metres into Måbødalen and turning into weather on the way down. [The falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%B8ringsfossen "the numbers, which I checked twice") are loud in a way that gets into your chest. I stood on the new bridge over the top and recorded them until a man with a selfie stick needed my place.

::audio[Vøringsfossen from the bridge, before the coaches]{file="sound-waterfall" title="The falls"}

Then up and away from all of it, onto the plateau, and in twenty minutes there was nobody: heather, a tarn, a reindeer track, the sky three times the size it is at home. The vidda is a different country from the fjord. Flat, brown, enormous, snow still lying in the hollows in July.

For the map I need the car park, the bridge, the safe edge and the unsafe one, the turn at the cairn, the tarn where we ate. Twelve kilometres, four hours with stops. I drew it that evening while it was still in my legs.
