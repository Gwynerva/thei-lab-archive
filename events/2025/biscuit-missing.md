He went out after supper the way he has every evening since May, down to the long grass under the Gravensteins to look at things only he can see, and he did not come back.

It barely gets dark here in June. That made it worse, somehow: at one in the morning I could see the whole orchard in the grey light and not one ginger cat in it. Jonas walked the road to the bottom with a torch he did not need. I stood at the back door shaking the treat tin, which has never once failed. It failed.

I lay down at three without sleeping. At ten past five there was the noise at the door, the short indignant one, and there he was on the step, wet to the belly, a bit of hay in his ruff, entirely unbothered and wanting breakfast. He had been in the barn loft, Jonas thinks, which has mice and a broken board and no way down that a cat of nine likes to use until he has to.

I held him for longer than he wanted. He allowed it, and then ate two breakfasts.
