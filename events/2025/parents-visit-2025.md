They came on the Saturday ferry with two suitcases, one of which was entirely jars.

Mum found the redcurrant bushes behind the barn on the first morning, before I had shown her anything, and by Monday there were eleven jars of jam on the windowsill, made on the camping stove in a pan borrowed from Sigrid. They have known each other three days. They talk every day now, Mum in English and Sigrid in a kind of English she keeps for people she has decided to like.

Dad spent the week at the bottom of the orchard with his watch and a stick marked in tenths, measuring the tide. A metre, on a good day. *Barely worth the name*, he said, disgusted, and then measured it again every day until they left.

On Wednesday I came round the barn and found him on a ladder at the pantry window with Jonas holding the bottom, which was not what the cardiologist meant by gentle exercise. He said he was only looking. He was not only looking.

On the last evening the four of us sat on the porch until half eleven in a light that would not go, and nobody said much, and it was the best evening of the summer.
