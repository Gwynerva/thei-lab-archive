Four hours in a minibus from Zugdidi, up a valley that kept getting narrower and greener and steeper, the driver overtaking on bends with a calm I would like for myself. Then Mestia, and the towers.

There are dozens of them, square and grey and taller than they have any need to be, standing among the houses like a crowd waiting for something. Every family had one, to climb into when there was a feud or an avalanche. Some are nine hundred years old. From our guesthouse balcony I counted twenty-three before the peaks behind turned pink and then nothing.

::media[The welcome committee, in the lane outside the guesthouse]{file="mestia-4"}

Jonas is packing and repacking his rucksack on the bed. I have laid the map of the trek out on the floor and written the villages beside it in a little column: Zhabeshi, Adishi, Iprari, Ushguli. Four days, four villages. My knee is fine. I keep telling it so.
