Day two was short: Zhabeshi to Adishi by lunchtime, over a ridge and down into a village of a few houses, three towers and a great many cows. So in the afternoon, instead of resting like sensible people, we walked up the valley to look at tomorrow.

Tomorrow is the river. It comes out of the bottom of Adishi glacier, a wall of broken blue ice hanging between black peaks, and runs grey and fast and cold across the valley floor in four or five braided channels. There is no bridge. In the morning there will be men with horses who carry walkers across for a few lari, and walkers who wade it and regret it.

We sat on a boulder below the icefall for an hour. Every so often something in the glacier cracked like a gun, and a little later the sound came back off the far wall. Jonas read the river the way he reads weather. I looked at the ice and thought about Folgefonna, which I see every morning from the kitchen and have never once been this close to.

*Later.* We took the horses. The water came up to the horse's belly, my boots stayed dry and my pride did not, and I would do it exactly the same again.
