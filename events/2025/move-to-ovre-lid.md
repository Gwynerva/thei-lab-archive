## The van

We hired the biggest van the place in Åsane would give us and filled it twice. Jonas drove. I sat beside him with Biscuit's carrier on the floor between my feet and the cello upright between my knees, which is not how anyone is supposed to sit in a van, and the cello did not mind. Out past Norheimsund, along the water, onto the ferry at Kvanndal. On the ferry Biscuit made one long low noise, the one he keeps for the vet, and then gave up and slept.

From the ferry Utne comes at you slowly: the white hotel, the boathouses, the orchards in rows up the slope, all of it still bare in March. Then the road up, the last bend, and the house.

## The first night

There are fourteen windows and not one curtain. A fjord at night is darker than anything in Bergen; there is no city on the other side, only Folgefonna, and you cannot see it, only feel that something large and white is there. We slept on the mattress on the floor of the room with two windows facing west, Biscuit between us, both of us in hats, because the chimney man has said no fire until he has been.

In the morning the glacier was there, exactly where Jonas said it would be, catching the sun first.

## What the house had kept

Ragnhild left more than she took. The clock in the parlour, which I wound and which started at once, as if it had been waiting. A drawer of string sorted by thickness. Forty years of seed packets, labelled in pencil. A calendar from 1987 on the back of the pantry door, with the days the ferry did not run crossed out.

The kitchen is a kitchen in name only: the cooker does not work, the sink drains into a bucket, and the cupboard doors are held shut with a bent nail each. We cook on the camping stove on the porch. Sigrid came on the second evening with a pot of fish soup and the opinion that we should have waited until May, and stayed to tell us which of the forty trees were worth keeping. All of them, in the end, after an argument with herself.

:hint[Øvre Lid]{note="the upper slope; the farm below it was simply Lid"} has a way of making you stop in doorways. I keep doing it. Jonas says I am measuring. I think I am listening.

## Five days

- Saturday: the first load, the ferry, the mattress on the floor
- Sunday: the second load; the rain came in sideways and we were glad of it, because it showed us where the roof is weakest
- Monday: Biscuit came out from under the bed and took the west window sill as his
- Tuesday: the boxes with maps on their lids went to the rooms on the maps, more or less
- Wednesday: we took the van back to Bergen and came home on the bus and the ferry, and I said *home* without noticing

Make do and mend, Nana used to say. On Wednesday evening I sewed the button back on Jonas's work shirt at the kitchen table, by the light of the camping lantern. It counts.
