The shortest day: six hours and fifty-seven minutes of daylight in Edinburgh, which is not much, so I thought I'd better see all of it.

Up Calton Hill in the dark with a flask and a cheese scone. About fifteen of us up there, in hats, stamping our feet by the unfinished monument, everyone pretending not to be excited. The sky went from navy to grey to a thin orange line, and at 8.42 exactly the sun came up over the shoulder of Arthur's Seat, and somebody clapped.

::media[Turning round on the hill as it came up, the city still in shadow]{file="video-edinburgh-2" layout="natural"}

From here on the days get longer. A few seconds tomorrow, then more, then more. I find that unreasonably comforting.

Hot chocolate on Leith Walk on the way home, and in bed by ten, like a farmer.
