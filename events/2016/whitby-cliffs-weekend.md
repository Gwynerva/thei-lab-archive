Home for the weekend, mostly to see Mum and Dad, partly to escape the library. I came back with a dissertation. I didn't plan that.

## Friday: the train

Edinburgh to York, York to Middlesbrough, Middlesbrough to Whitby on the little Esk Valley train that stops at every farm. Four and a half hours. I read two papers on coastal erosion and drew a map of the line in the margin of one of them. Dad picked me up at the station in the van that smells of rope.

## Saturday: Saltwick

Dad wanted to walk. Dad always wants to walk. We went along the West Cliff to the whalebone arch first, so he could tell me which hotels have lost their gardens since he was a boy, then over the swing bridge, up the 199 steps, past the abbey, and along the cliff path south to Saltwick Bay, with the wind coming straight off the sea and the caravans rattling.

Halfway along he stopped at a fence post and said, very casually:

> That post was ten yards further in when I was a lad. The path was on the other side of it.

I asked how he knew. He said he used to sit on it. That is not a measurement, but it's not *nothing* either.

::media[Black Nab from the cliff top, the ledges showing at low tide]{file="saltwick-bay-4" layout="stretch"}

At the bay itself you can see the cliff coming apart: clay slumped over the rock, fresh scars where bits have gone since the summer, fence posts hanging off the edge on their wire like loose teeth. Dad poked at a slump with his boot and said the sea was "having its tea".

## Sunday: the question

On the train home I wrote it down:

1. How fast is the cliff at Saltwick going back?
2. Has it sped up?
3. Can I measure it from old maps, aerial photos and something new?

The Ordnance Survey mapped this coast in 1853. There are aerial photos from the war onwards. The university has a drone, and a man called Sam who flies it. And Dad has a history of Whitby from 1817 on the shelf in the shop, which I've borrowed, scanned and will give back, Dad, I promise.\
(Probably.)

So that's it. Not "something about GIS and the coast", which is what my proposal said on Thursday. *How fast is Whitby falling into the sea?* Mum says it sounds gloomy. Dad says it sounds about right.
