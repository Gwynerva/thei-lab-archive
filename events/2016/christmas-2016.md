Home for Christmas. The train was packed, the Esk Valley line was late, and Whitby was exactly where I left it, which is the point of Whitby.

Christmas Eve in the shop with Dad, selling last-minute rope to people who absolutely did not need rope. Christmas morning at Mum and Dad's, Nana brought over from the Bay in the van in her best coat. Clare was on shift in A&E until Boxing Day, so we saved her a plate and she ate it standing up at the counter, still in her lanyard.

Nana noticed the loose button on my coat before I'd taken it off. She had a needle out of her handbag in about four seconds, sewed it on at the kitchen table while the sprouts boiled over, and bit the thread off like it had insulted her.

:::quote[Nana June, at the kitchen table]{align="center"}
A stitch in time is still a stitch, love. Doesn't matter when you do it.
:::

Boxing Day we walked the West Cliff to the whalebones and back, all of us, Nana on Dad's arm. Cold, bright, wind like a slap.\
Perfect.
