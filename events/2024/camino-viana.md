Rain from Esposende onwards: the warm, heavy Atlantic kind that soaks you from inside your jacket. Bergen rain is better organised.

Ines walked it with me, apologising for the weather every hour as if it were hers. Tomorrow, at Caminha, she goes back to Porto and work, and I go on alone. Twenty-five kilometres today, with the sea somewhere off to the left, invisible and loud.

Rain: day 1 of the Camino. I counted out of habit.

We came into Viana over the long iron bridge across the Lima with water running out of our sleeves, and I stood in the doorway of the cathedral for twenty minutes, under the carved saints, watching the square empty and fill. An old woman in black went in past me and patted my arm without looking, as if I were a dog she knew.

Albergue: bunk 14, a hot shower, socks on the radiator, *bacalhau* in a café where the television showed football and nobody watched.

My left heel has a blister the shape of Ireland. I've given it a name.
