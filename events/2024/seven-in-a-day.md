Two days after the last day at Nordvåg, I walked all seven.

I'd climbed each of them once, one at a time, between August 2021 and October 2022, and always meant to do them in one go, the way half of Bergen does in early summer with a number pinned to their chest. We didn't have numbers. Jonas and I left the door in Sandviken at five in the morning with two litres of water each, a bag of *skillingsboller* and a map I'd drawn the week before.

## The route

:::list{counter="lower-roman"}
1. Lyderhorn, first, from the west, after the early bus
2. Damsgårdsfjellet, along its ridge
3. Løvstakken, steep, with the whole city below
4. Ulriken, up the stairs, the highest at 643 metres
5. Rundemanen, across Vidden, which took three hours
6. Fløyen, a little way down, for waffles
7. Sandviksfjellet, the last, above our own roof
:::

## How it went

Lyderhorn at half six was ours alone, mist below us, the islands floating in it. Damsgårdsfjellet and Løvstakken went by in a blur of stairs and wet roots and Jonas eating bun after bun. Then Ulriken by the Sherpa stairs, built by men from Nepal, which everyone in Bergen tells you about as if they'd laid the stones themselves. On the top, at eleven, I sat by the mast and thought: four, more than halfway. I was wrong, because Vidden is long. It is the part that isn't a mountain at all but a plain of rock and bog and small tarns and wind, and it goes on, and on.

Somewhere on Vidden I stopped thinking about the last three years. Not in a grand way. My legs hurt and my feet were wet and there was no room left for anything else.

## The last one

Sandviksfjellet at seven in the evening. It's the smallest of them and it nearly finished me. From the top you can see our street, the white house, our roof, and, if you squint, the window where Biscuit would be sitting, furious that dinner was late.

Thirty-four kilometres, about 2,300 metres of climbing by my map, fourteen hours door to door. Biscuit, when we got in, was exactly as furious as predicted. Jonas fed him on his knees.

---

On the way down Jonas said: "So. What now?" He meant dinner. I heard the other question too, and for once it didn't frighten me.
