The Minho is the border. On the map it's a blue line. At Caminha it's a wide grey estuary with sandbanks, a mountain on the far side, and no bridge for miles.

The ferry wasn't running: the tide, or the sandbanks, or a Thursday. A man with a small open boat was taking pilgrims across instead, five at a time, for six euros each, with a life jacket apiece that would have floated a sheep. Ten minutes on the water. A German couple, two sisters from Cork, me.

Halfway across, the clocks change. Spain is an hour ahead. The man said so, and all five of us looked at our phones at the same moment, like a choir.

Then sand, and a path through pines, and the first yellow arrow in Galicia painted on a rock, and Monte Santa Trega standing over everything.

New country. Same feet.
