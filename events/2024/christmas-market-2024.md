A wooden hut two metres wide at the Christmas market, a trestle table, a string of lights, Jonas on the cash box and Anna on the hot chocolate, and on the board behind me all six maps: Sandviken, Nordnes, the seven mountains, Bryggen, the fish market, and a new one of Utne with a small house above it that nobody else will notice.

Saturday was slow and cold. On Sunday it snowed lightly at eleven, and suddenly everyone in Bergen wanted a map of where they lived.

Forty-one prints in two days. Sandviken sold out by three on Sunday. A man bought the seven mountains for his father, who walked them every year until he couldn't. A girl of about ten paid for the fish market with her own money, all in coins, and asked me to sign it to her, spelling her name out letter by letter.

My first stall was in Edinburgh, seven years ago: fourteen notebooks, frozen hands, the mittens Nana knitted me. I wore the mittens today. They still work.
