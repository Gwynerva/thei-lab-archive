Twelve people on a screen, eight Monday evenings (with two weeks off for half-term and my nerves), one map each by the end. A geologist in Tromsø, two planners from a council in Wales, a student in Nairobi, a retired teacher in Stavanger who wanted to map her grandmother's farm, and seven others I came to know by what was behind them: a bookcase, a cat, a ski poster, a baby who joined most weeks.

On the first evening I was so nervous I talked for forty minutes without stopping, and then found I'd been muted for the last five.

What teaching it taught me:

- nobody reads the instructions, including me
- every map starts with someone wanting to show someone else a place
- the hardest thing in GIS is still projections, and the second hardest is naming files
