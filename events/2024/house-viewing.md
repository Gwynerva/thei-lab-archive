## Getting there

The ferry from Kvanndal to Utne in grey November light, Jonas's van, Sigrid in the passenger seat because she said she'd know the turning, and she didn't. Up from the village on a gravel road with grass down the middle, bend after bend, past the Nesheims' cider press, and then a gate, and a sign half buried in bracken: *Øvre Lid*.

## The house

Ragnhild moved to the home in Odda in the spring. She's ninety. She gave Jonas the key in an envelope in August and said someone should live there before the roof decided for them.

It's white and wooden, built in 1891, two storeys on a shelf of land above the fjord, the mountain behind it and Folgefonna across the water, a white edge in the cloud.

What we saw:

- the house
  - [fourteen windows](<ref:section:farmhouse/the-house#h:Fourteen windows> "I counted them twice"), every one single-glazed, some held in with putty and hope
  - a kitchen from 1962 and a wood stove from long before that
  - stairs so steep you go down them backwards
- the barn, bigger than the house, last year's hay still in the loft
- the orchard: forty trees, old and unpruned, a few last apples on the high branches nobody could reach
- the view, which is the one thing you can't renovate and don't need to

## What Sigrid said

We stood in the cold kitchen with our coats on and our breath showing, and I said what I'd been thinking since the gate: it's too much. The roof, the windows, the money, the winters. Me.

Sigrid, who hadn't said a word for an hour, put her hand flat on the wall by the stove, the way you'd touch a horse.

> Houses like this don't need people who know what they're doing. They need people who stay.

Then she went out to look at the trees and left us to it.

## After

Driving down in the dark, the fjord black, the ferry lit up below us, Jonas said "Well?" and I said "Well." Which in our house now means yes.

Ragnhild wants rent, not a sale, for now; then, if we stay, a price. We'll see her in Odda next weekend. I've already drawn the house on the map of Hardanger on our kitchen wall: a small square with fourteen dots.
