New Year's Eve, nine in the morning, the steps at Nordnes. Four degrees in the water. The same women, and the same old man in the same woolly hat, who has done this every winter for thirty years and still says "brrr" out loud, like a cartoon.

The last three, from the notebook:

98. Sunday, grey, four minutes, a seal's head out by the buoy (or a log)
99. Monday, sleet, three minutes, swore
100. Today, still water, a pink sky over Løvstakken, five minutes, nobody counting but me

A hundred swims since [the first one](ref:event:first-winter-swim "forty seconds, in December sleet"). Some of them in summer, which hardly count; most in the dark months, which count double.

The old man shook my hand on the steps. The women had brought a flask of coffee and a tin of *krumkaker*, and somebody had a bottle of something orange, which I declined, at nine in the morning, mostly.

A year ago I couldn't choose a loaf of bread. This morning I walked into the sea in December and stayed for five minutes.\
*Godt nytt år.*
